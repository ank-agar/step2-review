## Sialolithiasis

### Core idea

Sialolithiasis = a salivary gland stone.

Break it down:

* sialo- = saliva/salivary gland
* lith = stone
* -iasis = condition

So it literally means:

> stone disease of the salivary gland/duct.

---

### What is happening

A calcified stone forms inside a salivary duct, most often the submandibular duct, also called Wharton's duct.

That stone blocks saliva from draining into the mouth.

So when the person eats, saliva production increases, but the saliva gets stuck behind the stone.

That causes:

* painful swelling near the jaw/floor of mouth
* worse pain with meals
* decreased saliva flow
* possible infection/inflammation of the gland

---

### Why meals make it worse

Food stimulates salivary glands.

Normally:

* gland makes saliva
* saliva flows through duct
* saliva enters mouth

In sialolithiasis:

* gland makes saliva
* stone blocks the duct
* pressure builds up behind the blockage
* gland swells and hurts

This is why the classic clue is:

> recurrent salivary gland pain/swelling during meals.

---

### Why submandibular gland is most common

Submandibular saliva is thicker and more mucin-rich, and Wharton's duct has to drain upward against gravity.

So saliva is more likely to stagnate there.

Stagnant saliva makes it easier for calcium salts to precipitate and form a stone.

---

### Imaging clue

On ultrasound, the stone is bright/echogenic and creates posterior acoustic shadowing.

Posterior acoustic shadowing = the sound waves cannot pass through the hard stone well, so a dark shadow appears behind it.

CT (computed tomography) can also show the calcified stone well.

---

## One-line intuition

Sialolithiasis is basically a "kidney stone" idea, but in a salivary duct: a calcified plug blocks fluid flow and causes painful backup when saliva production increases.

---

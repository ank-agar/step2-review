### Core idea

Tinea barbae is a dermatophyte (keratin-eating fungus) infection of the beard/mustache area.

Break it down:

* **tinea** = fungal “ringworm” infection
* **barbae** = beard

So:

> fungal infection of beard hair follicles.

---

### Why it looks inflamed

Dermatophytes like keratin (the tough protein in skin, hair, and nails).

In tinea barbae, the fungus gets into coarse facial hairs and hair follicles, so the immune system reacts around the follicle.

That causes:

* red, swollen plaques
* pustules around hairs
* crusting
* loose/easily pulled hairs
* sometimes a boggy inflammatory mass called a kerion (very inflamed fungal folliculitis)

---

### Classic source

Often from animals, especially:

* cattle
* horses

The common organisms are zoophilic dermatophytes (animal-associated fungi), like:

* **Trichophyton verrucosum** from cattle
* **Trichophyton mentagrophytes** from animals

That animal source is why it can be more inflammatory than mild ringworm.

---

### Diagnosis

Confirm with:

* KOH prep (potassium hydroxide microscopy) of skin scrapings/hair
* fungal culture

KOH helps dissolve skin cells so fungal hyphae (branching fungal filaments) can be seen.

---

### Treatment

Usually needs oral antifungals because the infection involves hair follicles.

Common options:

* terbinafine
* itraconazole
* griseofulvin

Topical antifungal cream alone often fails because it does not penetrate deeply enough into infected hairs/follicles.

---

## One-line intuition

Tinea barbae is basically “ringworm of the beard,” but because it invades hair follicles, it becomes pustular and inflamed and usually needs oral treatment.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Tinea barbae can mimic bacterial folliculitis, but follicular pustules plus easily plucked beard hairs should make you think dermatophyte infection.

### Core idea

Papillary muscle rupture is when a papillary muscle tears, usually after an MI (myocardial infarction/heart attack).

Papillary muscles normally pull on the chordae tendineae (tendon-like strings attached to heart valves) so the mitral valve does not flop backward during systole (ventricular contraction).

If the papillary muscle ruptures:

* the mitral valve loses its anchor
* the valve leaflet flails backward
* blood shoots from the LV (left ventricle) back into the LA (left atrium)

That causes acute severe MR (mitral regurgitation).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **New loud holosystolic murmur:** blood leaks backward through the mitral valve during all of systole
* **Pulmonary edema:** blood backs up into the LA, then pulmonary veins, then lungs
* **Cardiogenic shock:** forward blood flow to the body drops because a lot of LV blood is going backward
* **Occurs days after MI:** necrotic (dead) heart muscle becomes weak and can tear

---

### Important association

Most commonly involves the **posteromedial papillary muscle**.

Why?

* posteromedial papillary muscle usually has single blood supply from the RCA (right coronary artery)
* anterolateral papillary muscle has dual blood supply from the LAD (left anterior descending artery) and LCX (left circumflex artery)

So the posteromedial muscle is more vulnerable to ischemia (low blood flow).

---

## One-line intuition

Papillary muscles are the “valve anchors”; if one snaps after an MI, the mitral valve flails open and causes sudden severe mitral regurgitation.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Post-MI mechanical complications classically happen **3-7 days after MI** because macrophages digest dead tissue, making the heart wall/muscle weak and rupture-prone.

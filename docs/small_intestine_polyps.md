### Core idea

Small intestine polyps are abnormal tissue growths that stick out from the lining of the small intestine.

Small intestine = duodenum + jejunum + ileum.

Polyp = a bump/growth projecting into the lumen (the hollow inside space of the gut tube).

---

### Why they matter

Most are benign (not cancer), but they matter because they can:

* bleed
* cause iron deficiency anemia
* block the bowel
* act as a lead point for intussusception
* rarely become cancerous, depending on type

Intussusception = one segment of bowel telescopes into another segment.

---

### Why symptoms happen

* **Bleeding/anemia:** the polyp surface can be fragile and ulcerate
* **Abdominal pain/vomiting:** a large polyp can narrow the lumen and obstruct flow
* **Intussusception:** the bowel grabs the polyp like a handle and pulls that segment inward
* **Often no symptoms:** small polyps may not disturb flow or bleed

---

### Common associations

High-yield small bowel polyp associations:

* **Peutz-Jeghers syndrome** = hamartomatous polyps + dark mucocutaneous spots
* **Familial adenomatous polyposis** = many adenomatous polyps; can involve the duodenum
* sporadic adenomas
* inflammatory polyps
* lipomas

Hamartoma = disorganized overgrowth of tissue native to that location.

Adenoma = gland-forming benign tumor that can be premalignant.

---

### How they are found

Small intestine polyps are harder to see than colon polyps because most of the small bowel is not reached by regular upper endoscopy or colonoscopy.

So doctors may use:

* capsule endoscopy
* CT enterography (computed tomography imaging optimized for small bowel)
* balloon enteroscopy

Enteroscopy = endoscopy designed to reach deeper into the small intestine.

---

## One-line intuition

A small intestine polyp is basically a mucosal bump growing into the gut tube; if big enough, it can bleed or physically block/telescope the bowel.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Peutz-Jeghers syndrome is the classic Step 1 association with small intestine hamartomatous polyps, mucocutaneous hyperpigmentation, and increased cancer risk.

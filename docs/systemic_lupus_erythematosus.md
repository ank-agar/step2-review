Systemic lupus erythematosus, or **SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus)**, is a chronic autoimmune disease where the body makes antibodies against its own nuclear material.

Basically:

* immune system attacks self
* especially DNA (genetic material) and nuclear proteins
* forms immune complexes (antibody-antigen clumps)
* immune complexes deposit in tissues
* inflammation happens all over the body

That is why it is **systemic**: it can hit skin, joints, kidneys, blood cells, serosal surfaces (thin linings around organs), and the nervous system.

---

### Core idea

SLE is a **type III hypersensitivity disease**.

Type III hypersensitivity means:

> immune complexes deposit in tissues and activate complement (inflammatory blood proteins).

So the damage is not usually because one antibody directly blocks one receptor.

It is more like:

> antibody + self-antigen complexes get stuck in tissues, then complement and neutrophils cause inflammation.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Malar rash:** immune inflammation in sun-exposed facial skin
* **Photosensitivity:** UV light (ultraviolet light) increases cell damage, exposing nuclear antigens
* **Arthritis:** immune complexes inflame joints
* **Kidney disease:** immune complexes deposit in glomeruli (kidney filtering units)
* **Pleuritis/pericarditis:** inflammation of serosa (thin lining around lungs/heart)
* **Anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia:** immune destruction or inflammation affecting blood cells

Kidney involvement is called **lupus nephritis**.

Nephritis means kidney inflammation.

Classic biopsy clue:

* **wire-loop lesions**

because immune complex deposits thicken glomerular capillary loops, making them look like rigid loops of wire.

---

### Important antibodies

High-yield SLE antibodies:

* **ANA (antinuclear antibody):** very sensitive, but not specific
* **anti-dsDNA (anti-double-stranded DNA antibody):** specific, associated with lupus nephritis
* **anti-Smith antibody:** very specific
* **antiphospholipid antibodies:** can cause thrombosis (blood clots) and pregnancy loss

Think:

> ANA screens, anti-dsDNA tracks kidneys, anti-Smith confirms.

---

### Complement

Complement levels are often low during active SLE.

Why?

Because immune complexes activate and consume complement.

So active lupus can show:

* low C3 (complement component 3)
* low C4 (complement component 4)
* high anti-dsDNA

especially if lupus nephritis is flaring.

---

## One-line intuition

SLE is the immune system attacking nuclear material, forming immune complexes that get deposited all over the body.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Drug-induced lupus is classically associated with **anti-histone antibodies** and drugs like hydralazine, procainamide, and isoniazid.

### Core idea

Vulvar dysplasia means abnormal, precancerous growth of squamous cells in the vulvar epithelium.

* vulvar = involving the vulva, the external female genital area
* dysplasia = disordered cell growth
* epithelium = surface lining tissue
* precancerous = not invasive cancer yet, but can progress to cancer

The more formal term is VIN (vulvar intraepithelial neoplasia).

Break it down:

* intraepithelial = still inside the epithelial layer
* neoplasia = new abnormal growth

So VIN means:

> abnormal squamous cells growing within the vulvar surface lining, without invading deeper tissue.

---

### Why it matters

Vulvar dysplasia is important because it can be a precursor to vulvar squamous cell carcinoma (cancer of squamous epithelial cells).

But the key Step 1 idea:

* dysplasia/VIN = abnormal cells are still above the basement membrane
* invasive cancer = abnormal cells have crossed the basement membrane

So the basement membrane is the big line between "precancer" and "invasive cancer."

---

### Main causes

There are 2 major pathways:

* HPV-related VIN, usually from high-risk HPV (human papillomavirus), especially HPV 16
* non-HPV VIN, often associated with lichen sclerosus (chronic inflammatory thinning/whitening of vulvar skin)

HPV causes dysplasia because viral proteins mess with tumor suppressors:

* E6 inhibits p53
* E7 inhibits Rb (retinoblastoma protein)

That removes cell-cycle brakes, so squamous cells proliferate abnormally.

---

### What it can look like

It may cause:

* vulvar itching
* burning or pain
* white, red, brown, or raised vulvar patches
* sometimes no symptoms

Diagnosis is by biopsy (taking tissue to look under the microscope), because appearance alone cannot reliably tell benign irritation from dysplasia or cancer.

---

## One-line intuition

Vulvar dysplasia is like cervical dysplasia, but on vulvar squamous epithelium: abnormal cells are growing in the surface layer, but they have not invaded yet.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: HPV (human papillomavirus) 16 is strongly associated with vulvar HSIL (high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion), and HPV proteins E6/E7 knock out p53/Rb tumor suppressor function.

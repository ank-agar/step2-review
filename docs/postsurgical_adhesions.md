# Postsurgical adhesions

### Core idea

Postsurgical adhesions are bands of scar tissue that form after surgery and make tissues stick together when they normally should slide freely.

Most commonly this happens in the abdomen or pelvis, where loops of bowel, peritoneum (thin lining of the abdominal cavity), uterus, ovaries, or abdominal wall can get stuck to each other.

---

### Why surgery causes them

Surgery injures tissue.

Injury causes:

* inflammation
* fibrin deposition (sticky clot-like protein mesh)
* fibroblast activation (scar-making cells)
* collagen deposition (strong scar protein)

Normally, the body breaks down the temporary fibrin mesh as healing finishes.

But if fibrin is not cleared well, it becomes organized into permanent scar tissue.

So the intuition is:

> surgery creates a sticky healing surface, and if that sticky layer matures into scar, nearby organs get glued together.

---

### Why they matter

Adhesions can cause:

* chronic abdominal or pelvic pain
* infertility if fallopian tubes or ovaries are distorted
* small bowel obstruction (blockage of the small intestine)
* harder future surgeries because normal anatomy is distorted

---

### Small bowel obstruction connection

If an adhesion wraps around or pulls on bowel, it can kink the intestine.

That causes:

* crampy abdominal pain
* vomiting
* distention
* constipation or no flatus (no passing gas)

This is why prior abdominal surgery is a huge clue in a patient with small bowel obstruction.

---

## One-line intuition

Postsurgical adhesions are internal scar bands that glue organs together after surgery.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: the most common cause of small bowel obstruction in patients with prior abdominal surgery is adhesions.

# Anemia of chronic disease

![Image](https://dm5migu4zj3pb.cloudfront.net/manuscripts/21000/21441/medium/JCI0421441.f1.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539871/bin/Hepcidin_001.jpg)

![Image](https://imagebank.hematology.org/getimagebyid/63529?size=3)

### Core idea

Anemia of chronic disease is anemia caused by **long-term inflammation**.

The body has iron, but it hides the iron away so the bone marrow cannot use it well.

Usually happens with chronic inflammatory states like:

* chronic infection
* autoimmune disease, like rheumatoid arthritis
* cancer
* chronic kidney disease

So the key idea is:

> iron is present, but trapped.

---

### Main mechanism

Inflammation increases IL-6 (interleukin-6, an inflammatory cytokine).

IL-6 tells the liver to make more **hepcidin**.

Hepcidin is the hormone that blocks iron movement into the blood.

It does this by inhibiting **ferroportin**.

Ferroportin = the iron export channel that lets iron leave:

* intestinal cells
* macrophages (immune cells that recycle old RBCs, or red blood cells)

So high hepcidin causes:

* decreased iron absorption from the gut
* decreased iron release from macrophages
* low serum iron
* less iron available for hemoglobin production

Hemoglobin = the oxygen-carrying protein inside RBCs.

---

### Why ferritin is high

Ferritin is the intracellular iron storage protein.

In anemia of chronic disease, iron gets stored inside macrophages instead of being released into blood.

So:

* serum iron is low
* ferritin is normal or high

This is the big contrast with iron deficiency anemia.

In iron deficiency anemia, the body truly lacks iron, so ferritin is low.

In anemia of chronic disease:

> the pantry has iron, but inflammation locked the pantry door.

---

### Lab pattern

Classic iron studies:

* decreased serum iron
* decreased TIBC (total iron-binding capacity)
* normal or increased ferritin
* normal or decreased transferrin

TIBC (total iron-binding capacity) roughly reflects transferrin, the iron-carrying protein in blood.

Why TIBC decreases:

The liver makes less transferrin during inflammation because the body is trying to keep iron away from microbes and inflamed tissue.

So the body is basically saying:

> stop transporting iron around.

---

### Cell size

Anemia of chronic disease is usually:

* normocytic anemia early
* sometimes microcytic anemia later

Normocytic = normal MCV (mean corpuscular volume), meaning RBC size is normal.

Microcytic = low MCV, meaning RBCs are small.

Why it can become microcytic:

If iron is unavailable for long enough, the marrow cannot make enough hemoglobin, so RBCs become smaller.

---

### Chronic kidney disease connection

Chronic kidney disease can cause anemia of chronic disease for 2 reasons:

* inflammation increases hepcidin
* damaged kidneys make less EPO

EPO (erythropoietin) is the kidney hormone that tells bone marrow to make RBCs.

So in chronic kidney disease:

> less iron availability + less EPO = decreased RBC production.

---

## One-line intuition

Anemia of chronic disease = inflammation raises hepcidin, which traps iron in storage and starves the bone marrow of usable iron.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Step 1 contrast: iron deficiency anemia has **low ferritin and high TIBC**, while anemia of chronic disease has **normal/high ferritin and low TIBC**.

### Core idea

Periorbital cellulitis means bacterial infection of the eyelid/skin around the eye **in front of the orbital septum**.

Orbital septum = thin connective tissue barrier separating:

* preseptal space = eyelid/skin side
* postseptal/orbital space = deeper orbit with eye muscles, fat, optic nerve

So periorbital cellulitis is basically:

> cellulitis around the eye, but not yet behind the septum.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Red, swollen eyelid:** infection causes local inflammation and edema (fluid swelling)
* **Tenderness/warmth:** inflammatory blood flow increases in the skin
* **Usually normal eye movement:** extraocular muscles (muscles that move the eye) are behind the septum and usually not involved
* **Usually no proptosis:** proptosis means eye bulging forward; that happens when deeper orbital tissues swell
* **Usually normal vision:** optic nerve and globe are not the main site of infection

---

### Big distinction

Periorbital cellulitis = **anterior to septum**

Orbital cellulitis = **posterior to septum**

Orbital cellulitis is more dangerous because infection is near:

* extraocular muscles
* optic nerve
* cavernous sinus
* brain/meninges (brain coverings)

Red flags for orbital cellulitis:

* pain with eye movement
* restricted eye movement
* proptosis
* decreased vision
* fever/toxic appearance

---

### Common causes

Often follows:

* insect bite
* eyelid trauma/cut
* stye or eyelid infection
* sinusitis

Common bacteria:

* Staphylococcus aureus
* Streptococcus species

---

## One-line intuition

Periorbital cellulitis is a skin/eyelid infection **outside the orbit**; orbital cellulitis is when it crosses deeper behind the septum and threatens vision/brain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On exams, **pain with extraocular movements** strongly points toward orbital cellulitis, not simple periorbital cellulitis.

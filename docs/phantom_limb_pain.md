# Phantom limb pain

Phantom limb pain is pain felt in a limb that is no longer there, usually after amputation.

So the patient may say:

* "my missing foot burns"
* "my missing hand is cramping"
* "my missing leg feels crushed"

Even though the limb is gone, the nervous system still has a map of that limb.

---

### Core idea

The limb is gone, but the brain's body map is still there.

Your brain has a sensory map called the somatosensory homunculus (body map in the sensory cortex).

After amputation:

* the limb stops sending normal sensory input
* damaged nerves near the stump can fire abnormally
* the spinal cord and brain may become hypersensitive
* nearby brain-map areas can reorganize into the old limb area

So the brain may misinterpret abnormal signals as pain coming from the missing limb.

---

### Why the pain happens

Think of it like this:

The brain expects signals from the limb.

But after amputation, the incoming signal is missing or distorted.

That mismatch can create pain because:

* injured peripheral nerves can form neuromas (disorganized nerve growths)
* the spinal cord can amplify pain signals
* the cortex (outer brain layer) still has a representation of the missing limb
* cortical remapping (nearby brain areas taking over unused brain territory) can confuse localization

So the pain is real, but the location is "phantom."

---

### Important distinction

Phantom limb pain:

* pain felt in the missing limb

Residual limb pain:

* pain felt in the stump/residual limb

This distinction matters because stump pain may come from wound issues, infection, poor prosthetic fit, or neuroma.

---

### Why mirror therapy can help

Mirror therapy uses a mirror to make the intact limb look like the missing limb.

The patient moves the intact limb while watching the mirror image.

This gives the brain visual feedback that the missing limb is moving normally.

That can reduce the mismatch between:

* what the brain expects
* what the body is actually sending

One-line intuition:

Mirror therapy tricks the brain into "seeing" the phantom limb move, which can help quiet the painful body-map mismatch.

---

## One-line intuition

Phantom limb pain is real pain from a missing limb because the nervous system still has a painful, active map of that limb.

---

## Pearl

Phantom limb pain is a type of neuropathic pain (pain from abnormal nerve signaling), so it often feels burning, shooting, electric, or cramping rather than dull/achy.

### Core idea

Ptosis = drooping of the upper eyelid.

It happens when the eyelid elevator system is weak or not getting the right nerve signal.

The main eyelid lifters are:

* levator palpebrae superioris muscle = main upper eyelid lifting muscle
* CN III (oculomotor nerve) = nerve that powers the levator
* Müller muscle = small sympathetic-innervated eyelid muscle

So if the muscle, nerve, neuromuscular junction, or sympathetic input fails, the eyelid falls.

---

### Why it happens

Ptosis can come from:

* **CN III palsy:** levator palpebrae superioris loses motor input
* **Horner syndrome:** loss of sympathetic input to Müller muscle
* **myasthenia gravis:** antibodies block ACh receptors (acetylcholine receptors) at the NMJ (neuromuscular junction), so eyelid muscles fatigue
* **congenital ptosis:** levator muscle did not develop/function normally
* **mechanical ptosis:** something weighs the lid down, like swelling, tumor, or excess tissue

---

### How to separate the big Step 1 causes

**CN III palsy:**

* ptosis
* eye points “down and out”
* possible dilated pupil if parasympathetic fibers are involved

**Horner syndrome:**

* mild ptosis
* miosis (small pupil)
* anhidrosis (decreased sweating)

**Myasthenia gravis:**

* fluctuating ptosis
* worse with use/fatigue
* improves with rest or ice pack test

---

## One-line intuition

The eyelid droops because the “eyelid elevator” is not pulling hard enough.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Ptosis + miosis + anhidrosis = Horner syndrome, caused by loss of sympathetic innervation.

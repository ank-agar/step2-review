## Craniopharyngioma

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/08/Hypothalamus_and_Pituitary.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6e/Craniopharyngioma2.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b6/Adamantinomatous_craniopharyngioma_-_very_low_mag.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/89/Papillary_craniopharyngioma.jpg)

Craniopharyngioma is a benign tumor near the pituitary gland, usually in the sellar/suprasellar region.

* sellar = near the sella turcica, the bony seat where the pituitary sits
* suprasellar = above the sella

It comes from remnants of Rathke pouch, which is embryologic tissue that forms the anterior pituitary.

---

### Core idea

Think:

> leftover embryologic pituitary tissue grows into a mass near the optic chiasm.

The optic chiasm is where optic nerve fibers cross, so compression causes visual problems.

Classic symptom:

* bitemporal hemianopia

Bitemporal hemianopia means loss of the outer visual fields in both eyes.

---

### Why the findings happen

Because the tumor sits near the pituitary, hypothalamus, and optic chiasm:

* **Visual field loss:** compresses the optic chiasm
* **Headache:** mass effect/increased pressure
* **Growth delay in children:** pituitary hormone disruption
* **Hypopituitarism:** decreased pituitary hormone output
* **Diabetes insipidus:** posterior pituitary/hypothalamic involvement causes low ADH (antidiuretic hormone)

Diabetes insipidus means the kidney cannot concentrate urine well, so patients pee a lot and get very thirsty.

---

### Pathology clue

Craniopharyngiomas often have:

* cysts
* calcifications
* cholesterol-rich fluid

This is why CT (computed tomography) can show calcifications, while MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) is better for mapping the mass near the pituitary/optic chiasm.

---

## One-line intuition

Craniopharyngioma is a benign Rathke pouch tumor that grows above the pituitary and causes vision + endocrine problems by compression.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Craniopharyngioma is classically seen in children and often causes bitemporal hemianopia because it compresses the optic chiasm from below.

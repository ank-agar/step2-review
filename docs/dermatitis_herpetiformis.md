### Core idea

Dermatitis herpetiformis is an intensely itchy, blistering rash associated with celiac disease.

Name is misleading:

* herpetiformis = grouped like herpes lesions
* but it is not caused by herpes virus

---

### Why it happens

Gluten exposure triggers IgA (immunoglobulin A, an antibody type) immune response.

That IgA deposits in the dermal papillae (small projections in the upper dermis of skin).

This causes:

* neutrophil attraction
* inflammation
* tiny vesicles/blisters
* very itchy rash

Classic distribution:

* elbows
* knees
* buttocks
* scalp/shoulders

---

### Diagnosis

Best test:

* skin biopsy with direct immunofluorescence

Classic finding:

* granular IgA deposits at dermal papillae

---

## One-line intuition

Dermatitis herpetiformis = celiac-related IgA deposits in skin causing intensely itchy grouped vesicles.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Treatment is gluten-free diet plus dapsone for rapid itch relief; dapsone can cause hemolysis, especially in G6PD (glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase) deficiency.

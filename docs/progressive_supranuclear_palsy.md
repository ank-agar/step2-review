# Progressive supranuclear palsy

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/4670/3008928/b413b0b08222/fneur-01-00147-g001.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK526098/bin/Figure__1.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8e/Steele-olszewski-richardson_disease.jpg)

Progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP) is a neurodegenerative disease (brain cells slowly die over time) that causes:

* parkinsonism (Parkinson-like movement symptoms)
* early falls
* vertical gaze palsy (trouble moving the eyes up/down)
* axial rigidity (stiffness mainly in the neck/trunk)
* cognitive changes

Break the name apart:

* progressive = gets worse over time
* supranuclear = problem is above the cranial nerve nuclei (control centers for eye muscles)
* palsy = weakness/paralysis of movement

So PSP means:

> a progressive disease where higher brainstem control of eye movement fails, especially vertical gaze.

---

### Core idea

PSP is a tauopathy (disease from abnormal tau protein buildup).

Tau builds up especially in the:

* midbrain
* basal ganglia
* brainstem gaze centers

That is why the classic finding is:

* trouble looking down first, then up/down overall

Downward gaze matters a lot because people need it to see the ground/stairs, so patients fall early.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Vertical gaze palsy:** degeneration of vertical gaze centers in the rostral midbrain (upper midbrain)
* **Early backward falls:** impaired postural reflexes + inability to look down well
* **Parkinsonism:** basal ganglia involvement causes bradykinesia (slow movement) and rigidity
* **Poor levodopa response:** unlike Parkinson disease, this is not mainly just dopamine loss in the substantia nigra
* **Hummingbird sign on MRI:** midbrain atrophy (shrinking) with relative preservation of the pons creates a bird-like silhouette

---

### How to distinguish from Parkinson disease

Parkinson disease usually has:

* resting tremor
* asymmetric symptoms
* good response to levodopa
* falls later

PSP usually has:

* early falls
* vertical gaze palsy
* axial rigidity
* poor levodopa response

---

## One-line intuition

PSP is Parkinson-like disease plus early falls and vertical gaze problems because tau damages the midbrain gaze/posture circuitry.

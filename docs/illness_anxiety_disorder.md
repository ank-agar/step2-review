### Core idea

Illness anxiety disorder is intense, persistent fear of having or getting a serious illness, even when medical evaluation is reassuring.

Old name:

* hypochondriasis

Key idea:

* the main problem is **worry about illness**
* not the actual physical symptoms

Usually the person has:

* no symptoms, or only mild symptoms
* high anxiety about health
* repeated body checking, Googling, or reassurance seeking
* or avoidance of doctors/hospitals because they fear bad news

---

### Why it happens

A normal body sensation gets misread as dangerous.

Example:

* headache → “what if this is a brain tumor?”
* chest tightness → “what if I’m having a heart attack?”
* fatigue → “what if this is cancer?”

Then anxiety activates the sympathetic nervous system (fight-or-flight system), causing real physical symptoms:

* palpitations
* sweating
* dizziness
* shortness of breath
* muscle tension

Those symptoms feel like “proof” that something is wrong, so the cycle continues.

---

### The cycle

* body sensation
* catastrophic interpretation
* anxiety rises
* more physical symptoms
* checking/Googling/reassurance
* brief relief
* doubt returns

So reassurance helps briefly, but it reinforces the brain’s idea that “I only survived because I checked.”

---

### Step 1 distinction

**Illness anxiety disorder:**

* fear of disease
* minimal or no somatic symptoms

**Somatic symptom disorder:**

* actual physical symptoms are prominent
* distress is focused on the symptoms themselves

Somatic means body-related.

---

### Treatment

Main treatment:

* CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy)

CBT helps the patient:

* stop catastrophizing normal sensations
* reduce checking/reassurance behaviors
* tolerate uncertainty

SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors) can also help if anxiety is significant.

---

## One-line intuition

Illness anxiety disorder is when the brain treats normal body sensations like dangerous medical evidence.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For USMLE, illness anxiety disorder requires health-related worry for at least **6 months**, with few or no physical symptoms.

# Venous thromboembolism

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK507708/bin/DVT.jpg)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/blood-clots/media/images/vteriskinfogr.png)

### Core idea

Venous thromboembolism (VTE) means a blood clot forms in the venous system and can travel.

It includes 2 related problems:

* deep vein thrombosis (DVT): clot in a deep vein, usually in the leg
* pulmonary embolism (PE): clot breaks off and travels to the lung arteries

So the simple pathway is:

> leg vein clot -> travels through right heart -> blocks pulmonary artery

---

### Break the word apart

* venous = in a vein
* thrombo- = clot
* embolism = something travels through blood and gets stuck somewhere else

So:

> VTE is a vein clot that can embolize, meaning travel and lodge downstream.

---

### Why it happens

Think Virchow triad:

* stasis: slow blood flow, like immobility after surgery or long travel
* endothelial injury: vessel wall damage, like trauma or surgery
* hypercoagulability: blood is easier to clot, like cancer, pregnancy, estrogen therapy, or inherited thrombophilias

Why veins?

Venous blood flows slower and at lower pressure than arterial blood, so it is more prone to clotting when flow gets sluggish.

---

### What it looks like clinically

DVT (deep vein thrombosis) can cause:

* unilateral leg swelling
* calf pain/tenderness
* warmth/redness

PE (pulmonary embolism) can cause:

* sudden dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* pleuritic chest pain (sharp pain worse with breathing)
* tachycardia (fast heart rate)
* hemoptysis (coughing blood)
* syncope (fainting) if massive

---

## One-line intuition

VTE is basically a deep vein clot that becomes dangerous because it can break off and plug the lungs.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Cancer causes hypercoagulability, so an unexplained or recurrent VTE (venous thromboembolism) can be a clue to occult malignancy, especially pancreatic adenocarcinoma.

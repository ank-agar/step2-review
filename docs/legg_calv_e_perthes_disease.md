### Core idea

Legg-Calve-Perthes disease is **avascular necrosis (bone death from loss of blood supply)** of the femoral head in a child.

The femoral head is the "ball" of the hip joint.

So basically:

> blood supply to the femoral head drops -> bone dies -> femoral head weakens/flattens -> child limps

---

### Who gets it

Classically:

* boys
* ages 4-10
* usually unilateral (one-sided)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Hip/groin pain:** the dying femoral head irritates the hip joint
* **Knee pain:** hip pain can be referred to the knee through shared nerve pathways
* **Limp:** the child avoids loading the painful hip
* **Limited abduction/internal rotation:** the damaged femoral head no longer moves smoothly in the acetabulum (hip socket)
* **Flattened femoral head on x-ray:** dead bone is mechanically weak, so body weight can collapse it

---

### Stages

Think:

* ischemia (low blood flow)
* necrosis (bone death)
* fragmentation
* reossification (new bone formation)
* remodeling

The key is that kids can regrow/remodel bone, but if the femoral head heals in a flattened shape, it can cause early osteoarthritis later.

---

## One-line intuition

Legg-Calve-Perthes is like a temporary "heart attack" of the femoral head in a child: low blood flow makes the ball of the hip die, crumble, and then regrow.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 contrast: **SCFE (slipped capital femoral epiphysis)** is usually an obese adolescent with the femoral head slipping off the neck, while **Legg-Calve-Perthes** is usually a younger boy with avascular necrosis of the femoral head.

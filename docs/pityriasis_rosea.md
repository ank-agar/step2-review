### Core idea

Pityriasis rosea is a benign, self-limited rash that usually starts with one larger scaly patch called a **herald patch**.

Then, days to weeks later, lots of smaller oval scaly patches appear, usually on the trunk.

---

### Classic pattern

Think:

* first: **herald patch** = the “announcement” patch
* later: smaller “daughter” patches
* distribution: follows skin tension lines on the back
* pattern: **Christmas tree rash**

So the visual is:

> one big patch shows up first, then many smaller oval patches spread along the trunk like branches.

---

### Why it happens

The exact cause is not fully proven, but it is thought to be related to viral reactivation, especially:

* HHV-6 (human herpesvirus 6)
* HHV-7 (human herpesvirus 7)

Important: this is **not** the same as HSV (herpes simplex virus), which causes cold sores or genital herpes.

---

### Why the rash looks scaly

The skin becomes inflamed, and the epidermis (outer skin layer) turns over abnormally.

That causes:

* pink/salmon-colored plaques
* fine scale
* oval lesions
* sometimes a collarette of scale, meaning a thin rim of peeling scale just inside the edge

---

### Clinical vibe

Usually:

* teenagers or young adults
* trunk > face/hands/feet
* mild itching possible
* resolves on its own in about 6-10 weeks
* no scarring

Treatment is mostly for comfort:

* topical steroids for itching
* antihistamines for itching
* reassurance

---

## One-line intuition

Pityriasis rosea is a temporary viral-like rash where one “herald” patch announces a later Christmas-tree spread of smaller scaly trunk lesions.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On Step 1, if you see **herald patch + Christmas tree distribution**, think **pityriasis rosea**, not tinea corporis (ringworm), because tinea is fungal and usually forms fewer annular lesions rather than a widespread trunk eruption after one herald patch.

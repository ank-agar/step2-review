### Core idea

Paget disease of the breast is breast cancer showing up as an eczematous nipple rash.

Usually, malignant ductal cells spread from an underlying breast cancer through the lactiferous ducts (milk ducts) into the nipple epidermis (outer skin layer).

Most commonly associated with:

* DCIS (ductal carcinoma in situ = cancer cells still confined within ducts)
* invasive ductal carcinoma

---

### What it looks like

Think:

* unilateral nipple/areola redness
* itching or burning
* crusting/flaking/scaling
* ulceration or bleeding
* possible nipple discharge

The trap is that it can look like eczema.

But eczema is usually more diffuse/bilateral and improves with topical therapy.

Paget disease is suspicious when it is persistent and unilateral.

---

### Why the nipple gets involved

The cancer usually starts in the breast ducts.

Then malignant cells migrate upward through ducts toward the nipple and enter the epidermis.

So the nipple skin becomes inflamed, crusty, and abnormal because cancer cells are sitting in the skin.

On histology, you see Paget cells:

* large malignant cells
* pale/clear cytoplasm
* prominent nuclei
* scattered within the epidermis

---

### Step 1 association

Paget disease of the breast is classically linked to underlying ductal carcinoma.

So if a question stem says:

> older woman + unilateral crusting/eczema-like nipple lesion

think:

> underlying breast carcinoma until proven otherwise.

---

## One-line intuition

Breast duct cancer crawls up into the nipple skin, making the nipple look like eczema.

---

## Pearl

Paget disease of the breast is often HER2-positive (human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-positive), and biopsy of the nipple/skin is needed to confirm the diagnosis.

Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis is **ALS (amyotrophic lateral sclerosis)**, also known as **Lou Gehrig disease**.

Core idea:

* progressive degeneration of **upper motor neurons** (neurons from brain to spinal cord)
* progressive degeneration of **lower motor neurons** (neurons from spinal cord/brainstem to muscle)

So ALS gives **both UMN (upper motor neuron) and LMN (lower motor neuron) signs**.

---

### Break down the name

* **a-** = no
* **myo-** = muscle
* **trophic** = nourishment

So **amyotrophic** = muscles are not being "fed" by nerves, so they waste away.

* **lateral** = lateral corticospinal tracts are affected
* **sclerosis** = scarring/hardening from degeneration

So the name basically means:

> motor neuron degeneration causing muscle wasting + scarring of lateral spinal cord tracts.

---

### Classic findings

UMN (upper motor neuron) damage causes:

* spasticity
* hyperreflexia
* Babinski sign

LMN (lower motor neuron) damage causes:

* muscle weakness
* muscle atrophy
* fasciculations (visible muscle twitches)

That combo is the giveaway:

> weak, wasting, twitching muscles + brisk reflexes.

---

### Why symptoms happen

Motor neurons normally tell skeletal muscle to contract.

In ALS:

* motor neurons die
* muscle loses nerve input
* denervated muscle becomes weak and atrophic
* surviving motor units can fire abnormally, causing fasciculations

Later, bulbar muscles can be affected:

* dysarthria (difficulty speaking)
* dysphagia (difficulty swallowing)

Respiratory muscles can also be affected:

* diaphragm weakness
* respiratory failure

---

### Important negative

ALS usually **spares sensation**.

So the patient has motor problems, but sensory nerves are relatively intact:

* touch is preserved
* pain sensation is preserved
* vision/hearing are not the main issue

---

## One-line intuition

ALS is like the brain-to-muscle wiring progressively dying, so muscles lose control input and waste away, but sensory wiring is mostly spared.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 clue: ALS has **both UMN (upper motor neuron) and LMN (lower motor neuron) signs**, with **no sensory loss**.

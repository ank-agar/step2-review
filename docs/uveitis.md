### Core idea

Uveitis = inflammation of the uvea (middle vascular layer of the eye).

The uvea includes:

* iris
* ciliary body
* choroid

So basically:

> uveitis = immune/inflammatory cells inside the eye, especially around the pigmented vascular layer.

---

### Why symptoms happen

* **Eye pain:** inflammation irritates sensitive eye structures
* **Red eye:** vessels around the cornea become inflamed, causing ciliary flush (red ring near the cornea)
* **Photophobia (light sensitivity):** the inflamed iris hurts when the pupil moves with light
* **Blurry vision:** inflammatory cells/protein cloud the aqueous humor (fluid in the front of the eye)
* **Floaters:** inflammation can spill into the vitreous (gel in the back of the eye)

---

### Anterior uveitis

Anterior uveitis means inflammation in the front of the uvea:

* iris
* ciliary body

This is the classic painful red eye with photophobia.

You may see keratic precipitates (inflammatory cell deposits on the inner cornea).

---

### Important associations

Think autoimmune/systemic inflammatory disease:

* HLA-B27 disease (human leukocyte antigen B27 disease)
* ankylosing spondylitis
* reactive arthritis
* psoriatic arthritis
* inflammatory bowel disease
* sarcoidosis
* Behcet disease

Infections can also cause it:

* herpes simplex virus
* varicella-zoster virus
* syphilis
* tuberculosis
* toxoplasmosis

---

## One-line intuition

Uveitis is when the immune system lights up the vascular/pigmented middle layer of the eye, so the eye becomes painful, red, light-sensitive, and blurry.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HLA-B27-associated anterior uveitis is classically:

* acute
* unilateral
* recurrent
* painful red eye with photophobia

So if Step 1 gives back pain/stiffness plus recurrent painful red eye, think ankylosing spondylitis with anterior uveitis.

### Core idea

Addison disease = **primary adrenal insufficiency**.

That means the adrenal glands themselves are damaged, so they cannot make enough:

* cortisol
* aldosterone

Primary = problem is in the adrenal gland.

Adrenal insufficiency = not enough adrenal hormones.

---

### What cortisol normally does

Cortisol is a stress hormone.

It helps maintain:

* blood pressure
* blood sugar
* energy during illness/stress

So low cortisol causes:

* fatigue
* weakness
* weight loss
* low blood sugar
* poor stress tolerance

---

### What aldosterone normally does

Aldosterone is a mineralocorticoid hormone, meaning it controls salt/water balance.

It normally:

* keeps sodium
* keeps water
* dumps potassium

So low aldosterone causes:

* hyponatremia = low sodium
* hyperkalemia = high potassium
* hypotension = low blood pressure
* dehydration

---

### Why hyperpigmentation happens

In Addison disease, cortisol is low.

Low cortisol removes negative feedback on the pituitary gland, so the pituitary makes more ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone).

ACTH comes from the same precursor as MSH (melanocyte-stimulating hormone), which stimulates melanocytes (pigment-making skin cells).

So:

> low cortisol -> high ACTH -> more melanocyte stimulation -> darkened skin/mucosa

This is why Addison disease can cause darkening in:

* palmar creases
* gums
* elbows/knees
* skin folds

---

### Causes

Most common cause in developed countries:

* autoimmune destruction of the adrenal cortex

Other causes:

* tuberculosis
* adrenal hemorrhage
* metastatic cancer
* fungal infection

Adrenal cortex = outer adrenal gland layer that makes steroid hormones.

---

### Labs

Classic labs:

* low cortisol
* high ACTH
* low aldosterone
* high renin
* hyponatremia
* hyperkalemia

Renin is a kidney enzyme/hormone signal that rises when the body thinks blood pressure or kidney blood flow is too low.

---

## One-line intuition

The adrenal glands are broken, so the body loses cortisol and aldosterone; the pituitary screams louder with ACTH, causing hyperpigmentation.

---

## Pearl

Primary adrenal insufficiency has **hyperpigmentation and hyperkalemia**; secondary adrenal insufficiency usually does **not**, because ACTH is low and aldosterone is mostly preserved.

Copay means copayment.

It is a fixed amount of money you pay for a covered health service.

Example:

* $25 every time you see your PCP (primary care physician)
* $50 every time you see a specialist
* $10 every time you pick up a generic medication

The key idea:

> Copay = fixed dollar amount.

So if your visit costs $200 and your copay is $25:

* you pay $25
* insurance handles the rest according to the plan

---

### Why it exists

Insurance companies use copays so the patient shares a small predictable cost each time they use care.

This discourages unnecessary visits but still keeps routine care affordable.

---

### Copay vs coinsurance

Copay is a flat fee.

Coinsurance is a percentage.

Example:

* copay: $30 no matter how expensive the visit is
* coinsurance: 20% of the bill, so a bigger bill means you pay more

So:

> Copay = same amount each time  
> Coinsurance = percentage of the total cost

---

### Copay vs deductible

Deductible means the amount you must pay before insurance starts covering many costs.

A copay usually does not mean you are paying down your deductible.

Example:

* deductible: $1,000 per year
* copay: $30 per visit

You may still owe the full deductible even after paying several copays, depending on the plan.

---

## One-line intuition

A copay is the small fixed "entry fee" you pay when you use a covered service.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Do not confuse copay with coinsurance:

* copay = fixed dollar amount
* coinsurance = percentage
* deductible = yearly threshold before fuller insurance coverage kicks in

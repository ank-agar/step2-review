## REM sleep behavior disorder

![Image](https://pub.mdpi-res.com/brainsci/brainsci-11-01588/article_deploy/html/images/brainsci-11-01588-g001.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK390347/bin/f67.jpg)

![Image](https://www.netterimages.com/images/vtn/000/000/063/63387-150x150.jpg)

REM sleep behavior disorder is when a person physically acts out dreams during REM sleep.

REM sleep = rapid eye movement sleep, the sleep stage where vivid dreaming usually happens.

Normally during REM sleep, your body has **atonia** (temporary muscle paralysis).

So normal REM is:

* brain is dreaming
* eyes move rapidly
* skeletal muscles are “turned off”

In REM sleep behavior disorder, that muscle paralysis is lost.

So the patient can:

* punch
* kick
* yell
* jump out of bed
* injure themselves or their bed partner

---

### Core idea

The dream is normal-ish, but the body is not paralyzed like it should be.

So instead of:

> dream stays in the brain

it becomes:

> dream gets acted out by the body

---

### Why this happens

During REM sleep, brainstem circuits normally suppress spinal motor neurons.

Brainstem = lower part of the brain that controls basic body functions.

Spinal motor neurons = nerve cells in the spinal cord that activate muscles.

If those REM-paralysis circuits fail, the person keeps muscle tone during REM sleep.

That is called **REM sleep without atonia**.

REM sleep without atonia = REM sleep where the muscles are not properly paralyzed.

---

### Diagnosis clue

The key test is polysomnography.

Polysomnography = an overnight sleep study that records brain waves, eye movements, muscle activity, breathing, and oxygen levels.

The classic finding is:

* REM sleep
* plus increased electromyography activity

Electromyography = EMG (electromyography), a recording of muscle electrical activity.

So on the sleep study:

> REM sleep + high muscle activity = REM sleep without atonia

---

### Important association

REM sleep behavior disorder can be an early sign of synucleinopathies.

Synucleinopathies = neurodegenerative diseases with abnormal alpha-synuclein protein buildup.

Major associations:

* Parkinson disease
* dementia with Lewy bodies
* multiple system atrophy

This is why REM sleep behavior disorder in an older adult is not just a “sleep thing.”

It can be a clue to future neurodegenerative disease.

---

## One-line intuition

REM sleep behavior disorder = the brain is dreaming, but the body’s normal REM paralysis is missing, so the patient acts out the dream.

---

## HY pearl

REM sleep behavior disorder is strongly associated with Parkinson disease and dementia with Lewy bodies because both are alpha-synuclein disorders.

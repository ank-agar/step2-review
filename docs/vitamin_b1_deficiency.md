### Core idea

Vitamin B1 = thiamine.

Thiamine is needed to make **TPP (thiamine pyrophosphate)**, which is a cofactor for enzymes that help make energy.

So vitamin B1 deficiency mainly hurts high-energy tissues:

* brain
* peripheral nerves
* heart

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Thiamine is needed for:

* **pyruvate dehydrogenase:** pyruvate -> acetyl-CoA (acetyl coenzyme A)
* **alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase:** TCA cycle (tricarboxylic acid cycle)
* **branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase:** branched-chain amino acid metabolism
* **transketolase:** PPP (pentose phosphate pathway)

If thiamine is low:

* less ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is made
* neurons cannot maintain normal function
* pyruvate gets pushed toward lactate
* lactic acidosis can happen

Intuition:

> B1 is like a power-plug vitamin. Without it, glucose cannot efficiently become usable energy.

---

### Classic diseases

Severe thiamine deficiency can cause **beriberi**.

There are 2 major forms:

* **Dry beriberi:** peripheral neuropathy (nerve damage outside the brain/spinal cord), weakness, paresthesias (tingling/burning sensations)
* **Wet beriberi:** high-output heart failure, edema (fluid swelling), tachycardia (fast heart rate)

Why "wet"?

Because heart failure causes fluid buildup.

Why "dry"?

Because it is mainly nerve dysfunction without the big fluid-overload picture.

---

### Wernicke-Korsakoff

Thiamine deficiency can also cause **Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome**.

**Wernicke encephalopathy** is the acute form:

* confusion
* ophthalmoplegia (eye movement weakness/paralysis)
* ataxia (uncoordinated gait)

**Korsakoff syndrome** is the chronic memory form:

* anterograde amnesia (cannot form new memories)
* confabulation (unintentionally making up stories to fill memory gaps)

Intuition:

The brain is starving for ATP, especially areas involved in memory and coordination, so you get confusion, gait problems, eye findings, and memory loss.

---

### Important association

Classically seen with:

* chronic alcohol use
* malnutrition/starvation
* prolonged vomiting
* bariatric surgery (weight-loss surgery that changes stomach/intestine anatomy)

Alcohol causes thiamine deficiency because it decreases:

* intake
* absorption
* storage
* activation of thiamine

---

## One-line intuition

Vitamin B1 deficiency = low thiamine -> low ATP in nerves/brain/heart -> beriberi plus Wernicke-Korsakoff.

---

## Pearl

Give **thiamine before glucose** in a malnourished or alcohol-use patient, because glucose metabolism consumes thiamine and can precipitate/worsen Wernicke encephalopathy.

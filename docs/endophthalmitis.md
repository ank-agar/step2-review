### Core idea

Endophthalmitis is a severe infection/inflammation inside the eye, especially involving the:

* aqueous humor (watery fluid in the front chambers of the eye)
* vitreous humor (gel-like fluid filling the back of the eye)

Break the word apart:

* endo- = inside
* ophthalm = eye
* -itis = inflammation

So:

> inflammation/infection inside the eye.

---

### Why it happens

Most commonly, organisms get into the eye after:

* eye surgery, especially cataract surgery
* intravitreal injection (injection into the vitreous humor)
* penetrating trauma

Less commonly, infection spreads through the blood from somewhere else in the body. That is called endogenous endophthalmitis (infection coming from inside the body through the bloodstream).

---

### What it looks like

Classic findings:

* painful red eye
* decreased vision
* hypopyon (layer of white blood cells/pus in the anterior chamber)
* hazy vitreous humor, so the fundus (back of the eye seen on exam) is hard to visualize

The key intuition:

The inside of the eye is normally a clear optical space. When it fills with inflammatory cells and organisms, light cannot pass cleanly, so vision drops fast.

---

### Why it is scary

This is an ophthalmologic emergency because infection inside the eye can rapidly damage the retina (light-sensing layer of the eye) and cause permanent vision loss.

Treatment is urgent intravitreal antibiotics (antibiotics injected into the vitreous humor), sometimes with vitrectomy (surgical removal of infected vitreous gel).

---

## One-line intuition

Endophthalmitis = bacteria or fungi get inside the normally sterile eye fluid, causing pus/inflammation that clouds vision and can destroy the retina.

---

## Pearl 🔥

After cataract surgery, a painful red eye with decreased vision and hypopyon is endophthalmitis until proven otherwise.

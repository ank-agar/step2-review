### Core idea

Metabolic alkalosis = blood is too basic because **HCO3- (bicarbonate)** is too high or **H+ (hydrogen ion/acid)** is too low.

So on labs:

* increased pH
* increased HCO3- (bicarbonate)
* compensatory increased CO2 (carbon dioxide)

Why CO2 increases:

* the lungs slow breathing
* this retains CO2 (carbon dioxide)
* CO2 acts like acid in blood
* so it helps bring pH back down

---

### Main causes

Think:

* vomiting
* loop/thiazide diuretics
* mineralocorticoid excess, like hyperaldosteronism
* excess alkali intake, like lots of antacids

Vomiting is classic.

Why?

The stomach loses HCl (hydrochloric acid).

If you lose acid from the body, the blood becomes relatively basic.

So:

> vomiting = loss of H+ (hydrogen ion) = metabolic alkalosis

---

### Why hypokalemia happens

Hypokalemia = low K+ (potassium).

In metabolic alkalosis, H+ (hydrogen ion) shifts out of cells to help buffer the blood.

To keep charge balanced:

* K+ (potassium) shifts into cells
* serum K+ falls

Also, vomiting/diuretics can cause volume depletion.

Volume depletion activates aldosterone.

Aldosterone makes the kidney:

* reabsorb Na+ (sodium)
* waste K+ (potassium)
* waste H+ (hydrogen ion)

That worsens:

* hypokalemia
* alkalosis

---

### Chloride clue

Urine chloride helps separate causes:

* low urine Cl- (chloride) = vomiting or volume depletion, usually saline-responsive
* high urine Cl- (chloride) = ongoing diuretics or mineralocorticoid excess

Why chloride matters:

The kidney needs Cl- (chloride) to excrete HCO3- (bicarbonate).

So if chloride is low, the kidney holds onto bicarbonate, and alkalosis persists.

---

## One-line intuition

Metabolic alkalosis is basically: **too much base or too little acid in the blood, usually from vomiting or diuretics.**

---

## Pearl

Vomiting and loop/thiazide diuretics both commonly cause:

* metabolic alkalosis
* hypokalemia
* low volume state

But vomiting usually has **low urine Cl- (chloride)**, while active diuretic use usually has **high urine Cl- (chloride)**.

Wet gangrene = tissue death plus infection.

Gangrene means necrosis (death of tissue from loss of blood supply). Wet gangrene happens when that dead or poorly perfused tissue gets infected, so it becomes:

* swollen
* moist
* blistered
* foul-smelling
* dark red, purple, blue, or black

---

### Core idea

Think:

> dead tissue + bacteria = wet, spreading, dangerous gangrene

Dry gangrene is mostly ischemia (low blood flow) without major infection.

Wet gangrene is ischemia plus infection, so it spreads faster and can cause sepsis (life-threatening systemic inflammation from infection).

---

### Why it looks wet

The bacteria and inflammation cause:

* edema (fluid swelling)
* pus/discharge
* tissue breakdown
* liquefactive necrosis (enzymes digest tissue into a soft/liquid mess)

That is why it looks moist and swollen instead of dry and shriveled.

---

### Common setup

Classic risk factors:

* diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar damaging vessels and nerves)
* PAD (peripheral artery disease; narrowed arteries in the limbs)
* trauma
* infected wounds
* immunosuppression (weakened immune system)

Diabetes is especially important because:

* poor blood flow makes tissue ischemic
* neuropathy (nerve damage) means injuries may go unnoticed
* high glucose helps bacteria grow
* impaired immunity makes infection harder to control

---

### Why it is an emergency

Wet gangrene can rapidly spread through soft tissue and enter the bloodstream.

Treatment usually needs:

* broad-spectrum antibiotics (antibiotics that cover many bacteria)
* urgent surgical debridement (removal of dead/infected tissue)
* sometimes amputation
* restoring blood flow if possible

Antibiotics alone often are not enough because dead tissue has poor blood supply, so the drug cannot reach it well.

---

## One-line intuition

Wet gangrene is infected dead tissue: low blood flow kills the tissue, then bacteria invade and make it swollen, wet, foul-smelling, and rapidly dangerous.

## HY Step 1 pearl

Gas gangrene is a severe subtype associated with Clostridium perfringens, an anaerobic (oxygen-avoiding) gram-positive rod that produces alpha toxin, causing myonecrosis (muscle tissue death) and gas in tissue.

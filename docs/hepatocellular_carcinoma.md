Hepatocellular carcinoma, or HCC (hepatocellular carcinoma), is a primary liver cancer that arises from hepatocytes (liver cells).

Primary means it starts in the liver itself, not that another cancer spread to the liver.

---

### Core idea

HCC usually happens in a chronically damaged liver, especially with:

* cirrhosis (scarred, nodular liver)
* chronic HBV (hepatitis B virus)
* chronic HCV (hepatitis C virus)
* aflatoxin exposure (toxin from moldy grains/peanuts)
* alcohol-related liver disease
* NAFLD (nonalcoholic fatty liver disease)
* hemochromatosis (iron overload)

So the big intuition:

> chronic liver injury = repeated cell death/regeneration = more chances for DNA mutations = cancer.

---

### Why cirrhosis causes cancer

When hepatocytes keep getting injured, the liver keeps trying to regenerate.

More regeneration means more DNA replication.

More DNA replication means more chances for mutations.

Over time, one clone of hepatocytes can become malignant (cancerous) and grow into HCC.

---

### Important clues

HCC can present with:

* RUQ pain (right upper quadrant pain)
* weight loss
* hepatomegaly (enlarged liver)
* worsening cirrhosis symptoms
* increased AFP (alpha-fetoprotein)

AFP (alpha-fetoprotein) is a tumor marker that can be elevated in HCC.

---

### Classic associations

HBV (hepatitis B virus) is especially important because it can cause HCC even without cirrhosis.

Why?

HBV is a DNA virus that can integrate into host DNA, which can directly promote mutations/cancer.

HCV (hepatitis C virus) usually causes HCC through chronic inflammation leading to cirrhosis first.

---

### Imaging intuition

HCC tends to get blood supply from the hepatic artery (artery supplying the liver).

That is why on contrast imaging it classically shows:

* arterial enhancement
* venous/delayed washout

Meaning:

it lights up early when arterial contrast arrives, then fades compared with the surrounding liver later.

---

## One-line intuition

HCC is liver-cell cancer that usually arises after years of chronic liver injury and regeneration, especially in cirrhosis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HBV (hepatitis B virus) can cause hepatocellular carcinoma even without cirrhosis because it can integrate into host DNA.

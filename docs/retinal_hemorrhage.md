### Core idea

Retinal hemorrhage means bleeding in the retina, which is the light-sensing layer in the back of the eye.

Think:

* retina = camera film/sensor
* hemorrhage = blood leaking out of vessels

So retinal hemorrhage is basically:

> blood leaking into or around the retina.

---

### Why it happens

The retina has tiny fragile blood vessels. If those vessels get damaged or backed up with pressure, they can leak blood.

Common causes include:

* diabetes mellitus
* hypertension
* retinal vein occlusion, meaning blockage of a retinal vein
* trauma
* blood disorders like leukemia, anemia, or thrombocytopenia, meaning low platelets
* increased intracranial pressure, meaning increased pressure inside the skull

---

### What it can look like

Different shapes tell you where the blood is sitting:

* **Flame-shaped hemorrhages:** superficial retina, tracking along nerve fiber layer
* **Dot-blot hemorrhages:** deeper retinal layers, so they look rounder
* **Roth spots:** retinal hemorrhages with pale/white centers
* **Preretinal hemorrhage:** blood in front of the retina, often boat-shaped

Why flame-shaped?

Because the superficial retinal nerve fibers run in a linear pattern, so leaked blood spreads along those fibers instead of forming a perfect circle.

---

### Symptoms

Small retinal hemorrhages can be asymptomatic.

Larger or macular hemorrhages can cause:

* blurry vision
* floaters
* dark spots
* painless vision loss

Macula means the central high-acuity part of the retina, so bleeding there affects reading/detail vision more.

---

## One-line intuition

Retinal hemorrhage is blood leaking from fragile retinal vessels, and the shape of the bleed helps localize which retinal layer is involved.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Roth spots are classically associated with infective endocarditis, but they are not specific; they can also be seen with leukemia, anemia, diabetes, and hypertension.

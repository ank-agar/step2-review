# Embolic stroke

![Image](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/sites/default/files/inline-images/image.jpg)

![Image](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/-/scassets/images/org/health/articles/embolic-stroke)

![Image](https://iprsoftwaremedia.com/67/files/20179/59f343032cfac22ba70b5c20_Clot%20in%20brain%20illustration/Clot%20in%20brain%20illustration_38fdbee5-e841-4ad1-a97d-4a8f9dfee69c-prv.jpg)

An embolic stroke is an ischemic stroke (stroke from blocked blood flow) caused by an embolus (a clot or piece of material that travels through blood).

Core idea:

* a clot forms somewhere else
* it breaks off
* it travels to the brain
* it gets stuck in a smaller brain artery
* brain tissue downstream loses oxygen and starts dying

So embolic = traveling blockage.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The blocked artery supplies a specific brain region.

That region controls specific functions, so you can get sudden:

* face droop
* arm/leg weakness
* trouble speaking
* vision loss
* confusion
* dizziness/ataxia (poor coordination)

The key word is sudden, because blood flow gets cut off abruptly.

---

### Common source

Classic source is atrial fibrillation, or AFib (irregular atrial heartbeat).

Why?

In AFib, the atria (upper heart chambers) quiver instead of squeezing well.

Blood sits still, especially in the left atrial appendage (small pouch of the left atrium).

Stasis (slow/still blood flow) promotes clot formation.

Then:

left atrium -> left ventricle -> aorta -> carotid artery -> brain artery

That clot lodges in the brain and causes an embolic stroke.

---

### Thrombotic vs embolic

* Thrombotic stroke: clot forms right there in the brain/neck artery
* Embolic stroke: clot forms elsewhere and travels to the brain

Easy memory:

* thrombus = local clot
* embolus = traveling clot

---

## One-line intuition

An embolic stroke is basically a traveling clot that gets stuck in a brain artery and starves that brain region of oxygen.

---

## Pearl

AFib is a major cause of embolic stroke, so patients are often treated with anticoagulation (blood-thinning medication) to prevent clot formation.

# Dressler syndrome

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18080.jpg)

![Image](https://img.grepmed.com/uploads/4917/syndrome-diagnosis-postmyocardlal-cardiology-infarctlon-original.png)

Dressler syndrome is delayed autoimmune pericarditis (inflammation of the pericardium, the sac around the heart) after cardiac injury, classically after MI (myocardial infarction/heart attack).

Classic timing:

* weeks after MI
* often around 2-10 weeks later

---

### Core idea

Heart damage exposes cardiac/pericardial antigens (immune targets from injured heart tissue).

Then the immune system forms an inflammatory response against the pericardium.

So think:

> MI happens → immune system sees heart proteins → delayed pericarditis.

---

### What you see

Symptoms/signs are basically pericarditis:

* sharp pleuritic chest pain (pain worse with breathing)
* better when sitting up and leaning forward
* worse lying flat
* fever
* pericardial friction rub
* pericardial effusion (fluid around the heart)
* diffuse ST elevation and PR depression on ECG (electrocardiogram)

---

### Why it is delayed

It takes time for the immune system to build the response.

That is why Dressler syndrome is not the immediate post-MI chest pain problem.

Immediate/early post-MI pericarditis = direct inflammation from a transmural MI (full-thickness heart wall infarct).

Dressler = delayed immune reaction.

---

### Treatment idea

Usually:

* aspirin
* colchicine

NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) can treat pericarditis generally, but after MI, aspirin is preferred because some NSAIDs may interfere with myocardial healing.

---

## One-line intuition

Dressler syndrome is your immune system attacking the inflamed pericardium weeks after a heart attack.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, distinguish:

* early fibrinous pericarditis: 1-3 days after transmural MI
* Dressler syndrome: weeks later, autoimmune/type III hypersensitivity-like immune complex reaction

## Familial adenomatous polyposis

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1345/bin/fap-Image001.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538233/bin/FAP.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538233/bin/FPC.jpg)

Familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP) is an autosomal dominant inherited colon cancer syndrome caused by mutation in the APC (adenomatous polyposis coli) tumor suppressor gene.

Core idea:

* APC normally acts like a brake on colon epithelial cell growth
* if APC is mutated, that brake is weak
* colon cells overgrow into hundreds to thousands of adenomatous polyps
* adenomatous polyps are precancerous gland-forming growths

So FAP is basically:

> inherited APC mutation -> tons of adenomas in the colon -> very high colorectal cancer risk.

Why cancer risk is so high:

* one inherited APC mutation is already present in every cell
* the second normal APC copy can be lost in colon cells
* once both APC copies are gone, beta-catenin signaling increases
* beta-catenin drives cell proliferation
* more proliferation = more chances for more mutations = colorectal adenocarcinoma

Classic findings:

* hundreds to thousands of colon polyps
* starts young, often teenage years
* near-100% risk of colorectal cancer if untreated
* can also have duodenal adenomas, desmoid tumors, osteomas, dental abnormalities, and CHRPE (congenital hypertrophy of the retinal pigment epithelium)

Gardner syndrome is basically FAP plus extra findings like:

* osteomas
* epidermoid cysts
* desmoid tumors
* dental abnormalities

## One-line intuition

FAP is when the colon is genetically set up to make tons of precancerous polyps because the APC tumor-suppressor brake is broken.

## Pearl 🔥

FAP = APC mutation on chromosome 5, autosomal dominant, and prophylactic colectomy (preventive colon removal) is often needed because colorectal cancer risk is extremely high without treatment.

Tracheitis = inflammation/infection of the trachea (windpipe).

The trachea is the air tube below the larynx (voice box) that carries air to the bronchi (large airways going into the lungs).

---

### Core idea

The tracheal lining gets inflamed and swollen, so the airway becomes narrower.

That causes:

* cough
* fever
* stridor (high-pitched noisy breathing from upper airway narrowing)
* trouble breathing if severe

---

### Bacterial tracheitis

This is the high-yield dangerous version.

Usually it happens after a viral URI (upper respiratory infection), then bacteria infect the already-damaged tracheal lining.

Common cause:

* Staphylococcus aureus

Why it can be scary:

* bacteria cause pus/exudate (inflammatory debris)
* the swollen trachea + thick secretions can obstruct the airway

So the child often looks more toxic than simple viral croup.

---

### How to think of it

Croup = swollen upper airway from virus.

Bacterial tracheitis = croup-like symptoms PLUS pus in the trachea.

So if a kid has barking cough/stridor but looks very sick, has high fever, and does not improve with racemic epinephrine, think bacterial tracheitis.

---

### Treatment idea

Main priorities:

* secure the airway if needed
* IV (intravenous) antibiotics

Airway comes first because obstruction can kill quickly.

---

## One-line intuition

Tracheitis is an inflamed windpipe; bacterial tracheitis is dangerous because pus and swelling can plug the airway.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Bacterial tracheitis is classically a child with croup-like stridor who appears toxic and does not improve with racemic epinephrine.

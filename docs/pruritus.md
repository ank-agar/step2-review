### Core idea

Pruritus = itch.

More formally:

> an unpleasant skin sensation that makes you want to scratch.

It can come from:

* skin inflammation
* systemic disease
* nerve dysfunction
* medications

---

### Why itching happens

Something activates itch-sensitive nerve fibers in the skin.

Common triggers:

* histamine from mast cells
* cytokines (immune signaling proteins), especially IL-31 (interleukin-31)
* bile acids or other retained substances in cholestasis (impaired bile flow)
* uremic toxins in CKD (chronic kidney disease)
* dry skin irritating superficial nerves

Those nerves send signals:

skin -> spinal cord -> brain

The brain interprets it as itch and creates the urge to scratch.

---

### Why scratching feels good but worsens it

Scratching briefly activates pain/touch pathways, which can suppress itch signals.

But repeated scratching damages the skin barrier, causing:

* more inflammation
* more exposed nerve endings
* more itch mediators
* thicker skin over time

That is the itch-scratch cycle.

---

### Clinical clues

Pruritus with a rash usually points to a skin disease, like:

* atopic dermatitis (eczema)
* urticaria (hives)
* scabies
* contact dermatitis

Pruritus without a primary rash makes you think systemic or neurologic causes, like:

* cholestasis
* CKD (chronic kidney disease)
* iron deficiency
* lymphoma
* thyroid disease
* drug reaction

Scratching itself can create secondary lesions:

* excoriations = scratch marks
* lichenification = thickened skin from chronic rubbing/scratching
* prurigo nodules = itchy firm bumps from chronic scratching

---

## One-line intuition

Pruritus is your itch nerve pathway firing; scratching helps for a second but can damage skin and make the pathway even more sensitive.

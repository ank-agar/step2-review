# Myasthenia gravis in pregnancy

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Wikipedia_Project_Myasthenia_gravis_%285%29.svg/960px-Wikipedia_Project_Myasthenia_gravis_%285%29.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/62/Neuromuscular_junction.svg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/69a9/3251916/bf291e979d85/CDI2012-985646.001.jpg)

### Core idea

Myasthenia gravis in pregnancy = a pregnant patient has myasthenia gravis (MG, autoimmune weakness from impaired nerve-to-muscle signaling at the neuromuscular junction).

The main issue is:

* mom can have worsening fatigable weakness
* respiratory muscles can weaken in severe disease
* maternal IgG (immunoglobulin G, the antibody type that crosses the placenta) can cross to the fetus/newborn

So the baby can temporarily have neonatal myasthenia gravis (newborn weakness caused by mom's antibodies).

---

### Why weakness happens

Normally:

* nerve releases acetylcholine (ACh, the neurotransmitter that tells skeletal muscle to contract)
* ACh binds acetylcholine receptors (AChRs, receptors on muscle)
* muscle contracts

In MG:

* antibodies attack AChRs at the neuromuscular junction
* fewer working receptors means the muscle gets a weaker signal
* repeated use makes weakness worse because the signal is already barely strong enough

That is why symptoms are fatigable, meaning they worsen with use and improve with rest.

---

### Why pregnancy matters

Pregnancy does not create MG, but it can make MG unpredictable.

Symptoms may:

* improve
* worsen
* stay the same

The highest-risk times are often:

* first trimester
* postpartum period, meaning the weeks after delivery

Why?

Pregnancy and delivery are physiologic stressors, and immune activity shifts during and after pregnancy.

---

### Maternal risks

Watch for worsening:

* ptosis (drooping eyelid)
* diplopia (double vision)
* dysphagia (difficulty swallowing)
* dysarthria (difficulty speaking clearly)
* proximal weakness, meaning shoulder/hip weakness
* dyspnea (shortness of breath)

Respiratory weakness is the dangerous one because MG affects skeletal muscle, including the diaphragm.

Diaphragm = the main breathing muscle.

---

### Fetal/newborn issue

Mom's IgG antibodies can cross the placenta.

If those antibodies bind fetal/newborn AChRs:

* weak cry
* poor feeding
* hypotonia (low muscle tone/floppy baby)
* respiratory weakness

This is usually transient neonatal myasthenia gravis, meaning temporary newborn MG because the baby did not make the antibodies; the baby just received them from mom.

As maternal antibodies clear, symptoms improve.

---

### Delivery intuition

The uterus is smooth muscle, so MG usually does not stop uterine contractions.

But pushing uses skeletal muscle.

So:

* first stage of labor can be normal
* second stage may be harder if mom's skeletal muscles fatigue
* assisted delivery may be needed if exhaustion/weakness develops

---

### Treatment logic

Pyridostigmine is commonly used.

Pyridostigmine = acetylcholinesterase inhibitor, meaning it blocks breakdown of ACh.

More ACh stays in the neuromuscular junction, so the remaining AChRs get more chances to be activated.

For severe worsening:

* IVIG (intravenous immunoglobulin, pooled antibodies given through a vein)
* plasmapheresis (filtering antibodies out of blood)

These help quickly reduce the effect of the bad antibodies.

---

### Drugs to be careful with

Important meds can worsen MG:

* magnesium sulfate
* aminoglycosides, like gentamicin
* fluoroquinolones, like ciprofloxacin

Magnesium matters a lot in pregnancy because magnesium sulfate is used for seizure prevention in preeclampsia/eclampsia.

In MG, magnesium can worsen weakness by impairing ACh release at the neuromuscular junction.

---

## One-line intuition

Myasthenia gravis in pregnancy is mom's antibody-mediated neuromuscular weakness, with extra concern that IgG can cross the placenta and cause temporary newborn weakness.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1: **avoid magnesium sulfate in myasthenia gravis** because it can worsen neuromuscular weakness; if a pregnant MG patient has preeclampsia/eclampsia, this becomes a classic management issue.

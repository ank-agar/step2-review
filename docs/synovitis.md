### Core idea

Synovitis = inflammation of the synovium (thin lining inside a synovial joint that makes synovial fluid).

Think:

* synovium gets inflamed
* it thickens
* it makes extra fluid
* the joint becomes painful, swollen, warm, and stiff

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The synovium normally makes slippery synovial fluid to lubricate the joint.

When it is inflamed:

* **Pain:** inflammatory mediators irritate nerves
* **Swelling/effusion:** inflamed synovium leaks/makes extra fluid into the joint space
* **Warmth:** inflammation increases blood flow
* **Stiffness:** fluid + thickened synovium physically limit movement

Effusion means excess fluid inside or around a joint.

---

### Common causes

Synovitis can happen from:

* rheumatoid arthritis (RA; autoimmune inflammatory arthritis)
* osteoarthritis (degenerative joint disease)
* gout/pseudogout (crystal arthritis)
* infection
* trauma or overuse

---

## One-line intuition

The joint lining gets angry and swollen, so the joint fills with inflammatory fluid and becomes painful/stiff.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), chronic synovitis can form **pannus** (invasive inflamed granulation tissue), which eats into cartilage and bone → joint erosions/deformity.

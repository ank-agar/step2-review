Type 1 diabetes mellitus is an autoimmune disease where the immune system destroys pancreatic beta cells (insulin-producing cells in the islets of Langerhans).

So the core problem is:

* beta cell destruction
* low insulin
* high blood glucose

---

### Why glucose gets high

Insulin is the hormone that moves glucose from blood into cells, especially:

* muscle
* fat
* liver

In type 1 diabetes mellitus:

* beta cells are destroyed
* insulin becomes very low or absent
* glucose stays trapped in the blood
* cells act like they are starving, even though blood glucose is high

That is why patients can have:

* polyuria (excessive urination)
* polydipsia (excessive thirst)
* weight loss
* fatigue

---

### Why diabetic ketoacidosis happens

DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis) happens because insulin is so low that the body thinks it is fasting/starving.

So it switches to fat breakdown:

* low insulin -> fat breakdown
* fat breakdown -> ketone production
* ketones are acidic -> metabolic acidosis (acid buildup in the blood)

This is why type 1 diabetes mellitus has a high risk of DKA.

---

### Classic antibodies

Common autoantibodies include:

* anti-GAD (anti-glutamic acid decarboxylase)
* anti-islet cell antibodies
* anti-insulin antibodies
* anti-ZnT8 (anti-zinc transporter 8)

These are basically evidence that the immune system is attacking beta-cell-related targets.

---

### Type 1 vs type 2 intuition

Type 1 diabetes mellitus:

* no insulin production
* autoimmune beta-cell destruction
* low C-peptide (marker of endogenous insulin production)
* DKA risk high

Type 2 diabetes mellitus:

* insulin resistance
* insulin often high early
* C-peptide normal/high early
* DKA risk lower because some insulin is usually still present

---

## One-line intuition

Type 1 diabetes mellitus is the pancreas losing its insulin-making beta cells, so blood sugar rises and the body starts burning fat into acidic ketones.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HLA-DR3 and HLA-DR4 are strongly associated with type 1 diabetes mellitus, and patients are also at increased risk for other autoimmune diseases like celiac disease and autoimmune thyroid disease.

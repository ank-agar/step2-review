Peutz-Jeghers syndrome is an autosomal dominant inherited disease with:

* mucocutaneous pigmentation
* hamartomatous GI polyps
* increased cancer risk

Mucocutaneous means involving mucosa + skin.

GI means gastrointestinal, meaning stomach/intestines.

Hamartomatous means a disorganized overgrowth of tissue that normally belongs there.

---

### Core idea

Think:

> dark spots around the mouth + intestinal polyps + cancer risk.

The classic gene is:

* STK11, also called LKB1

STK11 is a tumor suppressor gene, meaning it normally helps hold back abnormal growth.

So if STK11 is mutated, cells lose some growth control.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Dark spots on lips/oral mucosa:** abnormal melanin pigmentation in mucocutaneous areas
* **Polyps:** disorganized growth of normal GI tissue
* **Abdominal pain/obstruction:** polyps can act as a lead point for intussusception
* **Bleeding/anemia:** polyps can bleed slowly
* **Cancer risk:** tumor suppressor loss increases risk of several cancers

Intussusception means one segment of bowel telescopes into another segment.

Anemia means low red blood cells or low hemoglobin.

---

### Important cancer risks

Peutz-Jeghers increases risk of both GI and non-GI cancers, including:

* colorectal cancer
* pancreatic cancer
* gastric cancer
* small intestinal cancer
* breast cancer
* ovarian/testicular tumors

Do not think the syndrome is just benign freckles and benign polyps.

The polyps are hamartomatous, but the syndrome still has high cancer risk.

---

## One-line intuition

Peutz-Jeghers is an STK11 tumor-suppressor syndrome where mucosal freckling tells you the gut is making hamartomatous polyps.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 clue:

* **STK11 mutation + perioral pigmentation + hamartomatous polyps = Peutz-Jeghers syndrome**

And the classic polyp histology has **arborizing smooth muscle**, meaning branching/tree-like smooth muscle inside the polyp.

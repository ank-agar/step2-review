# IgA deficiency

![Image](https://anatomynote.com/pictures/lymphatic-system/001/iga-immunity-diagram.jpg)

![Image](https://cwoer.ccbcmd.edu/science/microbiology/lecture/unit6/humoral/abystructure/images/U3fig9c.jpg)

IgA deficiency means low or absent IgA (immunoglobulin A), with normal IgG (immunoglobulin G) and IgM (immunoglobulin M).

IgA is the main antibody at mucosal surfaces, meaning areas exposed to the outside world:

* respiratory tract
* gastrointestinal tract
* genitourinary tract
* saliva, tears, breast milk

So if IgA is missing, the mucosal “front door” is weaker.

---

### Classic presentation

Most patients are asymptomatic.

If symptomatic, think:

* recurrent sinus infections
* recurrent ear infections
* recurrent pneumonia
* chronic diarrhea
* giardiasis

Giardia causes diarrhea because IgA normally helps defend the gut lumen against organisms sticking to mucosa.

---

### Why the infections happen

IgA normally sits in mucus and neutralizes pathogens before they attach to epithelial cells.

So:

* low IgA → weaker mucosal neutralization
* pathogens attach more easily
* recurrent respiratory/GI infections happen

This is why the infections are mostly mucosal, not severe systemic infections.

---

### Big association

IgA deficiency is associated with:

* atopy, meaning allergic disease
* autoimmune disease
* celiac disease

Important testing point:

If a patient has celiac disease but IgA deficiency, the usual IgA anti-tTG test can be falsely negative.

anti-tTG means anti-tissue transglutaminase antibody.

---

### Transfusion reaction

Some IgA-deficient patients make anti-IgA antibodies.

If they receive blood products containing IgA, they can have anaphylaxis, meaning a severe allergic reaction.

So they may need washed blood products.

---

## One-line intuition

IgA deficiency = missing mucosal antibody, so the nose/lungs/gut lose their first-line antibody protection.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Selective IgA deficiency is the most common primary immunodeficiency, and Step 1 loves the combo of recurrent mucosal infections + normal IgG/IgM + possible anaphylaxis to blood products.

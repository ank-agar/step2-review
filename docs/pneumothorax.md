### Core idea

Pneumothorax = air in the pleural space.

Pleural space = the tiny space between:

* visceral pleura (membrane stuck to the lung)
* parietal pleura (membrane lining the chest wall)

That space is normally negative pressure, which helps keep the lung expanded.

So if air enters that space, the lung loses its "suction" against the chest wall and collapses.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Sudden chest pain:** irritation/stretching of the pleura
* **Shortness of breath:** less lung is available for gas exchange
* **Decreased breath sounds:** air is not moving well into the collapsed lung
* **Hyperresonance to percussion:** extra air in the chest makes it sound more hollow
* **Absent lung markings on chest X-ray:** the black area is air outside the lung, not normal air inside alveoli (tiny air sacs)

---

### Tension pneumothorax

Tension pneumothorax = air enters the pleural space but cannot escape.

It acts like a one-way valve:

* more air keeps building up
* pressure rises
* lung collapses more
* mediastinum (middle chest structures: heart, trachea, great vessels) shifts away
* venous return (blood returning to the heart) decreases

That causes:

* hypotension (low blood pressure)
* jugular venous distension (bulging neck veins)
* tracheal deviation away from the affected side

This is an emergency.

---

### Classic causes

* spontaneous rupture of apical blebs (small air blisters at the top of the lung)
* trauma, like rib fracture or stab wound
* mechanical ventilation (machine-assisted breathing)
* underlying lung disease, like COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease)

---

## One-line intuition

A pneumothorax is air where air should not be: outside the lung in the pleural space, breaking the negative-pressure seal that keeps the lung open.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Primary spontaneous pneumothorax classically occurs in tall, thin young men due to rupture of apical blebs.

For tension pneumothorax, do not wait for chest X-ray if unstable: treat immediately with needle decompression, then chest tube.

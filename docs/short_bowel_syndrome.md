### Core idea

Short bowel syndrome is malabsorption because there is not enough functioning small intestine left.

Usually this happens after surgical removal of a large part of the small bowel.

So the issue is:

* less intestinal surface area
* less time for food/fluid to be absorbed
* loss of specific absorption sites

Result:

* diarrhea
* dehydration
* electrolyte loss
* weight loss/malnutrition
* vitamin deficiencies

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The small intestine is where most nutrients and fluid get absorbed.

If you remove a big chunk of it:

* food moves through too fast
* nutrients stay in the lumen
* water follows osmotically
* stool becomes watery

That is why short bowel syndrome causes chronic diarrhea and malnutrition.

---

### Why ileum loss is especially bad

The ileum is the last part of the small intestine.

It absorbs:

* vitamin B12
* bile acids

So ileal resection can cause:

* vitamin B12 deficiency
* bile acid loss
* fat malabsorption
* steatorrhea (fatty, bulky, foul-smelling stool)

Why fat malabsorption?

Bile acids normally help absorb fat. If the ileum is gone, bile acids are lost in stool instead of recycled back to the liver.

---

### Colon connection matters

If the colon is still connected, patients usually do better.

Why?

The colon can still absorb:

* water
* electrolytes
* short-chain fatty acids

So keeping the colon helps reduce dehydration and diarrhea.

---

### Treatment idea

Support nutrition while the remaining bowel adapts.

May need:

* oral rehydration
* high-calorie diet
* vitamin/mineral replacement
* antidiarrheal meds
* TPN (total parenteral nutrition, meaning nutrition given through the bloodstream instead of the gut)

Over time, the remaining intestine can adapt by becoming better at absorbing.

---

## One-line intuition

Short bowel syndrome = not enough small intestine left to absorb nutrients, fluid, bile acids, and vitamins.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Ileal resection classically causes vitamin B12 deficiency and bile acid malabsorption.

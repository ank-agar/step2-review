# Vesicoureteral reflux

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19502.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/Grade_1_to_5_Vesico_Ureteric_Reflux.svg/1280px-Grade_1_to_5_Vesico_Ureteric_Reflux.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK563262/bin/vur.jpg)

Vesicoureteral reflux (VUR) means urine flows backward from the bladder into the ureter and sometimes up to the kidney.

Normally:

* kidney makes urine
* urine goes down the ureter
* urine enters the bladder
* a flap-like valve at the ureter-bladder junction prevents backflow

In VUR, that anti-reflux valve does not work well.

So urine can go:

> bladder → ureter → kidney

instead of only:

> kidney → ureter → bladder

---

### Why this matters

The big problem is not just backward urine.

The problem is that infected urine can reflux upward and reach the kidney.

That can cause:

* recurrent UTIs (urinary tract infections)
* pyelonephritis (kidney infection)
* renal scarring
* hypertension later from damaged kidney tissue

---

### Why it happens

Most classic pediatric VUR is congenital (present from birth).

The ureter enters the bladder at a bad angle or has too short of a tunnel through the bladder wall.

That tunnel normally gets squeezed shut when the bladder contracts.

If the tunnel is too short, the bladder squeeze pushes urine backward instead of closing the valve.

---

### Grading

VUR is graded I to V:

* Grade I: urine refluxes into ureter only
* Grade II: reaches kidney but no dilation
* Grade III: mild dilation
* Grade IV: more dilation
* Grade V: severe dilation/tortuous ureter

Higher grade = more backflow + more dilation + higher risk of kidney damage.

---

## One-line intuition

VUR is a leaky bladder-to-ureter valve that lets urine shoot backward toward the kidney.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

VUR is classically diagnosed with a VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram), where contrast is put into the bladder and x-rays check whether it refluxes back up into the ureters/kidneys.

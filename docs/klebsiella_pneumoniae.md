## Klebsiella pneumoniae

![Image](https://mdpi-res.com/ijerph/ijerph-17-06278/article_deploy/html/images/ijerph-17-06278-g001-550.jpg)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/klebsiella/media/images/klebsiella.jpg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/PHIL_Images/6689/6689_lores.jpg)

Klebsiella pneumoniae is a gram-negative rod (bacterium that stains pink/red on Gram stain and has a rod shape).

Classic traits:

* encapsulated
* lactose fermenter
* non-motile
* urease positive
* facultative anaerobe (can grow with or without oxygen)

The biggest Step 1 idea is the capsule.

Capsule = thick polysaccharide coat (sugar-based outer layer) around the bacteria.

Why the capsule matters:

* blocks phagocytosis (immune cells eating bacteria)
* makes colonies look mucoid/slimy
* helps it cause severe pneumonia and abscesses

So think:

> Klebsiella = thick sticky capsule = hard for immune cells to grab.

---

### What infections does it cause?

Klebsiella pneumoniae can cause:

* pneumonia
* urinary tract infection
* bacteremia (bacteria in the blood)
* liver abscess
* wound infections

It is especially associated with hospital-acquired infection, diabetes, alcoholism, and ventilator use.

---

### Pneumonia clue

Klebsiella pneumonia classically causes:

* thick bloody sputum
* "currant jelly" sputum
* lobar pneumonia
* lung abscesses sometimes

Why "currant jelly" sputum?

Because the infection can cause tissue destruction and bleeding, and the capsule makes secretions thick and mucoid.

---

### Lab clue

On MacConkey agar, Klebsiella is pink because it ferments lactose.

MacConkey agar = lab plate that selects for gram-negative bacteria and shows lactose fermentation.

Pink colony = lactose fermenter.

Mucoid colony = capsule.

---

### Drug resistance clue

Klebsiella is famous for antibiotic resistance.

Important terms:

* ESBL (extended-spectrum beta-lactamase): enzyme that breaks many penicillins and cephalosporins
* KPC (Klebsiella pneumoniae carbapenemase): enzyme that breaks carbapenems, which are powerful beta-lactam antibiotics

So if a question stem says multidrug-resistant hospital gram-negative rod, Klebsiella is very possible.

## One-line intuition

Klebsiella pneumoniae is a sticky encapsulated gram-negative rod that ferments lactose and causes severe hospital pneumonia, urinary tract infection, bacteremia, and liver abscess.

### Core idea

Myocarditis = inflammation of the myocardium (heart muscle).

Break it down:

* myo- = muscle
* card = heart
* -itis = inflammation

So myocarditis literally means:

> inflamed heart muscle.

---

### Why it matters

The myocardium is the muscle layer that squeezes blood forward.

If it gets inflamed:

* muscle cells get injured
* the heart pumps weaker
* electrical signals can become unstable

So patients can get:

* chest pain
* shortness of breath
* fatigue
* palpitations
* arrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms)
* heart failure in severe cases

---

### Common cause

Most classic Step 1 cause:

* Coxsackie B virus

But myocarditis can also happen after other infections, autoimmune disease, drugs, toxins, or immune checkpoint inhibitors (cancer immunotherapy drugs that activate T cells).

---

### Why troponin can be high

Troponin (cardiac muscle injury marker) rises because inflamed immune cells damage cardiomyocytes (heart muscle cells).

That damage lets intracellular proteins leak into the blood.

So:

> myocarditis can look like a myocardial infarction (heart attack), but the problem is inflammation, not usually a blocked coronary artery.

---

### What biopsy shows

Classic finding:

* lymphocytic infiltrate
* myocyte necrosis

Lymphocytes (immune cells) are in the heart muscle, and myocytes (muscle cells) are dying.

That is the key idea: immune inflammation is physically injuring the heart muscle.

---

## One-line intuition

Myocarditis is when infection or immune activation makes the heart muscle swollen and irritated, so it can pump poorly and trigger abnormal rhythms.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Myocarditis can heal, but it can also progress to dilated cardiomyopathy (enlarged, weak heart chambers) because damaged myocardium remodels and stretches over time.

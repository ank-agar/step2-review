### Core idea

Neonatal tetanus is tetanus in a newborn, usually from **Clostridium tetani** entering through the unhealed umbilical stump.

Usually the setup is:

* mother is not immunized
* baby gets no protective maternal antibodies
* umbilical cord is cut or treated with something nonsterile
* spores enter the stump and make toxin

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Clostridium tetani makes **tetanospasmin** (the tetanus neurotoxin).

That toxin blocks release of:

* GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid)
* glycine

These are inhibitory neurotransmitters, meaning they normally tell muscles to relax.

So if you lose inhibition:

> motor neurons fire too much → muscles stay tight and spasm.

That gives:

* trismus/lockjaw
* poor feeding
* generalized rigidity
* spasms
* opisthotonus (severe back arching from muscle spasm)

---

### One-line intuition

Neonatal tetanus is basically: **dirty umbilical stump infection → tetanus toxin → loss of inhibitory signaling → stiff, spasming newborn.**

---

### Why maternal vaccination matters

The tetanus vaccine is a **toxoid vaccine** (inactivated toxin vaccine).

Mom makes anti-tetanus antibodies, and IgG (immunoglobulin G) crosses the placenta to protect the newborn.

So the baby is protected at birth even before they can make their own strong vaccine response.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Tetanus toxin blocks inhibitory neurotransmitter release, while botulinum toxin blocks acetylcholine release.

Easy contrast:

* tetanus = too much muscle contraction/spastic paralysis
* botulinum = too little muscle contraction/flaccid paralysis

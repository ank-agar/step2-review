## Subconjunctival hemorrhage

### Core idea

Subconjunctival hemorrhage = bleeding underneath the conjunctiva (thin clear membrane covering the white part of the eye).

So blood gets trapped between:

* conjunctiva
* sclera (white outer wall of the eye)

That is why it looks like a bright red patch on the white of the eye.

---

### Why it looks scary but is usually benign

The blood is superficial.

It is not inside the eye, not in the retina (light-sensing back layer of the eye), and usually does not affect vision.

So classic findings are:

* painless red patch
* normal vision
* no discharge
* no photophobia (light sensitivity)

---

### Common triggers

Anything that suddenly raises venous pressure (pressure in veins) can pop a tiny conjunctival vessel:

* coughing
* sneezing
* vomiting
* heavy lifting
* straining

Also think about:

* trauma
* hypertension (high blood pressure)
* anticoagulants (blood thinners)
* bleeding disorders if recurrent

---

## One-line intuition

A tiny surface blood vessel breaks, so blood spreads under the clear conjunctival layer like red paint under plastic wrap.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Subconjunctival hemorrhage is usually painless with normal vision; painful red eye or vision loss should make you think of more serious causes like acute angle-closure glaucoma, keratitis (corneal inflammation), uveitis (inflammation of the uveal tract), or scleritis (deep scleral inflammation).

Clostridial myonecrosis = **gas gangrene**.

Break it down:

* **clostridial** = caused by *Clostridium* bacteria
* **myo-** = muscle
* **necrosis** = tissue death

So clostridial myonecrosis means:

> *Clostridium* infection causing rapid muscle death.

Most common cause:

* **Clostridium perfringens** (**C. perfringens**, shortened species name)

---

### Core idea

This happens when *Clostridium perfringens* gets into a deep wound with low oxygen.

Low oxygen matters because *Clostridium* is an **anaerobe** (organism that grows best without oxygen).

Then it releases toxins that:

* destroy muscle cells
* destroy red blood cells
* damage blood vessels
* make gas in tissues

That gas causes **crepitus** (crackling feeling under the skin when pressed).

---

### Why it spreads so fast

The key toxin is **alpha toxin** (a lecithinase/phospholipase C enzyme that breaks cell membranes).

Cell membranes are made of phospholipids, so alpha toxin basically chews up the membrane.

That causes:

* muscle cell death
* hemolysis (red blood cell destruction)
* poor blood flow
* even lower oxygen

So the bacteria create their own perfect environment:

> low oxygen -> more clostridial growth -> more toxin -> more tissue death -> even lower oxygen

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* severe pain
* swollen tense tissue
* skin discoloration or bullae (large blisters)
* crepitus from gas
* systemic toxicity (whole-body illness), shock, fever

On imaging:

* gas in soft tissue planes

On Gram stain (lab stain used to classify bacteria by cell wall pattern):

* gram-positive rods

---

## One-line intuition

Gas gangrene is when *Clostridium perfringens* infects oxygen-poor muscle and releases membrane-destroying toxins, causing rapidly spreading muscle necrosis plus gas.

---

## Pearl

**C. perfringens alpha toxin = lecithinase/phospholipase C**, which damages cell membranes and causes the classic **double-zone hemolysis** (two rings of red blood cell breakdown) on blood agar.

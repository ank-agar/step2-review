### Core idea

Thin basement membrane disease is a usually benign kidney condition where the glomerular basement membrane, or **GBM** (glomerular basement membrane), is abnormally thin.

The **glomerulus** (tiny kidney filtering unit) normally filters blood while keeping red blood cells and large proteins inside the bloodstream.

If the GBM is too thin, red blood cells can leak through more easily.

Main finding:

* persistent microscopic hematuria (blood in urine seen under a microscope)

---

### Why blood shows up in urine

Think of the GBM like the middle layer of a coffee filter.

Normally:

* blood stays in the capillary
* filtered fluid enters Bowman's space (space where early urine collects)
* red blood cells are too big to pass through

In thin basement membrane disease:

* the GBM is structurally thinner
* the filter is a little leakier to red blood cells
* so you get microscopic hematuria

Usually, the filter is not severely damaged, so kidney function stays normal.

---

### How it presents

Classic presentation:

* asymptomatic patient
* incidental microscopic hematuria on urinalysis (urine test)
* normal blood pressure
* normal kidney function
* little or no proteinuria (protein in urine)

It can run in families, so it is also called benign familial hematuria.

---

### Diagnosis

The key biopsy finding is on electron microscopy, or **EM** (electron microscopy):

* diffusely thin GBM

Light microscopy (regular microscope appearance of tissue) is often normal because the problem is too ultrastructural/small to see well without EM.

---

## One-line intuition

Thin basement membrane disease = a slightly too-thin kidney filter that lets red blood cells sneak into urine, but usually preserves kidney function.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Thin basement membrane disease can look like Alport syndrome because both can cause hematuria, but Alport syndrome is more severe and classically has hearing loss + eye problems due to type IV collagen defects.

### Core idea

Polyangiitis literally means:

* poly = many
* angi = blood vessels
* itis = inflammation

So polyangiitis = inflammation of many blood vessels.

In Step 1, this usually points to **ANCA-associated vasculitis**.

ANCA (anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody) = antibodies that activate neutrophils.

Neutrophils then damage vessel walls, especially small vessels.

---

### Why the findings happen

Inflamed small vessels become leaky and damaged, so organs lose normal blood flow.

That causes:

* **palpable purpura:** blood leaks into skin
* **hematuria:** inflamed glomeruli (kidney filters) leak red blood cells
* **proteinuria:** damaged glomeruli leak protein
* **hemoptysis:** lung capillaries bleed
* **neuropathy:** tiny vessels feeding nerves get inflamed

The big intuition:

> Vessel inflammation causes ischemia + leakage.

Ischemia = poor blood flow causing tissue injury.

---

### The high-yield polyangiitis family

Main Step 1 diseases:

* **GPA (granulomatosis with polyangiitis):** upper airway + lungs + kidneys
* **MPA (microscopic polyangiitis):** lungs + kidneys, but no granulomas
* **EGPA (eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis):** asthma + eosinophilia + vasculitis

Granuloma = organized collection of immune cells trying to wall something off.

Eosinophilia = increased eosinophils, usually associated with allergy/asthma/parasites.

---

### ANCA patterns

Classic associations:

* **c-ANCA (cytoplasmic ANCA)** targets PR3 (proteinase 3) → GPA
* **p-ANCA (perinuclear ANCA)** targets MPO (myeloperoxidase) → MPA and EGPA

Think:

* **GPA = c-ANCA**
* **MPA/EGPA = p-ANCA**

---

## One-line intuition

Polyangiitis is autoimmune neutrophil-driven blood vessel inflammation, so the presentation depends on which small vessels get damaged.

---

## Pearl 🔥

GPA classically causes:

* chronic sinusitis/nasal symptoms
* lung nodules or hemoptysis
* rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis

That triad = upper airway + lung + kidney.

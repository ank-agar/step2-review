## Histrionic personality disorder

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK590043/bin/ch10basic-Image006.jpg)

### Core idea

Histrionic personality disorder (HPD) is a Cluster B personality disorder (dramatic, emotional, or erratic personality disorder) where the person has:

* excessive emotionality
* attention-seeking behavior
* discomfort when they are not the center of attention

Think:

> “I need attention and approval, so I act dramatic or seductive to get it.”

---

### Classic findings

HPD is associated with:

* inappropriate seductive or provocative behavior
* rapidly shifting, shallow emotions
* dramatic/theatrical behavior
* using physical appearance to draw attention
* impressionistic speech, meaning speech that sounds emotional but lacks details
* suggestibility, meaning easily influenced by others
* thinking relationships are closer than they actually are

---

### Why the findings happen

The core problem is unstable self-worth that depends heavily on outside attention.

So if attention drops:

* they feel uncomfortable or undervalued
* they exaggerate emotions to regain focus
* they may act flirtatious, dramatic, or overly familiar

It is not psychosis (loss of contact with reality). It is a long-term personality pattern.

---

### Differentiate from similar disorders

* Borderline personality disorder (BPD) = unstable relationships, unstable self-image, self-harm, fear of abandonment
* Narcissistic personality disorder (NPD) = grandiosity, need for admiration, lack of empathy
* Antisocial personality disorder (ASPD) = violating rights of others, criminality, no remorse
* Bipolar mania = episodic elevated mood, decreased need for sleep, pressured speech, risky behavior

HPD is more:

> “Look at me / approve of me.”

---

## One-line intuition

HPD is “center-stage personality”: dramatic emotional expression used to get attention and reassurance.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On Step 1, HPD is ego-syntonic, meaning the behavior feels normal to the patient. So they usually do **not** come in saying, “My attention-seeking is the problem”; others around them often notice the dysfunction first.

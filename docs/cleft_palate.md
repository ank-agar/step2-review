### Core idea

Cleft palate = a congenital opening in the palate (roof of the mouth).

Basically, the two sides of the palate fail to fuse during embryologic development.

So there is an abnormal connection between:

* oral cavity (mouth)
* nasal cavity (nose space)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Feeding problems:** milk can pass from mouth into nose because the normal roof barrier is open
* **Nasal regurgitation:** same idea — food/liquid escapes upward into the nasal cavity
* **Speech problems:** air leaks through the nose, so sounds become hypernasal
* **Recurrent otitis media (middle ear infection):** abnormal palate muscles can impair Eustachian tube (tube connecting middle ear to nasopharynx) function

---

### Embryology intuition

The palate forms when the palatal shelves grow toward each other and fuse in the midline.

If fusion fails:

> the roof of the mouth stays split open.

That split can involve:

* hard palate (bony front roof of mouth)
* soft palate (muscular back roof of mouth)
* uvula

---

### Important association

Cleft palate can be isolated, but Step 1 likes it with Pierre Robin sequence:

* micrognathia (small jaw)
* glossoptosis (downward/backward tongue displacement)
* cleft palate

Why?

The small jaw makes the tongue sit too far back/up during development, physically blocking normal palatal shelf fusion.

---

## One-line intuition

Cleft palate is a failed midline fusion of the roof of the mouth, creating a mouth-to-nose opening that causes feeding, speech, and ear problems.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY association: Pierre Robin sequence causes cleft palate because the small mandible (lower jaw) keeps the tongue in the way of palatal shelf fusion.

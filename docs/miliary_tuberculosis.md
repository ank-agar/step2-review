### Core idea

Miliary tuberculosis is **disseminated TB (tuberculosis)** where *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* spreads through the blood and seeds many tiny granulomas (organized immune cell collections) throughout the body.

“Miliary” = looks like **millet seeds**.

So the image/boards buzzword is:

* tiny, diffuse nodules all over both lungs
* can also involve liver, spleen, bone marrow, kidneys, meninges (brain coverings)

---

### Why it happens

Usually TB starts in the lung or lymph nodes.

If the immune system does not contain it, the TB focus can erode into blood vessels.

Then TB spreads hematogenously (through the bloodstream), meaning it gets carried everywhere blood goes.

That is why miliary TB is not just “bad lung TB” — it is **systemic TB (whole-body TB)**.

---

### Why the CXR looks like that

On chest X-ray, the lungs get filled with many tiny nodules.

Each nodule is a small tuberculous granuloma.

Because blood spreads the bacteria everywhere, the nodules are:

* bilateral
* diffuse
* tiny
* relatively uniform

One-line intuition:

> TB got into the bloodstream, so it “sprinkled” tiny granulomas everywhere like millet seeds.

---

### Symptoms

Often nonspecific at first:

* fever
* night sweats
* weight loss
* fatigue
* cough
* shortness of breath

If it spreads to meninges (brain coverings), think TB meningitis (tuberculosis infection of the meninges), which can cause headache, confusion, cranial nerve findings, or altered mental status.

---

### Who is at risk?

Classically:

* very young children
* elderly patients
* HIV (human immunodeficiency virus)
* immunosuppression
* malnutrition

Basically: weaker cell-mediated immunity (T-cell/macrophage immune response) means TB is less contained.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Miliary TB is a **type IV hypersensitivity / Th1 (T helper 1) macrophage disease** conceptually: TB control depends on Th1 cells making IFN-gamma (interferon-gamma), which activates macrophages to form granulomas.

So defects in cell-mediated immunity increase risk of disseminated TB.

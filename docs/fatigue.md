# Fatigue

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/Icons_symptoms_ME_CFS.svg/500px-Icons_symptoms_ME_CFS.svg.png)

![Image](https://me-pedia.org/w/images/3/3b/CFS-ANS-immune.png)

Fatigue means abnormal tiredness or low energy that makes it hard to do normal activity.

It is a **symptom**, not a diagnosis.

So if a patient says “fatigue,” think:

* Is the body not making enough oxygen/energy?
* Is the brain/mood/sleep system not restoring energy?
* Is there inflammation or chronic disease draining energy?

---

### Core idea

Fatigue = the body feels like it cannot produce or use energy normally.

Common Step 1 buckets:

* **Anemia:** low RBCs (red blood cells) → low oxygen delivery → tired
* **Hypothyroidism:** low thyroid hormone → slow metabolism → tired, cold, weight gain
* **Depression:** low mood + poor sleep/appetite/concentration → low energy
* **Infection/inflammation:** cytokines (immune signaling proteins) make the brain feel sick/tired
* **Sleep problems:** poor restoration → daytime fatigue
* **Cancer/chronic disease:** inflammation + catabolism (breakdown state) → fatigue

---

### Why fatigue happens

Your cells need:

* oxygen
* glucose/fatty acids
* ATP (adenosine triphosphate, the cell’s energy molecule)
* normal hormones
* normal sleep/brain signaling

If any part is off, the patient feels low energy.

Example:

* anemia → less hemoglobin (oxygen-carrying protein in RBCs)
* less oxygen delivery → less aerobic metabolism
* less ATP production → fatigue

---

### Central vs peripheral fatigue

**Central fatigue** = the brain/CNS (central nervous system: brain and spinal cord) is not driving activity well.

Examples:

* depression
* poor sleep
* chronic inflammation
* neurologic disease

**Peripheral fatigue** = the muscle itself cannot keep contracting well.

Examples:

* prolonged exercise
* mitochondrial disease
* electrolyte abnormalities
* neuromuscular junction problems

One-line intuition:

> Central fatigue = “the brain won’t send enough drive.”  
> Peripheral fatigue = “the muscle cannot execute the drive.”

---

### Don’t confuse fatigue with weakness

Fatigue:

* “I feel exhausted.”
* Strength may be normal on exam.

Weakness:

* “I physically cannot generate force.”
* Strength is reduced on exam.

This matters because true weakness points more toward neurologic or muscle disease.

---

## One-line intuition

Fatigue is the body’s “low battery” symptom, caused by poor oxygen delivery, poor ATP production, inflammation, hormone problems, sleep problems, or brain/mood disorders.

---

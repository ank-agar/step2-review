### Core idea

**Histoplasma capsulatum** is a **dimorphic fungus** (fungus that changes form depending on temperature).

Think:

* **cold soil = mold**
* **warm body = yeast**

It causes **histoplasmosis** (fungal infection usually starting in the lungs).

---

### How you get it

It lives in soil contaminated with:

* bird droppings
* bat droppings

People inhale tiny spores, especially after disturbing soil.

Classic exposure:

* caves
* chicken coops
* construction/demolition

---

### Why it affects macrophages

After inhalation:

* spores enter the lungs
* convert into yeast at body temperature
* get eaten by macrophages

But instead of dying, Histoplasma survives **inside macrophages**.

Macrophages then carry it through the **reticuloendothelial system** (macrophage-rich organs), especially:

* liver
* spleen
* bone marrow
* lymph nodes

That is why disseminated histoplasmosis can cause:

* hepatosplenomegaly (enlarged liver and spleen)
* pancytopenia (low red cells, white cells, and platelets)
* fever
* weight loss

---

### What it looks like

In tissue, Histoplasma is:

* small oval yeast
* often inside macrophages
* narrow-based budding

Do not confuse with Blastomyces:

* **Histoplasma = small, intracellular, narrow-based budding**
* **Blastomyces = broad-based budding**

---

## One-line intuition

Histoplasma is a dimorphic fungus from bird/bat-dropping soil that gets inhaled into lungs, turns into yeast, hides inside macrophages, and can spread through macrophage-rich organs.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Histoplasma is associated with **calcified granulomas** (organized immune walls around infection) in the lung and hilar lymph nodes, so it can look like tuberculosis on imaging.

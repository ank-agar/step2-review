### Core idea

Dilated cardiomyopathy is when the heart chambers, especially the left ventricle, become:

* enlarged
* stretched out
* weak at squeezing

So the main problem is **systolic dysfunction** (poor contraction/pumping).

---

### Why the heart gets weak

Think:

> big floppy ventricle = weak pump

The ventricle dilates, so the muscle fibers are stretched too far apart.

At first, stretching helps contraction a little through the Frank-Starling mechanism (more filling stretches cardiac muscle and increases contraction).

But when it stretches too much:

* actin and myosin overlap poorly
* contraction gets weaker
* ejection fraction drops
* blood backs up into lungs/body

Ejection fraction means the percent of blood the ventricle pumps out with each beat.

---

### Classic causes

High-yield causes include:

* alcohol toxicity
* doxorubicin toxicity
* viral myocarditis
* Chagas disease
* peripartum cardiomyopathy
* genetic mutations, especially cytoskeletal proteins like dystrophin

Basically: anything that damages cardiac myocytes can make the ventricle weak and dilated.

---

### What it causes

Dilated cardiomyopathy can lead to heart failure symptoms:

* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* pulmonary edema (fluid in lungs)
* peripheral edema (leg swelling)
* fatigue from low cardiac output

It can also cause:

* mitral regurgitation because dilation pulls the valve ring apart
* arrhythmias because stretched myocardium conducts electricity abnormally
* mural thrombus because blood stagnates in a poorly contracting chamber

Mural thrombus means a clot stuck to the wall of the heart chamber.

---

## One-line intuition

The ventricle becomes a big stretched-out bag, so it holds a lot of blood but cannot squeeze it forward well.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Dilated cardiomyopathy is classically associated with **eccentric hypertrophy**: sarcomeres added in series, causing chamber dilation.

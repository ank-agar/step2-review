## Intestinal malrotation

### Core idea

Intestinal malrotation = the bowel does not rotate/fix normally during fetal development.

Normally, the midgut rotates **270 degrees counterclockwise** around the **SMA (superior mesenteric artery)**.

In malrotation, the bowel ends up in the wrong position, often with:

* small intestine mostly on the right
* cecum in the wrong place
* narrow mesenteric base
* Ladd bands crossing over the duodenum

Duodenum = first part of the small intestine.

Mesentery = tissue that suspends bowel and carries blood vessels.

Ladd bands = abnormal fibrous bands that can compress the duodenum.

---

### Why it matters

The dangerous complication is **midgut volvulus**.

Volvulus = twisting of bowel around itself.

Why malrotation causes volvulus:

* normal bowel has a broad mesenteric base, so it is stable
* malrotation creates a narrow stalk-like mesenteric base
* narrow base lets the bowel twist around the SMA
* twisting can cut off blood supply to the midgut

So the scary sequence is:

> malrotation → narrow mesentery → volvulus → bowel ischemia

Ischemia = low blood flow causing tissue injury.

---

### Classic presentation

Think newborn/infant with:

* bilious vomiting
* abdominal distension
* abdominal pain/irritability
* possible bloody stool if ischemia occurs

Bilious vomiting = green vomiting from bile, meaning obstruction is distal to the ampulla of Vater.

Ampulla of Vater = where bile/pancreatic ducts empty into the duodenum.

---

### Diagnosis

Best test is usually an **upper GI series**.

GI = gastrointestinal.

Upper GI series = contrast x-ray study that tracks contrast through the stomach and duodenum.

Classic finding:

* **DJJ (duodenojejunal junction)** is abnormally on the right side instead of the left

Duodenojejunal junction = where duodenum becomes jejunum.

---

### Treatment

Treatment is a **Ladd procedure**.

This usually involves:

* untwisting volvulus if present
* cutting Ladd bands
* widening the mesenteric base
* placing small bowel on the right and colon on the left
* often removing the appendix

Appendix is often removed because malrotation can put the appendix in an abnormal location, making future appendicitis harder to diagnose.

---

## One-line intuition

The intestines did not do their normal fetal “spin and anchor,” so they are positioned loosely and can twist around their blood supply.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Bilious vomiting in a newborn is **intestinal obstruction until proven otherwise**, and malrotation with midgut volvulus is a surgical emergency because it can rapidly infarct the midgut.

Infarct = tissue death from loss of blood supply.

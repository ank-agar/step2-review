## Menorrhagia

Menorrhagia means heavy menstrual bleeding.

More specifically:

* menstrual bleeding that is too heavy
* or lasts too long
* but still happens at regular cycle intervals

So the key idea is:

> regular periods, but the bleeding amount/duration is excessive.

---

### Why it happens

Normally, during menstruation, the endometrium (uterine lining) sheds in a controlled way.

In menorrhagia, either:

* the lining is too thick/unstable
* the uterus cannot contract well enough to stop bleeding
* hormones are not regulating the cycle properly
* or clotting is impaired

That leads to more blood loss than normal.

---

### Common causes

Think:

* **fibroids** = benign smooth muscle tumors of the uterus
* **adenomyosis** = endometrial tissue grows into the myometrium (uterine muscle)
* **endometrial polyps** = small overgrowths of uterine lining
* **anovulation** = no ovulation, so progesterone is low and the lining sheds irregularly/heavily
* **bleeding disorders**, especially von Willebrand disease (defective platelet adhesion due to low/abnormal von Willebrand factor)

---

### Why fibroids cause heavy bleeding

Fibroids, especially submucosal fibroids (fibroids right under the uterine lining), distort the endometrium.

That does 2 big things:

* increases surface area that can bleed
* prevents the uterus from contracting cleanly to compress blood vessels

So the uterus has a harder time "clamping down" on bleeding vessels.

---

### What it can cause

Chronic heavy bleeding can lead to:

* iron deficiency anemia
* fatigue
* dizziness
* pallor
* shortness of breath with exertion

Iron deficiency anemia means low hemoglobin from depleted iron stores.

---

## One-line intuition

Menorrhagia is when periods come on schedule, but the uterus loses too much blood each cycle.

---

## Pearl

Heavy menstrual bleeding since menarche (first period) should make you think of a bleeding disorder, especially von Willebrand disease.

## Hyphema

Hyphema = blood in the anterior chamber (front fluid-filled space of the eye between the cornea and iris).

Think:

* cornea = clear front window
* iris = colored part of the eye
* anterior chamber = little space between them
* hyphema = that space gets filled with blood

---

### Core idea

Usually this happens after blunt trauma to the eye.

The trauma shears tiny vessels in the iris or ciliary body (structure that helps make aqueous humor, the eye fluid), so blood leaks into the anterior chamber.

Because blood is heavier than aqueous humor (clear fluid in the eye), it layers at the bottom when the patient is upright.

That is why it can look like a red horizontal fluid level in front of the iris.

---

### Why it matters

Blood in the anterior chamber can clog the trabecular meshwork (drainage pathway for aqueous humor).

If aqueous humor cannot drain:

* intraocular pressure, or IOP (pressure inside the eye), increases
* optic nerve can be damaged
* secondary glaucoma can happen

So the big danger is not just "blood in the eye" — it is increased eye pressure causing vision loss.

---

### Classic causes

Most common:

* blunt eye trauma
* sports injury
* paintball/BB gun injury
* car accident
* postoperative bleeding after eye surgery

Can also happen spontaneously with:

* sickle cell disease
* bleeding disorders
* abnormal new blood vessels
* eye tumors, rarely

---

### One-line intuition

Hyphema is basically a bruise inside the front chamber of the eye, and the pooled blood can block fluid drainage and raise eye pressure.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In sickle cell disease, even a small hyphema is high risk because red blood cells (RBCs) can sickle in the anterior chamber and clog drainage, causing increased IOP (intraocular pressure).

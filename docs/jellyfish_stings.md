# Jellyfish stings

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/45/Fluids-05-00020-g002.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/8860.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/33/Facial_sting%2C_probably_by_a_jellyfish.jpg)

Jellyfish stings happen when tentacles touch skin and fire tiny venom needles called **nematocysts** (microscopic stinging capsules).

Think:

* tentacle touches skin
* nematocysts discharge
* venom enters skin
* pain + red linear welts happen

The reason the rash is often **line-shaped** is that the tentacle drags across the skin, leaving a track of venom exposure.

---

### Why it hurts

Nematocysts work like tiny spring-loaded syringes.

When triggered, they shoot a barbed thread into the skin and inject venom.

That venom causes:

* pain/burning
* erythema (redness)
* swelling
* pruritus (itching)
* sometimes vesicles (small fluid-filled blisters)

Severe species, especially some box jellyfish, can cause systemic symptoms (whole-body symptoms), like:

* muscle cramps
* nausea/vomiting
* hypotension (low blood pressure)
* arrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms)
* cardiac arrest

---

### What to do

Basic treatment:

* remove visible tentacles carefully
* rinse with seawater, not freshwater
* use hot water immersion if available
* treat pain

Freshwater can make remaining nematocysts fire more, because it changes osmotic pressure (water movement across a membrane due to concentration differences).

Hot water helps because many marine venoms are heat-labile (damaged by heat).

---

## One-line intuition

A jellyfish sting is basically **millions of microscopic venom needles firing into the skin at once**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Do **not** rinse most jellyfish stings with freshwater, because it can trigger undischarged nematocysts to release more venom.

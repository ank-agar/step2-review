### Core idea

Papillary necrosis = death of the renal papillae (tips of the renal pyramids where urine drains into the calyces).

Think:

* renal pyramid = cone-shaped medulla region
* papilla = very tip of that cone
* necrosis = tissue death

So papillary necrosis is basically:

> the kidney pyramid tips die and can slough off into the urine.

---

### Why the papilla dies

The renal papilla is already vulnerable because it has:

* low oxygen tension
* high solute concentration
* relatively poor blood supply from the vasa recta (straight capillaries of the renal medulla)

So anything that worsens ischemia (low blood flow/oxygen) can kill it.

Classic causes:

* NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs), especially chronic analgesic use
* diabetes mellitus
* sickle cell disease or sickle cell trait
* acute pyelonephritis (kidney infection)
* urinary tract obstruction

Why NSAIDs do it:

* prostaglandins normally dilate renal blood vessels
* NSAIDs block prostaglandins
* less dilation = less medullary blood flow = papillary ischemia

Why sickle cell does it:

* the renal medulla is low oxygen and hypertonic
* that promotes RBC (red blood cell) sickling
* sickled RBCs block small vessels
* papilla becomes ischemic and dies

---

### What it causes

Dead papilla can break off and enter the collecting system, causing:

* gross hematuria (visible blood in urine)
* flank pain
* tissue fragments in urine
* obstruction if the sloughed papilla blocks urine flow
* AKI (acute kidney injury) if severe

On imaging, a sloughed papilla can look like a filling defect because contrast surrounds dead tissue instead of filling the space normally.

---

## One-line intuition

Papillary necrosis is an ischemic infarct of the kidney pyramid tips, classically from NSAIDs, diabetes, sickle cell, pyelonephritis, or obstruction.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, sickle cell trait can present with painless hematuria from renal papillary necrosis because the renal medulla is low oxygen + hypertonic, which makes sickling more likely there.

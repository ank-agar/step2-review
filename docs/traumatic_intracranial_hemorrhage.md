## Traumatic intracranial hemorrhage

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/15/An_illustration_of_the_different_types_of_brain_hemorrhage.png/960px-An_illustration_of_the_different_types_of_brain_hemorrhage.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f2/Trauma_subdural_arrows.jpg/960px-Trauma_subdural_arrows.jpg)

### Core idea

Traumatic intracranial hemorrhage means bleeding inside the skull after trauma.

Break it down:

* traumatic = caused by injury
* intra- = within
* cranial = skull
* hemorrhage = bleeding

So this is basically:

> blood collecting somewhere inside the skull after head trauma.

---

### Why it is dangerous

The skull is a fixed box.

So if blood enters the skull:

* blood volume goes up
* intracranial pressure (ICP, pressure inside the skull) goes up
* brain tissue gets compressed
* cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP, pressure pushing blood into the brain) can fall

That can cause:

* headache
* vomiting
* confusion
* focal neurologic deficits
* seizure
* coma
* herniation

---

### Main traumatic bleed patterns

**Epidural hematoma (EDH, blood between skull and dura mater)**

* usually middle meningeal artery tear
* classically after temporal bone fracture
* lens-shaped/biconvex on CT (computed tomography)
* may have lucid interval, meaning patient seems okay then rapidly worsens

Why lens-shaped?

* dura mater is tightly attached at skull sutures
* blood cannot cross sutures easily
* so it bulges inward like a lens

---

**Subdural hematoma (SDH, blood between dura mater and arachnoid mater)**

* usually bridging vein tear
* common in elderly patients, alcohol use disorder, and shaken baby syndrome
* crescent-shaped on CT (computed tomography)
* can cross suture lines

Why slower?

* veins are lower pressure than arteries
* so symptoms may develop more gradually than epidural hematoma

---

**Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH, blood between arachnoid mater and pia mater)**

* blood enters cerebrospinal fluid (CSF, fluid around brain and spinal cord)
* can happen after trauma
* causes meningeal irritation

Findings:

* severe headache
* neck stiffness
* photophobia
* blood in sulci/cisterns on CT (computed tomography)

---

**Intraparenchymal hemorrhage (IPH, bleeding inside brain tissue itself)**

* often from brain contusion
* coup-contrecoup injury can cause this

Coup-contrecoup means:

* coup = injury at site of impact
* contrecoup = injury on opposite side as brain rebounds inside skull

---

## One-line intuition

Head trauma can tear arteries, veins, or brain tissue, and the blood has nowhere to go because the skull is a rigid box.

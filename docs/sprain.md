### Core idea

A **sprain** is an injury to a **ligament** (tough connective tissue that connects bone to bone).

Most common example:

* ankle twists inward
* lateral ankle ligaments get overstretched or torn
* pain + swelling + bruising happen around the joint

---

### Sprain vs strain

Easy distinction:

* **Sprain = ligament injury**
* **Strain = muscle or tendon injury**

A **tendon** is connective tissue that connects muscle to bone.

---

### Why the swelling and bruising happen

When the ligament is stretched or torn:

* small blood vessels tear
* inflammatory fluid leaks into the area
* the joint becomes swollen, painful, and stiff

Bruising happens because blood leaks into nearby soft tissue.

---

### Severity

Sprains are usually graded by how much the ligament is damaged:

* **Grade 1:** stretched ligament, no major tear
* **Grade 2:** partial tear
* **Grade 3:** complete tear, joint may be unstable

So the worse the tear, the more unstable the joint becomes.

---

## One-line intuition

A sprain is when a joint gets forced past its normal range of motion and the bone-to-bone “stabilizing rope” gets stretched or torn.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For ankle sprains, the most commonly injured ligament is the **ATFL** (anterior talofibular ligament), usually from an **inversion injury** (foot rolls inward).

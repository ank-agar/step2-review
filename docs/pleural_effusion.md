## Pleural effusion

### Core idea

Pleural effusion = extra fluid in the pleural space.

Pleural space = the thin potential space between:

* visceral pleura (pleura stuck to the lung)
* parietal pleura (pleura stuck to the chest wall)

So the fluid is **outside the lung**, not inside the airways.

---

### Intuition

Think of the lung like a balloon inside a tight bag.

If fluid fills the bag, it compresses the balloon.

So patients can get:

* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* decreased breath sounds
* dullness to percussion
* pleuritic chest pain (sharp pain worse with breathing)

---

### Why it happens

Fluid builds up when either:

* too much fluid is pushed out of blood vessels
* lymphatics (fluid drainage channels) cannot remove it
* inflammation makes vessels leaky

Big categories:

* heart failure: increased hydrostatic pressure (pushing pressure from blood)
* pneumonia: inflammation near the pleura
* cancer: lymphatic blockage or leaky pleura
* pulmonary embolism: clot in pulmonary artery causing inflammation
* cirrhosis/nephrotic syndrome: low oncotic pressure (protein-pulling pressure)

---

### Transudate vs exudate

Transudate = watery fluid from pressure problems.

Classic causes:

* congestive heart failure (CHF; heart cannot pump forward well)
* cirrhosis
* nephrotic syndrome (kidney protein loss)

Exudate = protein/cell-rich fluid from inflammation or malignancy.

Classic causes:

* pneumonia
* cancer
* tuberculosis (TB; Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection)
* pulmonary embolism

---

### Imaging clue

On chest x-ray:

* blunted costophrenic angle (normally sharp corner where diaphragm meets chest wall)
* meniscus sign (curved fluid line)

Why?

Fluid follows gravity and layers at the lung bases first, so the sharp bottom corner gets filled in.

---

## One-line intuition

Pleural effusion is fluid outside the lung that compresses the lung and makes breathing harder.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Light's criteria separates exudate from transudate using pleural fluid protein and LDH (lactate dehydrogenase; enzyme marker of tissue/cell injury).

Exudate = inflammatory/malignant until proven otherwise.

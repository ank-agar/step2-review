### Core idea

Obesity hypoventilation syndrome, or **OHS (obesity hypoventilation syndrome)**, is:

* obesity
* daytime hypoventilation
* high PaCO2 (arterial partial pressure of carbon dioxide) while awake

Basically:

> the patient is not breathing deeply/effectively enough, so CO2 builds up.

---

### Why obesity causes hypoventilation

Extra weight on the chest/abdomen makes breathing mechanically harder:

* diaphragm cannot move down as well
* chest wall is less compliant
* lungs take smaller breaths

So each breath moves less air.

Less air out = less CO2 exhaled = **hypercapnia (high carbon dioxide in blood)**.

---

### Key blood gas pattern

OHS causes:

* increased PaCO2 (arterial carbon dioxide)
* decreased PaO2 (arterial oxygen)
* usually normal A-a gradient (alveolar-arterial oxygen gradient)

Why normal A-a gradient?

The lungs can still transfer gas across the alveoli. The main problem is that the patient is not ventilating enough air in the first place.

So this is hypoventilation, not a primary diffusion/VQ mismatch problem.

---

### Relationship to sleep apnea

OHS is commonly associated with **OSA (obstructive sleep apnea)**.

OSA means the upper airway collapses during sleep, causing repeated breathing pauses.

But the big distinction:

* OSA alone: breathing problem mainly during sleep
* OHS: CO2 is high even while awake

That daytime hypercapnia is what makes OHS more severe.

---

### Symptoms

Think chronic low oxygen + high CO2:

* daytime sleepiness
* morning headaches
* fatigue
* shortness of breath
* cyanosis (bluish discoloration from low oxygen)
* pulmonary hypertension (high pressure in lung arteries)
* right-sided heart failure/cor pulmonale (right heart failure due to lung disease)

---

## One-line intuition

The body is too loaded down to ventilate well, so CO2 gets trapped even while awake.

---

## Pearl 🔥

OHS is a cause of chronic respiratory acidosis:

* high PaCO2 (arterial carbon dioxide)
* kidney compensation with high HCO3- (bicarbonate)

So an obese patient with daytime sleepiness + elevated serum bicarbonate should make you think chronic CO2 retention.

### Core idea

Prostatitis = inflammation of the prostate gland.

The prostate sits:

* under the bladder
* around the urethra
* in front of the rectum

So when it gets inflamed, it causes urinary + pelvic symptoms.

---

### Classic symptoms

Think:

* dysuria (painful urination)
* urinary frequency/urgency
* pelvic or perineal pain
* painful ejaculation
* tender/boggy prostate on digital rectal exam
* fever/chills if acute bacterial prostatitis

Perineal = the area between the scrotum and anus.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Pain:** inflammation irritates nerves around the prostate
* **Dysuria/frequency:** swollen prostate irritates/compresses the urethra
* **Urinary retention:** severe swelling can block urine flow
* **Fever:** bacteria trigger systemic inflammation
* **Tender prostate:** inflamed tissue becomes swollen and painful to touch

One-line intuition:

> The prostate wraps around the urethra, so an inflamed prostate causes pee problems plus deep pelvic pain.

---

### Types

Main buckets:

* **Acute bacterial prostatitis:** sudden fever, chills, dysuria, very tender prostate
* **Chronic bacterial prostatitis:** recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection), often same organism
* **Chronic pelvic pain syndrome:** chronic pelvic pain without clear bacterial infection
* **Asymptomatic inflammatory prostatitis:** WBCs (white blood cells) in prostate fluid/semen but no symptoms

Most common bacterial cause:

* **E. coli (Escherichia coli)**

---

### Treatment idea

Antibiotics need to penetrate prostate tissue.

Common options:

* fluoroquinolones
* TMP-SMX (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole)

Chronic cases need longer treatment because the prostate is harder for antibiotics to reach.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In **acute bacterial prostatitis**, avoid vigorous prostate massage because it can push bacteria into the blood and cause bacteremia (bacteria in the bloodstream).

Also: prostatitis can elevate PSA (prostate-specific antigen), so PSA is not specific for prostate cancer during inflammation.

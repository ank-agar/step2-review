### Core idea

Schistosomiasis is infection by **Schistosoma**, a trematode (fluke worm).

The big idea:

* freshwater exposure
* cercariae penetrate skin
* worms live in veins
* eggs get trapped in tissues
* the immune reaction to eggs causes disease

Cercariae are the free-swimming larval form that comes from snails.

---

### Life cycle intuition

Think:

* human urine/stool releases eggs into freshwater
* eggs hatch and infect snails
* snails release cercariae
* cercariae penetrate human skin
* adult worms mature in the portal venous system
* eggs lodge in bowel, liver, or bladder tissue

So the snail is the intermediate host, meaning the parasite needs the snail to complete part of its life cycle.

---

### Why symptoms happen

The adult worms are not the main problem.

The main damage comes from eggs causing **granulomas** (organized immune cell collections that wall off something hard to clear).

That inflammation can cause:

* fever and eosinophilia (high eosinophils, a white blood cell type that responds to parasites)
* abdominal pain/diarrhea
* liver fibrosis (scarring)
* portal hypertension (high pressure in the portal venous system draining the gut to the liver)
* hematuria (blood in urine), especially with Schistosoma haematobium

---

### Species to know

**Schistosoma mansoni**

* lateral spine on egg
* intestinal/liver disease

**Schistosoma japonicum**

* small lateral spine
* intestinal/liver disease

**Schistosoma haematobium**

* terminal spine on egg
* urinary tract disease
* classically causes hematuria
* associated with squamous cell carcinoma of the bladder

Squamous cell carcinoma means cancer from squamous epithelial cells.

---

### Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis:

* eggs in stool for S. mansoni and S. japonicum
* eggs in urine for S. haematobium

Treatment:

* praziquantel

---

## One-line intuition

Schistosomiasis is the freshwater snail-associated fluke where **skin-penetrating cercariae become venous worms, and trapped eggs cause granulomatous inflammation**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

S. haematobium = terminal spine egg + hematuria + increased risk of squamous cell carcinoma of the bladder.

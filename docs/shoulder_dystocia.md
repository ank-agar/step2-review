Shoulder dystocia is an obstetric emergency where:

* the fetal head delivers
* but the shoulders get stuck behind the maternal pelvis

Usually, the anterior shoulder gets trapped behind the pubic symphysis (front pelvic bone joint).

Break the word apart:

* shoulder = fetal shoulder
* dys- = difficult/abnormal
* -tocia = childbirth/labor

So:

> difficult delivery because the baby's shoulder is stuck.

### Core idea

The head is out, but the body cannot follow because the shoulder is mechanically blocked.

Think:

* head delivers
* shoulder jams on pubic symphysis
* baby cannot descend normally

This is why it is an emergency: once the head is out, the baby still needs rapid delivery to avoid hypoxia (low oxygen).

---

### Risk factors

Big ones:

* fetal macrosomia (large baby)
* maternal diabetes mellitus (high blood glucose disease)
* prior shoulder dystocia
* obesity
* prolonged labor
* operative vaginal delivery, like vacuum or forceps

Why diabetes matters:

* maternal glucose crosses placenta
* fetus makes more insulin
* insulin acts like a growth factor
* baby gets bigger, especially shoulders/trunk

So diabetic pregnancies can produce babies with relatively large shoulders.

---

### Classic clue

Turtle sign:

* fetal head comes out
* then retracts back against the perineum (area between vagina and anus)

Intuition:

The head is pulled back because the stuck shoulder is holding the body inside.

---

### Management intuition

First-line maneuver:

* McRoberts maneuver = hyperflex maternal hips, bringing knees toward the chest

Why it works:

* rotates the pelvis
* flattens the sacrum (posterior pelvic bone)
* helps free the impacted anterior shoulder

Often combined with:

* suprapubic pressure = pressure above the pubic bone

Important:

* do NOT use fundal pressure (pushing on the top of the uterus)

Why:

* fundal pressure pushes the baby harder into the obstruction
* can worsen impaction and increase uterine rupture risk

---

### Major complication

Neonatal brachial plexus injury.

Brachial plexus = nerve network from the neck that supplies the shoulder, arm, and hand.

If too much traction is put on the fetal head/neck, those nerves can stretch or tear.

Classic result:

* Erb palsy = upper brachial plexus injury
* arm is adducted/internally rotated, often called "waiter's tip"

---

## One-line intuition

Shoulder dystocia = the head delivers but the shoulder gets stuck behind the pubic symphysis, so you reposition the pelvis with McRoberts and suprapubic pressure to free it.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

For Step-style questions, the answer is usually McRoberts maneuver first, then suprapubic pressure; avoid fundal pressure.

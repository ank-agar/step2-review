# Herpetic gingivostomatitis

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/3f32/2822239/84dce71d7620/IPID2010-262415.001.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/a206/3736476/e78156001c21/bcr2013200074f01.jpg)

Herpetic gingivostomatitis is a painful viral infection of the mouth and gums caused by HSV (herpes simplex virus), usually HSV-1.

Break the word apart:

* herpetic = caused by herpes virus
* gingivo- = gingiva, meaning gums
* stomatitis = inflammation of the mouth

So literally:

> herpes infection causing inflamed gums and mouth ulcers.

---

### Classic presentation

Usually in children with:

* fever
* malaise (feeling sick/tired)
* painful mouth sores
* swollen, red, bleeding gums
* trouble eating or drinking
* cervical lymphadenopathy (swollen neck lymph nodes)

The lesions are often vesicles that rupture into ulcers.

Vesicles are small fluid-filled blisters.

Ulcers are shallow breaks in the mucosa (moist lining of the mouth).

---

### Where are the lesions?

HSV (herpes simplex virus) gingivostomatitis can involve the:

* gums
* lips
* tongue
* buccal mucosa (inside of the cheeks)
* hard palate

Big clue:

> diffuse painful oral ulcers + angry red gums = herpetic gingivostomatitis.

---

### Why the findings happen

HSV infects epithelial cells (surface lining cells) in the oral mucosa.

The virus replicates inside those cells, damaging them.

That causes:

* vesicles from infected swollen epithelial cells
* ulcers when vesicles rupture
* red painful gums from inflammation
* fever/malaise from systemic immune response
* poor intake because swallowing and chewing hurt

The big practical complication is dehydration because kids may refuse fluids.

---

### What happens after the first infection?

After primary infection, HSV travels up sensory nerves and becomes latent in the trigeminal ganglion.

Latent means the virus is still present but quiet/inactive.

The trigeminal ganglion is the sensory nerve ganglion for much of the face and mouth.

Later reactivation can cause:

* herpes labialis
* cold sores around the lip

---

### Herpetic gingivostomatitis vs herpangina

Herpetic gingivostomatitis:

* HSV (herpes simplex virus)
* anterior/diffuse mouth
* gums are very inflamed
* lips, tongue, buccal mucosa, hard palate

Herpangina:

* usually coxsackievirus
* posterior mouth
* soft palate, uvula, tonsillar pillars
* less gum involvement

---

## One-line intuition

Herpetic gingivostomatitis is the first big oral HSV-1 infection: painful mouth ulcers plus red swollen gums, then the virus hides in the trigeminal ganglion.

---

## HY pearl

Tzanck smear in HSV (herpes simplex virus) can show multinucleated giant cells, but it cannot distinguish HSV-1 from HSV-2.

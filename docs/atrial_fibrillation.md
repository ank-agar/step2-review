### Core idea

Atrial fibrillation, or AFib (atrial fibrillation), is a chaotic, irregular rhythm of the atria (upper heart chambers).

Instead of the SA node (sinoatrial node, the heart's normal pacemaker) sending one clean signal, many random atrial signals fire at once.

So the atria:

* quiver instead of squeeze well
* send irregular impulses to the AV node (atrioventricular node, the electrical gate between atria and ventricles)
* cause an irregularly irregular ventricular rhythm

---

### What the ECG shows

ECG (electrocardiogram) classically shows:

* no clear P waves
* irregularly irregular R-R intervals (uneven spacing between QRS complexes)
* narrow QRS complexes usually, because conduction through the ventricles is still normal

Why no P waves?

Because P waves represent organized atrial depolarization (electrical activation of the atria), and AFib has disorganized atrial activity.

---

### Why it matters

The big danger is stroke.

Reason:

* atria do not contract well
* blood sits still, especially in the left atrial appendage (small pouch off the left atrium)
* stagnant blood can form a thrombus (blood clot)
* clot can embolize (travel through blood) to the brain
* brain artery blockage causes ischemic stroke (stroke from loss of blood flow)

---

### Common causes/risk factors

Think anything that stretches, irritates, or stresses the atria:

* hypertension (high blood pressure)
* mitral valve disease
* heart failure
* hyperthyroidism (too much thyroid hormone)
* alcohol use, especially binge drinking
* older age

---

## One-line intuition

Atrial fibrillation is chaotic atrial electrical activity, so the atria quiver and the ventricles beat irregularly irregularly.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, AFib = absent P waves + irregularly irregular rhythm + stroke risk from left atrial appendage thrombus.

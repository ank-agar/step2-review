# Sialadenitis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Salivary_glands_numbered.svg/960px-Salivary_glands_numbered.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549845/bin/submandibular__stone.jpg)

Sialadenitis = inflammation of a salivary gland.

Break the word apart:

* sial- = saliva
* aden = gland
* -itis = inflammation

So:

> inflamed salivary gland.

Usually involves the major salivary glands:

* parotid gland = big salivary gland near the ear/cheek
* submandibular gland = salivary gland under the jaw
* sublingual gland = salivary gland under the tongue

---

### Core idea

The gland gets inflamed because saliva is not draining well or because microbes infect it.

Most intuitive mechanism:

* saliva normally flows out through a duct
* a stone, dehydration, or low saliva flow blocks/stagnates that flow
* stagnant saliva lets bacteria grow
* the gland becomes swollen, painful, and inflamed

This is why pain/swelling can get worse with meals:

* eating makes the gland produce saliva
* but the duct is blocked
* pressure builds up inside the gland

---

### Common causes

* **Sialolithiasis** (salivary stone): stone blocks the duct
* **Dehydration/low saliva flow:** saliva gets thick and stagnant
* **Bacterial infection:** classically Staph aureus (Staphylococcus aureus)
* **Viral infection:** mumps can cause parotitis (inflammation of the parotid gland)
* **Autoimmune disease:** Sjogren syndrome can cause chronic salivary gland inflammation and dry mouth

---

### What you see clinically

* painful swelling near cheek or under jaw
* tenderness over the gland
* fever if infected
* dry mouth sometimes
* pus from the duct opening sometimes
* worse pain with eating if obstruction is involved

---

## One-line intuition

Sialadenitis is a salivary gland getting swollen and infected/inflamed because saliva flow is blocked or stagnant.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Submandibular stones are common because submandibular saliva is more mucous/thick and has to flow upward through Wharton duct, so it is easier for stasis and stones to form.

### Core idea

A laryngeal foreign body is an object stuck in the larynx (voice box).

The larynx is the airway area that contains the vocal cords and sits above the trachea (windpipe).

So this is dangerous because the object is sitting right at the entrance to the lower airway.

---

### What it can cause

* choking
* stridor (high-pitched noisy breathing from upper airway narrowing)
* hoarseness
* cough
* trouble breathing
* cyanosis (bluish skin from low oxygen)

Why hoarseness?

Because the foreign body can irritate or physically block the vocal cords, so they cannot vibrate normally.

---

### Why it is an emergency

The larynx is a narrow tube.

Even a small object can block airflow, especially in kids because their airway diameter is already small.

Small decrease in radius = huge increase in resistance to airflow.

That is why a child can go from coughing to severe respiratory distress quickly.

---

### Diagnosis

Often clinical:

* sudden choking episode
* cough/stridor
* decreased air movement

X-ray may miss it if the object is radiolucent (does not show clearly on X-ray), like plastic or food.

Direct visualization with laryngoscopy (looking at the larynx with a scope) is often needed if suspicion is high.

---

### Treatment

Remove the object urgently.

Usually this is done with rigid bronchoscopy or direct laryngoscopy, depending on location.

Do not blindly sweep the mouth unless you can clearly see the object, because you can push it deeper.

---

## One-line intuition

A laryngeal foreign body is basically something stuck at the vocal cord/airway gate, so it can cause hoarseness + stridor + sudden airway obstruction.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Stridor means upper airway obstruction until proven otherwise.

Wheezing is usually lower airway narrowing, but stridor points you toward larynx/trachea-level obstruction.

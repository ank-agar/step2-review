## Aplastic anemia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3f/Hematopoiesis_%28human%29_diagram_en.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459249/bin/Aplastic__Anemia.jpg)

### Core idea

Aplastic anemia is **bone marrow failure**.

The marrow is supposed to make all 3 major blood cell lines:

* red blood cells
* white blood cells
* platelets

In aplastic anemia, the hematopoietic stem cells (blood-forming stem cells) are damaged or suppressed, so the marrow becomes **hypocellular** (too few cells).

So you get:

* low red blood cells → anemia/fatigue
* low white blood cells → infections
* low platelets → bleeding/bruising

This is called **pancytopenia** (low counts of all blood cell lines).

---

### Why the marrow looks “empty”

Normal marrow is busy making blood cells.

In aplastic anemia, the stem cell factory shuts down, so the marrow space gets replaced by fat.

So on bone marrow biopsy:

* hypocellular marrow
* lots of fat
* very few hematopoietic cells (blood-forming cells)

Intuition:

> The problem is not that blood cells are being destroyed outside the marrow. The problem is that the marrow is not making them in the first place.

---

### Key lab pattern

Classic findings:

* pancytopenia (low red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets)
* low reticulocytes (immature red blood cells)
* hypocellular bone marrow

The reticulocyte count is low because the marrow is not responding properly.

That is important because anemia normally should make the marrow release more reticulocytes.

---

### Causes to remember

Can be idiopathic (unknown cause), but Step 1 likes:

* radiation
* chemotherapy
* benzene
* chloramphenicol
* viral infection, especially hepatitis
* autoimmune T-cell destruction of marrow stem cells
* Fanconi anemia, an inherited DNA repair disorder

---

## One-line intuition

Aplastic anemia = the bone marrow factory is shut down, so all blood cell lines drop.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Aplastic anemia causes **pancytopenia with hypocellular fatty marrow**, while leukemia can also cause pancytopenia but usually has a marrow packed with malignant cells.

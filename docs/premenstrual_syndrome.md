# Premenstrual syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/Menstrual_Cycle_Diagram.svg/960px-Menstrual_Cycle_Diagram.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500020/bin/Hormone__Secretion__and__Feedback4.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/PMDD_cycle_2.webp/960px-PMDD_cycle_2.webp.png)

Premenstrual syndrome, or **PMS (premenstrual syndrome)**, is a group of physical + mood symptoms that happen in the **luteal phase (after ovulation, before bleeding starts)**.

Classic timing:

* starts days to 1-2 weeks before menses
* improves once menstruation starts

Common symptoms:

* mood swings / irritability
* anxiety or low mood
* breast tenderness
* bloating
* headache
* fatigue
* food cravings

---

### Core idea

PMS is not usually from "too much hormone."

It is more like:

> the brain and body are sensitive to the normal cyclic changes in ovarian hormones.

Especially changes in:

* estrogen
* progesterone
* serotonin signaling

**Serotonin (mood-related neurotransmitter)** changes help explain why PMS can cause irritability, sadness, cravings, and sleep changes.

---

### Why it happens before the period

After ovulation, the corpus luteum makes progesterone.

If pregnancy does not happen:

* progesterone falls
* estrogen also shifts/falls
* the uterine lining sheds
* period begins

That late-luteal hormone withdrawal can trigger PMS symptoms in susceptible patients.

---

### PMS vs PMDD

**PMDD (premenstrual dysphoric disorder)** is the severe mood-dominant version.

Think:

* PMS = bothersome cyclic symptoms
* PMDD = severe cyclic mood symptoms that impair life/function

---

## One-line intuition

PMS is the body and brain reacting to the normal hormone shifts right before menstruation.

---

## Pearl 🔥

PMS/PMDD symptoms must be **cyclic**: they show up in the luteal phase and improve after menses starts. If symptoms are constant all month, think of another diagnosis like major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, hypothyroidism, or anemia.

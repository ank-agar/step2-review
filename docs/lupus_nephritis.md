### Core idea

Lupus nephritis means kidney inflammation caused by SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus).

The main problem is:

* anti-dsDNA antibodies (anti-double-stranded DNA antibodies)
* bind DNA/other nuclear material
* form immune complexes (antibody-antigen clumps)
* deposit in the glomeruli (kidney filtering units)
* activate complement (immune protein cascade)
* cause inflammation and kidney damage

So it is a type III hypersensitivity reaction (immune-complex-mediated disease).

---

### Why the findings happen

The glomerulus is basically a tiny blood filter.

When immune complexes get stuck there, the filter becomes inflamed and leaky:

* **Proteinuria (protein in urine):** damaged filtration barrier leaks protein
* **Hematuria (blood in urine):** inflamed capillaries leak RBCs (red blood cells)
* **RBC casts (red blood cell casts):** RBCs enter kidney tubules and mold into casts
* **Hypertension (high blood pressure):** damaged kidneys retain salt/water and activate RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system)
* **Low complement:** immune complexes consume complement proteins, especially C3 and C4

---

### Classic test clues

Think lupus nephritis when a patient with SLE has:

* hematuria
* proteinuria
* RBC casts
* elevated creatinine
* low C3/C4
* positive anti-dsDNA antibodies

Kidney biopsy can show a “full house” pattern on immunofluorescence, meaning deposits of multiple immune proteins:

* IgG (immunoglobulin G)
* IgA (immunoglobulin A)
* IgM (immunoglobulin M)
* C3
* C1q

---

### One-line intuition

Lupus nephritis is lupus immune complexes clogging and inflaming the kidney filter.

---

## Pearl

The most common and most severe Step 1 subtype is class IV diffuse proliferative lupus nephritis, where more than 50% of glomeruli are involved.

# Drowning

### Core idea

Drowning = respiratory impairment from being submerged/immersed in liquid.

The main problem is not usually "too much water in the lungs."

The main problem is:

* water blocks/irritates the airway
* oxygen cannot get into blood
* hypoxemia (low oxygen in blood) injures the brain and heart

---

### What happens physiologically

Water hits the airway and can cause:

* laryngospasm (vocal cords clamp shut)
* aspiration (material enters the airway/lungs)
* surfactant loss/dilution
* pulmonary edema (fluid in the lung air spaces)

Surfactant = the slippery substance made by type II pneumocytes (alveolar cells that make surfactant) that keeps alveoli (tiny lung air sacs) open.

So if water disrupts surfactant:

* alveoli collapse
* gas exchange worsens
* blood oxygen drops
* severe cases progress to ARDS (acute respiratory distress syndrome, severe inflammatory lung failure)

---

### One-line intuition

Drowning kills by hypoxia (low oxygen to tissues): the lungs stop exchanging oxygen, then the brain and heart fail.

---

### Important clarification

"Dry drowning" is not a useful medical diagnosis.

Sometimes laryngospasm limits water entry, but the dangerous pathway is still the same:

* airway problem
* low oxygen
* possible cardiac arrest

---

## Pearl 🔥

In drowning resuscitation, oxygenation/ventilation is the priority because drowning cardiac arrest is usually hypoxic, not primarily arrhythmic.

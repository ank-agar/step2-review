## Rectocele

### Core idea

A **rectocele** is when the **rectum bulges forward into the posterior vaginal wall**.

Posterior vaginal wall = back wall of the vagina.

So think:

* bladder is in front of the vagina
* rectum is behind the vagina
* if the tissue between vagina and rectum weakens, the rectum can pouch into the vagina

---

### Why it happens

The key structure is the **rectovaginal septum** (supportive tissue layer between the rectum and vagina).

If that support gets weak or torn:

* the rectum loses its normal posterior support
* pressure during straining pushes it forward
* a bulge forms into the vaginal canal

Common risk factors:

* vaginal childbirth
* chronic constipation/straining
* pelvic floor weakness
* aging or estrogen loss after menopause

---

### Symptoms

Can cause:

* vaginal bulge/pressure
* constipation
* incomplete stool evacuation
* need to press on the vagina/perineum to help pass stool

Perineum = area between the vaginal opening and anus.

---

### Important distinction

Rectocele is a type of **pelvic organ prolapse** (descent/bulging of pelvic organs due to weak support).

Compare:

* **cystocele** = bladder bulges into anterior vaginal wall
* **rectocele** = rectum bulges into posterior vaginal wall
* **uterine prolapse** = uterus descends downward

---

## One-line intuition

A rectocele is basically a "pouching" of the rectum into the vagina because the wall between them got weak.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Rectocele is associated with **obstructed defecation** (difficulty emptying stool), so patients may report needing **digital splinting** (pressing with fingers on the vagina/perineum to support the bulge and pass stool).

### Core idea

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH) = a baby is born with a hole/defect in the diaphragm (muscle separating chest from abdomen).

Because there is a hole, abdominal organs can move up into the chest, especially:

* intestines
* stomach
* spleen
* liver sometimes

Most commonly, this happens on the left side through a posterolateral defect called a Bochdalek hernia.

---

### Why it causes respiratory distress

The problem is not just “organs are in the wrong place.”

The big issue is:

* bowel/stomach move into the thorax (chest)
* they compress the developing lung
* the lung becomes small and underdeveloped

That underdeveloped lung is called pulmonary hypoplasia (incomplete lung development).

So after birth, the baby may have:

* severe respiratory distress
* cyanosis (blue discoloration from low oxygen)
* decreased breath sounds on one side
* bowel sounds in the chest
* scaphoid abdomen (sunken-looking abdomen because abdominal contents moved upward)

---

### Why the heart shifts

If bowel fills the left chest, it physically pushes the mediastinum (central chest compartment containing the heart, trachea, and major vessels) to the right.

So on chest X-ray, you can see:

* bowel loops in the chest
* heart shifted away from the hernia
* small/poorly expanded lung
* less gas in the abdomen

Intuition:

> The abdomen contents steal space from the lung.

---

### Embryology connection

The diaphragm normally forms by fusion of multiple embryologic parts, including the pleuroperitoneal membranes (embryologic sheets that help close the connection between chest and abdomen).

If they fail to close properly, there is a persistent opening.

That opening lets abdominal organs herniate into the chest.

---

### One-line intuition

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia = a hole in the diaphragm lets gut enter the chest, compressing the developing lungs and causing pulmonary hypoplasia.

---

## Pearl 🔥

CDH is classically a left-sided Bochdalek hernia, and the main cause of death is pulmonary hypoplasia + pulmonary hypertension (high pressure in lung blood vessels), not the diaphragm hole itself.

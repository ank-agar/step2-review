### Core idea

A solitary pulmonary nodule, or SPN (solitary pulmonary nodule), is a single round spot in the lung that is:

* less than 3 cm
* surrounded by normal lung tissue
* not associated with obvious lymph node enlargement or lung collapse

Pulmonary = lung.

Nodule = small rounded lesion.

If it is bigger than 3 cm, it is usually called a mass, which is more concerning for cancer.

---

### Why it matters

Most SPNs (solitary pulmonary nodules) are found incidentally, meaning by accident, on CXR (chest X-ray) or CT (computed tomography).

The big question is:

> Is this benign, or is this lung cancer?

Benign causes include:

* healed granuloma, which is a walled-off inflammatory lesion from old infection
* hamartoma, which is a benign overgrowth of normal lung tissue components
* old TB (tuberculosis) or fungal infection

Malignant causes include:

* primary lung cancer
* metastasis, meaning cancer spread from somewhere else

---

### What makes it scary

Features that increase concern for malignancy:

* older age
* smoking history
* larger size
* growth over time
* spiculated borders, meaning irregular star-like edges
* upper-lobe location

Why spiculation matters:

Cancer invades and pulls on surrounding tissue, so the border can look jagged instead of smooth.

---

## One-line intuition

A solitary pulmonary nodule is a single small “coin-like” spot in the lung, and the job is to decide whether it is an old scar/benign growth or early cancer.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Pulmonary hamartoma classically has “popcorn” calcifications and is a benign cause of a solitary pulmonary nodule.

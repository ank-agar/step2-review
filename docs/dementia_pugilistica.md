# Dementia pugilistica

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566126/bin/chapter1_f2.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK541013/bin/CTE2.jpg)

Dementia pugilistica = chronic traumatic encephalopathy, or CTE (chronic traumatic encephalopathy), classically in boxers.

Break it down:

* dementia = decline in cognition/memory
* pugilistica = related to boxing/fighting

So it basically means:

> progressive brain damage from repeated head trauma, especially repeated concussions/subconcussive hits.

Subconcussive hits = head impacts that may not cause obvious concussion symptoms but still stress the brain.

---

### Core idea

Repeated head trauma damages neurons over time and causes abnormal buildup of tau protein.

Tau protein normally stabilizes microtubules (cellular support/transport tracks inside neurons).

When tau becomes abnormal, it forms neurofibrillary tangles (twisted intracellular tau aggregates), which disrupt neuron function and eventually cause neurodegeneration.

---

### Symptoms

Can cause:

* memory problems
* confusion
* personality/behavior changes
* depression/impulsivity
* parkinsonism
* dementia

Parkinsonism = Parkinson-like motor findings such as tremor, rigidity, and slow movement.

---

### Why the findings happen

Repeated hits to the head cause mechanical stress on axons (long neuron projections).

That stress disrupts neuronal structure and makes tau misfold/aggregate.

So the pathway is:

repeated head trauma -> axonal injury -> abnormal tau buildup -> neuronal death -> cognitive + behavioral + motor decline

---

## One-line intuition

The brain gets repeatedly shaken/hit, tau protein clumps up inside neurons, and the person gradually develops dementia + behavior changes + Parkinson-like movement problems.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Dementia pugilistica/CTE is a **tauopathy** (disease involving abnormal tau protein), just like Alzheimer disease, but the classic history is **repetitive head trauma**.

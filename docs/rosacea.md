Rosacea is a chronic inflammatory skin condition that mainly affects the central face:

* cheeks
* nose
* forehead
* chin

Classic findings:

* facial flushing/redness
* telangiectasias (small dilated visible blood vessels)
* papules (small raised bumps)
* pustules (pus-filled bumps)
* sometimes eye irritation
* sometimes rhinophyma (thickened, bumpy nose)

---

### Core idea

The skin blood vessels and immune system are overly reactive.

So triggers like:

* heat
* alcohol
* spicy foods
* sunlight
* stress

can cause facial blood vessels to dilate, leading to redness/flushing.

---

### Why it looks like acne

Rosacea can cause papules and pustules, so it can look acne-like.

But unlike acne vulgaris:

* rosacea usually has **no comedones** (blackheads/whiteheads)
* rosacea is centered on the face
* flushing and telangiectasias are more prominent

---

### Ocular rosacea

Ocular means eye-related.

Rosacea can involve the eyelids/eyes and cause:

* red eyes
* gritty/burning sensation
* blepharitis (eyelid inflammation)
* dry eyes

---

## One-line intuition

Rosacea is basically facial skin with overreactive vessels + inflammation, causing flushing, redness, visible vessels, and acne-like bumps without comedones.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 distinction: **rosacea has papules/pustules but no comedones**, while acne vulgaris has comedones.

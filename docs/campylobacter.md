### Core idea

Campylobacter is a curved, gram-negative bacterium (bacteria with a thin peptidoglycan wall and outer membrane that stain pink/red on Gram stain) that classically causes inflammatory diarrhea after eating contaminated poultry.

Most Step 1 questions mean:

* Campylobacter jejuni
* undercooked chicken
* fever + abdominal cramps + bloody diarrhea
* curved or S-shaped rods

---

### Why poultry is the classic source

Campylobacter grows well at about 42°C, which is close to bird body temperature.

So it can live in the intestines of chickens without making the chicken sick, then contaminate meat during processing.

That is why the classic exposure is:

* undercooked poultry
* raw milk
* contaminated water
* animal feces exposure

---

### Why it causes bloody diarrhea

Campylobacter is invasive.

It does not just sit in the gut and secrete watery-toxin diarrhea. It invades and inflames the intestinal mucosa (inner lining of the intestine).

Inflamed mucosa leaks:

* blood
* neutrophils (acute inflammatory white blood cells)
* mucus

So the patient gets:

* fever
* crampy abdominal pain
* diarrhea that can become bloody

---

### Important complication

Campylobacter jejuni can trigger Guillain-Barre syndrome (GBS, an autoimmune demyelinating neuropathy causing ascending weakness).

Reason:

* Campylobacter has surface molecules that look like gangliosides (nerve membrane lipids)
* the immune system makes antibodies against the bacteria
* those antibodies can cross-react with peripheral nerves

This is molecular mimicry (immune response against a microbe accidentally attacks a similar-looking human structure).

---

## One-line intuition

Campylobacter is the undercooked-chicken, curved gram-negative bug that invades the gut and can later trigger Guillain-Barre syndrome.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Campylobacter grows best in microaerophilic conditions (low oxygen environment) at 42°C, which helps explain its association with birds/poultry.

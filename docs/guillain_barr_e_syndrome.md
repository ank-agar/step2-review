## Guillain-Barré syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/40/Guillain_Barre_Patterns.png)

![Image](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/mmanual-ssq1ci05/media/home/images/n/e/u/neu_insulating_nerve_ms.gif?mw=1080&sc_lang=en-us)

Guillain-Barré syndrome, or **GBS (Guillain-Barré syndrome)**, is an acute autoimmune attack on the **peripheral nerves** (nerves outside the brain and spinal cord).

Classic presentation:

* symmetric ascending weakness
* decreased or absent reflexes
* paresthesias (tingling/numbness)
* can progress to respiratory failure if diaphragm nerves are involved

---

### Core idea

The immune system attacks peripheral nerve myelin.

**Myelin** = fatty insulation around nerves that helps signals travel fast.

So if myelin gets damaged:

* nerve signals slow down
* muscles do not get strong signals
* weakness/paralysis happens
* reflex arcs fail, so reflexes decrease

---

### Why it happens after infection

Often occurs after:

* Campylobacter jejuni diarrhea
* viral respiratory infection

Reason:

The infection has antigens (immune-recognized molecules) that look similar to peripheral nerve components.

This is called **molecular mimicry** (immune system attacks a microbe, then accidentally attacks a similar-looking body tissue).

So:

> infection triggers antibodies → antibodies cross-react with peripheral nerves → demyelination → weakness

---

### Why weakness is “ascending”

GBS often starts in the legs and moves upward.

Think:

* long peripheral nerves to the legs are vulnerable
* damaged signal conduction shows up first as trouble walking
* then weakness can spread to arms, face, and breathing muscles

This is why respiratory status matters.

---

### Key test finding

Lumbar puncture (spinal tap) classically shows:

* high CSF protein
* normal WBC count

CSF = cerebrospinal fluid (fluid around the brain and spinal cord)  
WBC = white blood cell

This is called **albuminocytologic dissociation**:

* albumino = protein
* cytologic = cells
* dissociation = they do not rise together

So:

> high protein without many inflammatory cells.

---

### Treatment

Treat with:

* IVIG (intravenous immunoglobulin; pooled antibodies that calm/pathway-block the immune attack)
* plasmapheresis (filtering antibodies out of the blood)

Do **not** rely on steroids for classic GBS.

---

## One-line intuition

GBS is post-infectious immune damage to peripheral nerve myelin, causing symmetric ascending weakness with low reflexes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

GBS = **LMN (lower motor neuron) signs**: flaccid weakness + decreased reflexes.

That helps separate it from spinal cord lesions, which usually give **UMN (upper motor neuron) signs** like spasticity and hyperreflexia below the lesion.

### Core idea

A ureteral stricture is a narrowing of the ureter (tube that carries urine from the kidney to the bladder).

Think:

> scarred/tight ureter = urine cannot drain well.

So urine backs up above the narrowing.

---

### What happens upstream

If urine gets blocked in the ureter, pressure builds backward into the kidney:

* hydroureter = dilated ureter
* hydronephrosis = dilated renal pelvis/calyces (urine-collecting spaces inside the kidney)
* flank pain = kidney capsule stretches from pressure
* recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection) = stagnant urine lets bacteria grow
* kidney damage = chronic back pressure injures renal tissue

---

### Common causes

Usually from scarring after:

* kidney stone passage or ureteroscopy (scope procedure inside the ureter)
* surgery near the ureter
* radiation therapy
* trauma
* chronic inflammation
* congenital narrowing (present from birth)

---

### Why the findings happen

The ureter is supposed to be an open tube with peristalsis (smooth muscle squeezing waves that move urine).

If the lumen (inside hollow space) becomes narrowed:

* urine flow slows
* pressure rises behind the blockage
* the kidney collecting system dilates
* long-term pressure can reduce kidney function

So the main danger is not the narrow tube itself.

The danger is silent obstruction damaging the kidney.

---

## One-line intuition

A ureteral stricture is basically a scar-like bottleneck in the kidney-to-bladder pipe, causing urine backup into the kidney.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Do not confuse ureteral stricture with urethral stricture:

* ureter = kidney to bladder
* urethra = bladder to outside world

Ureteral obstruction causes hydronephrosis because the blockage is above the bladder.

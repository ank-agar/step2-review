### Core idea

Erysipelas is a superficial skin infection involving the:

* upper dermis (middle skin layer)
* superficial lymphatics (tiny lymph drainage vessels)

It is usually caused by **Group A Streptococcus** (Streptococcus pyogenes bacteria).

---

### What it looks like

Classic findings:

* bright red rash
* raised border
* sharply demarcated edge
* warmth
* tenderness
* fever/chills sometimes

The key visual clue is:

> well-demarcated, raised, red plaque.

---

### Why it looks raised and sharply bordered

Erysipelas is more superficial than cellulitis.

Because it mainly affects the upper dermis and superficial lymphatics, inflammation and edema (fluid swelling) stay closer to the surface.

That gives it the classic:

* raised edge
* clear border
* shiny red appearance

Cellulitis is deeper, so it tends to look more flat and poorly demarcated.

---

### Common locations

Usually on:

* face
* lower legs

It can happen after a break in the skin, like:

* cut
* ulcer
* fungal infection between toes
* surgical wound

because bacteria need an entry point.

---

## One-line intuition

Erysipelas is like superficial cellulitis: Group A strep infects the upper dermis/lymphatics, causing a hot, tender, bright-red rash with a sharp raised border.

---

## Pearl

Erysipelas is classically **Group A strep**, so it is usually **nonpurulent** (not pus-forming), unlike many Staphylococcus aureus skin infections that can form abscesses.

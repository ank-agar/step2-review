### Core idea

Fungal meningitis is inflammation/infection of the meninges (protective membranes around the brain and spinal cord) caused by fungi.

Think:

* fungus usually starts in lungs after inhalation
* spreads through blood
* reaches CNS (central nervous system)
* infects CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) and meninges

Most HY cause:

* Cryptococcus neoformans

especially in:

* HIV/AIDS (human immunodeficiency virus/acquired immunodeficiency syndrome)
* transplant patients
* chronic steroid use
* other immunocompromised states

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Meninges get inflamed, so you get:

* headache: inflamed meninges are pain-sensitive
* neck stiffness: stretching inflamed meninges hurts
* fever: infection triggers inflammatory cytokines (immune signaling proteins)
* photophobia (light sensitivity): meningeal irritation can make light painful
* confusion: infection/inflammation can affect brain function

Fungal meningitis is often more subacute/chronic than bacterial meningitis, so symptoms may build over days to weeks.

---

### CSF findings

CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) often shows:

* lymphocytes increased
* protein increased
* glucose decreased
* opening pressure increased, especially in cryptococcal meningitis

Why low glucose?

The fungus and inflammatory cells are metabolically active, so they consume glucose in the CSF.

Why high protein?

Inflammation makes the blood-brain barrier (barrier between blood and brain/CSF) leakier, so more protein enters the CSF.

---

### HY organisms

Cryptococcus neoformans:

* classically in AIDS when CD4 count is very low
* has a thick polysaccharide capsule
* India ink stain can show a clear halo around the yeast

Coccidioides:

* fungus from the Southwest United States
* can cause chronic meningitis

Candida:

* more likely with neurosurgery, shunts, or very sick hospitalized patients

---

## One-line intuition

Fungal meningitis is usually a slow meningitis in an immunocompromised patient because inhaled fungi spread from lung to blood to meninges.

---

## HY pearl

Cryptococcus neoformans has a thick capsule, so think:

* India ink stain = clear halo
* cryptococcal antigen test in CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) = very useful diagnosis
* AIDS patient with headache + high opening pressure = cryptococcal meningitis until proven otherwise

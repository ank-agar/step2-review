## Femoral hernia

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18025.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482388/bin/Gray546.jpg)

### Core idea

A femoral hernia is when bowel or abdominal tissue pushes through the femoral canal.

The femoral canal is a small space in the upper thigh/groin area, just below the inguinal ligament.

Think:

* inguinal hernia = above the inguinal ligament
* femoral hernia = below the inguinal ligament

---

### Anatomy intuition

The femoral canal sits medial to the femoral vein.

Medial means closer to the midline of the body.

So a femoral hernia classically bulges:

* below the inguinal ligament
* medial to the femoral vein
* in the upper thigh/groin region

---

### Why it matters

Femoral hernias have a high risk of incarceration and strangulation.

* incarceration = hernia gets stuck and cannot be pushed back in
* strangulation = blood supply gets cut off

Why?

Because the femoral canal is narrow and rigid, so bowel can get trapped more easily.

---

### Who gets it?

More common in females, especially older females.

Reason:

* wider pelvis
* larger femoral canal
* increased intra-abdominal pressure can push tissue through weak spots

Intra-abdominal pressure means pressure inside the belly.

---

## One-line intuition

A femoral hernia is bowel slipping through a tight hole below the inguinal ligament, which is why it is more likely to strangulate.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, femoral hernia = below inguinal ligament + medial to femoral vein + high strangulation risk.

## Pancreatic pseudocyst

### Core idea

A pancreatic pseudocyst is a walled-off fluid collection near the pancreas after pancreatitis (inflammation of the pancreas) or pancreatic trauma.

It is called **pseudo** because it is not a true cyst:

* true cyst = has an epithelial lining (cell layer lining the inside)
* pseudocyst = no epithelial lining

Instead, the wall is made of fibrous tissue and granulation tissue (healing inflammatory tissue).

---

### What is inside it?

The fluid is basically leaked pancreatic juice:

* pancreatic enzymes
* inflammatory debris
* blood/necrotic material sometimes

Reasoning:

Pancreatitis damages pancreatic ducts/acinar cells (enzyme-producing pancreatic cells), so enzyme-rich fluid leaks out. The body tries to contain it by walling it off with scar-like inflammatory tissue.

---

### Why it happens after pancreatitis

Think:

pancreatitis → pancreatic enzyme leakage → local tissue digestion → fluid collection → body walls it off

The pancreas makes digestive enzymes, so when those enzymes leak into the wrong place, they irritate and digest surrounding tissue.

That creates a fluid pocket near the pancreas.

---

### Symptoms

Can cause:

* epigastric pain (upper middle abdominal pain)
* nausea/vomiting
* early satiety (feeling full quickly)
* palpable abdominal mass sometimes
* fever if infected

Large pseudocysts can compress nearby structures:

* stomach/duodenum → vomiting or early satiety
* common bile duct (tube carrying bile from liver/gallbladder to intestine) → jaundice

---

## One-line intuition

A pancreatic pseudocyst is spilled pancreatic enzyme fluid that the body walls off after pancreatitis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Step 1 loves the phrase: **pseudocyst has no epithelial lining**.

That is the key feature separating it from a true cyst.

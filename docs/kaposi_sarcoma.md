# Kaposi sarcoma

Kaposi sarcoma is a vascular tumor caused by HHV-8 (human herpesvirus 8).

It classically causes purple/red-brown lesions on:

* skin
* mouth
* GI tract (gastrointestinal tract)
* lungs

It is especially associated with immunosuppression, especially AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome).

---

### Core idea

HHV-8 infects endothelial cells.

Endothelial cells = cells that line blood vessels and lymphatic vessels.

Then the virus makes them:

* proliferate
* form abnormal leaky vessels
* recruit inflammation
* avoid apoptosis (programmed cell death)

So the lesion becomes a blood-vessel-heavy tumor.

That is why it looks purple:

* lots of abnormal vessels
* RBCs (red blood cells) leak out
* leaked blood gives a bruise-like color

---

### Why it happens in AIDS

AIDS causes low CD4 T cells (helper T cells).

CD4 T cells normally help control viruses and tumors.

So with low CD4 count:

* HHV-8 is less controlled
* infected endothelial cells survive
* vascular tumor growth becomes more likely

This is why Kaposi sarcoma is an AIDS-defining illness.

---

### Histology

Classic biopsy findings:

* spindle cells
* slit-like vascular spaces
* extravasated RBCs (red blood cells outside vessels)
* hemosiderin (iron pigment from old blood)
* HHV-8 positive staining

Spindle cells = long, thin tumor cells.

Slit-like vascular spaces = narrow abnormal blood-filled channels.

---

## One-line intuition

Kaposi sarcoma is HHV-8-driven overgrowth of leaky endothelial cells, so it makes purple vascular tumors, especially when the immune system is weak.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY association:

* Kaposi sarcoma = HHV-8
* primary effusion lymphoma = HHV-8
* multicentric Castleman disease = HHV-8

So if Step 1 says purple skin/oral lesions in an AIDS patient, think HHV-8 immediately.

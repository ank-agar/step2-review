### Core idea

Irritant contact dermatitis is skin inflammation caused by direct injury from an irritant.

It is not an allergy.

Common irritants:

* soaps
* detergents
* water/wet work
* solvents
* acids or alkalis
* friction

---

### What happens

The irritant damages the epidermal barrier.

Epidermis = outer skin layer.

Barrier damage causes:

* loss of protective oils
* increased water loss
* keratinocyte injury
* cytokine release
* inflammation

Cytokines = inflammatory signaling proteins.

Keratinocytes = main epidermal skin cells.

---

### Why it looks red, dry, cracked

If the outer skin barrier is damaged, the skin cannot hold water well.

So you get:

* dryness
* scaling
* fissures/cracks
* erythema
* burning or stinging

Erythema = redness from increased blood flow.

Fissures = painful skin cracks.

---

### Key clue

Irritant contact dermatitis usually stays where the irritant touched the skin.

Example:

* repeated handwashing → hand dermatitis
* wet cement exposure → sharply localized dermatitis on exposed skin

---

### Irritant vs allergic contact dermatitis

Irritant contact dermatitis:

* direct chemical/physical damage
* no prior sensitization needed
* often burning > itching
* can happen quickly after exposure

Allergic contact dermatitis:

* type IV hypersensitivity
* needs prior sensitization
* often itching > burning
* delayed, usually 24-48 hours later

Type IV hypersensitivity = delayed T-cell-mediated immune reaction.

T cells = lymphocytes that coordinate cell-mediated immunity.

---

## One-line intuition

Irritant contact dermatitis is basically the skin barrier getting stripped or injured faster than it can repair itself.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember: irritant contact dermatitis is innate, nonimmune direct toxicity, while allergic contact dermatitis is type IV hypersensitivity.

### Core idea

Tenosynovial giant cell tumor (TGCT) is a usually benign tumor-like overgrowth of synovium.

Synovium = the lining of:

* joints
* tendon sheaths
* bursae

So the name means:

* teno- = tendon
* synovial = synovium/joint lining
* giant cell = has multinucleated giant cells on histology
* tumor = abnormal mass/proliferation

---

### Main forms

* **Localized TGCT:** small, nodular mass, often in fingers; also called giant cell tumor of tendon sheath
* **Diffuse TGCT:** more spread-out synovial disease, often in the knee; older name is pigmented villonodular synovitis (PVNS)

Diffuse = more locally aggressive and more likely to damage the joint.

---

### Why it causes symptoms

The synovium grows too much, so it takes up space inside/around the joint.

That causes:

* swelling
* pain
* stiffness
* decreased range of motion
* sometimes joint destruction over time

The tumor often has hemosiderin.

Hemosiderin = iron-storage pigment from old blood breakdown.

That is why it can look brown/yellow grossly and dark on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).

---

### Pathology intuition

TGCT is driven by excess colony-stimulating factor 1 (CSF1).

CSF1 attracts macrophages.

Macrophages = immune cells that eat debris and can fuse into multinucleated giant cells.

So a small number of abnormal synovial cells can recruit a huge crowd of macrophages/giant cells.

That is the “landscape effect” idea:

> a few tumor cells call in many inflammatory cells.

---

### What you see on histology

Classic findings:

* multinucleated giant cells
* macrophages
* hemosiderin-laden macrophages
* foamy histiocytes

Histiocytes = tissue macrophages.

---

## One-line intuition

TGCT is an overgrown synovial lining that recruits macrophages, making a joint/tendon-sheath mass full of giant cells and hemosiderin.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Diffuse TGCT = pigmented villonodular synovitis (PVNS), classically involving the knee and showing low signal on MRI because hemosiderin contains iron.

### Core idea

Osteopenia means **low bone mineral density**, but not low enough to be called osteoporosis.

Think:

* normal bone = strong scaffold
* osteopenia = scaffold is starting to thin
* osteoporosis = scaffold is very thin and fracture-prone

So osteopenia is basically the **middle zone between normal bone and osteoporosis**.

---

### Diagnosis

Usually diagnosed by **DXA scan** (**dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry**, a bone density scan).

T-score:

* normal: **greater than -1**
* osteopenia: **-1 to -2.5**
* osteoporosis: **less than -2.5**

T-score means how your bone density compares to a healthy young adult.

---

### Why it happens

Bone is constantly remodeled by:

* **osteoclasts** = cells that break down bone
* **osteoblasts** = cells that build bone

Osteopenia happens when bone breakdown slowly exceeds bone formation.

Common reasons:

* aging
* low estrogen after menopause
* low vitamin D
* low calcium intake
* chronic steroid use
* smoking
* immobility

Why estrogen matters:

Estrogen normally keeps osteoclast activity down. When estrogen drops, osteoclasts become more active, so bone gets resorbed faster.

---

### Why it matters

Osteopenia itself is often asymptomatic.

The concern is:

* weaker bones
* higher fracture risk
* progression to osteoporosis

So you usually catch it before the patient has a fracture.

---

## One-line intuition

Osteopenia = bone density is below normal, but it has not crossed the osteoporosis threshold yet.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Chronic glucocorticoids increase fracture risk because they **decrease osteoblast activity** and **increase bone resorption**, so steroid use is a classic risk factor for osteopenia/osteoporosis.

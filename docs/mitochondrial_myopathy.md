### Core idea

Mitochondrial myopathy = muscle disease caused by defective mitochondria.

Mitochondria normally make ATP (adenosine triphosphate), which is the cell's usable energy.

So if mitochondria do not work well:

* muscle cannot make enough ATP
* muscle fatigues easily
* exercise becomes difficult
* lactic acid can build up

---

### Why muscle is hit hard

Muscle uses a lot of energy, especially during exercise.

So mitochondrial dysfunction shows up as:

* exercise intolerance
* proximal muscle weakness
* muscle pain
* fatigue
* sometimes ophthalmoplegia (eye movement weakness)

Intuition:

> Muscle is like a high-energy machine. If the power plants are broken, the machine gets weak fast.

---

### Why lactic acidosis can happen

Normally mitochondria use OXPHOS (oxidative phosphorylation) to make lots of ATP using oxygen.

If OXPHOS is impaired, cells rely more on anaerobic glycolysis (making energy without oxygen).

That produces lactate, so you can get:

* lactic acidosis
* worse symptoms with exertion

---

### Classic biopsy clue

Muscle biopsy can show ragged red fibers.

Ragged red fibers = muscle fibers with abnormal buildup of mitochondria under the sarcolemma (muscle cell membrane).

They look "ragged" because the mitochondria pile up irregularly around the edges of the muscle fiber.

---

### Inheritance clue

Many mitochondrial diseases are maternally inherited.

That means:

* mom can pass it to all children
* affected dad passes it to none of his children

Why?

Because mtDNA (mitochondrial DNA) comes from the egg, not the sperm.

---

## One-line intuition

Mitochondrial myopathy is when defective muscle mitochondria cannot make enough ATP, so muscle runs out of energy too quickly.

---

## Pearl 🔥

MERRF (myoclonic epilepsy with ragged red fibers) is a classic mitochondrial disease with:

* myoclonus (sudden jerky movements)
* epilepsy (seizures)
* ragged red fibers on muscle biopsy

### Core idea

Ramsay-Hunt syndrome is basically:

* shingles reactivation near the ear
* that hits CN VII (cranial nerve 7, the facial nerve)
* causing facial paralysis + painful ear vesicles

The virus is VZV (varicella-zoster virus), the same virus that causes chickenpox and shingles.

---

### Classic triad

Think:

* ipsilateral facial paralysis
* ear pain
* vesicles in or around the ear

Ipsilateral means "same side."

So if the virus reactivates on the right facial nerve:

* right facial droop
* right ear pain
* right ear vesicles

---

### Why the findings happen

After chickenpox, VZV (varicella-zoster virus) hides dormant in nerve ganglia.

In Ramsay-Hunt, it reactivates in the geniculate ganglion, which is a sensory ganglion of CN VII (facial nerve).

That causes:

* **facial paralysis:** CN VII controls facial expression muscles
* **ear vesicles:** CN VII has sensory fibers around parts of the ear
* **ear pain:** inflamed sensory nerve fibers hurt
* **loss of taste:** CN VII carries taste from the anterior 2/3 of the tongue
* **hyperacusis:** CN VII innervates stapedius, which dampens loud sounds
* **dry eye:** CN VII helps parasympathetic tearing

Hyperacusis means sounds feel abnormally loud.

---

### Important distinction from Bell palsy

Bell palsy is idiopathic facial nerve palsy.

Idiopathic means "unknown cause."

Ramsay-Hunt is facial nerve palsy **with VZV reactivation**, so look for:

* severe ear pain
* vesicles in the ear canal, auricle, palate, or tongue
* possible hearing loss, tinnitus, or vertigo

Tinnitus means ringing in the ear.

Vertigo means a false spinning sensation.

---

## One-line intuition

Ramsay-Hunt = shingles in the facial nerve near the ear, so you get facial droop plus painful ear blisters.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

Ramsay-Hunt syndrome can involve CN VIII (cranial nerve 8, the vestibulocochlear nerve) because it is anatomically close to CN VII, so patients can also get hearing loss, tinnitus, and vertigo.

### Core idea

Thrombocytopenia means **low platelets**.

Break the word apart:

* thrombo- = clot
* cyto- = cell
* -penia = low

So thrombocytopenia basically means:

> too few platelet clotting fragments in the blood.

Platelets are small blood fragments that help form the first plug at sites of vessel injury.

---

### Why the bleeding happens

Platelets are mainly responsible for **primary hemostasis** (the first platelet plug).

If platelets are low:

* small vessel leaks are not sealed well
* blood leaks into skin and mucosa
* patient gets superficial bleeding

Classic findings:

* petechiae (tiny pinpoint red-purple bleeding spots)
* purpura (larger purple skin bleeding spots)
* easy bruising
* gum bleeding
* nosebleeds
* heavy menstrual bleeding

This is different from coagulation factor problems, which cause deeper bleeding like hemarthrosis (bleeding into joints).

---

### Big causes

Think of thrombocytopenia as:

* **not making enough platelets**
* **destroying/using up platelets**
* **trapping platelets in the spleen**

Examples:

* decreased production: bone marrow failure, leukemia, chemotherapy, alcohol, vitamin B12 (cobalamin) or folate deficiency
* increased destruction: ITP (immune thrombocytopenic purpura), HIT (heparin-induced thrombocytopenia), drugs
* consumption: DIC (disseminated intravascular coagulation), TTP (thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura), HUS (hemolytic uremic syndrome)
* sequestration: splenomegaly (enlarged spleen)

---

### Labs

Main lab:

* low platelet count

Usually:

* increased bleeding time
* normal PT (prothrombin time)
* normal PTT (partial thromboplastin time)

Why PT and PTT are usually normal:

PT and PTT test coagulation factor pathways, not platelet number.

Exception:

* DIC (disseminated intravascular coagulation) can cause low platelets **and** abnormal PT/PTT because both platelets and clotting factors get consumed.

---

## One-line intuition

Thrombocytopenia is a platelet-number problem, so the body cannot make good surface-level platelet plugs, causing petechiae, purpura, bruising, and mucosal bleeding.

---

## Pearl

Platelet disorders cause **mucocutaneous bleeding** (skin and mucous membrane bleeding), while coagulation factor disorders cause **deep bleeding** like hemarthrosis.

# GERD

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19609.jpg)

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17892/N01024-H_thumbnail.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/16/Peptic_stricture.png)

GERD (gastroesophageal reflux disease) = chronic reflux of stomach contents back up into the esophagus (food tube).

Core idea:

* the stomach has acid
* the esophagus is not built to handle acid
* the LES (lower esophageal sphincter) is supposed to close and block reflux
* if the LES is weak/relaxes too much, acid goes upward

So GERD is basically:

> weak anti-reflux barrier → acid enters esophagus → burning/inflammation.

---

### Why symptoms happen

* **Heartburn:** acid irritates the esophageal mucosa (lining)
* **Regurgitation:** stomach contents physically come back up
* **Chest discomfort:** esophageal irritation can mimic cardiac-type pain
* **Chronic cough/hoarseness:** reflux can reach the throat/larynx (voice box) and irritate it
* **Dysphagia (difficulty swallowing):** chronic inflammation can cause narrowing/stricture

---

### Risk factors

Things that increase reflux:

* obesity
* pregnancy
* hiatal hernia (part of stomach slides above diaphragm)
* smoking
* fatty foods, chocolate, caffeine, alcohol
* lying down after meals

Why obesity/pregnancy matter:

* increased abdominal pressure pushes stomach contents upward.

---

### Complications

Chronic acid injury can cause:

* esophagitis (inflammation of the esophagus)
* peptic stricture (scar-related narrowing)
* Barrett esophagus (intestinal metaplasia of lower esophagus)
* increased risk of esophageal adenocarcinoma

Metaplasia = one mature cell type changes into another mature cell type because of chronic stress.

---

## One-line intuition

GERD is when the stomach's acid barrier fails, so acid keeps splashing into the esophagus and injuring it.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Barrett esophagus = nonkeratinized stratified squamous epithelium of the esophagus changes to intestinal-type columnar epithelium, increasing risk for esophageal adenocarcinoma.

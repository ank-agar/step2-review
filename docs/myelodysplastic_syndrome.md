### Core idea

Myelodysplastic syndrome, or MDS (myelodysplastic syndrome), is a clonal bone marrow disorder where the marrow makes blood cells badly.

Break it down:

* myelo- = bone marrow / myeloid blood cell line
* dysplastic = abnormal development
* syndrome = group of findings

So MDS basically means:

> abnormal marrow development causing ineffective blood cell production.

---

### What happens

The bone marrow is usually hypercellular, meaning packed with lots of developing blood cells.

But those cells are defective and die before becoming useful mature cells.

So the weird paradox is:

* marrow is full
* blood is empty

That causes cytopenias (low blood cell counts), especially:

* anemia (low red blood cells) → fatigue, pallor, shortness of breath
* neutropenia (low neutrophils, infection-fighting white blood cells) → infections
* thrombocytopenia (low platelets) → bruising, bleeding, petechiae (tiny pinpoint skin bleeds)

---

### Why the cells look weird

The problem is the hematopoietic stem cell (blood-forming stem cell).

Because the stem cell clone is abnormal, its descendants mature abnormally:

* red blood cells can look oddly shaped
* neutrophils can have abnormal nuclei, like pseudo-Pelger-Huet cells (hyposegmented neutrophils)
* platelets/megakaryocytes (platelet-producing marrow cells) can be abnormal

That is why MDS is called a dysplasia (abnormal cell formation), not just low production.

---

### Big Step 1 association

MDS can transform into acute myeloid leukemia, or AML (acute myeloid leukemia).

Why?

Because MDS is already a clonal myeloid stem cell disorder. If that abnormal clone keeps acquiring mutations, blasts (immature blood cells) can build up.

Once blasts are high enough, it becomes AML.

---

### Risk factors

Classically seen in older adults.

Also associated with prior:

* chemotherapy
* radiation
* benzene exposure

because these damage DNA in marrow stem cells.

---

## One-line intuition

MDS is a broken bone marrow factory: lots of defective workers are being made, but few functional blood cells leave the factory.

---

## Pearl

MDS often has a hypercellular bone marrow with peripheral pancytopenia (low red cells, white cells, and platelets in the blood).

That is the key contrast from aplastic anemia, where the marrow is hypocellular (empty/fatty marrow).

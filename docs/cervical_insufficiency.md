### Core idea

Cervical insufficiency means the cervix (lower opening of the uterus) is too weak to stay closed during pregnancy.

So the cervix painlessly:

* shortens
* thins
* dilates

usually in the second trimester, before true labor contractions.

---

### Why it causes pregnancy loss or preterm birth

Think of the cervix like the “closed door” holding the pregnancy inside the uterus.

If the door is weak, pressure from the growing pregnancy pushes it open too early.

That can lead to:

* bulging fetal membranes
* rupture of membranes
* second-trimester pregnancy loss
* preterm birth (birth before 37 weeks)

Key intuition:

> the problem is mechanical weakness, not painful uterine contractions.

---

### Ultrasound clue

On ultrasound, you may see a short cervix and funneling.

Funneling means the internal os (opening of cervix closest to the uterus) starts opening first, so the membranes dip down into the cervical canal.

It can progress like:

* T-shape = closed
* Y-shape = early funneling
* V-shape = worse funneling
* U-shape = more advanced opening

---

### Treatment idea

Cervical cerclage means placing a stitch around the cervix to help keep it closed.

This is especially considered if there is a strong history of cervical insufficiency or a high-risk short cervix during pregnancy.

---

### Risk factors

Things that can weaken the cervix:

* prior cervical surgery
* LEEP (loop electrosurgical excision procedure)
* cone biopsy
* cervical trauma from prior delivery
* congenital connective tissue problems
* DES (diethylstilbestrol) exposure in utero

---

## One-line intuition

Cervical insufficiency = a weak cervix opens too early, causing painless second-trimester dilation and risk of pregnancy loss or preterm birth.

---

## HY pearl

Classic test clue: painless cervical dilation in the second trimester with minimal or no contractions.

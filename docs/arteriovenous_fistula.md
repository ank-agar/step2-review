# Arteriovenous fistula

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Blausen_0049_ArteriovenousFistula.png/500px-Blausen_0049_ArteriovenousFistula.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK563296/bin/Types__of__Hemodialysis.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK27392/bin/ch11f1a.gif)

An arteriovenous fistula, or AV fistula (arteriovenous fistula), is an abnormal or surgically created connection between:

* an artery (high-pressure vessel carrying blood away from the heart)
* a vein (low-pressure vessel carrying blood back to the heart)

Normally blood goes:

> artery -> arteriole -> capillary -> venule -> vein

In an AV fistula, blood skips the capillaries (tiny exchange vessels) and goes directly:

> artery -> vein

---

### Core idea

High-pressure arterial blood gets shunted into a low-pressure vein.

So the vein gets:

* bigger
* thicker
* higher-flow
* easier to access with needles

This is why AV fistulas are intentionally made for hemodialysis (blood filtering treatment used in kidney failure).

---

### Why it matters for dialysis

Regular veins are too thin and low-flow for repeated dialysis needle sticks.

But if you connect a vein to an artery:

* arterial pressure makes the vein enlarge
* the vein wall thickens
* blood flow becomes strong enough for dialysis

This process is called maturation (the fistula becoming large/strong enough to use).

---

### Physical exam finding

You can often feel a thrill (vibration from turbulent blood flow) over the fistula.

You can also hear a bruit (whooshing sound from turbulent blood flow) with a stethoscope.

Intuition:

> fast arterial blood entering a vein creates turbulence, so you feel/hear buzzing.

---

### Why complications happen

Because the fistula is a low-resistance shortcut:

* blood bypasses capillaries
* venous return to the heart increases
* preload (blood returning to the heart) increases
* total peripheral resistance, or TPR (resistance in systemic blood vessels), decreases

If the shunt is large, the heart may pump more and more to keep up, causing high-output heart failure (heart failure from excessive circulatory demand despite strong pumping).

It can also cause steal syndrome (ischemia of the hand because blood is diverted into the fistula instead of going distally to the fingers).

---

## One-line intuition

An AV fistula is a shortcut from artery to vein: useful for dialysis because it makes a big high-flow vein, but risky because it steals/bypasses normal capillary flow.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, AV fistula = decreased TPR (total peripheral resistance) + increased venous return/preload, which can lead to high-output heart failure.

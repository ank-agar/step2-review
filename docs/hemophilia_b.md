# Hemophilia B

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK580494/bin/Coagulation__Pathway__Frontiers.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19095.jpg)

### Core idea

Hemophilia B is an X-linked recessive bleeding disorder caused by deficiency of factor IX (clotting factor 9).

Another name:

* Christmas disease

Not because of the holiday — it was named after a patient named Stephen Christmas.

---

### Why bleeding happens

Factor IX is part of the intrinsic coagulation pathway (the clotting pathway measured by PTT, or partial thromboplastin time).

Normally:

* factor XI activates factor IX
* factor IX works with factor VIII
* together they help activate factor X
* factor X eventually helps make fibrin clot

So if factor IX is missing:

* clot formation is weak
* bleeding continues longer
* deep tissue bleeding happens

This is why hemophilia causes:

* hemarthrosis (bleeding into joints)
* deep muscle hematomas (collections of blood)
* prolonged bleeding after surgery/trauma

---

### Lab pattern

Classic labs:

* increased PTT (partial thromboplastin time)
* normal PT (prothrombin time)
* normal bleeding time
* normal platelet count

Why?

PT is normal because the extrinsic pathway is okay.

Bleeding time is normal because platelets are okay.

The problem is secondary hemostasis (fibrin clot formation), not primary hemostasis (platelet plug formation).

---

### Inheritance

X-linked recessive means:

* males are usually affected
* females are usually carriers
* no father-to-son transmission

Reason:

Fathers give sons a Y chromosome, not an X chromosome.

---

## One-line intuition

Hemophilia B = factor IX deficiency, so the intrinsic pathway cannot build a strong fibrin clot.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Hemophilia A and B look very similar clinically and both have increased PTT, but:

* Hemophilia A = factor VIII deficiency
* Hemophilia B = factor IX deficiency

Both are X-linked recessive and cause deep bleeding/hemarthrosis, unlike platelet disorders, which more often cause mucosal bleeding/petechiae (tiny pinpoint skin bleeds).

### Core idea

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is the most common kidney cancer in adults.

It usually arises from the proximal convoluted tubule (PCT, the early part of the nephron tubule that reabsorbs lots of filtered stuff).

So think:

> kidney tubule epithelial cell becomes cancerous.

---

### Classic presentation

Classic triad:

* hematuria (blood in urine)
* flank pain (pain on the side/back near the kidney)
* palpable abdominal mass

But the triad is usually late.

Many cases are found incidentally on computed tomography (CT, cross-sectional X-ray imaging).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Hematuria:** tumor invades kidney tissue/collecting system, so blood leaks into urine
* **Flank pain/mass:** tumor grows and stretches/distorts the kidney capsule
* **Varicocele:** tumor can invade/compress the renal vein, especially on the left side
* **Polycythemia:** tumor can make erythropoietin (EPO, hormone that stimulates red blood cell production)
* **Hypercalcemia:** tumor can make parathyroid hormone-related peptide (PTHrP, a hormone-like protein that raises calcium)

---

### Pathology clue

Clear cell renal cell carcinoma is the most common subtype.

The cells look "clear" because they contain lots of lipid/glycogen, which gets washed out during tissue processing.

Grossly, it can look yellow because of lipid-rich tumor cells.

---

## One-line intuition

Renal cell carcinoma is a kidney tubule cancer that often hides until it bleeds, grows large, or makes weird hormones.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Renal cell carcinoma is associated with von Hippel-Lindau disease (VHL disease, an inherited tumor syndrome), due to loss of the VHL tumor suppressor gene.

### Core idea

Subclavian steal syndrome is when a blockage/narrowing in the subclavian artery (artery under the clavicle that supplies the arm) makes blood flow backward down the vertebral artery (artery that supplies the posterior brain) to feed the arm.

So the arm basically "steals" blood from the posterior circulation (blood supply to brainstem/cerebellum/occipital lobes).

---

### Why this happens

Normally:

* heart pumps blood into subclavian artery
* subclavian artery sends blood to the arm
* vertebral artery branches off and sends blood upward to the brain

But if the proximal subclavian artery (part before the vertebral artery branch) is stenosed (narrowed):

* pressure past the blockage drops
* the arm still needs blood
* blood reverses direction in the vertebral artery
* blood goes from brain circulation down into the arm

That reversed vertebral flow is the "steal."

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* arm symptoms
* posterior brain symptoms
* unequal arm blood pressures

Symptoms can include:

* dizziness/vertigo (spinning sensation)
* syncope (fainting)
* ataxia (poor coordination)
* diplopia (double vision)
* arm claudication (arm pain/fatigue with use)

Key exam clue:

* lower blood pressure in the affected arm
* weaker pulse in the affected arm

---

### Why arm exercise makes it worse

Using the affected arm increases demand for blood.

That makes the low-pressure area past the subclavian blockage even more "hungry," so more blood gets pulled backward from the vertebral artery.

That is why neurologic symptoms can be triggered by arm exertion.

---

## One-line intuition

A blocked subclavian artery makes the arm siphon blood backward from the vertebral artery, causing posterior brain hypoperfusion (low blood flow).

---

## Pearl 🔥

Subclavian steal is usually due to atherosclerosis (cholesterol plaque disease), and the board-style clue is neurologic symptoms plus a blood pressure difference between arms.

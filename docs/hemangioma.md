### Core idea

A hemangioma is a benign vascular tumor, meaning a non-cancerous growth made of blood vessels.

Most classic Step 1 version:

* infantile hemangioma
* bright red "strawberry" lesion
* appears in the first weeks of life
* grows quickly at first
* then slowly involutes, meaning shrinks/regresses over time

---

### Why it looks red

It is packed with proliferating endothelial cells.

Endothelial cells are the cells that line blood vessels.

So the logic is:

> more tiny blood vessels = more blood-filled spaces = red/purple lesion

Superficial lesions look bright red because they are close to the skin surface.

Deeper lesions can look bluish because the blood vessel mass is under more tissue.

---

### Important distinction

Hemangioma is a vascular tumor.

Vascular malformation is different.

* **Hemangioma:** endothelial cells proliferate, grows after birth, then often regresses
* **Vascular malformation:** abnormal vessel structure, grows with the child, usually does not regress

That is why hemangiomas often have a rapid growth phase and then an involution phase.

---

### Histology

On histology, capillary hemangioma shows many small blood vessel channels lined by endothelial cells.

Those little vascular channels are full of RBCs (red blood cells), which is why the lesion is so red grossly.

---

## One-line intuition

A hemangioma is basically a benign overgrowth of blood vessel lining cells, so it forms a red blood-vessel mass that often shrinks with time.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Infantile hemangiomas can be treated with propranolol, a beta blocker, if they are large, ulcerating, near the eye/airway, or causing functional problems.

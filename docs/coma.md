### Core idea

Coma is a state of **unresponsiveness** where the patient:

* cannot be awakened
* does not have normal sleep-wake cycles
* does not respond meaningfully to external stimuli

Think:

> brain is alive, but the “wakefulness system” is offline.

---

### What has to be damaged?

Consciousness needs 2 big things:

* **ARAS (ascending reticular activating system):** brainstem system that keeps the cortex awake
* **bilateral cerebral cortex:** both sides of the thinking/awareness part of the brain

So coma usually comes from:

* brainstem damage affecting the ARAS
* diffuse bilateral cortical dysfunction
* metabolic/toxic problems suppressing the whole brain

---

### Why coma happens

If the ARAS is damaged, the cortex does not get the “stay awake” signal.

If both cerebral hemispheres are diffusely impaired, the brain cannot generate awareness even if the brainstem is intact.

Common causes:

* trauma
* stroke or hemorrhage
* hypoxia (low oxygen)
* hypoglycemia (low blood glucose)
* drug overdose
* hepatic encephalopathy (brain dysfunction from liver failure)
* increased ICP (intracranial pressure)

---

### How to measure it

Use **GCS (Glasgow Coma Scale)**, which scores:

* eye opening
* verbal response
* motor response

Score range:

* 15 = awake/normal
* 3 = deepest coma

Low GCS = worse consciousness.

---

## One-line intuition

Coma is when the brain’s arousal system or both cerebral hemispheres are so impaired that the patient cannot wake up or interact.

---

## Pearl 🔥

A unilateral cerebral cortex lesion usually does **not** cause coma by itself.

For coma, think:

* **brainstem ARAS lesion**
* or **bilateral cortical dysfunction**
* or **diffuse metabolic/toxic suppression**

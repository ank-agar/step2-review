## Roseola

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/17255.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Roseola_on_a_21-month-old_girl.jpg/250px-Roseola_on_a_21-month-old_girl.jpg)

### Core idea

Roseola is a viral illness in young kids caused mostly by:

* HHV-6 (human herpesvirus 6)
* sometimes HHV-7 (human herpesvirus 7)

Classic pattern:

* high fever for about 3-5 days
* fever suddenly goes away
* then a pink maculopapular rash appears

Maculopapular means:

* macules = flat spots
* papules = small raised bumps

---

### Why the rash comes after the fever

During the first few days, the virus is causing systemic infection, so the main finding is:

* high fever

Then the immune system starts controlling the virus. As the fever breaks, the immune reaction in the skin causes the rash.

So the key timing is:

> fever first, rash after defervescence

Defervescence means the fever goes down.

---

### Rash pattern

Roseola rash usually:

* starts on the trunk
* spreads to neck, face, and extremities
* is usually not itchy
* is usually not painful
* resolves on its own

This is why parents often panic right when the child is actually improving.

---

### Important complication

The high fever can trigger febrile seizures.

Febrile seizure means a seizure caused by fever in a young child, not necessarily epilepsy.

---

## One-line intuition

Roseola is the toddler illness where the kid has scary high fever for a few days, then the fever breaks and the rash suddenly appears.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Roseola is also called exanthem subitum or sixth disease, and the Step 1 buzzword is:

* HHV-6
* high fever
* rash after fever resolves

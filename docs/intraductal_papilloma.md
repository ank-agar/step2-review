### Core idea

An intraductal papilloma is a benign tumor growing inside a breast duct.

Break the word apart:

* intra- = inside
* ductal = duct, meaning a tube that carries fluid
* papilloma = finger-like benign epithelial growth

So it is basically:

> a small finger-like growth inside a milk duct.

---

### Classic presentation

Most classic finding:

* spontaneous bloody nipple discharge

Often:

* from one breast
* from one duct
* near the nipple, especially if solitary

---

### Why bloody discharge happens

The papilloma grows into the duct lumen (open space inside the duct).

Because it has a fibrovascular core:

* fibro- = fibrous connective tissue
* vascular = blood vessels

it can bleed easily into the duct.

That blood then exits through the nipple, causing bloody nipple discharge.

---

### What it looks like

Histology (microscope appearance):

* branching papillary projections
* fibrovascular stalks
* lined by epithelial and myoepithelial cells

Myoepithelial cells are contractile cells around breast ducts/glands.

Why that matters:

* preserved myoepithelial cells usually support a benign process
* loss of myoepithelial cells is more concerning for invasion

---

### Cancer risk intuition

Intraductal papilloma itself is benign.

But it can be biopsied or removed because doctors want to rule out:

* atypia (abnormal-looking cells)
* DCIS (ductal carcinoma in situ, cancer cells still confined inside ducts)
* invasive carcinoma

Intuition:

Bloody nipple discharge can be benign, but because duct lesions can overlap clinically, tissue diagnosis is often needed.

---

## One-line intuition

A benign, vascular, finger-like growth inside a breast duct can bleed into the duct and cause spontaneous bloody nipple discharge.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 pearl: Intraductal papilloma is a classic benign cause of bloody nipple discharge, especially when the discharge is spontaneous and unilateral.

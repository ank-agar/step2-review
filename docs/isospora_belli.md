# Isospora belli

Isospora belli, now called **Cystoisospora belli**, is a protozoan parasite (single-celled parasite) that infects the small intestine.

It causes **cystoisosporiasis** (intestinal infection from Cystoisospora), classically with:

* watery, nonbloody diarrhea
* abdominal cramps
* malabsorption
* weight loss

Big association:

* AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome)
* other immunocompromised states

---

### Core idea

You ingest the mature oocyst (hard environmental egg-like parasite form) from contaminated food or water.

Then it invades small-intestinal epithelial cells (lining cells), causing diarrhea and malabsorption.

---

### Why the diarrhea happens

The parasite damages enterocytes (intestinal absorptive cells), so the gut cannot absorb nutrients and water normally.

So you get:

* watery diarrhea
* weight loss
* malabsorption

In immunocompromised patients, the immune system cannot clear it well, so diarrhea can become chronic.

---

### Diagnosis

Look for **large oval oocysts** in stool.

They are:

* acid-fast positive on modified acid-fast stain
* oval/elliptical
* can autofluoresce under UV (ultraviolet) microscopy

---

### Treatment

Treat with **TMP-SMX** (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole).

This is the same drug combo often used for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia prophylaxis in AIDS.

---

## One-line intuition

Isospora belli is an AIDS-associated intestinal parasite that causes chronic watery diarrhea by invading small-intestinal lining cells.

---

## Pearl 🔥

High-yield Step 1 clue: **acid-fast oocysts + watery diarrhea in an AIDS patient** should make you think of coccidian parasites (spore-forming protozoa), especially Cryptosporidium, Cyclospora, and Cystoisospora.

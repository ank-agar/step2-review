# Supraspinatus syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e9/Supraspinatus.PNG)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19614.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5a/Rotator_cuff_tear.jpg)

### Core idea

Supraspinatus syndrome means irritation/inflammation/degeneration of the supraspinatus tendon, usually from impingement under the acromion (bony roof over the shoulder joint).

The supraspinatus is one of the rotator cuff muscles.

Rotator cuff = SITS:

* supraspinatus
* infraspinatus
* teres minor
* subscapularis

---

### What supraspinatus normally does

Supraspinatus helps:

* start arm abduction (moving arm away from body)
* stabilize the humeral head (top of upper arm bone) inside the shoulder socket

So if it is irritated, shoulder abduction hurts.

---

### Why it hurts

The supraspinatus tendon passes through a narrow space under the acromion.

If that space gets tight, the tendon gets pinched.

Pinching causes:

* tendon inflammation
* pain with overhead movement
* weakness from pain inhibition
* sometimes partial or full-thickness rotator cuff tear

Think:

> tendon scraping under the acromion every time the patient raises the arm.

---

### Classic findings

Pain is usually worse with:

* overhead activity
* reaching behind the back
* lying on the affected shoulder
* arm abduction

Classic exam clues:

* painful arc from about 60-120 degrees of abduction
* positive empty can test
* weakness or pain with resisted abduction

Empty can test = patient abducts arms and points thumbs downward like emptying cans; pain/weakness suggests supraspinatus pathology.

---

### Why the painful arc happens

At low angles, the tendon is not maximally compressed.

From about 60-120 degrees, the supraspinatus tendon is squeezed under the acromion, so pain increases.

Above that range, the scapula (shoulder blade) rotates upward and gives the tendon more room, so pain may improve.

---

## One-line intuition

Supraspinatus syndrome is basically a rotator cuff impingement problem where the supraspinatus tendon gets pinched under the acromion, causing painful arm abduction.

---

## Pearl

Supraspinatus initiates the first 15 degrees of abduction and is innervated by the suprascapular nerve (C5-C6); the deltoid then takes over most abduction and is innervated by the axillary nerve.

### Core idea

Hepatic encephalopathy means brain dysfunction from liver failure or portosystemic shunting.

* hepatic = liver
* encephalopathy = brain dysfunction
* portosystemic shunting = portal blood bypasses the liver and enters systemic circulation

So the simple idea is:

> the liver is not detoxifying blood well, so toxins reach the brain.

---

### Main toxin

The big toxin to know is ammonia.

Ammonia is normally made in the gut from protein breakdown and bacterial metabolism.

Normally:

* gut makes ammonia
* portal vein carries it to liver
* liver converts ammonia to urea
* urea gets excreted by kidneys

In cirrhosis or liver failure:

* liver cannot process ammonia well
* portal blood may bypass the liver through shunts
* ammonia builds up in blood
* ammonia crosses the blood-brain barrier

Blood-brain barrier = tight barrier that controls what enters the brain from blood.

---

### Why ammonia causes confusion

In the brain, astrocytes try to detoxify ammonia by turning it into glutamine.

Astrocytes = support cells in the central nervous system.

Glutamine pulls in water osmotically, so astrocytes swell.

That causes:

* impaired neurotransmission
* confusion
* sleep-wake reversal
* personality changes
* decreased alertness
* coma in severe cases

Intuition:

The liver fails as a filter, ammonia floods the brain, astrocytes swell, and brain signaling gets messy.

---

### Classic finding

Asterixis is the classic exam finding.

Asterixis = flapping movement when the patient extends arms and wrists, caused by brief loss of posture tone.

It is often called a flapping tremor, but technically it is negative myoclonus.

Myoclonus = sudden jerky muscle movement.

Negative myoclonus = sudden brief loss of muscle contraction.

---

### Common triggers

Hepatic encephalopathy is often triggered by something that increases ammonia or stresses the body:

* GI bleed (gastrointestinal bleed)
* infection
* constipation
* dehydration
* high protein load
* sedatives like benzodiazepines
* electrolyte abnormalities, especially hypokalemia

Hypokalemia = low potassium.

Why GI bleed matters:

Blood in the gut is basically a big protein meal. Gut bacteria break it down and make more ammonia.

---

### Treatment idea

Lactulose is first-line.

Lactulose = nonabsorbable sugar that traps ammonia in the gut and makes the patient stool it out.

It works by:

* acidifying the colon
* converting ammonia, NH3, into ammonium, NH4+
* NH4+ is charged, so it stays in the gut
* increased stooling removes it

Rifaximin can be added.

Rifaximin = nonabsorbable antibiotic that decreases ammonia-producing gut bacteria.

---

## One-line intuition

Hepatic encephalopathy is when a failing or bypassed liver lets ammonia reach the brain, causing astrocyte swelling and altered mental status.

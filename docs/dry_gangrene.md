Dry gangrene = tissue death from poor blood supply, usually in an extremity like toes, feet, or fingers.

Think:

* artery gets narrowed/blocked
* less oxygen reaches tissue
* tissue dies
* dead tissue dries out and turns black

So dry gangrene is basically:

> ischemic necrosis with drying/mummification.

Ischemia = inadequate blood flow.

Necrosis = tissue death.

Mummification = tissue becomes dry, shrunken, and black.

---

### Why it looks dry and black

Dry gangrene usually happens slowly from chronic arterial insufficiency.

Arterial insufficiency = not enough blood coming through arteries.

Because blood flow is low:

* little fluid enters the tissue
* bacteria do not thrive as easily
* the tissue dries out instead of becoming swollen/pus-filled
* hemoglobin breakdown and dead tissue make it dark brown/black

This is why it looks like a dry, black, shriveled toe/finger.

---

### Common causes

Most important causes:

* peripheral artery disease, or PAD (atherosclerotic narrowing of arteries outside the heart/brain)
* diabetes mellitus, or DM (high blood sugar causing vascular disease and poor wound healing)
* smoking
* severe atherosclerosis (cholesterol plaque buildup in arteries)
* embolus (clot/debris traveling and blocking an artery)
* frostbite

---

### Dry vs wet gangrene

Dry gangrene:

* due mainly to ischemia
* dry, black, shriveled tissue
* usually slower
* less infection at first

Wet gangrene:

* ischemia plus infection
* swollen, moist, foul-smelling tissue
* can spread fast
* higher risk of sepsis

Sepsis = life-threatening systemic inflammation from infection.

---

## One-line intuition

Dry gangrene is what happens when a body part loses blood supply slowly enough that it dies and dries out before getting infected.

---

## HY pearl

Dry gangrene is classically due to coagulative necrosis from ischemia, while wet gangrene has superimposed bacterial infection and is much more likely to cause sepsis.

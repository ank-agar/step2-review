# Apparent life-threatening event

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470403/bin/CPR_Infant_Closed_vs_Open_Airway.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e1/Cyanotic_neonate.jpg)

**Apparent life-threatening event (ALTE)** is the old term for a scary episode in an infant where the baby seems like they might be dying.

The newer term is **BRUE (brief resolved unexplained event)**.

BRUE means:

* brief
* resolved
* unexplained
* event in an infant younger than 1 year

Classic features:

* apnea (not breathing)
* cyanosis (blue discoloration from low oxygen) or pallor (pale color)
* choking or gagging
* limpness or stiffness
* altered responsiveness

---

### Core idea

The baby suddenly looks really bad, then quickly returns to normal.

So the key is:

> scary episode + infant + resolved by the time you evaluate them.

---

### Why it happens

Many cases are not from one obvious disease.

Possible mechanisms include:

* reflux causing airway irritation
* choking or aspiration (material entering the airway)
* infection
* seizure
* cardiac arrhythmia (abnormal heart rhythm)
* child abuse/nonaccidental trauma

The airway image is useful because infants have small, easily obstructed airways. A tiny change in position, secretions, reflux, or poor coordination can transiently block airflow.

---

### Why the name changed

ALTE (apparent life-threatening event) was based on how scary it looked to the caregiver.

BRUE (brief resolved unexplained event) is more objective:

* brief = short
* resolved = baby is back to baseline
* unexplained = no clear cause after history and physical exam

So BRUE avoids overcalling every scary episode as truly life-threatening.

---

## One-line intuition

An ALTE/BRUE is basically: **a baby suddenly stops breathing or turns blue/pale, then returns to normal, and you cannot immediately explain why.**

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember: **ALTE is the old name; BRUE is the current name.** BRUE requires age **less than 1 year** and symptoms like cyanosis/pallor, abnormal breathing, tone change, or altered responsiveness.

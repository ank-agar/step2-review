# Ethylene glycol poisoning

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK600985/bin/ch3f9.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1f/Calcium_oxalate_crystals_in_urine.jpg)

Ethylene glycol poisoning = poisoning from **antifreeze**.

The key idea:

* ethylene glycol itself is not the main problem
* its **metabolites** (breakdown products) are the problem

Your body metabolizes it using **alcohol dehydrogenase** (enzyme that breaks down alcohols):

* ethylene glycol
* glycolaldehyde
* glycolic acid
* oxalic acid
* calcium oxalate crystals

The toxic stuff causes:

* **anion gap metabolic acidosis** (too much acid in blood from unmeasured acidic molecules)
* kidney injury
* calcium oxalate crystals in urine
* sometimes hypocalcemia (low calcium)

---

### Why acidosis happens

Ethylene glycol gets turned into acidic molecules, especially **glycolic acid**.

So the blood becomes acidic.

That gives:

* low pH
* low bicarbonate
* high anion gap

Think:

> ethylene glycol becomes acids, so you get anion gap metabolic acidosis.

---

### Why kidney injury happens

Oxalic acid binds calcium and forms **calcium oxalate** crystals.

These crystals deposit in renal tubules (kidney tubes), causing tubular damage.

So you may see:

* flank pain
* hematuria (blood in urine)
* acute kidney injury
* envelope-shaped or needle-shaped calcium oxalate crystals in urine

---

### Timing intuition

Early:

* looks like alcohol intoxication
* CNS depression (brain slowing, confusion, coma)
* high osmolar gap

Later:

* high anion gap metabolic acidosis
* calcium oxalate crystals
* kidney failure

Why?

Because early on you still have the parent alcohol in blood, which raises the **osmolar gap** (extra dissolved particles in blood).

Later, it has been metabolized into acids, so the **anion gap** rises.

---

### Treatment

Give **fomepizole**.

Fomepizole inhibits **alcohol dehydrogenase**.

So it blocks:

> ethylene glycol turning into toxic acidic metabolites.

Ethanol can also work because it competes for alcohol dehydrogenase, but fomepizole is preferred.

Severe cases may need **hemodialysis** (blood filtering machine), especially with severe acidosis, renal failure, or very high ethylene glycol level.

---

## One-line intuition

Ethylene glycol is antifreeze that gets metabolized into acids and oxalate, causing anion gap metabolic acidosis plus calcium oxalate kidney injury.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Ethylene glycol poisoning classically causes:

* increased osmolar gap early
* increased anion gap later
* calcium oxalate crystals in urine

Step 1 clue: antifreeze ingestion + kidney failure + calcium oxalate crystals = ethylene glycol.

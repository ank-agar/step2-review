### Core idea

Compartment syndrome = increased pressure inside a closed muscle compartment.

A compartment is a group of muscles, nerves, and blood vessels wrapped by fascia (tough connective tissue).

The key problem:

* muscle swells
* fascia cannot stretch much
* pressure rises
* capillaries get compressed
* blood cannot perfuse the tissue
* muscle and nerve ischemia (low oxygen from low blood flow) happens

---

### Intuition

Think of the muscle as swelling inside a tight box.

The box is fascia.

If the muscle expands but the box cannot expand, pressure builds up and squeezes the tiny blood vessels shut.

That is why this is dangerous even if pulses are still present.

---

### Classic causes

Usually after trauma, especially:

* fractures, especially tibial fractures
* crush injury
* tight cast or bandage
* burns
* reperfusion injury (blood flow returns after ischemia)

Reperfusion injury means tissue was starved of blood, then blood flow comes back and causes swelling/inflammation.

---

### Symptoms

Most important early finding:

* pain out of proportion to the injury
* pain with passive stretch of the involved muscle

Other findings:

* tense/swollen compartment
* paresthesias (pins-and-needles sensation)
* weakness/paralysis later

The classic "5 Ps":

* pain
* pallor
* paresthesia
* paralysis
* pulselessness

But pulselessness is late.

---

### Why pulses can still be present

Compartment pressure first collapses capillaries (tiny vessels where oxygen exchange happens).

Large arteries have higher pressure, so they may still stay open.

So:

> normal pulses do not rule out compartment syndrome.

---

### Treatment

Acute compartment syndrome is a surgical emergency.

Treatment is fasciotomy (cutting open the fascia).

Why it works:

* fascia is the tight wrapper
* cutting it open releases the pressure
* blood flow can return to muscle and nerves

---

## One-line intuition

Compartment syndrome is swollen muscle trapped inside tight fascia, causing pressure high enough to shut down capillary blood flow.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, the biggest trap is thinking "no pulse = compartment syndrome."

Actually, pulselessness is late.

The high-yield early clue is severe pain with passive stretch.

# Müllerian agenesis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/55/Mullerian_duct_development.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d5/Rokitansky_illustration.png)

![Image](https://nccommons.org/m/e/e2/Gonadal_and_mullerian_agenesis_%28Radiopaedia_159376-130635_Axial_148%29.jpg)

Müllerian agenesis = congenital failure of the Müllerian ducts (embryologic tubes that form the uterus, fallopian tubes, cervix, and upper vagina) to develop.

Also called:

* MRKH syndrome (Mayer-Rokitansky-Küster-Hauser syndrome)

Classic presentation:

* primary amenorrhea (no first period)
* normal breast development
* normal pubic/axillary hair
* normal external genitalia
* absent uterus
* short or absent upper vagina
* normal ovaries
* 46,XX karyotype (typical female chromosome pattern)

---

### Why puberty looks normal

The ovaries are normal, so they still make estrogen.

Estrogen causes:

* breast development
* normal female body habitus

But the uterus is absent, so there is no endometrium (uterine lining) to shed.

So:

> normal puberty, but no periods.

---

### Why ovaries are present

Ovaries do **not** come from the Müllerian ducts.

Müllerian ducts form:

* fallopian tubes
* uterus
* cervix
* upper vagina

Ovaries come from the gonadal ridge (embryologic tissue that forms gonads), so they can be normal even when Müllerian structures are missing.

---

### Key Step 1 contrast

Müllerian agenesis vs androgen insensitivity syndrome:

* Müllerian agenesis: 46,XX, normal ovaries, normal female testosterone level
* AIS (androgen insensitivity syndrome): 46,XY, testes present, high testosterone, scant pubic/axillary hair

Both can have:

* primary amenorrhea
* absent uterus

But AIS has little body hair because androgen receptors do not work.

---

## One-line intuition

The ovaries work, so puberty happens; the uterus is missing, so menstruation cannot happen.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Müllerian agenesis is associated with renal anomalies, especially unilateral renal agenesis (missing one kidney), because reproductive and urinary tract development are embryologically linked.

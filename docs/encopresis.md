Encopresis = repeated stool soiling in inappropriate places, usually in a child who is already toilet trained.

Most commonly, it is due to chronic constipation causing overflow incontinence (leakage around retained stool).

---

### Core idea

The child holds in stool because pooping hurts.

Then:

* stool sits in the rectum
* colon absorbs more water
* stool becomes harder and bigger
* rectum stretches out
* rectal sensation decreases
* liquid stool leaks around the hard stool

So the child may look like they have diarrhea, but the real problem is constipation.

---

### Why the accidents happen

The rectum is supposed to feel stretch and tell the brain:

> go poop now.

But with long-standing stool retention, the rectum becomes chronically stretched.

That causes:

* poor sensation
* weak urge to defecate
* overflow leakage

This is why encopresis is usually not “bad behavior.” The child often genuinely does not feel it happening.

---

### Classic presentation

Think of:

* school-age child
* stool stains or accidents in underwear
* history of constipation
* large painful stools
* abdominal pain
* withholding behavior, like crossing legs or hiding

Sometimes parents say:

> “My child has diarrhea.”

But it may actually be liquid stool leaking around impacted stool.

---

### Treatment intuition

You have to empty the rectum first.

Treatment usually involves:

* cleanout/disimpaction (removing impacted stool)
* polyethylene glycol, or PEG (osmotic laxative that pulls water into stool)
* maintenance laxatives
* scheduled toilet sitting after meals
* no punishment

Why after meals?

Because eating triggers the gastrocolic reflex (colon movement after food enters the stomach), making it easier to stool.

---

### One-line intuition

Encopresis is usually constipation so severe that the rectum stretches, stops sensing stool well, and liquid stool leaks around the blockage.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 idea: encopresis is commonly from functional constipation, but always think about Hirschsprung disease if there is delayed passage of meconium (first newborn stool) more than 48 hours after birth, chronic constipation from infancy, abdominal distension, or failure to thrive.

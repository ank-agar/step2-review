### Core idea

Avian influenza = bird flu.

It is infection with influenza A virus that mainly lives in birds.

Most important human-associated subtypes:

* H5N1
* H7N9

H = hemagglutinin, the viral protein that helps the virus bind to host cells.

N = neuraminidase, the viral protein that helps new viruses leave infected cells.

So H5N1 means:

* hemagglutinin type 5
* neuraminidase type 1

---

### Why birds are the main reservoir

Wild aquatic birds, like ducks and geese, are natural reservoirs.

Reservoir means the organism where the pathogen normally lives and persists.

These birds can shed virus in:

* saliva
* mucus
* feces

So the virus spreads easily through contaminated water, cages, farms, or bird droppings.

---

### How humans get it

Humans usually get avian influenza after close exposure to infected birds or contaminated material.

Classic risk setting:

* poultry farm worker
* live bird market exposure
* handling sick or dead birds

The virus enters through:

* eyes
* nose
* mouth
* inhaled droplets or dust

This is why conjunctivitis (eye redness/inflammation) can happen, especially with some avian flu strains.

---

### Why it can be severe

Avian influenza is not well-adapted to humans.

But if it does infect humans, it can cause intense lower respiratory tract disease:

* viral pneumonia
* acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS; severe inflammatory lung injury causing low oxygen)
* respiratory failure

Intuition:

Seasonal flu is already adapted to spread human-to-human.

Avian flu is more like a bird virus that accidentally enters a human, so it may not spread efficiently, but it can trigger severe disease when it reaches the lungs.

---

### Low pathogenic vs highly pathogenic

LPAI = low pathogenic avian influenza.

HPAI = highly pathogenic avian influenza.

This classification is based on how sick it makes chickens, not necessarily how sick it makes humans.

High pathogenicity in poultry means:

* rapid spread in flocks
* severe bird illness
* high bird death rate

---

### Why people worry about pandemics

Influenza A has a segmented RNA (ribonucleic acid) genome.

Segmented means its genome is split into separate pieces.

If two different influenza A viruses infect the same cell, they can swap genome segments.

This is called reassortment.

The scary possibility:

* human flu virus provides efficient human-to-human spread
* avian flu virus provides novel H/N proteins that humans have little immunity against
* reassortment creates a new pandemic-capable strain

One-line intuition:

Bird flu is dangerous because birds are a huge influenza mixing pool, and influenza A can reshuffle its genome.

---

### Treatment/prevention idea

Treatment can include neuraminidase inhibitors, like oseltamivir.

Oseltamivir inhibits neuraminidase, so new flu viruses have a harder time leaving infected cells.

Prevention is mostly exposure control:

* avoid sick/dead birds
* personal protective equipment (PPE; masks, gloves, eye protection)
* poultry surveillance
* culling infected flocks when needed

---

## One-line intuition

Avian influenza is influenza A from birds that usually does not spread well between humans, but can cause severe pneumonia and is high-yield because influenza A can reassort.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Influenza A is the one associated with antigenic shift because it has a segmented genome and animal reservoirs.

Antigenic shift = sudden major change from reassortment.

Antigenic drift = gradual minor mutation over time.

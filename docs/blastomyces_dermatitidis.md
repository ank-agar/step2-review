Blastomyces dermatitidis is a **dimorphic fungus** (a fungus that changes form depending on temperature).

Key idea:

* in the environment/soil: mold form
* in the body: yeast form

So remember:

> Mold in the cold, yeast in the heat.

It causes **blastomycosis** (fungal infection caused by Blastomyces).

---

### Classic Step 1 morphology

Blastomyces is famous for:

* **broad-based budding yeast**
* thick cell wall
* usually in lungs first

Broad-based budding means the daughter yeast cell attaches to the parent yeast cell with a wide connection.

This is different from narrow-based budding, where the bud looks like it is attached by a tiny neck.

---

### How infection happens

You inhale spores from soil/decaying wood.

Then in the warm lung, the fungus converts from mold into yeast.

Why?

* soil temperature favors mold
* body temperature favors yeast
* yeast form is better at surviving in human tissue

This is why many systemic fungi are dimorphic.

---

### Symptoms/clinical picture

Usually starts as a pneumonia-like illness:

* fever
* cough
* chest pain
* shortness of breath

It can disseminate (spread from the original infection site) to:

* skin
* bone
* prostate
* CNS (central nervous system; brain and spinal cord)

Skin lesions can look verrucous (wart-like) or ulcerative.

---

### Geography

Classically found near:

* Ohio River valley
* Mississippi River valley
* Great Lakes

Think moist soil + decaying organic matter.

---

## One-line intuition

Blastomyces is a dimorphic fungus inhaled from soil that turns into broad-based budding yeast in the lung and can spread to skin/bone.

---

## Pearl

**Broad-based budding = Blastomyces.**

For Step 1, if they show a systemic fungal infection with pneumonia + skin/bone lesions + broad-based budding yeast, the answer is Blastomyces dermatitidis.

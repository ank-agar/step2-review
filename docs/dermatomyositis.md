Dermatomyositis is an autoimmune inflammatory myopathy (muscle inflammation disease) with skin findings.

Break it down:

* dermato- = skin
* myo- = muscle
* -itis = inflammation

So:

> inflammation of skin + muscle.

Classic findings:

* proximal muscle weakness
* heliotrope rash
* Gottron papules

Proximal muscle weakness means weakness near the trunk:

* shoulders
* hips
* thighs
* upper arms

So patients have trouble:

* climbing stairs
* rising from a chair
* lifting arms above the head

---

### Why the skin + muscle findings happen

This is mainly immune-mediated small-vessel damage.

Autoantibodies and complement (immune proteins that punch holes in targets) damage tiny blood vessels in muscle and skin.

That causes:

* muscle ischemia (low blood flow) → muscle fiber injury → weakness
* skin inflammation → purple/red rashes

The classic muscle biopsy clue is:

* perifascicular atrophy

Meaning muscle fibers at the edges of fascicles (bundles of muscle fibers) shrink first because the small blood vessels supplying them are injured.

---

### Classic rashes

* Heliotrope rash = violaceous (purple-colored) rash around the eyelids
* Gottron papules = purple/red papules over knuckles
* Shawl sign = rash over shoulders/upper back
* V-sign = rash over anterior chest in a V-shaped sun-exposed area

---

## One-line intuition

Dermatomyositis is an autoimmune attack on small vessels that shows up as proximal muscle weakness plus distinctive purple skin rashes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Adult dermatomyositis is associated with malignancy (cancer), especially ovarian, lung, pancreatic, stomach, and colorectal cancers.

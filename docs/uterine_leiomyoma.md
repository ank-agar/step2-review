Uterine leiomyoma = uterine fibroid.

It is a benign tumor of smooth muscle from the myometrium (muscle layer of the uterus).

Break the word apart:

* leio- = smooth
* myo- = muscle
* -oma = tumor

So leiomyoma literally means:

> smooth muscle tumor

In the uterus, that means a benign growth coming from uterine smooth muscle.

---

### Core idea

Fibroids are firm, well-circumscribed masses in or around the uterus.

They can be:

* intramural = inside the uterine muscle wall
* submucosal = under the mucosa/endometrium (inner uterine lining)
* subserosal = under the serosa (outer uterine surface)
* pedunculated = attached by a stalk

Location matters because symptoms depend on what the fibroid distorts.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* Heavy menstrual bleeding: especially submucosal fibroids distort the endometrium (uterine lining), so bleeding surface area increases
* Pelvic pressure/pain: the mass physically pushes on surrounding pelvic structures
* Urinary frequency: fibroid can press on the bladder
* Constipation: fibroid can press on the rectum
* Infertility or recurrent pregnancy loss sometimes: fibroid can distort the uterine cavity and interfere with implantation

Fibroids are estrogen-sensitive, so they often grow during reproductive years and may shrink after menopause.

---

### Pathology image intuition

Grossly, leiomyomas often look like pale, whorled, rubbery masses.

Microscopically, they show bundles of spindle-shaped smooth muscle cells.

Spindle-shaped = long, narrow cells that look like stretched-out ovals.

---

## One-line intuition

A uterine leiomyoma is a benign estrogen-sensitive smooth muscle ball growing in the uterus.

---

## Pearl

Submucosal fibroids are the ones most classically associated with heavy menstrual bleeding because they distort the endometrial cavity.

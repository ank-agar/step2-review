### Core idea

ACS (acute coronary syndrome) means sudden decreased blood flow through a coronary artery (heart artery).

Usually the cause is:

* atherosclerotic plaque (cholesterol plaque in an artery wall) ruptures
* platelets stick to it
* a thrombus (blood clot) forms
* blood flow to myocardium (heart muscle) drops

So ACS is basically:

> plaque rupture + clot = sudden heart ischemia.

Ischemia means low oxygen delivery to tissue.

---

### The 3 big types

ACS includes:

* unstable angina
* NSTEMI (non-ST elevation myocardial infarction)
* STEMI (ST elevation myocardial infarction)

MI (myocardial infarction) means heart muscle death.

---

### How to separate them

Think:

* ECG (electrocardiogram) tells you ST changes
* troponin tells you if heart muscle died

Troponin is a cardiac muscle protein released into blood when myocardial cells are injured.

| Type | Blockage | ECG | Troponin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unstable angina | partial/intermittent | no ST elevation, may have ST depression/T-wave inversion | normal |
| NSTEMI | partial/intermittent | no ST elevation, may have ST depression/T-wave inversion | elevated |
| STEMI | complete | ST elevation | elevated |

---

### Why STEMI has ST elevation

STEMI usually means complete coronary artery occlusion.

That causes transmural ischemia (ischemia through the full thickness of the heart wall).

Full-thickness injury changes the electrical current across the myocardium, so the ECG shows ST elevation.

---

### Why NSTEMI does not have ST elevation

NSTEMI is usually partial occlusion.

Some blood still gets through, so ischemia is often subendocardial.

Subendocardial means the inner layer of the heart wall, which is vulnerable because it is farthest from the epicardial coronary blood supply and gets compressed during systole (heart contraction).

So NSTEMI often gives:

* ST depression
* T-wave inversion
* elevated troponin

---

### Why unstable angina has normal troponin

Unstable angina has ischemia but no infarction.

So the heart muscle is oxygen-starved, but not dead enough to leak troponin.

That is why:

* chest pain can be serious
* ECG (electrocardiogram) can look abnormal
* but troponin stays normal

---

## One-line intuition

ACS is a sudden coronary clot problem; STEMI is usually full blockage, NSTEMI is partial blockage with cell death, and unstable angina is partial blockage without cell death.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Troponin separates NSTEMI from unstable angina:

* NSTEMI = troponin elevated
* unstable angina = troponin normal

Both can have no ST elevation.

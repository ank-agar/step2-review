## Acute upper respiratory infection

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Blausen_0766_RespiratoryEpithelium.png/330px-Blausen_0766_RespiratoryEpithelium.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19656.jpg)

### Core idea

An acute upper respiratory infection is a short-term infection of the upper airway:

* nose
* sinuses
* pharynx (throat)
* larynx (voice box)

Most of the time, this basically means a viral cold.

Acute = comes on quickly and lasts a short time.

URI = upper respiratory infection.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

A virus infects the respiratory mucosa (moist lining of the airway).

Your immune response causes inflammation, which leads to:

* runny nose: more mucus production
* nasal congestion: swollen nasal mucosa
* sore throat: inflamed pharynx
* cough: mucus/postnasal drip irritating the airway
* sneezing: reflex to clear irritated nasal passages
* mild fever/fatigue: cytokines (immune signaling proteins)

So the symptoms are not just the virus sitting there.

A lot of it is your immune system reacting to the virus.

---

### Common causes

Usually viral:

* rhinovirus
* coronavirus
* adenovirus
* respiratory syncytial virus
* influenza virus

Bacterial causes are less common.

---

## One-line intuition

The upper airway lining gets infected and inflamed, so it swells, leaks mucus, and triggers cough/sneeze reflexes.

---

## HY pearl

Most acute URIs are viral, so antibiotics usually do not help.

Step 1 clue: rhinovirus is the most common cause of the common cold, and it is a picornavirus (small RNA virus).

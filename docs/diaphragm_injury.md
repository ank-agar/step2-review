# Diaphragm injury

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/70/1113_The_Diaphragm.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/22/Thorax-diaphragm.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d7/PMC2739847_1749-7922-4-32-2.png)

Diaphragm injury usually means a tear/rupture of the diaphragm (the dome-shaped muscle separating the chest from the abdomen that helps you breathe).

Most often from:

* blunt trauma, like a motor vehicle collision
* penetrating trauma, like a stab or gunshot wound

---

### Core idea

The diaphragm is the wall between:

* thorax (chest cavity)
* abdomen (belly cavity)

If it tears, abdominal organs can move upward into the chest.

That is called a diaphragmatic hernia (abdominal contents herniate through a diaphragm defect into the thorax).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Shortness of breath:** bowel/stomach/liver can enter the chest and compress the lung
* **Bowel sounds in the chest:** intestines may physically be sitting in the thoracic cavity
* **Abdominal pain:** trauma often injures nearby organs too
* **Mediastinal shift:** large herniation can push the heart/trachea away
* **Chest X-ray findings:** bowel loops or stomach bubble may appear above the diaphragm

---

### Important pattern

Diaphragm rupture is more common on the **left side**.

Why?

The liver protects the right hemidiaphragm (right half of the diaphragm), so the left side is more exposed to rupture.

---

## One-line intuition

Diaphragm injury = a hole in the muscle wall between abdomen and chest, so belly organs can move into the chest and compress the lung.

---

## Pearl 🔥

After blunt trauma, suspect diaphragmatic rupture if chest imaging shows **bowel loops in the thorax** or an elevated/absent hemidiaphragm (one half of the diaphragm).

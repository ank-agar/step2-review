### Core idea

Neonatal jaundice is yellow skin/eyes in a newborn from too much bilirubin.

Bilirubin = yellow breakdown product of heme (iron-containing part of hemoglobin in red blood cells).

Most newborn jaundice is unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia.

Unconjugated = not yet processed by the liver, so it is not water-soluble.

---

### Why newborns get it

Newborns make more bilirubin and clear it worse:

* **More RBC (red blood cell) breakdown:** fetal RBCs have a shorter lifespan
* **Immature liver:** low UDP-glucuronyltransferase activity
* **More enterohepatic circulation:** bilirubin gets reabsorbed from the gut back into blood

UDP-glucuronyltransferase = liver enzyme that conjugates bilirubin, making it water-soluble so it can leave in bile/stool.

So the newborn is basically:

> making lots of bilirubin, but the liver is still bad at packaging it for excretion.

---

### Why phototherapy works

Phototherapy = blue light treatment.

Blue light converts unconjugated bilirubin into water-soluble photoisomers.

Photoisomers = same molecule rearranged into a form that can dissolve better.

That means bilirubin can be excreted without needing as much liver conjugation.

---

### Why severe cases are dangerous

Unconjugated bilirubin is lipid-soluble, so it can cross into the brain if levels get very high.

That can cause kernicterus.

Kernicterus = bilirubin deposition in the brain, especially basal ganglia, causing permanent neurologic damage.

---

### Physiologic vs pathologic

Physiologic jaundice:

* starts after 24 hours of life
* peaks around days 3-5
* due to normal newborn liver immaturity

Pathologic jaundice:

* starts in the first 24 hours
* very high or rapidly rising bilirubin
* may be due to hemolysis, infection, biliary disease, or enzyme defects

Hemolysis = RBC destruction.

---

## One-line intuition

Newborns turn yellow because they break down lots of RBCs, but their immature liver cannot conjugate bilirubin fast enough yet.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Jaundice in the **first 24 hours** is pathologic until proven otherwise; think hemolysis, especially ABO or Rh incompatibility.

ABO/Rh incompatibility = maternal antibodies attack newborn RBCs because of blood type mismatch.

# Pulmonary arteriovenous fistula

A pulmonary arteriovenous fistula is an abnormal direct connection between:

* pulmonary artery = brings deoxygenated blood from right heart to lung
* pulmonary vein = brings oxygenated blood from lung to left heart

Normally:

> pulmonary artery → lung capillaries → pulmonary vein

In a fistula:

> pulmonary artery → pulmonary vein

So the blood skips the lung capillaries.

---

### Core idea

It is a right-to-left shunt.

Right-to-left shunt means deoxygenated blood from the right side of circulation bypasses oxygenation and enters the left side/systemic circulation.

So oxygen-poor blood gets sent to the body.

This causes:

* hypoxemia = low oxygen in blood
* cyanosis = bluish discoloration from low oxygen
* dyspnea = shortness of breath
* clubbing sometimes = bulbous enlargement of fingertips from chronic low oxygen

---

### Why the findings happen

The lung capillary bed normally does 2 big jobs:

* oxygenates blood
* filters small clots/bacteria

If blood bypasses the capillaries:

* less oxygen pickup → hypoxemia/cyanosis
* unfiltered clots can enter systemic arteries → stroke
* unfiltered bacteria can enter systemic arteries → brain abscess

That is the big danger: the lung filter is bypassed.

---

### Important association

Pulmonary arteriovenous fistulas/malformations are classically associated with hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia (HHT).

Hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia (HHT) = autosomal dominant vascular disorder causing abnormal blood vessel connections and telangiectasias.

Telangiectasias = small dilated blood vessels near the skin or mucosal surface.

Classic clues:

* recurrent epistaxis = nosebleeds
* mucocutaneous telangiectasias = small dilated vessels on skin/mucosa
* AVMs (arteriovenous malformations) in lung, brain, liver, or GI tract

---

## One-line intuition

A pulmonary arteriovenous fistula is a shortcut from pulmonary artery to pulmonary vein, so blood skips oxygenation and skips filtration.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Pulmonary AVM (arteriovenous malformation) can cause paradoxical emboli.

Paradoxical embolus = a clot from the venous side crosses into arterial circulation and can cause stroke.

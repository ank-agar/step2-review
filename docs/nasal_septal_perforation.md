# Nasal septal perforation

Nasal septal perforation = a hole through the nasal septum.

The nasal septum is the wall that separates the left and right nasal cavities.

It is made of:

* anterior cartilage
* posterior bone
* mucosa on both sides

So a perforation means there is an abnormal opening connecting the 2 nasal passages.

---

### Core idea

Think:

> the middle wall of the nose gets damaged enough that tissue dies, leaving a hole.

The key mechanism is usually loss of blood supply to the septal cartilage/mucosa.

Cartilage depends on the overlying mucosa/perichondrium for nutrients, so if both sides are injured, the cartilage can necrose and disappear.

---

### Common causes

High-yield causes:

* septoplasty or nasal surgery
* trauma or nose picking
* cocaine use
* chronic nasal steroid use
* vasculitis, especially granulomatosis with polyangiitis
* infection

Cocaine is classic because it causes intense vasoconstriction, meaning blood vessels clamp down and tissue becomes ischemic.

Ischemia = low blood flow causing tissue injury.

---

### Symptoms

Small perforations can be asymptomatic.

When symptomatic, think:

* crusting
* recurrent epistaxis (nosebleeds)
* nasal whistling
* dryness
* nasal obstruction

Why whistling happens:

air flows through the abnormal hole, creating turbulent airflow, like air moving through a small opening.

Why crusting/bleeding happens:

turbulent dry airflow irritates the mucosa, so it dries out, cracks, crusts, and bleeds.

---

### Big complication

Large anterior perforations can weaken nasal support and cause saddle nose deformity.

Saddle nose deformity = collapse/depression of the nasal bridge.

This happens because the septal cartilage helps support the nose structurally.

---

## One-line intuition

Nasal septal perforation is a hole in the wall between the nostrils, usually from tissue ischemia/necrosis causing crusting, nosebleeds, and whistling airflow.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Cocaine can cause nasal septal perforation because it causes strong local vasoconstriction, leading to ischemic necrosis of the nasal septum.

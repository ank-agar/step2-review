### Core idea

Papillary thyroid cancer, also called papillary thyroid carcinoma, is the most common thyroid cancer.

It arises from thyroid follicular cells (thyroid hormone-producing epithelial cells).

“Papillary” means the tumor often forms finger-like projections called papillae.

---

### Classic findings

On microscopy, think:

* papillae with fibrovascular cores (finger-like projections with blood vessel/connective tissue centers)
* Orphan Annie eye nuclei (clear, empty-looking nuclei)
* nuclear grooves (folds/lines in the nucleus)
* psammoma bodies (round laminated calcifications)

Reasoning:

The tumor grows in papillary projections, and the cells have abnormal nuclear chromatin (DNA-protein material in the nucleus), making the nuclei look pale/cleared.

---

### Spread pattern

Papillary thyroid cancer usually spreads through lymphatics (lymph channels), especially to cervical lymph nodes (neck lymph nodes).

This is different from follicular thyroid cancer, which classically spreads hematogenously (through blood).

---

### Risk factors / associations

Important associations:

* prior neck radiation
* RET/PTC rearrangement (RET proto-oncogene fused/activated in papillary thyroid carcinoma)
* BRAF mutation, especially BRAF V600E (a mutation activating the MAPK pathway, a cell-growth signaling pathway)

---

## One-line intuition

Papillary thyroid cancer is the “lymph-spreading thyroid cancer” with pale Orphan Annie nuclei and psammoma bodies.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Papillary thyroid carcinoma spreads by lymphatics to cervical lymph nodes, but it still usually has an excellent prognosis.

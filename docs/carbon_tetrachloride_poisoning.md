# Carbon tetrachloride poisoning

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK598011/bin/ch3f9.jpg)

![Image](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug/compound/cid/5943/PNG)

### Core idea

Carbon tetrachloride poisoning is toxicity from **CCl4 (carbon tetrachloride)**, an old solvent/fire-extinguisher chemical that mainly damages:

* liver
* kidneys
* CNS (central nervous system)

Big Step 1 idea:

> CCl4 gets metabolized in the liver into free radicals, which cause lipid peroxidation and hepatocyte necrosis.

---

### Mechanism

CCl4 by itself is bad, but the real damage happens after liver metabolism:

* CCl4 enters hepatocytes
* CYP2E1 (cytochrome P450 2E1 enzyme) converts it into CCl3 radical (trichloromethyl free radical)
* CCl3 radical reacts with oxygen to form CCl3OO radical (trichloromethyl peroxy radical)
* these radicals attack cell membranes
* membrane damage causes hepatocyte injury/death

Why membrane damage?

Cell membranes are full of lipids, and free radicals love attacking lipids. This is called **lipid peroxidation** (free radical damage to membrane fats).

---

### What happens clinically

Early CNS (central nervous system) symptoms:

* headache
* dizziness
* confusion
* nausea/vomiting
* possible coma at high exposure

Later organ injury:

* hepatitis-like liver injury
* RUQ (right upper quadrant) pain
* jaundice
* elevated AST/ALT (aspartate aminotransferase/alanine aminotransferase liver enzymes)
* kidney injury

Important timing:

The patient may look only mildly sick at first, then develop worse liver/kidney injury later.

---

### Why alcohol makes it worse

Alcohol induces CYP2E1 (cytochrome P450 2E1 enzyme).

So:

* more CYP2E1
* more CCl4 converted into toxic radicals
* more liver injury

This is the same enzyme family concept as acetaminophen toxicity.

---

## One-line intuition

Carbon tetrachloride is like a chemical that the liver accidentally turns into a membrane-destroying free radical bomb.

---

## Pearl 🔥

CCl4 causes **centrilobular necrosis** because CYP450 (cytochrome P450 enzyme) activity is highest around the central vein in zone 3 of the hepatic acinus.

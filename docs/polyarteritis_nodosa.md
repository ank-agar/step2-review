### Core idea

Polyarteritis nodosa (PAN) is a **medium-vessel necrotizing vasculitis**.

Break that down:

* medium-vessel = arteries big enough to supply organs, like renal arteries and mesenteric arteries
* necrotizing = the vessel wall is being damaged/destroyed
* vasculitis = inflammation of blood vessels

So PAN is:

> inflamed medium arteries that get damaged, narrow, thrombose, and form aneurysms.

---

### Why the findings happen

The inflammation is **transmural** (through the full wall of the artery).

That causes 2 big problems:

* **narrowing/occlusion** -> ischemia (low blood flow) -> abdominal pain, neuropathy, skin ulcers, renal problems
* **wall weakening** -> aneurysms (outpouchings of arteries) -> “string of beads” on angiography

Kidney involvement commonly causes **hypertension** because renal artery ischemia activates RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system), which raises blood pressure.

---

### Classic associations

PAN is classically associated with:

* hepatitis B virus (HBV)
* fever, weight loss, malaise
* abdominal pain after eating from mesenteric ischemia
* mononeuritis multiplex
* renal artery disease causing hypertension
* livedo reticularis or painful skin nodules

Mononeuritis multiplex means multiple individual peripheral nerves are damaged in an asymmetric pattern.

---

### Key negative finding

PAN usually **spares the lungs**.

Also, classic PAN is usually **ANCA-negative**.

ANCA (anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody) is more associated with small-vessel vasculitides like microscopic polyangiitis and granulomatosis with polyangiitis.

---

## One-line intuition

PAN is like inflammation attacking medium arteries, making them both **too narrow to feed organs** and **too weak, causing aneurysms**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

PAN causes renal artery involvement with hypertension, but usually **does not cause glomerulonephritis** because glomeruli are capillaries/small vessels, and PAN mainly hits medium arteries.

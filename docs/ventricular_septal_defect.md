# Ventricular septal defect

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/heart-defects/media/images/VSD.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18095.jpg)

### Core idea

Ventricular septal defect (VSD) = a hole in the interventricular septum (wall between the right and left ventricles).

Most are congenital (present at birth).

---

### What happens to blood flow?

Normally:

* left ventricle (LV) pumps high-pressure blood to the body
* right ventricle (RV) pumps lower-pressure blood to the lungs

In VSD:

* LV pressure is higher than RV pressure
* so blood moves left to right
* oxygenated blood gets pushed back into the lungs

So the lungs get extra blood flow.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Small VSD:

* less blood crosses the hole
* often asymptomatic
* can close spontaneously
* loud harsh holosystolic murmur

Large VSD:

* lots of left-to-right shunting
* pulmonary overcirculation
* heart has to pump extra volume
* can cause heart failure signs: poor feeding, sweating, tachypnea (fast breathing), poor weight gain

Reasoning:

The bigger the hole, the less resistance there is to blood flow, so more blood can shunt from LV to RV.

---

### Murmur

Classic murmur:

* harsh holosystolic murmur
* best heard at the left lower sternal border

Holosystolic means it lasts through all of systole (ventricular contraction).

Why?

During systole, LV pressure stays higher than RV pressure, so blood keeps flowing through the hole the whole time.

---

## One-line intuition

VSD is a hole between the ventricles, so high-pressure LV blood leaks into the RV and overloads the lungs.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Small VSDs are often louder than large VSDs because the small hole creates more turbulence; large VSDs may be quieter but more dangerous due to pulmonary hypertension (high blood pressure in lung vessels) and possible Eisenmenger syndrome (late right-to-left shunt causing cyanosis).

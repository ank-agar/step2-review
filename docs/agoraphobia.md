### Core idea

Agoraphobia is anxiety about being in places where:

* escape feels hard
* help may not be available
* panic symptoms would feel embarrassing or dangerous

It is not just "fear of open spaces."

More accurately, it is fear of being trapped, helpless, or unable to escape if panic happens.

---

### Classic feared situations

Agoraphobia involves fear/anxiety in at least 2 of these:

* public transportation
* open spaces
* enclosed places
* standing in line or being in crowds
* being outside the home alone

---

### Why avoidance makes it worse

The patient feels anxious in a place, then leaves or avoids it.

That avoidance gives short-term relief, so the brain learns:

> "Leaving kept me safe."

But long-term, this reinforces the fear.

So the cycle is:

* feared situation
* panic symptoms
* catastrophic thought
* escape/avoidance
* temporary relief
* stronger future fear

---

### Treatment intuition

CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy) helps the patient gradually face feared situations and learn:

> "Anxiety is uncomfortable, but not dangerous."

SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors) can also help reduce baseline anxiety and panic symptoms.

---

## One-line intuition

Agoraphobia is fear of being stuck somewhere unsafe-feeling if panic hits, so the patient avoids places that feel hard to escape.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1: Agoraphobia is commonly associated with panic disorder, but DSM-5 (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition) allows agoraphobia to be diagnosed separately.

# Waldenstrom macroglobulinemia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/IgM_scheme.svg/500px-IgM_scheme.svg.png)

![Image](https://imagebank.hematology.org/getimagebyid/1178?size=3)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/1500.jpg)

Waldenstrom macroglobulinemia is a slow-growing B-cell cancer where abnormal lymphoplasmacytic cells make too much IgM (immunoglobulin M).

Break the name apart:

* macro = big
* globulin = antibody/protein in blood
* -emia = in the blood

So the core idea is:

> big antibody proteins building up in the blood.

The cancer cell is kind of between:

* B lymphocyte (antibody-making immune cell)
* plasma cell (fully mature antibody-secreting cell)

That is why it is also called lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma.

---

### Why symptoms happen

IgM (immunoglobulin M) is huge because it is a pentamer (5 antibody units stuck together).

So when there is too much IgM, blood becomes thick and sluggish.

This causes hyperviscosity syndrome (abnormally thick blood):

* blurry vision
* headache/confusion
* dizziness
* mucosal bleeding, like nosebleeds or gum bleeding

Why bleeding?

The IgM (immunoglobulin M) makes blood thick and can interfere with platelets/coagulation factors (clotting proteins), so clotting becomes less effective.

---

### Bone marrow effect

The malignant lymphoplasmacytic cells build up in bone marrow.

That crowds out normal blood production, causing:

* anemia (low red blood cells) → fatigue
* thrombocytopenia (low platelets) → bleeding/bruising
* sometimes leukopenia (low white blood cells) → infections

---

### Classic lab/path clue

Peripheral smear can show rouleaux formation (red blood cells stacked like coins).

Why?

Too much serum protein, especially IgM (immunoglobulin M), reduces the normal repulsion between red blood cells, so they stick together in stacks.

---

## One-line intuition

Waldenstrom macroglobulinemia = lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma making tons of big IgM (immunoglobulin M), which thickens blood and crowds bone marrow.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Waldenstrom macroglobulinemia is classically associated with IgM (immunoglobulin M), while multiple myeloma is usually IgG (immunoglobulin G) or IgA (immunoglobulin A).

Important Step 1 contrast: Waldenstrom usually does **not** cause punched-out lytic bone lesions, unlike multiple myeloma.

# Takayasu arteritis

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK499911/bin/aorticArch.jpg)

![Image](https://www.hopkinsvasculitis.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/05/arch-300x196.gif)

![Image](https://www.hopkinsvasculitis.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/05/pulmonary.jpg)

Takayasu arteritis is a **large-vessel vasculitis**.

Break that down:

* large-vessel = big arteries
* vasculitis = inflammation of blood vessels

It classically affects the:

* aorta
* aortic arch branches
* subclavian arteries
* carotid arteries
* sometimes renal arteries or pulmonary arteries

So the key idea is:

> inflammation thickens and scars big arteries, causing narrowing/occlusion.

---

### Classic patient

Usually:

* young woman
* often Asian descent

That is why it is sometimes called:

* **pulseless disease**

because narrowed subclavian arteries can make arm pulses weak or absent.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Inflamed artery wall becomes thick and fibrotic.

That causes:

* **decreased pulses:** less blood reaches the arms
* **arm claudication:** arm pain with use because muscle is not getting enough blood
* **different blood pressures between arms:** one subclavian artery may be more narrowed than the other
* **bruits:** turbulent blood flow through narrowed arteries
* **neurologic symptoms:** carotid/vertebral artery narrowing can reduce blood flow to brain
* **hypertension:** renal artery stenosis activates RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system)
* **aortic regurgitation:** inflammation can dilate the aortic root, so the aortic valve cannot close well

---

### Pathology

Takayasu arteritis is a:

* granulomatous vasculitis
* involving large arteries

Granulomatous means immune cells form organized inflammatory clusters.

This is similar histologically to giant cell arteritis, but the patient populations differ:

* Takayasu = younger patients
* giant cell arteritis = older patients

---

## One-line intuition

Takayasu arteritis is **big-vessel inflammation in a young woman that narrows aortic branches, causing weak pulses and unequal arm blood pressures.**

---

### Core idea

Intraparenchymal hemorrhage means bleeding **inside the brain parenchyma** (the functional brain tissue itself).

Break it down:

* intra- = within
* parenchymal = functional tissue of an organ
* hemorrhage = bleeding

So:

> blood leaking directly into brain tissue.

---

### Why it causes symptoms

Blood in the brain tissue is bad because it:

* compresses nearby neurons
* increases intracranial pressure (pressure inside the skull)
* irritates brain tissue and can cause seizures
* cuts off normal oxygen delivery to the affected area

So symptoms depend on **where the bleed is**:

* motor cortex involvement -> weakness
* sensory cortex involvement -> numbness
* cerebellum involvement -> ataxia (poor coordination)
* large bleed -> headache, vomiting, coma

---

### Common causes

Big Step 1 causes:

* hypertension (high blood pressure)
* cerebral amyloid angiopathy (amyloid deposition in brain vessel walls)
* trauma
* arteriovenous malformation, or AVM (abnormal artery-vein connection)
* anticoagulation (blood-thinning medication effect)

Hypertension is especially classic because chronic high pressure damages small penetrating arteries until they rupture.

---

### Imaging idea

On noncontrast CT (computed tomography), acute blood usually looks **bright/white**.

That is why CT is fast and useful when you suspect an acute brain bleed.

---

## One-line intuition

Intraparenchymal hemorrhage is a blood vessel bursting **inside the brain tissue**, creating a growing blood clot that compresses and injures nearby neurons.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Hypertensive intraparenchymal hemorrhage classically affects small deep vessels, especially in the **basal ganglia**, thalamus, pons, and cerebellum.

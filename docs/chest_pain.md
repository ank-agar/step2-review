## Chest pain

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/18/Coronary_arteries.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f4/A_man_having_a_Heart_Attack.png)

### Core idea

Chest pain means discomfort anywhere in the chest.

It can come from:

* heart
* lungs
* esophagus
* muscles/ribs
* anxiety/panic

But on exams and in real life, always first think:

> Is this cardiac ischemia?

Ischemia means low blood flow/oxygen to tissue.

---

### Why heart pain happens

The heart muscle needs oxygen from the coronary arteries.

If a coronary artery is narrowed or blocked:

* less oxygen reaches myocardium (heart muscle)
* myocardium switches toward anaerobic metabolism (low-oxygen energy production)
* metabolites build up
* pain fibers get activated

That causes the classic pressure-like chest pain of angina or myocardial infarction.

Angina = chest pain from temporary myocardial ischemia.

Myocardial infarction (MI) = heart attack; heart muscle injury/death from prolonged ischemia.

---

### Classic cardiac chest pain

Think:

* pressure/tightness/heaviness
* substernal pain (behind the sternum/breastbone)
* worse with exertion
* better with rest or nitroglycerin
* may radiate to left arm, jaw, neck, shoulder, or back

Radiation happens because visceral sensory fibers from the heart enter spinal cord levels that overlap with somatic regions like the arm/jaw, so the brain mislocalizes the pain.

---

### Other big causes

* Pulmonary embolism (PE; clot blocking blood flow in lung arteries): sudden pleuritic chest pain + shortness of breath
* Pericarditis (inflammation of the pericardium, the sac around the heart): sharp pain worse lying down, better leaning forward
* GERD (gastroesophageal reflux disease; acid reflux): burning pain after meals/lying down
* Costochondritis (inflammation of rib-sternum cartilage): reproducible tenderness when pressing on chest wall
* Panic attack: chest tightness with fear, hyperventilation, palpitations

Pleuritic = worse with breathing.

Palpitations = feeling the heartbeat strongly/irregularly.

---

### Red flag pattern

Chest pain is more concerning if it has:

* pressure-like quality
* exertional trigger
* shortness of breath
* sweating
* nausea
* radiation to arm/jaw
* syncope (fainting)
* hypotension (low blood pressure)

These push you toward ECG (electrocardiogram; heart electrical tracing) and troponins (blood markers of heart muscle injury).

---

## One-line intuition

Chest pain is a symptom, not a diagnosis; first rule out heart/lung emergencies, then think GI (gastrointestinal), muscle/rib, and anxiety causes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, myocardial ischemia classically causes chest pain because coronary artery obstruction decreases oxygen delivery to myocardium, and the pain can radiate to the left arm/jaw due to shared spinal sensory pathways.

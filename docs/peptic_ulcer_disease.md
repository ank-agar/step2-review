# Peptic ulcer disease

Peptic ulcer disease means an ulcer in a part of the GI tract (gastrointestinal tract) exposed to acid/pepsin.

Most commonly:

* stomach = gastric ulcer
* duodenum (first part of small intestine) = duodenal ulcer

An ulcer is basically a crater-like break in the mucosa (inner lining).

---

### Core idea

Normally, the stomach/duodenum are protected by:

* mucus
* bicarbonate (base that neutralizes acid)
* prostaglandins (protective signaling molecules that increase mucus/bicarbonate and blood flow)

Peptic ulcer disease happens when:

> acid/pepsin injury beats mucosal protection.

So think:

**too much injury or too little protection → ulcer.**

---

### Main causes

Most important causes:

* H. pylori (Helicobacter pylori, a spiral gram-negative bacterium)
* NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, like ibuprofen/naproxen/aspirin)

Why H. pylori causes ulcers:

* it lives in the stomach mucus layer
* it makes urease (enzyme that breaks urea into ammonia)
* ammonia helps buffer acid around the bacteria
* inflammation damages the mucosa

So the lining becomes weaker, and acid can burn deeper into it.

Why NSAIDs cause ulcers:

* NSAIDs inhibit COX (cyclooxygenase, enzyme that makes prostaglandins)
* less prostaglandin = less mucus, less bicarbonate, less blood flow
* weaker protection = acid injury

---

### Gastric vs duodenal ulcer intuition

Gastric ulcer:

* pain often worsens with meals
* food enters stomach → acid secretion/contact → pain
* can be associated with malignancy, so biopsy is important

Duodenal ulcer:

* pain often improves with meals
* food temporarily buffers acid
* pain can return a few hours later or at night

---

### Complications

Big things to watch for:

* bleeding → melena (black tarry stool) or hematemesis (vomiting blood)
* perforation → sudden severe abdominal pain + free air under diaphragm
* gastric outlet obstruction → vomiting, early satiety

---

## One-line intuition

Peptic ulcer disease is when acid and pepsin digest through the stomach/duodenal lining because the normal protective barrier is weakened.

---

## Pearl 🔥

NSAIDs cause ulcers by decreasing prostaglandins, so they decrease the stomach’s protective mucus and bicarbonate barrier.

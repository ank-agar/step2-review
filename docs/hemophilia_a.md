# Hemophilia A

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Gray299.png/250px-Gray299.png)

Hemophilia A is a bleeding disorder caused by deficiency of factor VIII (a clotting factor in the intrinsic coagulation pathway).

So the problem is:

* not enough factor VIII
* weak intrinsic pathway
* poor fibrin clot formation
* bleeding continues longer than normal

Factor VIII normally helps factor IXa (activated factor IX) activate factor X.

Think:

> VIII helps IX activate X.

If factor VIII is missing, the clotting cascade gets stuck before making enough thrombin (enzyme that converts fibrinogen into fibrin) and fibrin (the protein mesh that stabilizes a clot).

---

### Why bleeding happens

Platelets can still make the initial plug.

But the coagulation cascade (clotting factor chain reaction) cannot reinforce that plug well with fibrin.

That is why patients get:

* deep tissue bleeding
* hemarthrosis (bleeding into a joint)
* muscle hematomas (collections of blood in muscle)
* prolonged bleeding after trauma, surgery, or dental work

This is different from platelet problems, which usually cause mucosal bleeding like petechiae (tiny pinpoint skin bleeds), epistaxis (nosebleeds), or gum bleeding.

---

### Labs

Classic labs:

* increased PTT (partial thromboplastin time)
* normal PT (prothrombin time)
* normal platelet count
* normal bleeding time

Why?

* PTT tests the intrinsic pathway, where factor VIII lives
* PT tests the extrinsic pathway, mainly factor VII
* platelets are normal because this is a clotting factor problem, not a platelet problem

---

### Inheritance

Hemophilia A is usually X-linked recessive (mutation is on the X chromosome and usually shows disease in males).

So classically:

* males are affected
* females are carriers
* carrier mother can pass it to sons

---

## One-line intuition

Hemophilia A = missing factor VIII, so the intrinsic pathway cannot build a strong fibrin clot.

---

## HY pearl

Hemophilia A has increased PTT with normal PT, and the classic bleeding pattern is hemarthrosis (bleeding into joints).

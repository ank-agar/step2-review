## Acanthamoeba

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549863/bin/acanthamoeba__keratitis.jpg)

Acanthamoeba is a free-living amoeba, meaning it normally lives in the environment rather than needing a human host.

It is found in:

* soil
* freshwater
* tap water
* swimming pools/hot tubs
* contact lens cases/solutions

Main Step 1 association:

* **Acanthamoeba keratitis** = corneal infection, classically in contact lens users

Keratitis means inflammation/infection of the cornea, which is the clear front surface of the eye.

---

### Forms

Acanthamoeba has 2 major forms:

* **trophozoite** = active, feeding, motile form
* **cyst** = dormant, tough survival form

The cyst is the reason this organism is hard to kill.

It can survive harsh conditions, so contaminated contact lenses or lens cases can keep exposing the cornea.

---

### Why contact lenses matter

Contact lenses can cause tiny corneal microtrauma.

That creates a doorway.

Then if the lens is exposed to contaminated water, Acanthamoeba can attach to the damaged corneal surface and invade.

Classic risk:

* wearing contacts while swimming/showering
* rinsing contacts with tap water
* poor lens case hygiene

---

### Classic presentation

Acanthamoeba keratitis causes:

* severe eye pain
* redness
* photophobia (light sensitivity)
* blurry vision
* tearing
* ring-shaped corneal infiltrate later

Key phrase:

> pain out of proportion to exam

Meaning the patient may have intense pain even before the eye looks dramatically abnormal.

---

### Other disease association

In immunocompromised patients, Acanthamoeba can also cause:

* granulomatous amebic encephalitis, or GAE (granulomatous amoeba-caused brain inflammation)
* skin lesions
* disseminated infection

But for Step 1, the highest-yield association is still:

> contact lens wearer + keratitis = think Acanthamoeba

---

## One-line intuition

Acanthamoeba is an environmental amoeba with a tough cyst form that can contaminate contact lenses and infect the cornea.

---

## Pearl

Acanthamoeba has **no flagellated form**.

That helps distinguish it from **Naegleria fowleri**, which can have a flagellated form and classically causes rapidly fatal primary amebic meningoencephalitis (PAM), a brain infection after warm freshwater enters the nose.

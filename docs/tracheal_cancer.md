# Tracheal cancer

### Core idea

Tracheal cancer = a malignant tumor in the trachea (windpipe).

The trachea is the airway tube between:

* larynx (voice box)
* bronchi (large airways entering the lungs)

So the main problem is simple:

> tumor grows into the airway lumen, so air has less space to move.

Lumen = hollow inside space of a tube.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Dyspnea (shortness of breath):** the airway is physically narrowed
* **Stridor (high-pitched inspiratory breathing sound):** upper/central airway obstruction causes turbulent airflow
* **Wheezing:** can mimic asthma because airflow is narrowed
* **Cough:** tumor irritates the airway lining
* **Hemoptysis (coughing blood):** tumor is fragile and vascular, so it can bleed

Important intuition:

Asthma narrows small airways by bronchoconstriction (airway smooth muscle tightening).

Tracheal cancer narrows a big central airway by mass effect.

---

### Common types

The two classic primary tracheal cancers are:

* **SCC (squamous cell carcinoma):** associated with smoking
* **ACC (adenoid cystic carcinoma):** arises from salivary gland-type tissue in the airway wall

Primary = starts in the trachea.

Metastatic = spread from another cancer site.

---

### Why SCC is linked to smoking

Smoking chronically irritates respiratory epithelium (airway lining).

Chronic irritation can cause:

* squamous metaplasia = normal airway lining changes into tougher squamous cells
* dysplasia = abnormal premalignant growth
* carcinoma = malignant cancer

So the pathway is:

> smoke injury -> metaplasia -> dysplasia -> SCC.

---

## One-line intuition

Tracheal cancer is basically a tumor growing inside the windpipe, so it acts like a fixed airway blockage that can look like asthma but does not behave like typical asthma.

---

## Pearl 🔥

If a patient has "asthma" symptoms plus stridor, hemoptysis, or poor response to bronchodilators, think central airway obstruction, including a tracheal tumor.

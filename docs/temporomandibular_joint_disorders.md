## Temporomandibular joint disorders

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538486/bin/TMJ.jpg)

![Image](https://anatomytool.org/sites/default/files/16795-111.jpg)

![Image](https://assets.mayoclinic.org/content/dam/media/global/images/2023/06/26/mccms-Getty-1082003662-415x275.jpg)

Temporomandibular joint disorders = problems with the **TMJ (temporomandibular joint)**, the joint where the mandible (jaw bone) meets the temporal bone of the skull.

This joint lets you:

* open/close your mouth
* chew
* talk
* move the jaw side-to-side

The key structures are:

* mandibular condyle = rounded jaw bone head
* articular disc = soft cushion between bones
* muscles of mastication = chewing muscles

---

### Core idea

TMJ disorders happen when the jaw joint, disc, or chewing muscles become irritated or mechanically abnormal.

So patients can get:

* jaw pain
* clicking/popping
* limited mouth opening
* pain with chewing
* headache/ear pain
* jaw locking

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Clicking/popping:** the articular disc may slip out of place, then snap back during jaw movement
* **Jaw pain:** irritated joint capsule, disc, or chewing muscles send pain signals
* **Limited opening/locking:** the disc or inflamed joint blocks smooth condyle movement
* **Ear pain:** the TMJ sits right in front of the ear, so pain can feel like it is coming from the ear
* **Headache:** overactive chewing muscles can refer pain to the temple/head

---

### Common causes/triggers

* bruxism (teeth grinding)
* jaw clenching
* stress-related muscle tension
* trauma
* arthritis
* abnormal bite mechanics

---

## One-line intuition

The TMJ is a tiny sliding hinge with a cushion-disc; if the disc or chewing muscles get irritated, the jaw hurts, clicks, or locks.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: TMJ pain can mimic ear pain because the joint is innervated by branches of CN V3 (mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve), especially the auriculotemporal nerve.

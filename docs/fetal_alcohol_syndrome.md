## Fetal alcohol syndrome

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/21723.jpg)

![Image](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/vitalsigns/fasd/infographic_html_files/graphicA_1185px.jpg)

Fetal alcohol syndrome is the most severe form of FASD (fetal alcohol spectrum disorder).

It happens when alcohol exposure during pregnancy damages fetal development, especially:

* brain
* face
* growth
* heart sometimes

Classic findings:

* growth restriction
* microcephaly (small head)
* intellectual disability/developmental delay
* smooth philtrum (flat groove between nose and upper lip)
* thin upper lip
* short palpebral fissures (small eye openings)

---

### Why alcohol causes this

Alcohol crosses the placenta easily.

The fetus cannot metabolize alcohol well, so alcohol stays around and acts like a teratogen (substance that disrupts fetal development).

It especially disrupts:

* neural crest cells, which help form the face
* CNS (central nervous system) development, which explains learning problems, seizures, poor attention, and intellectual disability
* overall fetal growth, causing low birth weight and small size

So the intuition is:

> alcohol hits the developing brain and face while they are being built.

---

### One-line intuition

Fetal alcohol syndrome = prenatal alcohol exposure causing growth restriction + abnormal face + CNS (central nervous system) dysfunction.

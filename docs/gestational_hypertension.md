### Core idea

Gestational hypertension = new high blood pressure during pregnancy after 20 weeks gestation.

Key definition:

* blood pressure >= 140/90 mmHg
* starts after 20 weeks
* no proteinuria (protein in the urine)
* no end-organ damage (damage to organs like kidney, liver, brain, lungs, or platelets)

So it is basically:

> pregnancy-related high blood pressure without the systemic findings that would make it preeclampsia.

---

### Why after 20 weeks matters

Before 20 weeks, hypertension usually suggests chronic hypertension (high blood pressure that existed before pregnancy or early in pregnancy).

After 20 weeks, the placenta is now a major driver of maternal physiology.

So new hypertension after 20 weeks makes you think:

* gestational hypertension
* preeclampsia

The difference is whether there are extra organ findings.

---

### Gestational hypertension vs preeclampsia

Gestational hypertension:

* high BP (blood pressure)
* no proteinuria
* no severe features

Preeclampsia:

* high BP (blood pressure)
* plus proteinuria
* or end-organ damage

End-organ damage can include:

* thrombocytopenia (low platelets)
* elevated liver enzymes
* RUQ pain (right upper quadrant abdominal pain)
* kidney injury
* headache or vision changes
* pulmonary edema (fluid in the lungs)

---

### Why the findings happen

The placenta normally remodels maternal spiral arteries into wide, low-resistance vessels.

If that remodeling is abnormal:

* placenta gets relatively underperfused
* maternal blood vessels become more constricted/reactive
* systemic vascular resistance rises
* blood pressure rises

That is why pregnancy hypertension is so tied to the placenta.

The definitive cure for preeclampsia-spectrum disease is delivery of the placenta.

---

### Why we monitor closely

Gestational hypertension can stay mild, but it can progress to preeclampsia.

That is why clinicians check:

* BP (blood pressure)
* urine protein
* platelets
* creatinine (kidney function marker)
* liver enzymes
* fetal growth

because reduced placental blood flow can increase risk of:

* fetal growth restriction
* placental abruption
* preterm delivery

---

## One-line intuition

Gestational hypertension is pregnancy-triggered high blood pressure after 20 weeks, but without the proteinuria or organ damage that would make it preeclampsia.

---

## Pearl 🔥

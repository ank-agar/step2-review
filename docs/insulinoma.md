### Core idea

An insulinoma is a pancreatic beta-cell tumor that secretes too much insulin.

Beta cells are the insulin-producing cells in the pancreatic islets.

So the basic problem is:

* too much insulin
* especially when the body should be fasting
* causing hypoglycemia (low blood glucose)

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Insulin pushes glucose out of the blood and into tissues.

So if a tumor keeps releasing insulin, blood glucose drops.

Low glucose causes:

* confusion
* sweating
* tremor
* palpitations
* seizures or loss of consciousness in severe cases

The brain depends heavily on glucose, so neuro symptoms are a big clue.

---

### Classic diagnostic idea

Think:

* low glucose
* high insulin
* high C-peptide (connecting peptide, released when your body makes insulin)
* high proinsulin (insulin precursor)

Why C-peptide matters:

* endogenous insulin = made by the body, so C-peptide is high
* injected insulin = no C-peptide

So insulinoma looks like the body is making too much insulin on its own.

---

### Whipple triad

Classic triad:

* symptoms of hypoglycemia
* low measured plasma glucose during symptoms
* symptoms improve after giving glucose

This triad tells you the symptoms are really from low glucose.

---

## One-line intuition

Insulinoma is like a beta cell that ignores the fasting state and keeps dumping insulin, so glucose crashes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Insulinoma can be associated with MEN1 (multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1), which classically involves:

* parathyroid tumors
* pituitary tumors
* pancreatic endocrine tumors

### Core idea

A herniated disc is when the soft inner part of an intervertebral disc (the cushion between vertebrae) pushes out through the tougher outer ring.

Break the disc into 2 parts:

* nucleus pulposus = soft, gel-like center
* annulus fibrosus = tough outer fibrous ring

So:

> herniated disc = nucleus pulposus escapes through the annulus fibrosus.

---

### Why symptoms happen

The disc itself is not the main problem.

The problem is that the bulging/herniated material can compress a spinal nerve root (nerve branch leaving the spinal cord).

That causes:

* back pain
* radicular pain (pain shooting down a nerve distribution)
* numbness/tingling
* weakness

Classic example:

* lumbar disc herniation compresses nerve roots going to the leg
* this can cause sciatica (shooting pain down the buttock/posterior leg)

---

### Common direction

Most disc herniations go posterolateral.

Why?

The posterior longitudinal ligament (ligament running behind vertebral bodies) reinforces the midline posterior disc, so the disc tends to herniate off to the side.

That side path is where the nerve roots are.

---

## One-line intuition

The disc is like a jelly donut: the jelly center pushes through the outer ring and irritates/compresses a nearby nerve root.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In a posterolateral lumbar disc herniation, the nerve root affected is usually the one below the disc level.

Example:

* L4-L5 disc herniation usually compresses the L5 nerve root
* L5-S1 disc herniation usually compresses the S1 nerve root

# Subphrenic abscess

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Peritoneum.svg/1920px-Peritoneum.svg.png)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/e69c/12207833/ecff68b3559c/mi-05-05-00249-g00.jpg)

### Core idea

A **subphrenic abscess** is a pocket of pus under the diaphragm.

Break the word apart:

* **sub-** = under
* **phrenic** = diaphragm-related
* **abscess** = localized collection of pus

So:

> pus trapped under the diaphragm.

Usually it sits between the diaphragm and upper abdominal organs like the liver, spleen, or stomach.

---

### Why it happens

Most often after abdominal contamination, especially:

* recent abdominal surgery
* perforated ulcer
* ruptured appendix
* diverticulitis
* trauma

The key mechanism:

* bacteria leak into the peritoneal cavity (abdominal lining space)
* pus collects in a dependent/hidden space
* the diaphragm sits right above it, so symptoms can look abdominal **and** respiratory

---

### Classic symptoms

Think:

* fever
* abdominal pain
* shoulder pain
* cough or hiccups
* pleural effusion (fluid around the lung)
* elevated hemidiaphragm on chest x-ray

Shoulder pain happens because the diaphragm is irritated.

Reason:

* diaphragm sensation travels through the phrenic nerve
* phrenic nerve roots are C3-C5
* C3-C5 also refer pain to the shoulder

---

### Diagnosis

Best test is usually CT (computed tomography) scan.

CT can show:

* fluid collection
* gas bubbles
* air-fluid level
* nearby inflammation

---

### Treatment

Treatment is:

* antibiotics
* drainage

Drainage is important because antibiotics often cannot fully penetrate a walled-off abscess.

One-line intuition:

Antibiotics kill bacteria, but you still have to physically empty the pus pocket.

---

## One-line intuition

A subphrenic abscess is an infected pus pocket under the diaphragm, often after abdominal surgery or perforation, that can cause fever + belly pain + shoulder/respiratory symptoms.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Subphrenic abscess can cause **referred shoulder pain** because diaphragmatic irritation travels through the **phrenic nerve (C3-C5)**.

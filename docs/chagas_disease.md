Chagas disease = infection with **Trypanosoma cruzi (T. cruzi)**, a protozoan parasite spread by the **triatomine bug**, also called the **kissing bug**.

Key idea:

* bug bites you
* bug poops near the bite
* parasite in the feces gets rubbed into the bite wound, eye, or mouth
* parasite enters cells and causes long-term damage

Important: the bug bite itself is not the main problem.

The **feces** is the problem.

---

### Classic acute finding

**Romana sign** = painless swelling around one eye.

Why?

The infected bug feces gets rubbed into the conjunctiva (thin membrane over the eye), so the local tissue gets inflamed and swollen.

Acute infection can also cause:

* fever
* malaise (feeling generally sick)
* lymphadenopathy (swollen lymph nodes)
* hepatosplenomegaly (big liver and spleen)

But many people are asymptomatic.

---

### Chronic disease

Chagas is very high-yield because years later it can cause:

* dilated cardiomyopathy
* arrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms)
* heart failure
* megaesophagus
* megacolon

Break that down:

* **dilated cardiomyopathy** = enlarged, weakened heart that pumps poorly
* **megaesophagus** = enlarged esophagus, causing dysphagia (trouble swallowing)
* **megacolon** = enlarged colon, causing constipation

---

### Why heart + gastrointestinal tract?

T. cruzi likes to live inside cells as **amastigotes**.

An amastigote is the intracellular form of the parasite.

It especially damages:

* cardiac muscle
* enteric nerves, meaning nerves of the gastrointestinal tract

So the logic is:

* cardiac muscle damage -> dilated cardiomyopathy + arrhythmias
* gastrointestinal nerve damage -> poor peristalsis (wave-like movement of the gut) -> megaesophagus/megacolon

---

### Diagnosis

Acute infection:

* blood smear can show trypomastigotes, which are the flagellated blood form of the parasite

Chronic infection:

* serology, meaning antibody testing, is usually used

Reason:

In chronic disease, there are often too few parasites floating in the blood to easily see on a smear.

---

### Treatment

Main antiparasitic drugs:

* benznidazole
* nifurtimox

They work best early, before chronic heart/gastrointestinal damage is established.

---

## One-line intuition

Chagas is a kissing-bug feces-transmitted T. cruzi infection that can silently progress to dilated cardiomyopathy plus megaesophagus/megacolon years later.

---

## Pearl

High-yield Step 1 clue: **T. cruzi causes dilated cardiomyopathy with apical aneurysm**; apical aneurysm means an outpouching at the tip of the heart.

Think Latin America + kissing bug + megaesophagus/megacolon.

# Mannose-binding lectin deficiency

![Image](https://wikivet-dev.s3.amazonaws.com/d/d9/Complement_Mannose_Binding_Lectin_Pathway.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0b/Complement_pathway.png)

Mannose-binding lectin deficiency means low or dysfunctional **MBL (mannose-binding lectin)**.

MBL (mannose-binding lectin) is part of the **innate immune system** (fast, nonspecific immune defense).

Its job:

* bind **mannose** (a sugar found on many microbes)
* activate the **lectin pathway** of complement
* help coat microbes with **C3b** (complement protein 3b), which makes them easier for phagocytes to eat

---

### Core idea

MBL is like an early “microbe sugar detector.”

If MBL is deficient:

* microbes are not recognized as well
* complement activation through the lectin pathway is weaker
* less **opsonization** happens

Opsonization means coating a pathogen so immune cells can grab and eat it more easily.

---

### Why infections happen

Normally:

**MBL binds mannose on bacteria/fungi/viruses**
→ activates **MASP (MBL-associated serine protease)**
→ activates complement
→ makes **C3b**
→ phagocytes eat the pathogen

In MBL deficiency:

* less lectin pathway activation
* less C3b coating
* more susceptibility to recurrent infections, especially respiratory infections

This is usually most noticeable in kids or immunocompromised patients because they depend more on innate immunity.

---

### Important point

This is usually **less severe than C3 deficiency**.

Why?

Because the **classical pathway** (antibody-triggered complement pathway) and **alternative pathway** (pathogen-surface-triggered complement pathway) can still help compensate.

So think:

* C3 deficiency = major complement problem
* MBL deficiency = weaker early lectin-pathway recognition

---

## One-line intuition

MBL deficiency = poor binding to microbial mannose sugars, so the lectin complement pathway does not tag pathogens well for phagocytosis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

MBL (mannose-binding lectin) activates the **lectin pathway**, but unlike the **classical pathway**, it does **not** need antibodies.

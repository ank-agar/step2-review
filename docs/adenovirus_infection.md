# Adenovirus infection

![Image](https://www.nist.gov/sites/default/files/images/2023/03/13/Picture1_1.jpg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil///PHIL_Images/25107/25107_lores.jpg)

### Core idea

Adenovirus infection is a common viral infection caused by **adenovirus**, a **nonenveloped double-stranded DNA virus**.

It classically causes:

* pharyngitis (sore throat)
* conjunctivitis (pink eye)
* pneumonia
* gastroenteritis (stomach/intestine inflammation causing diarrhea/vomiting)
* hemorrhagic cystitis (bloody bladder inflammation)

---

### Why it causes those symptoms

Adenovirus likes to infect **epithelial cells** (surface-lining cells), especially in the:

* respiratory tract
* conjunctiva (thin membrane over the eye)
* GI tract (gastrointestinal tract)
* bladder

So depending on where it infects, you get:

* **throat/lung infection:** cough, fever, sore throat, pneumonia
* **eye infection:** red watery eyes = conjunctivitis
* **gut infection:** diarrhea/vomiting
* **bladder infection:** dysuria (painful urination) + hematuria (blood in urine)

---

### Classic buzzwords

Think:

* **fever + pharyngitis + conjunctivitis**
* outbreaks in close settings
* swimming pool-associated conjunctivitis
* military recruits

This is why adenovirus can show up as **pharyngoconjunctival fever**:

* pharyngo- = throat
* conjunctival = eye conjunctiva
* fever = systemic viral inflammation

---

### Key virology

Adenovirus is:

* nonenveloped
* double-stranded DNA
* icosahedral capsid (20-sided protein shell)

Why nonenveloped matters:

* no lipid envelope = tougher virus
* survives better on surfaces
* spreads easily by fomites (contaminated objects)

---

## One-line intuition

Adenovirus is a tough nonenveloped DNA virus that infects surface linings, so it causes “cold + pink eye + diarrhea/bladder symptoms” depending on the site.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 pearl: adenovirus has **linear double-stranded DNA** and is **nonenveloped**, which helps explain why it is environmentally hardy and can spread by contaminated surfaces.

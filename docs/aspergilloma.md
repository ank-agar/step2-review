## Aspergilloma

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil///PHIL_Images/29440/29440_lores.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/2a4e/9487495/a5b41cb3f2fb/err-19-118-349-f02.jpg)

Aspergilloma = a **fungus ball** made of Aspergillus hyphae (fungal filaments), mucus, fibrin, and cellular debris sitting inside a pre-existing cavity.

Most classic location:

* lung cavity left behind by old tuberculosis

Other possible cavities:

* sarcoidosis
* bronchiectasis (permanent airway dilation)
* old lung abscess
* emphysematous bullae (large air spaces from damaged lung)

---

### Core idea

Aspergillus usually does **not** invade normal lung tissue in aspergilloma.

Instead, it colonizes an empty space that was already there.

Think:

> old lung cave + inhaled Aspergillus spores = fungus ball.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Hemoptysis (coughing blood):** the fungus ball irritates nearby fragile blood vessels
* **Air crescent sign:** air surrounds the round fungus ball inside the cavity
* **Moves with position:** the ball is loose inside the cavity, so gravity can shift it
* **Often upper lobe:** old tuberculosis classically leaves upper-lobe cavities

---

### Step 1 contrast

Aspergillus can show up in different ways:

* **Aspergilloma:** fungus ball in old cavity
* **Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis:** allergy-like reaction in asthma/cystic fibrosis, with high IgE (immunoglobulin E)
* **Invasive aspergillosis:** tissue and blood vessel invasion in immunocompromised patients

Aspergilloma is the "colonizer in a pre-made hole" version.

---

## One-line intuition

Aspergilloma is Aspergillus growing like a ball inside an old lung cavity, especially after tuberculosis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Aspergillus forms **septate hyphae** (fungal filaments with internal walls) that branch at **acute angles**, classically around 45 degrees.

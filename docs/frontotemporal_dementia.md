## Frontotemporal dementia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bf/FTD_Frontal_Temporal_Lobes_Labeled.png)

![Image](https://www.mayoclinic.org/-/media/kcms/gbs/patient-consumer/images/2013/08/26/11/04/ds00874_im03229_bn7_frontothu_jpg.png)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/40de/5472209/27cf7a276b26/nihms859441f1.jpg)

Frontotemporal dementia, or FTD (frontotemporal dementia), is a neurodegenerative disease where the frontal and temporal lobes of the brain shrink/degenerate.

Break the word apart:

* fronto- = frontal lobe
* temporal = temporal lobe
* dementia = decline in cognition/behavior severe enough to affect daily life

So:

> degeneration of the frontal and temporal lobes.

---

### Core idea

FTD often causes early changes in:

* personality
* behavior
* judgment
* impulse control
* language

Memory can be relatively spared early, which helps distinguish it from Alzheimer disease.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The frontal lobe controls:

* inhibition
* judgment
* personality
* executive function

So frontal lobe degeneration can cause:

* disinhibition
* apathy
* socially inappropriate behavior
* compulsive/repetitive behavior
* poor judgment

The temporal lobe helps with:

* language
* meaning of words
* recognizing objects/people

So temporal lobe degeneration can cause:

* aphasia (language impairment)
* trouble naming objects
* loss of word meaning

---

### Important variants

Main clinical patterns:

* behavioral variant FTD: personality/behavior changes dominate
* primary progressive aphasia, or PPA (primary progressive aphasia): language problems dominate

PPA can look like trouble speaking fluently, trouble finding words, or trouble understanding word meaning.

---

## One-line intuition

FTD is dementia that hits the "personality/judgment" frontal lobe and "language/meaning" temporal lobe first.

---

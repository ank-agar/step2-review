# Ischemic stroke

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/44/Stroke_ischemic.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Circle_of_Willis_en.svg/120px-Circle_of_Willis_en.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bd/INFARCT.jpg)

An ischemic stroke is brain tissue injury from decreased blood flow.

Break it down:

* ischemic = lack of blood flow
* stroke = sudden neurologic deficit from brain injury

So:

> ischemic stroke = a blocked brain artery causing part of the brain to lose oxygen and die.

Most commonly, the blockage is from:

* thrombus (clot formed at the site)
* embolus (clot that traveled from somewhere else)

Classic example:

* atrial fibrillation (irregular atrial rhythm) forms a clot in the left atrium
* clot embolizes to the brain
* middle cerebral artery, or MCA (middle cerebral artery), gets blocked
* patient gets contralateral face/arm weakness and aphasia if dominant hemisphere is involved

Why symptoms happen:

Neurons need constant glucose and oxygen.

If blood flow stops:

* ATP (adenosine triphosphate) falls
* sodium-potassium pumps fail
* cells swell
* glutamate excitotoxicity injures neurons
* infarction (tissue death from ischemia) occurs

The big distinction:

* ischemic stroke = blocked vessel
* hemorrhagic stroke = bleeding vessel

Initial CT (computed tomography) is often done to rule out hemorrhage before giving tPA (tissue plasminogen activator), a clot-busting drug.

One-line intuition:

Ischemic stroke is basically a “brain heart attack”: an artery is blocked, so downstream brain tissue loses oxygen and dies.

HY pearl 🔥

Most common ischemic stroke territory is MCA (middle cerebral artery), and MCA stroke classically affects face/arm more than leg.

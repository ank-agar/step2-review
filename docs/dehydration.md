### Core idea

Dehydration = total body water is too low.

So the body has less fluid to keep:

* blood volume normal
* tissues perfused
* cells functioning
* temperature controlled through sweating

---

### What happens physiologically

If you lose water, plasma volume drops.

Plasma = the liquid part of blood.

Low plasma volume means:

* less venous return to the heart
* lower stroke volume
* lower blood pressure
* compensatory tachycardia

Tachycardia = fast heart rate.

So the body tries to defend blood pressure by squeezing vessels and holding onto salt/water.

---

### Why the classic findings happen

* **Thirst:** hypothalamus senses increased plasma osmolality
* **Dark urine:** kidneys conserve water, so urine becomes concentrated
* **Low urine output:** ADH (antidiuretic hormone) increases water reabsorption
* **Dry mucous membranes:** less extracellular fluid available to hydrate surfaces
* **Poor skin turgor:** less interstitial fluid, so skin tents instead of snapping back
* **Dizziness/orthostasis:** standing drops venous return even more
* **Confusion:** brain perfusion and electrolyte balance can worsen in severe dehydration

Osmolality = concentration of particles in fluid.

Interstitial fluid = fluid between cells.

Orthostasis = symptoms or blood pressure drop when standing.

---

### Important mechanism

Water loss activates:

* ADH (antidiuretic hormone)
* RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system)
* sympathetic nervous system

These all try to preserve circulation.

Think:

> Dehydration makes the body act like it is running out of circulating volume.

---

### Common causes

* vomiting
* diarrhea
* sweating/heat exposure
* poor intake
* diuretics
* diabetes mellitus causing osmotic diuresis
* diabetes insipidus causing free water loss

Diuretics = drugs that increase urine output.

Osmotic diuresis = urinating extra water because solute pulls water into the urine.

Diabetes insipidus = low ADH effect, causing excessive dilute urination.

---

## One-line intuition

Dehydration is not just “being thirsty” — it is low body water causing low circulating volume and concentrated body fluids.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: dehydration increases BUN:Cr ratio because low renal perfusion increases proximal urea reabsorption.

BUN:Cr ratio = blood urea nitrogen to creatinine ratio.

This is why prerenal azotemia often has:

* BUN:Cr > 20
* concentrated urine
* low urine sodium

Prerenal azotemia = kidney labs look bad because low blood flow reaches the kidney, not because the kidney tubules are primarily damaged.

### Core idea

Herpes simplex virus (HSV) is an enveloped double-stranded DNA virus that causes painful vesicles (small fluid-filled blisters), classically on:

* mouth/lips
* genitals
* fingers
* eye
* sometimes brain

There are 2 major types:

* HSV-1 (herpes simplex virus type 1): classically oral herpes/cold sores
* HSV-2 (herpes simplex virus type 2): classically genital herpes

But either type can infect either location.

---

### Key mechanism

HSV infects epithelial cells (surface lining cells), makes vesicles, then hides in sensory ganglia (collections of nerve cell bodies).

Important latency sites:

* HSV-1: trigeminal ganglion
* HSV-2: sacral ganglia

This is why herpes comes back.

The virus is not fully cleared. It goes latent in nerves, then reactivates with triggers like:

* stress
* fever
* sunlight
* immunosuppression

---

### Why the lesions hurt

HSV travels in sensory nerves, so the infected area often has:

* burning
* tingling
* pain
* grouped vesicles on an erythematous (red/inflamed) base

That "grouped vesicles" phrase is very classic.

---

### Histology clue

HSV causes multinucleated giant cells.

Break that down:

* multi = many
* nucleated = nuclei
* giant cell = one big fused cell

Why?

HSV can make infected cells fuse together, forming a big cell with multiple nuclei.

Classic pathology phrase:

* Cowdry type A inclusions (eosinophilic, meaning pink-staining, viral inclusions inside the nucleus)

---

## One-line intuition

HSV is a blister-forming DNA virus that infects skin/mucosa, hides in sensory nerves, and reactivates later.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HSV encephalitis classically affects the temporal lobes and is treated with acyclovir.

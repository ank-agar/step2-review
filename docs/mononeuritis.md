### Core idea

Mononeuritis = inflammation or damage of **one peripheral nerve**.

Break it down:

* mono = one
* neur = nerve
* -itis = inflammation

So the symptoms follow the territory of that one nerve:

* numbness
* tingling/burning pain
* weakness
* decreased reflexes if that nerve carries the reflex pathway

---

### Why the symptoms are focal

A peripheral nerve is like a mixed cable carrying:

* sensory fibers = feeling
* motor fibers = muscle movement
* autonomic fibers = sweating/blood vessel tone

If one nerve is injured, only the muscles/skin supplied by that nerve are affected.

Example:

* median nerve problem → hand symptoms in median nerve distribution
* peroneal nerve problem → foot drop

---

### Mononeuritis multiplex

This is the Step 1 version that gets tested a lot.

Mononeuritis multiplex = damage to **multiple separate individual nerves** in a patchy/asymmetric pattern.

Key idea:

* not one nerve only
* not symmetric stocking-glove neuropathy
* instead: random nerve territories get hit

Classic cause:

* vasculitis (blood vessel inflammation)

Why?

Small vessels supplying nerves, called **vasa nervorum** (blood vessels of nerves), get inflamed/blocked.

That causes nerve ischemia (low blood flow/oxygen), so different nerves infarct in different places.

---

### One-line intuition

Mononeuritis is one damaged nerve; mononeuritis multiplex is several individual nerves getting knocked out in a patchy pattern, often because vasculitis blocks their blood supply.

---

### Common associations

Think:

* diabetes mellitus
* polyarteritis nodosa
* ANCA-associated vasculitis (antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody-associated blood vessel inflammation)
* leprosy/Hansen disease
* rheumatoid arthritis

---

### Tests

Diagnosis is often supported by:

* EMG (electromyography, testing muscle electrical activity)
* NCS (nerve conduction study, testing how well nerves conduct signals)
* sometimes nerve biopsy if vasculitis is suspected

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Mononeuritis multiplex + systemic symptoms like fever, weight loss, kidney issues, skin purpura, or asthma/sinus disease should make you think **vasculitis damaging the vasa nervorum**.

### Core idea

**Francisella tularensis** is a small **Gram-negative coccobacillus** (bacteria that stain pink on Gram stain and are short rod/oval-shaped) that causes **tularemia** (rabbit fever).

Think:

* rabbits
* ticks/deer flies
* ulcer + swollen lymph nodes
* intracellular survival

---

### How you get it

Humans can get it from:

* tick or deer fly bite
* handling infected rabbits/rodents
* inhaling aerosolized bacteria
* contaminated food or water

No classic person-to-person spread.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

F. tularensis is **facultative intracellular** (can live inside cells, especially macrophages).

Macrophages (immune cells that normally eat microbes) ingest it, but the bacteria escape the **phagosome** (the internal “eating vesicle”) into the **cytosol** (fluid inside the cell), where they replicate.

So instead of being killed, it uses immune cells as a hiding/replication site.

That causes:

* **fever/systemic illness:** immune activation from infection
* **skin ulcer:** bacteria enter through skin at bite/contact site
* **lymphadenopathy** (swollen lymph nodes): bacteria drain to regional lymph nodes
* **pneumonia:** inhaled bacteria infect lungs

---

### Classic presentation

Most classic form:

* ulcer at entry site
* painful regional lymph node swelling
* fever

This is called **ulceroglandular tularemia** (skin ulcer + gland/lymph node involvement).

---

## One-line intuition

F. tularensis is “rabbit fever”: a highly infectious intracellular Gram-negative coccobacillus from ticks/rabbits that causes fever, ulcer, and big lymph nodes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

F. tularensis is a **facultative intracellular organism**, so Step 1 likes to group it with intracellular bugs like **Listeria**, **Salmonella**, **Brucella**, and **Legionella**.

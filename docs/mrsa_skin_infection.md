# MRSA skin infection

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/PHIL_Images/7826/7826_lores.jpg)

MRSA skin infection = skin/soft tissue infection caused by **methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus**.

Break it down:

* **Staphylococcus aureus** = gram-positive cocci (round bacteria) in clusters
* **methicillin-resistant** = resistant to many beta-lactam antibiotics (penicillin-like drugs)
* skin infection = usually cellulitis (deeper skin inflammation) or abscess (pus pocket)

The classic look is:

* red
* warm
* swollen
* painful
* pus-filled or draining

Often it looks like a “spider bite,” but most “spider bites” with pus are actually staph/MRSA until proven otherwise.

---

### Core idea

MRSA is basically:

> Staph aureus that makes pus + does not care about methicillin/nafcillin-type drugs.

So if you see a painful boil/abscess with pus, think **Staph aureus**, and if it is community-acquired or recurrent, think **MRSA**.

---

### Why pus happens

Staph aureus is very good at causing abscesses.

An abscess is a walled-off pocket of:

* neutrophils (acute inflammatory white blood cells)
* dead tissue
* bacteria
* fluid

That is why the lesion can feel fluctuant, meaning squishy like there is fluid underneath.

---

### Why normal beta-lactams may fail

MRSA carries **mecA**, a resistance gene.

mecA makes **PBP2a**:

* PBP = penicillin-binding protein
* PBP2a has low affinity for beta-lactams

Beta-lactams normally kill bacteria by blocking cell wall synthesis.

But with PBP2a:

> the antibiotic cannot bind well, so the bacteria keeps building its cell wall.

---

### Treatment intuition

If it is an abscess:

* drainage is the key step

Why?

Because antibiotics have trouble penetrating a sealed-off pus pocket. You have to physically get the pus out.

For MRSA coverage, common options include:

* TMP-SMX (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole)
* doxycycline
* clindamycin
* vancomycin for severe/systemic infection

Systemic infection means infection signs throughout the body, like fever, low blood pressure, or bacteremia (bacteria in the blood).

---

## One-line intuition

MRSA skin infection is a pus-forming staph infection where the main trick is **abscess formation + beta-lactam resistance**.

---

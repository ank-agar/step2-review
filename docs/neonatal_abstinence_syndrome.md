# Neonatal abstinence syndrome

### Core idea

Neonatal abstinence syndrome (NAS) is withdrawal in a newborn after exposure to addictive substances in utero (inside the uterus), especially opioids (pain-relieving drugs like heroin, fentanyl, oxycodone, methadone, or buprenorphine).

Also called neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome (NOWS) when opioids are the main cause.

Break it down:

* neonatal = newborn
* abstinence = stopping a substance
* syndrome = a pattern of findings

So NAS basically means:

> the baby was exposed to a drug before birth, then after delivery the drug supply suddenly stops, so the baby withdraws.

---

### Why it happens

During pregnancy, opioids can cross the placenta (organ that transfers oxygen/nutrients from mother to fetus).

The fetus gets used to having that opioid signal around.

After birth:

* placenta is gone
* drug exposure drops
* baby has an overactive nervous system

That overactivity causes the classic symptoms.

---

### Classic findings

Think: irritated baby + GI (gastrointestinal) upset + autonomic (automatic body-control system) activation.

Common findings:

* high-pitched crying
* irritability
* tremors
* hypertonia (increased muscle tone)
* poor feeding
* vomiting
* diarrhea
* poor weight gain
* sweating
* fever
* sneezing/yawning
* fast breathing
* seizures, rarely

---

### Timing

Symptoms usually start within 1-3 days after birth.

But longer-acting opioids like methadone or buprenorphine can cause later withdrawal because they leave the body more slowly.

---

### Treatment idea

First step is supportive care:

* low-stimulation room
* swaddling
* skin-to-skin contact
* frequent small feeds
* breastfeeding if safe

If severe, the baby may need medication like morphine, methadone, or buprenorphine, then slow tapering.

The logic is:

> give a controlled opioid dose, then gradually wean it so the newborn's nervous system does not crash into withdrawal.

---

## One-line intuition

NAS is a newborn's nervous system overreacting because a drug it was used to during pregnancy suddenly disappears after birth.

---

## HY pearl

For Step 1, associate neonatal abstinence syndrome with opioid exposure in pregnancy and a newborn with tremors, irritability, high-pitched cry, diarrhea, and poor feeding.

### Core idea

Uterine atony means the uterus loses tone and does not contract well after delivery.

Break it down:

* uterine = uterus
* atony = lack of tone

So the uterus becomes soft and "boggy" instead of firm.

---

### Why bleeding happens

After the placenta comes off, it leaves open maternal blood vessels in the uterine wall.

Normally, the myometrium (uterine smooth muscle layer) contracts and squeezes those vessels shut.

In uterine atony:

* uterus does not clamp down
* placental blood vessels stay open
* heavy vaginal bleeding occurs

This causes PPH (postpartum hemorrhage), meaning dangerous bleeding after delivery.

---

### Classic clue

Think:

* postpartum bleeding
* enlarged soft uterus
* "boggy" fundus

Fundus means the top part of the uterus.

---

### Risk factors

Anything that over-stretches or exhausts the uterus:

* twin pregnancy
* polyhydramnios (too much amniotic fluid)
* macrosomia (large baby)
* prolonged labor
* rapid labor
* chorioamnionitis (infection of fetal membranes/amniotic fluid)
* magnesium sulfate, because it relaxes smooth muscle

---

### Treatment logic

Goal: make the uterus contract.

First steps:

* uterine massage
* oxytocin, a uterotonic (drug that increases uterine contraction)

If still bleeding:

* methylergonovine, but avoid in hypertension (high blood pressure)
* carboprost, but avoid in asthma
* misoprostol, a prostaglandin (hormone-like drug that increases uterine tone)
* TXA (tranexamic acid), which helps stabilize clots

---

## One-line intuition

Uterine atony is a tired, floppy postpartum uterus that cannot squeeze the placental vessels shut, so the patient bleeds.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Uterine atony is the most common cause of PPH (postpartum hemorrhage). On exams, "boggy uterus" = give uterine massage + oxytocin.

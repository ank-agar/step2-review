### Core idea

Neisseria gonorrhoeae is a gram-negative diplococcus (a pink/red-staining bacterium that comes in pairs) that causes gonorrhea.

Think:

* gram-negative = thin peptidoglycan wall + outer membrane
* diplococcus = paired round bacteria
* gonorrhoeae = sexually transmitted infection causing purulent discharge

---

### What it looks like

Classic test image:

* gram-negative diplococci
* often inside neutrophils (white blood cells that fight bacteria)

That intracellular neutrophil finding is very high yield for gonorrhea.

---

### Why it causes pus

N. gonorrhoeae attaches to mucosal epithelium (wet lining of the urethra, cervix, rectum, throat, or conjunctiva) using:

* pili (hair-like attachment structures)
* Opa proteins (opacity-associated proteins that help tight attachment)

Then it triggers lots of neutrophils.

Neutrophils + bacteria + dead cells = purulent discharge.

So the classic presentation is:

* urethritis (urethral inflammation) with dysuria (painful urination) and discharge
* cervicitis (cervical inflammation), which can be mild or asymptomatic
* pelvic inflammatory disease, PID (infection ascending into uterus/fallopian tubes)

---

### Important complications

If it spreads upward in females:

* PID
* infertility
* ectopic pregnancy risk

Why?

Inflammation scars the fallopian tubes, so the egg has trouble traveling normally.

If it disseminates into blood:

* septic arthritis (infected joint)
* dermatitis (skin inflammation)
* tenosynovitis (inflammation of tendon sheath)

Classic triad for disseminated gonococcal infection:

* dermatitis
* tenosynovitis
* migratory polyarthritis (joint pain moving between joints)

---

### How to remember the lab facts

Neisseria species are:

* oxidase positive (make cytochrome c oxidase enzyme)
* gram-negative diplococci

Difference from Neisseria meningitidis:

* N. gonorrhoeae ferments glucose only
* N. meningitidis ferments maltose and glucose

Memory:

> Gonorrhea gets glucose only.

---

## One-line intuition

N. gonorrhoeae is a gram-negative paired bacterium that grabs mucosal surfaces with pili, causes neutrophil-heavy pus, and can ascend to PID or spread to joints.

---

## Pearl 🔥

N. gonorrhoeae has antigenic variation (frequent surface antigen changes), especially in pili, so reinfection is common and there is no strong lasting immunity.

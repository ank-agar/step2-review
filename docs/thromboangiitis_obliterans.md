# Thromboangiitis obliterans

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18089.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9b/55_year_old_female_with_advanced_Buerger%27s_disease.jpg)

Thromboangiitis obliterans, also called **Buerger disease**, is an inflammatory disease of small and medium blood vessels, especially in the:

* hands
* feet
* fingers
* toes

Break the word apart:

* thrombo- = clot
* angiitis = blood vessel inflammation
* obliterans = gets blocked off

So the literal idea is:

> inflamed blood vessels that form clots and get blocked.

---

### Core idea

This is strongly associated with **tobacco use**.

Tobacco triggers inflammation in small/medium arteries and veins, causing:

* vasculitis (blood vessel inflammation)
* thrombosis (clot formation)
* ischemia (low blood flow/oxygen to tissue)

If the fingers/toes do not get enough blood, they can develop:

* pain
* ulcers
* gangrene (tissue death from lack of blood supply)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Pain with use:** muscles/tissues need more oxygen, but blocked vessels cannot deliver enough blood
* **Cold/pale digits:** low blood flow to fingers/toes
* **Ulcers/gangrene:** severe ischemia causes tissue breakdown and death
* **Migratory thrombophlebitis:** clots + inflammation can move through superficial veins

Thrombophlebitis = vein inflammation with a clot.

---

### Classic patient

Usually:

* young male
* smoker
* distal extremity ischemia

Distal extremity = far-away parts of limbs, like fingers and toes.

---

## One-line intuition

Smoking makes the small vessels of the hands and feet inflamed and clotted, so the digits slowly lose blood supply.

---

## Pearl 🔥

The most important treatment is **complete smoking cessation**. Even small amounts of tobacco can keep the disease active.

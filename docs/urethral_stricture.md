### Core idea

A urethral stricture is narrowing of the urethra (tube that carries urine from the bladder out of the body).

Usually this narrowing is from scar tissue.

So the simple idea is:

> scar tissue squeezes the urine tube shut.

---

### Why symptoms happen

The urethra normally has an open lumen (inside hollow space).

If scar tissue narrows that lumen:

* urine has less space to flow through
* pressure builds behind the narrowing
* the bladder has to push harder

That causes:

* weak urinary stream
* spraying/splitting stream
* straining to urinate
* incomplete emptying
* urinary retention
* recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection)

---

### Common causes

Anything that injures or inflames the urethra can heal with scar tissue:

* catheterization/instrumentation
* trauma, especially straddle injury
* infection/urethritis (inflammation of the urethra)
* surgery
* lichen sclerosus (chronic inflammatory skin disease that can scar genital tissue)

Men get this more often because the male urethra is much longer.

---

### Diagnosis idea

A retrograde urethrogram is an X-ray study where contrast dye is pushed backward into the urethra.

If there is a stricture, the contrast column narrows or stops at that point.

---

### Treatment idea

Treatment depends on length and recurrence:

* dilation = stretch it open
* DVIU (direct vision internal urethrotomy) = cut the scar from inside
* urethroplasty = surgical reconstruction of the urethra

Short strictures may be treated endoscopically, but recurrent or long strictures often need urethroplasty.

---

## One-line intuition

A urethral stricture is basically a scar-ring in the pee tube, so urine flow becomes weak and obstructed.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Urethral stricture classically causes obstructive urinary symptoms with a weak stream, and a history of trauma or catheterization should make you think scar-related narrowing.

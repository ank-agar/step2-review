### Core idea

Third-degree AV block (third-degree atrioventricular block), also called complete heart block, means:

* atria beat on their own
* ventricles beat on their own
* no atrial impulses get through the AV node (atrioventricular node) to the ventricles

So the P waves and QRS complexes are completely independent.

---

### What you see on ECG

ECG (electrocardiogram) shows:

* regular P waves
* regular QRS complexes
* no consistent PR interval (P wave to QRS interval)
* ventricular rate slower than atrial rate

The key phrase is:

> P waves and QRS complexes do not talk to each other.

---

### Why this happens

Normally:

SA node (sinoatrial node) → atria → AV node (atrioventricular node) → His-Purkinje system → ventricles

In third-degree AV block:

* the SA node still fires, so P waves keep appearing
* the signal cannot pass through to the ventricles
* the ventricles must rely on an escape rhythm (backup pacemaker rhythm)

That escape rhythm is slower, so patients can get bradycardia (slow heart rate), dizziness, syncope (fainting), or hypotension (low blood pressure).

---

### Intuition

Think of the atria and ventricles as two drummers wearing headphones and playing different songs.

The atria are following the SA node.

The ventricles are following a slower backup pacemaker.

That is why the P waves and QRS complexes “march through” separately.

---

### High-yield causes

Common Step 1 causes include:

* ischemia (poor blood flow), especially inferior MI (myocardial infarction/heart attack)
* fibrosis or degeneration of the conduction system
* Lyme disease
* drugs that slow AV nodal conduction, like beta blockers, calcium channel blockers, and digoxin

---

## One-line intuition

Third-degree AV block = complete electrical divorce between atria and ventricles.

---

## Pearl 🔥

If QRS is narrow, the escape rhythm is usually junctional (from near the AV junction) and more stable.

If QRS is wide, the escape rhythm is ventricular and more dangerous because it is slower and lower in the conduction system.

# Hepatitis B

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Chronic_HBV_Diagram-Vector.svg/1280px-Chronic_HBV_Diagram-Vector.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK304352/bin/978-9283201342-C007-F001.001.gif)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil///PHIL_Images/20898/20898_lores.jpg)

Hepatitis B is liver inflammation caused by HBV (hepatitis B virus).

Core idea:

* HBV infects hepatocytes (liver cells)
* your immune system attacks infected hepatocytes
* that immune attack causes hepatitis (liver inflammation)

So the virus itself is not the main “burning” problem; the immune response against infected liver cells causes much of the damage.

---

### What kind of virus is it?

HBV is:

* enveloped
* partially double-stranded DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) virus
* replicates using reverse transcriptase (enzyme that makes DNA from RNA)

That last part is weird:

> HBV is a DNA virus, but it uses reverse transcriptase.

This is why drugs like tenofovir/entecavir work; they target viral DNA production.

---

### Transmission

Spread through blood and body fluids:

* sexual contact
* IV (intravenous) drug use
* needlestick exposure
* birth from infected mother to baby

Not mainly spread by casual contact.

---

### Acute vs chronic

Acute HBV = new infection.

Chronic HBV = infection lasting >6 months.

The younger you are when infected, the more likely it becomes chronic:

* newborns: high chronic risk
* adults: usually clear it

Why?

Babies have an immature immune response, so they tolerate the virus instead of clearing infected hepatocytes strongly.

---

### Key antigens/antibodies

HBsAg (hepatitis B surface antigen):

* means current infection
* if present >6 months = chronic HBV

Anti-HBs (antibody against hepatitis B surface antigen):

* means immunity
* from vaccine or recovery

Anti-HBc (antibody against hepatitis B core antigen):

* means real prior/current infection
* not from vaccine

IgM anti-HBc (immunoglobulin M antibody against hepatitis B core antigen):

* acute infection
* also positive during the window period

HBeAg (hepatitis B e antigen):

* high viral replication
* high infectivity

---

### Window period

Window period = HBsAg is gone, but anti-HBs is not detectable yet.

During this time, the best marker is:

> IgM anti-HBc

Intuition:

Surface antigen disappears before surface antibody shows up, but the core IgM antibody stays detectable during that gap.

---

### Pathology finding

Chronic hepatitis B can show ground-glass hepatocytes.

Ground-glass hepatocytes = liver cells with hazy pink cytoplasm due to buildup of HBsAg (hepatitis B surface antigen) in the endoplasmic reticulum (protein-folding organelle).

---

### Complications

Chronic HBV increases risk of:

* cirrhosis (scarring of the liver)
* HCC (hepatocellular carcinoma, primary liver cancer)

Why cancer risk?

HBV DNA can integrate into the host genome, and chronic inflammation causes repeated liver injury/regeneration. More regeneration = more chances for mutations.

---

## One-line intuition

Hepatitis B is an immune-mediated liver injury from a DNA virus that can become chronic, especially when acquired at birth, and chronic infection can lead to cirrhosis and HCC (hepatocellular carcinoma).

---

## HY pearl

HBV vaccine contains HBsAg (hepatitis B surface antigen), so vaccination gives anti-HBs (surface antibody) only, not anti-HBc (core antibody).

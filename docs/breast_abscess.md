### Core idea

A breast abscess is a localized pocket of pus inside breast tissue.

Abscess = walled-off infection full of:

* neutrophils
* dead tissue
* bacteria
* fluid/pus

Most commonly, this happens as a complication of mastitis (breast inflammation/infection), especially during breastfeeding.

---

### Why it happens

During breastfeeding, bacteria can enter through small nipple cracks and travel into milk ducts.

Then:

* bacteria infect breast tissue
* neutrophils rush in
* tissue gets inflamed and damaged
* pus collects in one spot
* the body walls it off

That walled-off pus collection is the abscess.

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

* painful breast lump
* redness
* warmth
* swelling
* fever sometimes
* may have fluctuant mass

Fluctuant means it feels fluid-filled or squishy because there is pus inside.

---

### Important distinction

Mastitis = inflamed/infected breast tissue.

Breast abscess = pus collection that forms when infection localizes.

So the intuition is:

> mastitis is the infection spreading through tissue; abscess is when that infection gets trapped into a pus pocket.

---

### Treatment idea

Antibiotics alone may not be enough because pus has poor blood supply.

Why?

Blood brings antibiotics, but the center of an abscess is mostly dead cells and thick pus, so antibiotics cannot penetrate well.

So treatment usually needs:

* antibiotics
* drainage or needle aspiration

Needle aspiration means using a needle to remove the pus, often with ultrasound guidance.

---

## One-line intuition

A breast abscess is mastitis that has organized into a painful pus-filled pocket.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Most lactational breast abscesses are due to Staphylococcus aureus, including MRSA (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus) in some patients.

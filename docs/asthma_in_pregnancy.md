## Asthma in pregnancy

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK551579/bin/Asthma_attack-illustration_NIH.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d0/Asthma-bronchial-tube-cross-section.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2d/Metered-Dose_Inhaler_Spacer_%28Adult%29.png)

Asthma in pregnancy = asthma happening during pregnancy, where the big concern is **maternal hypoxemia** (low oxygen in mom's blood) causing **fetal hypoxia** (low oxygen delivery to the fetus).

Core asthma problem:

* airway inflammation
* bronchoconstriction (tightening of airway smooth muscle)
* mucus plugging

So air has a harder time getting out, causing:

* wheezing
* cough
* chest tightness
* shortness of breath

---

### Why pregnancy matters

Pregnancy already increases oxygen demand.

So if asthma flares and mom cannot oxygenate well:

* mom gets less oxygen
* placenta receives less oxygenated blood
* fetus can have growth restriction, preterm birth risk, or distress

Intuition:

> the baby depends on mom's oxygenation, so uncontrolled asthma is usually more dangerous than properly used asthma meds.

---

### Treatment idea

Treat asthma in pregnancy basically like regular asthma:

* **SABA** (short-acting beta agonist), like albuterol, for rescue
* **ICS** (inhaled corticosteroid), like budesonide, for control/prevention

Why ICS helps:

* asthma is inflammatory
* steroids decrease airway inflammation
* less inflammation means less swelling, mucus, and hyperreactivity

Do not stop controller meds just because the patient is pregnant unless the clinician specifically changes the plan.

---

### What to watch for

Bad control signs:

* frequent rescue inhaler use
* nighttime symptoms
* worsening wheeze/cough
* decreased peak flow (how fast someone can blow air out)

These mean the airways are narrowing more often.

---

## One-line intuition

Asthma in pregnancy is dangerous mainly because mom's narrowed, inflamed airways can reduce oxygen delivery to the fetus.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1: **Budesonide** is the classic preferred ICS (inhaled corticosteroid) in pregnancy, and **albuterol** is the classic rescue inhaler.

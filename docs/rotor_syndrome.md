## Rotor syndrome

![Image](https://themedicalbiochemistrypage.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Rotor-syndrome-defect.jpg)

![Image](https://dm5migu4zj3pb.cloudfront.net/manuscripts/59000/59526/medium/JCI59526.f6.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b4/Bilirubin_metabolism_diagram.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/de/Jaundice_eye.jpg)

Rotor syndrome is a benign inherited disorder causing conjugated hyperbilirubinemia (high blood bilirubin, especially direct/conjugated bilirubin).

Key idea:

* bilirubin = yellow heme breakdown product
* conjugated bilirubin = bilirubin made water-soluble by the liver
* hyperbilirubinemia = too much bilirubin in blood

Rotor syndrome is due to loss of:

* OATP1B1 (organic anion transporting polypeptide 1B1)
* OATP1B3 (organic anion transporting polypeptide 1B3)

These are liver uptake transporters on hepatocytes (liver cells).

---

### What goes wrong

Normally, conjugated bilirubin can move between:

* blood
* hepatocytes (liver cells)
* bile

In Rotor syndrome, hepatocytes cannot efficiently re-uptake conjugated bilirubin from blood.

So conjugated bilirubin backs up in the blood.

That causes:

* mild jaundice (yellow skin/eyes)
* increased direct bilirubin
* otherwise usually normal liver tests

---

### Why it is conjugated bilirubin

The liver can still conjugate bilirubin.

So the problem is not making conjugated bilirubin.

The problem is handling/transporting it afterward.

That is why Rotor syndrome gives direct/conjugated hyperbilirubinemia.

---

### Inheritance

Autosomal recessive (need 2 bad copies of the gene pathway).

Autosomal recessive = both gene copies must be defective for disease to show.

---

### Rotor vs Dubin-Johnson

Both cause conjugated hyperbilirubinemia.

Big difference:

* Rotor syndrome: no black liver
* Dubin-Johnson syndrome: black liver from pigment deposition

Dubin-Johnson is due to MRP2 (multidrug resistance-associated protein 2) defect, which impairs secretion of conjugated bilirubin into bile.

Rotor is more about impaired hepatic uptake/storage of conjugated bilirubin.

---

## One-line intuition

Rotor syndrome = the liver can conjugate bilirubin, but cannot properly re-capture/store conjugated bilirubin from blood, so direct bilirubin stays elevated.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 distinction: Rotor syndrome has conjugated hyperbilirubinemia without a black liver; Dubin-Johnson syndrome has conjugated hyperbilirubinemia with a black liver.

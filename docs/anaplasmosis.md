### Core idea

Anaplasmosis is a tick-borne infection caused by **Anaplasma phagocytophilum**, an intracellular bacterium that infects **neutrophils** (white blood cells that kill bacteria).

It is spread by **Ixodes ticks**:

* Ixodes scapularis = blacklegged tick
* Ixodes pacificus = western blacklegged tick

These are the same tick family associated with Lyme disease and babesiosis.

---

### What it looks like

Classic symptoms:

* fever
* headache
* malaise (feeling very sick/tired)
* myalgias (muscle aches)

Labs often show:

* leukopenia (low white blood cells)
* thrombocytopenia (low platelets)
* elevated LFTs (liver function tests)

---

### Why the findings happen

Anaplasma lives inside neutrophils.

So on blood smear, you can see **morulae** (little clusters of bacteria inside a cell) within neutrophils.

That intracellular infection messes with immune cells and causes a systemic inflammatory illness:

* fever from cytokines (immune signaling proteins)
* low white blood cells because infected immune cells get disrupted
* low platelets from systemic inflammation/consumption
* elevated liver enzymes because infection/inflammation can irritate the liver

---

### Key comparison

Anaplasmosis:

* infects granulocytes, especially neutrophils
* caused by Anaplasma phagocytophilum

Ehrlichiosis:

* infects monocytes (a different type of white blood cell)
* commonly caused by Ehrlichia chaffeensis

Easy memory:

> Anaplasma = attacks neutrophils.

---

### Treatment

Treat with **doxycycline** (a tetracycline antibiotic).

Do not wait for confirmatory testing if suspicion is high, because tick-borne rickettsial-like illnesses can worsen quickly.

---

## One-line intuition

Anaplasmosis is like a tick-delivered bacterium hiding inside neutrophils, causing fever + low blood counts + elevated liver enzymes.

---

## HY pearl

If you see a tick-borne illness with **fever + leukopenia + thrombocytopenia + elevated LFTs**, think anaplasmosis/ehrlichiosis and treat with doxycycline.

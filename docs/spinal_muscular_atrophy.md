### Core idea

Spinal muscular atrophy, or SMA (spinal muscular atrophy), is a genetic disease where lower motor neurons die.

Lower motor neurons = nerves that directly tell muscles to contract.

The main problem is loss/mutation of:

* SMN1 (survival motor neuron 1 gene)

This causes low SMN protein (survival motor neuron protein), which motor neurons need to survive.

---

### What gets damaged?

The damaged cells are in the anterior horn of the spinal cord.

Anterior horn = front part of the spinal cord gray matter where lower motor neuron cell bodies live.

So the pathway is:

* SMN1 mutation
* low SMN protein
* anterior horn cell death
* muscle denervation
* weakness + atrophy

Denervation = muscle loses nerve input.

---

### Why muscles shrink

Muscle needs constant nerve stimulation to maintain size.

If the lower motor neuron dies, the muscle is still structurally normal at first, but it is no longer being told to contract.

So it becomes:

* weak
* floppy
* wasted/atrophic

That is why SMA causes muscle atrophy even though the primary problem is in the nerve, not the muscle.

---

### Classic findings

SMA causes lower motor neuron signs:

* hypotonia = low muscle tone, aka "floppy baby"
* weakness
* muscle atrophy
* fasciculations = small involuntary muscle twitches
* decreased or absent reflexes

It often affects proximal muscles more than distal muscles.

Proximal = closer to the trunk, like shoulders/hips.

Distal = farther from the trunk, like hands/feet.

---

### Why breathing problems happen

Respiratory muscles can also weaken.

Especially important:

* intercostal muscles = muscles between the ribs
* diaphragm = main breathing muscle

If these muscles are weak, the patient can develop respiratory failure.

This is a major cause of death in severe infantile SMA.

---

### Genetics

Most classic SMA is autosomal recessive.

Autosomal recessive = both copies of the gene must be abnormal to cause disease.

The key gene is:

* SMN1 on chromosome 5

There is also a backup-like gene:

* SMN2 (survival motor neuron 2 gene)

SMN2 makes some functional SMN protein, but not enough to fully replace SMN1.

So:

* fewer SMN2 copies = more severe disease
* more SMN2 copies = milder disease

---

## One-line intuition

SMA is basically: the body cannot make enough SMN protein, so anterior horn lower motor neurons die, and the muscles they control become weak and atrophic.

---

## HY pearl

SMA is an anterior horn cell disease, so think lower motor neuron findings:

* weakness
* atrophy
* fasciculations
* decreased reflexes

And the severity is modified by the number of SMN2 copies: more SMN2 = milder SMA.

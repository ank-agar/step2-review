# Avascular osteonecrosis

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/bc40/5394877/3a25652b9369/1011295.fig1.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c7/Head_of_femur_avascular_necrosis.jpg)

Avascular osteonecrosis means bone death from loss of blood supply.

Break it down:

* **a-vascular** = without blood vessels/blood supply
* **osteo** = bone
* **necrosis** = tissue death

So the core idea is:

> blood flow gets cut off → bone cells die → the bone weakens → the joint surface can collapse.

---

### Most classic location

The classic Step 1 site is the **femoral head** (ball part of the hip joint).

Why?

The femoral head has a vulnerable blood supply, especially from branches of the medial circumflex femoral artery (artery wrapping around the femur to supply the hip). If that blood supply is damaged, the femoral head can die.

---

### Why collapse happens

Bone is living tissue, so it needs oxygen and nutrients.

If blood flow stops:

* osteocytes (mature bone cells) die
* the bone repair system cannot keep up
* tiny fractures form under the cartilage
* the subchondral bone (bone just under cartilage) collapses
* the joint becomes painful and arthritic

This is why avascular necrosis can look like a smooth hip early, but later the femoral head becomes flattened/collapsed.

---

### Big causes to remember

High-yield causes:

* femoral neck fracture
* hip dislocation
* corticosteroid use
* alcohol use disorder
* sickle cell disease
* systemic lupus erythematosus (autoimmune disease that can affect joints, kidneys, skin, and blood vessels)
* decompression sickness

The shared theme is either:

* direct blood vessel injury
* blocked blood flow
* increased pressure inside bone marrow that compresses blood vessels

---

## One-line intuition

Avascular osteonecrosis is basically a “bone heart attack”: ischemia (low blood flow) kills bone, and the dead bone can collapse under weight-bearing.

---

## HY pearl

A displaced femoral neck fracture can cause avascular necrosis of the femoral head because it can tear the retinacular arteries (small vessels running along the femoral neck to supply the femoral head).

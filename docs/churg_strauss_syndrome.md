Churg-Strauss syndrome is now called **eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis**.

Break the name down:

* **eosinophilic** = lots of eosinophils (white blood cells involved in allergies/asthma/parasites)
* **granulomatosis** = granulomas (organized collections of immune cells)
* **polyangiitis** = inflammation of many blood vessels

So the core idea is:

> asthma + eosinophilia + small-vessel vasculitis.

---

### Classic presentation

Think of a patient with:

* adult-onset asthma
* allergic rhinitis/sinusitis
* high eosinophils
* neuropathy, especially mononeuritis multiplex (damage to multiple separate peripheral nerves)
* palpable purpura (raised purple skin lesions from small-vessel inflammation)
* lung infiltrates

---

### Why the findings happen

The immune system overactivates eosinophils.

Eosinophils release toxic granules that damage tissues, especially:

* lungs → asthma, cough, pulmonary infiltrates
* blood vessels → vasculitis (vessel inflammation)
* nerves → neuropathy
* skin → purpura
* heart → myocarditis (heart muscle inflammation), which can be dangerous

The vasculitis narrows and injures small vessels, so tissues downstream get inflamed/ischemic.

---

### Pathology buzzwords

Churg-Strauss shows:

* necrotizing vasculitis (vessel wall death from inflammation)
* eosinophil-rich inflammation
* granulomas

This is why the full name is so useful: it literally tells you the pathology.

---

## One-line intuition

Churg-Strauss is allergic/asthmatic inflammation that escalates into eosinophil-heavy systemic vasculitis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Churg-Strauss syndrome is classically associated with **p-ANCA/MPO-ANCA** (perinuclear antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody / myeloperoxidase antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody), but the highest-yield clue is usually **asthma + eosinophilia + vasculitis**.

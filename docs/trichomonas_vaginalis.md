### Core idea

Trichomonas vaginalis is a flagellated protozoan (single-celled parasite with whip-like motility structures) that causes trichomoniasis, a sexually transmitted infection.

It infects the:

* vagina/cervix
* urethra
* prostate sometimes

Classic female findings:

* frothy yellow-green vaginal discharge
* vaginal itching/burning
* dysuria (pain with urination)
* dyspareunia (pain with sex)
* strawberry cervix, meaning punctate cervical hemorrhages

Men are often asymptomatic, but can have urethritis (urethral inflammation).

---

### Why the findings happen

Trichomonas is motile because it has flagella (whip-like tails), so on wet mount microscopy it looks like a moving pear-shaped organism.

It damages and inflames vaginal/cervical epithelium:

* inflammation causes itching, burning, and pain
* inflammatory discharge can look frothy and yellow-green
* tiny cervical capillary irritation/bleeding causes strawberry cervix

It also raises vaginal pH (acidity level), usually above 4.5, because normal Lactobacillus (normal acid-producing vaginal bacteria) gets disrupted.

---

### Important Step 1 details

Trichomonas vaginalis has:

* trophozoite form only
* no cyst form
* sexual transmission
* diagnosis by NAAT (nucleic acid amplification test) or wet mount
* treatment with metronidazole or tinidazole

Treat sexual partners too, or reinfection is common.

---

## One-line intuition

Think: motile flagellated protozoan STI causing frothy discharge + strawberry cervix.

---

## HY pearl

Unlike Giardia lamblia, Trichomonas vaginalis does not have a cyst form, so it spreads mainly by direct sexual transmission rather than surviving well in the outside environment.

## St. Louis encephalitis virus

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/sle/media/images/SLETransmissionCycle16.9.png)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/sle/media/images/PHIL1027316.9.jpeg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/PHIL_Images/14991/14991_lores.jpg)

St. Louis encephalitis virus is a mosquito-borne **flavivirus** that can cause encephalitis (inflammation of the brain).

Break it down:

* St. Louis = named after an outbreak in St. Louis
* encephalitis = brain inflammation
* virus = infectious particle that replicates inside host cells

It is an **arbovirus** (arthropod-borne virus), meaning it is spread by insects, usually mosquitoes.

---

### Transmission

Main cycle:

* birds = amplifying hosts
* Culex mosquitoes = vector
* humans = dead-end hosts

Dead-end host means humans usually do **not** get enough virus in the blood to pass it back to mosquitoes.

So the intuition is:

> bird gets virus -> mosquito bites bird -> mosquito bites human -> human gets sick, but usually does not continue the cycle.

---

### What kind of virus is it?

St. Louis encephalitis virus is:

* enveloped
* positive-sense single-stranded RNA (ribonucleic acid)
* Flavivirus family

Same general family as:

* West Nile virus
* Japanese encephalitis virus
* yellow fever virus
* dengue virus
* Zika virus

---

### Why it causes symptoms

Most infections are asymptomatic (no symptoms).

If symptomatic, it can start like a viral illness:

* fever
* headache
* dizziness
* nausea
* weakness

If it invades the CNS (central nervous system), it can cause:

* meningitis (inflammation of the meninges, the coverings of the brain and spinal cord)
* encephalitis (inflammation of brain tissue)
* confusion
* seizures
* coma in severe cases

Why older adults are higher risk:

* weaker immune control
* more vulnerability to CNS inflammation
* less reserve if brain swelling/inflammation occurs

---

## One-line intuition

St. Louis encephalitis virus is basically a **Culex mosquito + bird flavivirus** that accidentally infects humans and can inflame the brain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember: **humans are dead-end hosts** for St. Louis encephalitis virus, similar to West Nile virus.

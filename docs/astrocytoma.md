## Astrocytoma

![Image](https://www.mayoclinic.org/-/media/kcms/gbs/patient-consumer/images/2019/01/11/10/47/astrocytoma-8col-3802786-001-0.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK559042/bin/Screenshot__2020-06-15__at__14.06.44.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/c59a/7975751/0c148f2da468/zj40050611560001.jpg)

Astrocytoma is a tumor made from astrocytes.

Astrocytes are star-shaped glial cells (support cells in the central nervous system, meaning brain and spinal cord).

So the basic idea is:

> astrocyte cell becomes neoplastic (tumor-forming) → glial brain tumor.

---

### Big picture

Astrocytomas can be:

* low-grade and slow-growing
* high-grade and aggressive

They often cause symptoms by taking up space in the brain:

* headache
* seizures
* nausea/vomiting from increased intracranial pressure (increased pressure inside the skull)
* focal neurologic deficits (weakness, vision changes, speech problems, etc.)

---

### Step 1 pattern

For exams, the classic one is:

* **pilocytic astrocytoma**
* usually in children
* often in the cerebellum
* relatively benign/low-grade
* histology shows Rosenthal fibers

Rosenthal fibers are eosinophilic (pink-staining) corkscrew-like protein aggregates inside astrocytic processes.

Break down pilocytic:

* pilo- = hair
* cytic = cell

So pilocytic astrocytoma has "hair-like" astrocyte processes.

---

### Why GFAP matters

Astrocytes contain GFAP (glial fibrillary acidic protein), an intermediate filament protein (cell structural protein).

So astrocytomas are usually GFAP-positive on staining because they come from astrocytes.

Intuition:

> if the tumor came from an astrocyte, it keeps astrocyte markers.

---

## One-line intuition

Astrocytoma = astrocyte-derived brain tumor; in kids, think pilocytic astrocytoma with Rosenthal fibers.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Pilocytic astrocytoma is the most common glioma (glial-cell tumor) in children and classically occurs in the cerebellum.

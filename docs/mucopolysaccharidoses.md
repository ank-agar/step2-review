### Core idea

Mucopolysaccharidoses are lysosomal storage diseases where the body cannot break down glycosaminoglycans.

Glycosaminoglycans (GAGs; long sugar chains in connective tissue, cartilage, cornea, skin, and extracellular matrix) used to be called mucopolysaccharides.

So the name literally means:

* mucopolysaccharide = glycosaminoglycan
* -osis = disease process

Basically:

> defective lysosomal enzyme -> GAG buildup -> enlarged, dysfunctional cells/tissues

---

### Why the findings happen

Lysosomes (cell recycling organelles) normally digest GAGs step by step.

If one enzyme is missing:

* GAGs cannot be fully degraded
* partially degraded GAGs accumulate inside lysosomes
* cells swell and tissues become thick/stiff

That causes:

* coarse facial features: GAG buildup in soft tissue
* hepatosplenomegaly (enlarged liver and spleen): storage material in reticuloendothelial tissues
* skeletal deformities: abnormal cartilage/bone remodeling
* joint stiffness: connective tissue thickening
* corneal clouding: GAG buildup in the cornea
* developmental delay sometimes: central nervous system (brain and spinal cord) involvement

---

### Big Step 1 pattern

Most mucopolysaccharidoses are autosomal recessive (need 2 bad copies of the gene).

Major exception:

* Hunter syndrome, or MPS II (mucopolysaccharidosis type 2), is X-linked recessive (usually affects males)

Classic comparison:

* Hurler syndrome, or MPS I (mucopolysaccharidosis type 1): alpha-L-iduronidase deficiency + corneal clouding
* Hunter syndrome, or MPS II (mucopolysaccharidosis type 2): iduronate sulfatase deficiency + no corneal clouding

Easy memory:

* Hunter = no cloudy corneas
* Hurler = has cloudy corneas

---

## One-line intuition

The lysosome cannot digest certain long sugar chains, so those sugars pile up in connective tissues and make organs, bones, joints, face, and sometimes brain abnormal.

---

## Pearl

For USMLE Step 1, if a stem says coarse facies + hepatosplenomegaly + developmental delay + corneal clouding, think Hurler syndrome; if it looks similar but says no corneal clouding and male child, think Hunter syndrome.

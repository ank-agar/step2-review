## Acute stress disorder

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538239/bin/HPA__Axis.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/54/Response_to_stress.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d7/Ptsd-brain.png)

Acute stress disorder is a trauma-related disorder where someone has PTSD-like symptoms after a traumatic event, but the timing is short:

* starts after trauma
* lasts **3 days to 1 month**
* if symptoms last **more than 1 month**, think PTSD (post-traumatic stress disorder)

So the key idea:

> Acute stress disorder = early PTSD-like reaction after trauma.

---

### Core symptoms

After trauma, the patient can have symptoms from these groups:

* **intrusion:** flashbacks, nightmares, unwanted memories
* **negative mood:** cannot feel positive emotions
* **dissociation:** feels detached, dazed, unreal, or cannot remember parts of the trauma
* **avoidance:** avoids reminders of the trauma
* **arousal:** hypervigilance, irritability, poor sleep, exaggerated startle

Dissociation is especially classic for acute stress disorder.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Trauma activates the brain's fear/stress system:

* amygdala = detects threat/fear
* sympathetic nervous system = "fight or flight"
* HPA axis (hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis) = cortisol stress response

This makes the body act like danger is still present even after the trauma is over.

That explains:

* nightmares/flashbacks: trauma memory keeps reactivating
* hypervigilance: brain keeps scanning for danger
* exaggerated startle: sympathetic system is turned up
* avoidance: patient tries to prevent re-triggering the fear response
* dissociation: brain mentally "disconnects" during overwhelming stress

---

### One-line intuition

The trauma is over, but the brain is still stuck in emergency mode during the first month.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, the biggest distinction is **time**:

* **Acute stress disorder:** 3 days to 1 month after trauma
* **PTSD (post-traumatic stress disorder):** more than 1 month after trauma

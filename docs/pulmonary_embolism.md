### Core idea

Pulmonary embolism means a blockage in a pulmonary artery (artery carrying blood from the right heart to the lungs).

Most commonly, it is a blood clot that forms as a DVT (deep vein thrombosis) in the leg, breaks off, travels through the venous system, passes through the right heart, and gets stuck in the lung.

So:

> clot from leg vein -> right heart -> pulmonary artery -> blocked lung blood flow

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Sudden shortness of breath:** part of the lung gets air but not blood, so gas exchange drops
* **Pleuritic chest pain:** pain with breathing because the blocked area can irritate the pleura (lining around the lung)
* **Tachycardia:** fast heart rate because the body tries to maintain oxygen delivery
* **Hemoptysis:** coughing blood, from pulmonary infarction (lung tissue death from poor blood flow), especially in smaller peripheral emboli
* **Syncope/hypotension:** a large clot can suddenly increase pressure in the pulmonary arteries, straining the right ventricle (right-sided heart pumping chamber)

---

### Big pathophys intuition

The lung area is still ventilated (getting air), but not perfused (getting blood).

That creates a V/Q mismatch (ventilation/perfusion mismatch), specifically:

* high V/Q
* dead space

because air reaches alveoli (tiny lung air sacs), but blood cannot pick up that oxygen.

---

### Classic risk factors

Think Virchow triad (3 reasons clots form):

* stasis (slow blood flow): immobility, long travel, hospitalization
* hypercoagulability (too much clotting): cancer, pregnancy, oral contraceptives, inherited thrombophilias
* endothelial injury (vessel wall damage): surgery, trauma

---

## One-line intuition

A pulmonary embolism is usually a leg clot that travels to the lungs and blocks blood flow, causing sudden hypoxemia (low blood oxygen) and right heart strain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

PE (pulmonary embolism) classically causes respiratory alkalosis (high blood pH from low carbon dioxide) because hypoxemia makes the patient hyperventilate.

# Acute pericarditis

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18149.jpg)

![Image](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/mmanual-ssq1ci05/media/professional/images/c/v/s/cvs_pericarditis_ecg.gif?mw=1200&sc_lang=en-us)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK431080/bin/Figure__1.jpg)

### Core idea

Acute pericarditis means sudden inflammation of the pericardium.

Pericardium = the sac-like covering around the heart.

So acute pericarditis is basically:

> an inflamed heart sac rubbing around the heart.

---

### Classic symptoms

The chest pain is usually:

* sharp
* pleuritic, meaning worse with inspiration (breathing in)
* worse when lying flat
* better when sitting up and leaning forward

Why?

When the pericardium is inflamed, the two pericardial layers rub against each other. Changing position changes how much those inflamed surfaces stretch and touch.

Leaning forward pulls the heart away from the posterior pericardium (back part of the heart sac), so pain can improve.

---

### Classic physical exam finding

Pericardial friction rub.

This is a scratchy sound heard with the stethoscope because the inflamed pericardial layers are rubbing together.

Think:

> inflamed sac + moving heart = rubbing sound.

---

### ECG finding

ECG (electrocardiogram, the test that records the heart's electrical activity) classically shows:

* diffuse ST elevation
* PR depression

Diffuse means it shows up in many leads, not just one coronary artery territory.

Why diffuse?

The pericardium surrounds the whole heart, so inflammation irritates the outer heart surface broadly instead of following one blocked artery.

This is different from MI (myocardial infarction, heart attack), where ST elevation usually localizes to one coronary artery region.

---

### Common causes

Most common overall:

* viral infection

Other Step 1 causes:

* post-MI pericarditis
* uremia, meaning high waste products in blood from kidney failure
* autoimmune disease, meaning immune system attacks self-tissue
* malignancy, meaning cancer
* radiation

---

### Major complication

Pericardial effusion = fluid buildup in the pericardial sac.

If fluid builds up fast, it can cause cardiac tamponade.

Cardiac tamponade means pressure around the heart prevents the heart from filling normally.

So:

* less ventricular filling
* less stroke volume, meaning less blood pumped per beat
* hypotension, meaning low blood pressure

---

## One-line intuition

Acute pericarditis = inflamed heart sac causing sharp positional chest pain, friction rub, and diffuse ST elevation with PR depression.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Post-MI (post-myocardial infarction) pericarditis has 2 classic timings:

* 1-3 days after MI: fibrinous pericarditis from local inflammation
* weeks later: Dressler syndrome, an autoimmune pericarditis after myocardial injury

Acute pericarditis chest pain improves when leaning forward; MI pain usually does not.

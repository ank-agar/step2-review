### Core idea

A dislocated lens = **ectopia lentis** (abnormal position of the eye lens).

Normally, the lens sits centered behind the pupil and focuses light onto the retina.

It is held in place by **zonular fibers** (tiny suspensory fibers connecting the lens to the ciliary body).

If those zonular fibers are weak or torn, the lens shifts out of place.

---

### Why vision gets blurry

The lens is like the focusing lens in a camera.

If it is off-center:

* light bends in the wrong direction
* the image does not focus cleanly on the retina
* patient gets blurry vision, double vision, myopia (nearsightedness), or astigmatism (uneven focusing)

So the problem is not the retina first - it is the focusing lens being physically displaced.

---

### Partial vs complete

* **Subluxation** = partial dislocation; lens is still partly in place
* **Luxation** = complete dislocation; lens is fully displaced

The lens can fall:

* anteriorly = forward into the anterior chamber (front fluid-filled space of the eye)
* posteriorly = backward into the vitreous cavity (gel-filled back part of the eye)

---

### Classic Step 1 associations

Think connective tissue weakness:

* Marfan syndrome - fibrillin-1 defect; lens classically moves up and out
* Homocystinuria - cystathionine beta-synthase deficiency; lens classically moves down and in
* Trauma - most common acquired cause

Why?

Because zonular fibers depend on strong connective tissue support. If the support fibers are weak, the lens loses its anchor and drifts.

---

## One-line intuition

A dislocated lens is a camera lens knocked off-center, so incoming light cannot focus cleanly.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Marfan syndrome and homocystinuria can both cause ectopia lentis, but:

* Marfan = lens goes **up and out**
* Homocystinuria = lens goes **down and in**

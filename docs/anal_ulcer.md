# Anal ulcer

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17882/N01012-H.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Diagram_showing_the_anatomy_of_the_anus_CRUK_282.svg/500px-Diagram_showing_the_anatomy_of_the_anus_CRUK_282.svg.png)

An **anal ulcer** is an open sore/break in the lining of the anus or anal canal.

The most common “anal ulcer” concept is an **anal fissure** = a linear tear/ulcer in the anal mucosa (thin moist lining).

### Core idea

Think:

* hard stool or inflammation injures the anal lining
* the lining splits open
* exposed sensory nerves cause sharp pain
* small blood vessels tear, causing bright red blood

So the classic symptoms are:

* severe pain with bowel movements
* bright red blood on toilet paper
* burning/spasm afterward

### Why it hurts so much

The lower anal canal is somatically innervated, meaning it has sharp, well-localized pain fibers like skin.

So a small tear there can hurt a lot.

### Why it may not heal

Pain makes the internal anal sphincter (smooth muscle ring controlling the anus) spasm.

Spasm decreases blood flow to the ulcer.

Less blood flow = less oxygen/nutrients = poor healing.

That creates a cycle:

> ulcer → pain → sphincter spasm → low blood flow → worse healing → chronic ulcer

### Causes to know

Common:

* constipation/hard stool
* diarrhea
* trauma

Important systemic causes:

* Crohn disease
* sexually transmitted infections, especially HSV (herpes simplex virus) or syphilis
* TB (tuberculosis)
* HIV (human immunodeficiency virus)-related infections
* anal cancer if nonhealing/atypical

### Step 1 association

Crohn disease can cause perianal disease:

* fissures
* ulcers
* fistulas
* abscesses

because Crohn has **transmural inflammation** (inflammation through the full bowel wall).

UC (ulcerative colitis) is more superficial and continuous, so fistulas/perianal complications are much more Crohn-y.

## One-line intuition

An anal ulcer is basically a painful open tear/sore in the anal lining, often from trauma or Crohn-type inflammation.

## Pearl 🔥

Posterior midline anal fissure is classic for routine constipation-related fissure; ulcers/fissures that are lateral, multiple, painless, or nonhealing should make you think Crohn disease, infection, immunosuppression, or malignancy.

## Motion sickness

![Image](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/sites/default/files/Documents/health/balance/images/inner_ear_EN_2-28-18.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/aa/Balance_Disorder_Illustration_B.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fb/Vestibular_system%27s_semicircular_canal-_a_cross-section.jpg)

Motion sickness is nausea/dizziness caused by a mismatch between:

* what your eyes see
* what your vestibular system (inner ear balance system) feels
* what proprioception (body position sense) feels

Classic example:

* you are reading in a car
* eyes say: "I am still"
* inner ear says: "I am moving/accelerating"
* brain gets conflicting motion signals
* nausea center gets activated

---

### Why nausea happens

The vestibular system sends motion/balance info to the brainstem.

The brainstem is near vomiting/autonomic centers, so mismatch can trigger:

* nausea
* vomiting
* sweating
* pallor
* dizziness

Autonomic means involuntary body responses, like sweating, heart rate changes, and gut activity.

---

### Key anatomy

The inner ear balance organs are:

* semicircular canals: sense rotational acceleration
* otolith organs: sense linear acceleration and gravity
  * utricle
  * saccule

So motion sickness is mainly a vestibular-visual conflict problem.

---

## One-line intuition

Motion sickness = your eyes and inner ear disagree about whether you are moving, so the brainstem responds with nausea/vomiting.

---

## Pearl

Scopolamine helps motion sickness because it blocks muscarinic acetylcholine receptors (parasympathetic nervous system receptors) in vestibular/vomiting pathways.

Also useful: first-generation H1 antihistamines (histamine-1 receptor blockers) like dimenhydrinate or meclizine.

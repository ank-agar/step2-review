### Core idea

Diabetes insipidus = problem with ADH (antidiuretic hormone), causing the kidney to lose too much free water.

ADH is also called vasopressin.

Normally ADH tells the collecting duct:

* insert aquaporin water channels
* reabsorb water
* concentrate urine

So if ADH is missing or not working:

* lots of dilute urine
* intense thirst
* risk of hypernatremia

Hypernatremia = high sodium in the blood.

---

### Why urine becomes dilute

ADH normally makes the collecting duct water-permeable.

Think:

> ADH opens the water doors.

Without ADH effect, water cannot be reabsorbed well from the collecting duct, so water stays in the urine.

Result:

* urine volume goes up
* urine osmolality goes down
* serum osmolality can go up

Osmolality = how concentrated a fluid is.

---

### Central vs nephrogenic

There are 2 major Step 1 types:

* central diabetes insipidus = not enough ADH made/released
* nephrogenic diabetes insipidus = kidney does not respond to ADH

Central = brain/pituitary problem.

Nephrogenic = kidney response problem.

---

### Central diabetes insipidus

Central diabetes insipidus happens when the hypothalamus/posterior pituitary pathway cannot release enough ADH.

Common causes:

* head trauma
* pituitary surgery
* tumors
* infiltrative disease, like Langerhans cell histiocytosis

Langerhans cell histiocytosis = abnormal proliferation of Langerhans cells, which can infiltrate tissues.

Treatment:

* desmopressin

Desmopressin = synthetic ADH analog.

---

### Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus

Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus means ADH is present, but the kidney ignores it.

Classic causes:

* lithium
* hypercalcemia
* hypokalemia
* inherited V2 receptor problem

V2 receptor = vasopressin receptor in the kidney collecting duct.

Why lithium causes it:

* lithium enters collecting duct cells
* disrupts ADH signaling
* reduces aquaporin response
* kidney cannot concentrate urine

Treatment idea:

* stop cause if possible
* thiazide diuretics
* low-salt diet
* sometimes amiloride, especially for lithium

---

### Desmopressin response

Desmopressin helps you separate central from nephrogenic diabetes insipidus.

After desmopressin:

* central diabetes insipidus: urine concentrates
* nephrogenic diabetes insipidus: little/no response

Why:

* central = missing ADH, so giving ADH fixes the signal
* nephrogenic = kidney cannot hear ADH, so giving more signal does not help much

---

### Diabetes insipidus vs diabetes mellitus

Both can cause polyuria.

Polyuria = excessive urination.

But the mechanism is different:

* diabetes insipidus: water diuresis, urine is very dilute
* diabetes mellitus: glucose osmotic diuresis, urine has glucose

Diabetes mellitus = high blood glucose causing glucose to spill into urine.

Insipidus means tasteless; mellitus means sweet.

---

## One-line intuition

Diabetes insipidus = ADH cannot make the kidney save water, so the patient pees out dilute water and gets very thirsty.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Central diabetes insipidus improves with desmopressin; nephrogenic diabetes insipidus does not respond well because the kidney is resistant to ADH.

### Core idea

Tarsal tunnel syndrome is compression of the tibial nerve as it passes through the tarsal tunnel.

The tarsal tunnel is a tight space on the medial ankle, meaning the inner ankle, behind the medial malleolus, meaning the bony bump on the inside of the ankle.

Think:

* carpal tunnel syndrome = median nerve compressed at wrist
* tarsal tunnel syndrome = tibial nerve compressed at ankle

---

### What gets compressed?

The tibial nerve runs under the flexor retinaculum, which is a band of connective tissue that holds ankle tendons and neurovascular structures in place.

Because the tunnel is narrow, swelling or a mass can squeeze the nerve.

Common causes:

* ankle trauma or sprain
* edema, meaning swelling
* ganglion cyst, meaning fluid-filled cyst near a joint/tendon
* varicose veins, meaning enlarged twisted veins
* flat feet
* arthritis

---

### Symptoms

Compression of the tibial nerve causes nerve-type symptoms in the sole of the foot:

* burning pain
* tingling
* numbness
* shooting pain into the heel, arch, or toes

Symptoms often worsen with standing or walking because pressure in the tunnel increases.

---

### Why the symptoms go to the bottom of the foot

The tibial nerve gives branches to the plantar foot, meaning the sole of the foot.

So if the nerve is squeezed at the ankle, the brain feels symptoms downstream in the heel/sole/toes.

This is like stepping on a hose:

* the blockage is at the ankle
* the symptoms show up in the foot region supplied by that nerve

---

### Exam clue

Tinel sign means tingling after tapping over an irritated nerve.

In tarsal tunnel syndrome, tapping behind the medial malleolus can reproduce tingling or pain into the sole.

---

## One-line intuition

Tarsal tunnel syndrome is basically "carpal tunnel of the ankle": the tibial nerve gets squeezed under the flexor retinaculum, causing burning/tingling pain in the plantar foot.

---

# Neonatal conjunctivitis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/Conjunctiva_anterior_view.svg/1280px-Conjunctiva_anterior_view.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/27/Gonococcal_ophthalmia_neonatorum.jpg/960px-Gonococcal_ophthalmia_neonatorum.jpg)

Neonatal conjunctivitis, also called ophthalmia neonatorum, is conjunctivitis (inflammation of the conjunctiva, the thin membrane covering the white of the eye and inner eyelid) in a newborn.

Core idea:

* baby passes through birth canal
* gets exposed to maternal organisms
* eye surface gets infected/inflamed
* presents with red eyes, eyelid swelling, tearing, and discharge

---

### Big causes by timing

**Chemical conjunctivitis**

* first 24 hours
* from eye prophylaxis irritation
* usually mild/self-limited

**Neisseria gonorrhoeae**

* usually 2-5 days after birth
* very purulent discharge
* dangerous because it can invade the cornea (clear front surface of the eye)
* can cause corneal ulceration/perforation/blindness

**Chlamydia trachomatis**

* usually 5-14 days after birth
* watery to mucopurulent discharge
* can also cause neonatal pneumonia

---

### Why gonorrhea is scarier

Neisseria gonorrhoeae can penetrate intact corneal epithelium (outer surface layer of the cornea).

So it is not just "pink eye."

It can rapidly damage the cornea, which is why gonococcal neonatal conjunctivitis is treated urgently.

---

### Treatment idea

* Gonorrhea: systemic ceftriaxone/cefotaxime, not just eye drops
* Chlamydia: oral macrolide, like erythromycin or azithromycin
* Prevention: erythromycin eye ointment at birth helps prevent gonococcal ophthalmia neonatorum

Important: topical erythromycin prophylaxis does **not** reliably prevent chlamydial conjunctivitis, so maternal screening/treatment matters.

---

## One-line intuition

Neonatal conjunctivitis is a newborn eye infection/inflammation, classically from organisms picked up during delivery; timing tells you the cause.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember:

* 0-1 day = chemical
* 2-5 days = gonorrhea
* 5-14 days = chlamydia

And the scary one is gonorrhea because it can rapidly destroy the cornea.

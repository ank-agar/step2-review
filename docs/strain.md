## Strain

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Calf_muscle_strain_and_tear.svg/1280px-Calf_muscle_strain_and_tear.svg.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19618.jpg)

A **strain** is an injury where a **muscle** or **tendon** is overstretched or torn.

**Tendon** = connective tissue that attaches muscle to bone.

So the key distinction is:

* **strain** = muscle/tendon injury
* **sprain** = ligament injury

**Ligament** = connective tissue that attaches bone to bone.

---

### Core idea

A strain is basically a “pulled muscle.”

The muscle fibers get stretched past their normal limit, and some fibers may tear.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Pain:** torn or overstretched muscle fibers irritate nerve endings
* **Swelling:** local inflammation brings fluid into the injured area
* **Bruising:** small blood vessels can tear and leak blood
* **Weakness:** damaged fibers cannot contract normally
* **Spasm/tightness:** the muscle reflexively contracts to protect the injured area

---

### Severity grades

* **Grade 1:** mild stretch or tiny tears; strength mostly preserved
* **Grade 2:** partial tear; more pain, swelling, bruising, and weakness
* **Grade 3:** complete tear; major weakness or inability to use the muscle

---

## One-line intuition

A strain is when the muscle-tendon unit is pulled too hard, so the fibers stretch, fray, or tear.

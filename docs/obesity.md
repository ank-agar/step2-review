### Core idea

Obesity = excess body fat that increases health risk.

Clinically, it is often screened with **BMI (body mass index)**:

* BMI 25-29.9 = overweight
* BMI 30 or higher = obesity
* BMI 40 or higher = severe obesity

BMI = weight adjusted for height.

---

### Why it causes problems

Fat tissue is not just passive storage.

Adipose tissue (fat tissue) acts like an endocrine organ, meaning it releases signaling molecules into the blood.

With excess adipose tissue:

* enlarged adipocytes (fat cells) become stressed
* macrophages (inflammatory immune cells) enter fat tissue
* inflammatory cytokines (immune signaling proteins) increase, especially TNF-alpha (tumor necrosis factor-alpha) and IL-6 (interleukin-6)
* adiponectin decreases

Adiponectin = a hormone from fat cells that normally improves insulin sensitivity.

So decreased adiponectin + increased inflammation leads to:

* insulin resistance
* type 2 diabetes mellitus
* dyslipidemia (abnormal blood lipids)
* hypertension (high blood pressure)
* fatty liver disease
* atherosclerosis (plaque buildup in arteries)

---

### Why insulin resistance happens

Insulin normally tells cells:

> take glucose out of the blood.

In obesity, excess fatty acids and inflammation interfere with insulin signaling.

So insulin is present, but tissues respond poorly.

Result:

* glucose stays in blood
* pancreas makes more insulin
* over time, beta cells (insulin-producing pancreatic cells) can burn out
* type 2 diabetes develops

---

### One-line intuition

Obesity is not just "extra weight"; it is metabolically active fat tissue creating chronic inflammation and insulin resistance.

---

## Pearl

Obesity increases estrogen because adipose tissue has aromatase (enzyme that converts androgens to estrogens), so it increases risk of endometrial hyperplasia and endometrial carcinoma.

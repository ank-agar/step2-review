# Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/24348.jpg)

![Image](https://www.gastrointestinalatlas.com/imagenes/CaEscamous1.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Esophageal_squamous_cell_carcinoma_-_a1_--_high_mag.jpg/1280px-Esophageal_squamous_cell_carcinoma_-_a1_--_high_mag.jpg)

Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma is a malignant tumor of the squamous cells lining the esophagus.

Break it down:

* esophageal = in the esophagus
* squamous cell = flat epithelial lining cell
* carcinoma = cancer from epithelial cells

So:

> cancer arising from the flat squamous lining of the esophagus.

---

### Core idea

The esophagus is normally lined by non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium.

That lining gets chronically irritated, then can progress:

* squamous dysplasia
* carcinoma in situ
* invasive squamous cell carcinoma

Carcinoma in situ means malignant cells are still above the basement membrane.

Invasive means the cancer has broken through the basement membrane into deeper tissue.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Progressive dysphagia:** tumor narrows the esophageal lumen, so solids get stuck first, then liquids later
* **Weight loss:** eating becomes difficult and cancer increases metabolic demand
* **Odynophagia:** painful swallowing from ulceration/invasion
* **Hoarseness:** possible recurrent laryngeal nerve involvement

Dysphagia means difficulty swallowing.
Odynophagia means painful swallowing.

---

### Risk factors

Classically associated with:

* smoking
* alcohol
* achalasia
* caustic injury
* very hot liquids
* Plummer-Vinson syndrome

Achalasia means impaired relaxation of the lower esophageal sphincter.
Plummer-Vinson syndrome means iron-deficiency anemia plus esophageal webs.

---

### Location clue

Squamous cell carcinoma usually occurs in the:

* upper third
* middle third

This is different from esophageal adenocarcinoma, which usually occurs in the lower third near the gastroesophageal junction.

Gastroesophageal junction means where the esophagus meets the stomach.

---

### Histology clue

Because it is a squamous cancer, histology may show:

* keratin pearls
* intercellular bridges

Keratin pearls are whorled pink keratin deposits made by squamous tumor cells.
Intercellular bridges are visible connections between squamous cells.

---

## One-line intuition

Chronic irritation of the esophageal squamous lining can make it turn malignant and grow inward, narrowing the tube and causing progressive dysphagia.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Step 1 loves this contrast:

* **Squamous cell carcinoma:** smoking/alcohol, upper or middle esophagus
* **Adenocarcinoma:** Barrett esophagus from GERD, lower esophagus

GERD means gastroesophageal reflux disease.

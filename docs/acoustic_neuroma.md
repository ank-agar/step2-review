Acoustic neuroma = vestibular schwannoma.

It is a benign tumor of Schwann cells (cells that make myelin around peripheral nerves) on CN VIII (cranial nerve 8, the vestibulocochlear nerve).

Even though it is called acoustic, it usually arises from the vestibular part of CN VIII, which is the balance part.

---

### Core idea

CN VIII carries:

* hearing information from the cochlea
* balance information from the vestibular system

So a tumor growing on CN VIII causes:

* unilateral sensorineural hearing loss
* tinnitus (ringing in the ear)
* dizziness/imbalance

Sensorineural hearing loss means the problem is in the inner ear, cochlear nerve, or brain pathway, not the external/middle ear.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The tumor grows near the internal auditory canal and cerebellopontine angle (space between the cerebellum and pons).

As it enlarges, it compresses nearby structures:

* **CN VIII compression:** hearing loss, tinnitus, vertigo/imbalance
* **CN VII compression:** facial weakness, because CN VII (facial nerve) runs nearby
* **CN V compression:** facial numbness, because CN V (trigeminal nerve) carries facial sensation
* **Brainstem/cerebellum compression:** gait problems or hydrocephalus (too much cerebrospinal fluid in the brain ventricles)

So the pattern is basically:

> one-sided ear symptoms first, then nearby cranial nerve/brainstem symptoms if it gets big.

---

### Important association

Bilateral acoustic neuromas are highly associated with NF2 (neurofibromatosis type 2).

NF2 is a tumor suppressor gene disorder classically causing:

* bilateral vestibular schwannomas
* meningiomas
* ependymomas

---

## One-line intuition

An acoustic neuroma is a slow-growing benign Schwann cell tumor on CN VIII, so it causes one-sided hearing/balance problems by compressing the hearing/balance nerve.

---

## Pearl 🔥

USMLE loves:

> bilateral vestibular schwannomas = NF2.

NF1 (neurofibromatosis type 1) is the one with café-au-lait spots and neurofibromas; NF2 is the one with bilateral acoustic neuromas.

## Coxiella burnetii

![Image](https://www.frontiersin.org/files/Articles/558481/fvets-07-558481-HTML/image_m/fvets-07-558481-g001.jpg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/PHIL_Images/18158/18158_lores.jpg)

Coxiella burnetii is a small gram-negative intracellular bacterium that causes **Q fever**.

Intracellular = it lives inside host cells.

Q fever classically comes from inhaling aerosols from infected animals, especially:

* sheep
* goats
* cattle

The highest-yield exposure clue is:

* farm animal exposure
* parturition products, meaning birth-related material like placenta/amniotic fluid
* unpasteurized milk exposure sometimes

---

### Core idea

Think:

> Coxiella = sheep/goats/cattle + pneumonia or hepatitis.

It is usually transmitted by inhalation, not by a tick bite.

That matters because Coxiella used to be grouped with rickettsial organisms, but for Step 1, the practical clue is aerosolized animal products.

---

### What it causes

Acute Q fever can cause:

* fever
* headache
* atypical pneumonia
* hepatitis, meaning liver inflammation

Chronic Q fever can cause:

* culture-negative endocarditis

Endocarditis = infection of the heart valves.

Culture-negative = blood cultures may not grow the organism even though infection is present.

---

### Why it causes pneumonia/hepatitis

You inhale Coxiella into the lungs.

Then macrophages, which are immune cells that eat microbes, take it up.

But Coxiella can survive inside the phagolysosome, which is the acidic killing compartment inside macrophages.

So instead of being destroyed, it hides and replicates inside cells, causing systemic inflammation.

That is why it can show up as:

* lung inflammation → atypical pneumonia
* liver inflammation → hepatitis

---

### Diagnosis clue

Diagnosis is usually by serology, meaning blood antibody testing.

Important antibody pattern:

* acute Q fever → phase II antibodies
* chronic Q fever → phase I antibodies

Do not overthink the phase names. Just memorize:

> II = acute, I = chronic.

---

### Treatment

Treatment is usually:

* doxycycline

Doxycycline is a tetracycline antibiotic commonly used for intracellular bacteria.

---

## One-line intuition

Coxiella burnetii is an intracellular zoonotic bacterium from livestock aerosols that causes Q fever: atypical pneumonia, hepatitis, and sometimes chronic culture-negative endocarditis.

---

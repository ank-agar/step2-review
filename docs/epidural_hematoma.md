### Core idea

Epidural hematoma means bleeding between:

* skull
* dura mater (tough outer meningeal layer covering the brain)

Break the word down:

* epi- = on top of
* dural = dura mater
* hematoma = collection of blood

So it is literally:

> blood sitting on top of the dura, under the skull.

---

### Why it happens

Classically, trauma fractures the temporal bone and tears the middle meningeal artery.

Because this is usually arterial bleeding, blood accumulates fast.

That rapid expansion increases ICP (intracranial pressure), which can compress the brain.

---

### Classic presentation

Step 1 classic story:

* head trauma
* brief loss of consciousness
* lucid interval (patient wakes up and seems okay)
* then rapid neurologic decline

Why?

At first, the bleed may be small enough that the patient looks okay.

But arterial blood keeps pumping into the epidural space, pressure rises, and the brain starts getting compressed.

---

### CT finding

On CT (computed tomography), epidural hematoma is classically:

* lens-shaped
* biconvex (bulging outward on both sides)
* does not cross suture lines

Why does it not cross suture lines?

The dura mater is tightly attached to the skull at the sutures, so the blood gets trapped in a localized pocket.

That is why it forms a lens shape instead of spreading widely.

---

### Epidural vs subdural

High-yield contrast:

| Feature | Epidural hematoma | Subdural hematoma |
|---|---|---|
| Vessel | Middle meningeal artery | Bridging veins |
| Speed | Fast | Slower |
| CT shape | Lens-shaped/biconvex | Crescent-shaped |
| Crosses sutures? | No | Yes |

Subdural hematoma is under the dura, so it can spread more along the brain surface.

---

## One-line intuition

Epidural hematoma is a fast arterial bleed between skull and dura that gets trapped by suture lines, making a lens-shaped bleed that can quickly compress the brain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Epidural hematoma can cause uncal herniation (medial temporal lobe gets pushed downward), compressing CN III (cranial nerve 3, the oculomotor nerve), leading to an ipsilateral (same-side) blown pupil.

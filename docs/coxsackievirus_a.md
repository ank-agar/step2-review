### Core idea

Coxsackievirus A is a **picornavirus**:

* **Pico** = small
* **RNA** = ribonucleic acid
* **Virus** = infectious particle

So it is a **small, non-enveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus**.

Positive-sense RNA means the viral RNA can act like **mRNA (messenger RNA)** right away, so host ribosomes can directly translate it into viral proteins.

---

### What it causes

Coxsackievirus A classically causes:

* **HFMD (hand, foot, and mouth disease)**
* **herpangina** = painful vesicles/ulcers in the posterior mouth and throat
* sometimes **aseptic meningitis** = meningitis with no bacterial growth on routine culture

HFMD gives:

* fever
* painful mouth sores
* vesicular rash on hands and feet

Vesicular means blister-like.

---

### Why the findings happen

The virus infects mucosal and skin epithelial cells.

That is why lesions show up in:

* mouth
* hands
* feet

The infected epithelial cells get damaged, fluid collects, and you see vesicles/blisters.

Mouth lesions hurt, so kids may refuse food or fluids and can get dehydrated.

---

### How it spreads

Usually fecal-oral spread.

Why?

Coxsackievirus A is **non-enveloped**.

Non-enveloped viruses are tougher, so they survive:

* stomach acid
* drying on surfaces
* GI (gastrointestinal) tract conditions

So they spread well through stool, saliva, droplets, and contaminated surfaces.

---

## One-line intuition

Coxsackievirus A is a tough little enterovirus that likes skin/mucosa, causing painful mouth ulcers plus hand/foot vesicles.

### Core idea

Cryptosporidium is a protozoan parasite (single-celled eukaryotic parasite) that causes watery diarrhea.

It is classically spread by:

* contaminated water
* swimming pools
* daycare outbreaks
* animal exposure, especially calves

The infectious form is the oocyst (hardy egg-like parasite form).

---

### What it does

Cryptosporidium infects the small intestine and damages the brush border (microvilli surface where absorption happens).

So the main problem is:

* less absorption
* more intestinal secretion
* watery, non-bloody diarrhea

Think:

> parasite sitting on the intestinal lining -> irritated absorptive surface -> lots of watery stool.

---

### Why immunocompromised patients get severe disease

In healthy people, infection is usually self-limited.

But in AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome), especially low CD4 cells (helper T cells), the immune system cannot control the parasite well.

So Cryptosporidium can cause:

* chronic watery diarrhea
* malabsorption
* weight loss
* biliary disease sometimes

CD4 T cells are important because they coordinate cell-mediated immunity (immune defense against intracellular organisms and many parasites).

---

### Diagnosis

Classic diagnosis is stool testing showing acid-fast oocysts.

Acid-fast means the organism keeps a red/pink stain even after acid wash because its wall resists decolorization.

High-yield tests:

* stool acid-fast stain
* stool antigen test
* polymerase chain reaction (PCR; DNA amplification test)

---

### Treatment

In immunocompetent patients:

* usually supportive care
* nitazoxanide can be used

In AIDS:

* antiretroviral therapy (ART; HIV treatment that restores immune function) is key

Why?

Because if the immune system stays weak, killing the parasite temporarily may not fully fix the chronic infection.

---

## One-line intuition

Cryptosporidium is a waterborne, acid-fast protozoan that infects the small intestine and causes watery diarrhea, especially severe and chronic in AIDS.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Cryptosporidium oocysts are chlorine-resistant, which is why outbreaks can happen from swimming pools even when the pool is chlorinated.

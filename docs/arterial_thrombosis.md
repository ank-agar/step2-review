# Arterial thrombosis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c5/Blood_clot_diagram.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4f/Arterial_thrombosis_causing_cyanosis.jpg)

### Core idea

Arterial thrombosis means a thrombus (blood clot formed inside a vessel) forms in an artery and blocks blood flow.

Arteries carry oxygen-rich blood to tissues, so blockage causes:

* ischemia (low oxygen delivery)
* infarction (tissue death from prolonged ischemia)

---

### Why it happens

Most arterial thrombi form when an atherosclerotic plaque (cholesterol-rich buildup in the artery wall) ruptures.

That exposes thrombogenic material (stuff that strongly triggers clotting), so:

* platelets stick
* platelets activate
* fibrin (clot-stabilizing protein) forms
* the artery can suddenly occlude

This is why arterial thrombosis is usually sudden and dangerous.

---

### What it can cause

Depends on which artery is blocked:

* coronary artery -> MI (myocardial infarction, heart attack)
* cerebral artery -> ischemic stroke
* limb artery -> acute limb ischemia

The key pattern is:

> artery blocked = tissue downstream loses oxygen.

---

### Arterial vs venous thrombosis

Arterial thrombi are usually platelet-rich because arteries have high-pressure, fast blood flow.

Venous thrombi are usually RBC-rich (red blood cell-rich) because veins have slower flow and more stasis (blood pooling).

So for treatment intuition:

* arterial clots -> antiplatelets are big
* venous clots -> anticoagulants are big

---

## One-line intuition

Arterial thrombosis is a sudden platelet-rich clot in an artery that cuts off oxygen delivery to whatever tissue that artery feeds.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Arterial thrombi are classically “white thrombi” because they are platelet-rich; venous thrombi are classically “red thrombi” because they trap more RBCs (red blood cells).

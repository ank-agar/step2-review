### Core idea

Community-acquired pneumonia, or CAP (community-acquired pneumonia), means:

* pneumonia (infection/inflammation of lung alveoli, the tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens)
* acquired outside the hospital or healthcare setting

So basically:

> lung air spaces fill with pus/fluid from an infection you got in the community.

---

### What is happening in the lung

Normally, alveoli (tiny air sacs) are open and filled with air.

In pneumonia:

* bacteria/virus enters alveoli
* immune cells rush in
* fluid + pus fills the air spaces
* that part of lung becomes consolidated

Consolidation means the lung tissue becomes more solid because air is replaced by inflammatory fluid.

This is why patients get:

* cough
* fever
* pleuritic chest pain (sharp pain worse with breathing)
* shortness of breath
* crackles on lung exam
* opacity/infiltrate on CXR (chest X-ray)

---

### Most common cause

The classic most common bacterial cause is:

* Streptococcus pneumoniae

Think:

* gram-positive lancet-shaped diplococci
* alpha-hemolytic
* optochin-sensitive
* bile soluble
* encapsulated

Encapsulated means it has a polysaccharide capsule that helps it avoid phagocytosis (immune-cell eating).

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Fever:** cytokines (immune signaling proteins) raise the body temperature set point
* **Productive cough:** alveoli/airways are filled with inflammatory fluid
* **Dyspnea (shortness of breath):** less air can reach infected alveoli
* **Pleuritic chest pain:** inflammation irritates pleura (lining around lungs)
* **Lobar infiltrate:** one lung lobe can fill with exudate (protein-rich inflammatory fluid)

---

## One-line intuition

CAP is an infection from outside the hospital that turns normally air-filled alveoli into pus/fluid-filled alveoli, so oxygen exchange drops.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Streptococcus pneumoniae classically causes lobar pneumonia and is especially associated with rusty sputum due to red blood cell breakdown in the inflamed alveoli.

### Core idea

Fetal growth restriction, or **FGR** (fetal growth restriction), means the fetus is not reaching its expected growth potential in the uterus.

It is often defined as:

* estimated fetal weight below the 10th percentile for gestational age
* or abdominal circumference below the 10th percentile

Gestational age means how many weeks pregnant the patient is.

---

### Big intuition

The fetus is small because something is limiting delivery of:

* oxygen
* nutrients
* blood flow

Most commonly, the issue is **placental insufficiency** (placenta cannot deliver enough blood/oxygen/nutrients to the fetus).

So think:

> bad placental supply -> fetus goes into energy-saving mode -> poor growth

---

### Why the findings happen

If the placenta is not delivering enough oxygen and nutrients, the fetus prioritizes vital organs:

* brain
* heart
* adrenal glands

This causes **brain-sparing**:

* head may be relatively preserved
* abdomen gets small first

Why abdomen?

The fetal liver stores glycogen and helps build body mass. If nutrients are low, the liver gets less growth signal, so **abdominal circumference** drops.

---

### Common causes

Maternal causes:

* hypertension (high blood pressure)
* preeclampsia (hypertension with end-organ dysfunction in pregnancy)
* smoking
* malnutrition
* vascular disease

Placental/cord causes:

* placental insufficiency
* abnormal umbilical cord insertion
* poor spiral artery remodeling

Fetal causes:

* chromosomal abnormalities
* congenital infections
* congenital anomalies

---

### What doctors check

Ultrasound estimates fetal size using measurements like:

* HC (head circumference)
* AC (abdominal circumference)
* FL (femur length)
* EFW (estimated fetal weight)

Doppler ultrasound checks blood flow, especially in the umbilical artery.

If umbilical artery resistance is high, that suggests the placenta is hard to push blood through.

---

## One-line intuition

FGR is when the fetus is too small because it is not getting enough growth support, most often from poor placental blood flow.

---

## HY pearl

Asymmetric FGR is classically due to placental insufficiency later in pregnancy: the head is relatively spared, but the abdomen is small.

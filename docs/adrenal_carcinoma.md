Adrenal carcinoma usually means **adrenocortical carcinoma** (**ACC**, cancer of the adrenal cortex).

Break it down:

* adrenal = adrenal gland, which sits on top of the kidney
* cortical = cortex, the outer hormone-producing layer
* carcinoma = malignant epithelial cancer

So:

> malignant cancer arising from the adrenal cortex.

---

### Core idea

The adrenal cortex makes steroid hormones, so adrenal carcinoma can cause symptoms by:

* growing as an aggressive mass
* invading nearby structures
* overproducing hormones

The big Step 1 association is **hormone excess**.

---

### What hormones can it make?

The adrenal cortex normally makes:

* **cortisol** = stress/glucose hormone
* **aldosterone** = salt-retaining hormone
* **androgens** = sex hormone precursors

If the tumor secretes hormones, you can see:

* **Cushing syndrome** from too much cortisol
* **hypertension** and **hypokalemia** from too much aldosterone
* **virilization** from excess androgens

Virilization means development of male-pattern features, like hirsutism (excess coarse hair), deep voice, or clitoromegaly.

---

### Why the findings happen

* Too much cortisol increases gluconeogenesis (making glucose), breaks down protein, and redistributes fat
  * causes hyperglycemia, muscle wasting, central obesity, moon facies, and purple striae
* Too much aldosterone makes the kidney retain sodium and water while wasting potassium
  * causes hypertension and hypokalemia
* Too many androgens create male-pattern hormone effects
  * especially noticeable in females or children

---

### One-line intuition

Adrenal carcinoma is a malignant adrenal cortex tumor, and because the cortex makes steroid hormones, the tumor may present as a cancer plus a hormone-excess syndrome.

---

## Pearl

Adrenocortical carcinoma is classically associated with **Li-Fraumeni syndrome** due to **TP53** mutation.

TP53 is a tumor suppressor gene, meaning it normally helps stop damaged cells from becoming cancer.

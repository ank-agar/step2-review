### Core idea

Niacin deficiency = low vitamin B3.

Severe niacin deficiency causes **pellagra**.

Classic findings are the **4 D’s**:

* dermatitis
* diarrhea
* dementia
* death, if untreated

---

### Why this happens

Niacin is needed to make:

* **NAD+** = nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide
* **NADP+** = nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate

These are electron-carrying cofactors used in metabolism.

So if niacin is low, cells cannot make enough energy or handle oxidative/metabolic stress well.

The tissues that get hit hardest are tissues with high turnover or high energy needs:

* skin → dermatitis
* gut → diarrhea
* brain → dementia/confusion

---

### The rash

The dermatitis is usually:

* photosensitive
* symmetric
* hyperpigmented
* rough/scaly
* on sun-exposed areas

Classic Step 1 clues:

* **Casal necklace** = hyperpigmented rash around the neck
* **pellagrous glove** = rash on the hands
* **pellagrous boot** = rash on the feet/legs

---

### Causes to remember

Niacin can come directly from diet, but the body can also make it from **tryptophan** (an amino acid).

So niacin deficiency can happen from:

* poor diet/malnutrition
* alcoholism
* malabsorption
* isoniazid use
* Hartnup disease
* carcinoid syndrome

Why carcinoid syndrome?

Carcinoid tumors use up tryptophan to make serotonin, so less tryptophan is available to make niacin.

Less tryptophan → less niacin → pellagra.

---

## One-line intuition

Niacin deficiency means not enough vitamin B3 to make NAD+ and NADP+, so high-energy tissues like skin, gut, and brain fail first.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Carcinoid syndrome can cause pellagra because tryptophan gets diverted into serotonin production instead of niacin production.

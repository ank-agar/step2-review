## Body dysmorphic disorder

Body dysmorphic disorder, or BDD (body dysmorphic disorder), is an obsessive focus on a perceived flaw in appearance that is either:

* not actually visible to others
* or very minor to others

But to the patient, it feels huge and embarrassing.

Common concerns:

* skin
* hair
* nose
* face shape
* muscle size
* body symmetry

---

### Core idea

BDD is basically an OCD-like loop focused on appearance.

The loop:

* perceived flaw
* anxiety/shame
* checking, comparing, grooming, camouflaging, reassurance-seeking
* temporary relief
* the brain learns, "I need to keep checking"
* obsession gets stronger

So the behavior feels helpful short-term, but it reinforces the disorder long-term.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The brain over-focuses on one detail instead of seeing the whole person.

Example:

* normal person sees: "my face"
* BDD brain sees: "my nose is wrong"

That selective attention makes the flaw feel bigger and more important than it really is.

Then compulsive behaviors happen:

* mirror checking
* avoiding mirrors
* taking repeated selfies
* comparing to others
* excessive grooming
* skin picking
* asking, "Do I look okay?"
* seeking cosmetic procedures

These lower anxiety briefly, but they do not fix the obsession.

---

### Important distinction

BDD is not just low self-esteem or vanity.

It causes real distress and impairment, like:

* avoiding school/work/social situations
* spending hours checking appearance
* depression or suicidality
* repeated cosmetic procedures despite minimal objective defect

Also:

* If the concern is mainly weight/fatness, think eating disorder first.
* If the concern is a specific "defect" like nose, skin, hair, or muscle size, think BDD.

---

### Treatment intuition

First-line treatment:

* CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy), especially exposure and response prevention
* SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors)

ERP (exposure and response prevention) means the patient practices facing the trigger without doing the ritual.

Example:

* look in mirror briefly
* do not zoom in on the "flaw"
* do not ask for reassurance
* let anxiety fall naturally

This teaches the brain:

> "I can tolerate the appearance fear without checking or fixing."

Cosmetic surgery usually does not help, because the problem is the distorted preoccupation, not the body part itself.

---

## One-line intuition

BDD is an OCD-spectrum disorder where the brain locks onto a perceived appearance flaw, and checking/fixing rituals keep the fear alive.

---

## HY pearl

BDD is classified under obsessive-compulsive and related disorders, and insight can vary: some patients know the belief may be exaggerated, while others are convinced the flaw is real.

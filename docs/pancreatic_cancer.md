## Pancreatic cancer

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ac/2424_Exocrine_and_Endocrine_Pancreas.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/1192.jpg)

### Core idea

Pancreatic cancer is a malignant tumor (cancer that can invade and spread) arising in the pancreas.

Most cases are:

* pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC; cancer from the duct-forming glandular epithelial cells of the pancreas)

Why ductal?

Because the exocrine pancreas (enzyme-secreting part of the pancreas) drains digestive enzymes through ducts, and those duct cells are the usual cells that become malignant.

---

### Why the findings happen

Classically, cancer in the head of the pancreas causes:

* painless jaundice
* dark urine
* pale stools
* weight loss
* Courvoisier sign (palpable, nontender gallbladder with jaundice)

Why?

The head of the pancreas sits right next to the common bile duct (tube that drains bile from liver/gallbladder into the intestine).

So a tumor there can compress the common bile duct:

* bile cannot reach the intestine
* conjugated bilirubin backs up into blood
* stool becomes pale because less bile pigment enters the gut
* urine becomes dark because conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble and gets peed out

---

### Why it presents late

The pancreas is deep in the abdomen, so early tumors may not cause obvious symptoms.

Body/tail tumors especially may grow silently until they cause:

* abdominal/back pain
* weight loss
* metastasis (spread to distant sites)

Back pain happens because the pancreas is retroperitoneal (behind the peritoneal lining of the abdominal cavity), so tumor invasion can irritate nearby nerves.

---

### Important associations

Risk factors include:

* smoking
* chronic pancreatitis (long-term inflammation of the pancreas)
* older age
* diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar due to insulin problems)
* hereditary pancreatitis
* BRCA2 mutation (DNA repair gene mutation also associated with breast/ovarian cancer)

Tumor marker:

* CA 19-9 (carbohydrate antigen 19-9) can be elevated, but it is not good as a screening test.

---

## One-line intuition

Pancreatic cancer is usually duct-cell adenocarcinoma that grows silently until it blocks the bile duct, invades nerves, or spreads.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1: Pancreatic cancer can cause migratory thrombophlebitis, also called Trousseau syndrome, because adenocarcinomas can make the blood hypercoagulable (more likely to clot).

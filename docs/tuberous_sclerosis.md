### Core idea

Tuberous sclerosis is an autosomal dominant disorder where benign hamartomas (disorganized overgrowths of normal tissue) form in multiple organs.

Most important organs:

* brain
* skin
* kidneys
* heart
* lungs
* eyes

---

### Why it happens

Usually due to mutation in:

* **TSC1** (tuberous sclerosis complex 1 gene) on chromosome 9
* **TSC2** (tuberous sclerosis complex 2 gene) on chromosome 16

These genes normally suppress **mTOR** (mechanistic target of rapamycin), which is a growth-signaling pathway.

So if TSC1 or TSC2 is broken:

* less braking on mTOR
* too much cell growth signaling
* hamartomas form in different tissues

Intuition:

> TSC genes are like tumor-suppressor brakes on growth. Lose the brakes → benign overgrowths everywhere.

---

### Classic findings

Brain:

* cortical tubers (abnormal brain cortex growths)
* seizures
* developmental delay/intellectual disability
* subependymal nodules (nodules near the ventricles)

Skin:

* facial angiofibromas (benign vascular/fibrous bumps on the face)
* hypomelanotic macules, also called ash-leaf spots (light-colored skin patches)
* shagreen patch (thick, leathery skin plaque, often lower back)
* periungual fibromas (fibrous growths near nails)

Kidney:

* angiomyolipomas (benign tumors made of blood vessels, smooth muscle, and fat)

Heart:

* rhabdomyomas (benign cardiac muscle tumors), especially in infants

Lung:

* lymphangioleiomyomatosis (abnormal smooth muscle growth in lungs), especially in women

---

### Why seizures happen

The cortical tubers disrupt normal brain architecture.

That abnormal cortex becomes electrically irritable, so patients can get seizures.

---

## One-line intuition

Tuberous sclerosis = broken TSC1/TSC2 growth brakes → overactive mTOR → benign hamartomas in brain, skin, kidney, heart, lung, and eye.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, connect tuberous sclerosis with:

* **ash-leaf spots**
* **facial angiofibromas**
* **seizures**
* **renal angiomyolipomas**
* **cardiac rhabdomyomas**

And remember: **TSC1/TSC2 inhibit mTOR**, so mTOR inhibitors like sirolimus/everolimus can help treat some TSC-related growths.

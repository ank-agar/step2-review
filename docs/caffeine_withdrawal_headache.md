# Caffeine withdrawal headache

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Caffeine_and_adenosine.png/960px-Caffeine_and_adenosine.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Circle_of_Willis_en.svg/960px-Circle_of_Willis_en.svg.png)

### Core idea

A caffeine withdrawal headache happens when someone who regularly uses caffeine suddenly stops or cuts down.

Caffeine normally blocks adenosine receptors.

* adenosine = a molecule that promotes sleepiness and blood vessel dilation
* receptor = a cell surface target that a molecule binds to

So caffeine is basically an adenosine blocker.

---

### Why the headache happens

When you drink caffeine regularly, your brain adapts by becoming more sensitive to adenosine.

Then if caffeine suddenly disappears:

* adenosine activity increases
* cerebral blood vessels dilate
* brain blood flow increases
* pain-sensitive structures around the vessels get stretched/activated

That vascular change is a major reason the headache feels throbbing or pressure-like.

---

### Symptoms

Common withdrawal symptoms:

* headache
* fatigue
* sleepiness
* irritability
* poor concentration
* nausea sometimes

The headache usually starts within about 12-24 hours after stopping caffeine and improves if caffeine is taken again.

---

## One-line intuition

Caffeine keeps adenosine "quiet"; when caffeine is removed, adenosine rebounds and dilates brain vessels, causing headache.

---

## Pearl

Caffeine is an adenosine receptor antagonist, and it is included in some headache medications because adenosine blockade can help constrict dilated cerebral vessels.

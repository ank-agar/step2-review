## Arnold-Chiari malformation

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Sagittal_MRI_scan_of_brain_of_patient_with_Chiari_malformation.jpg/960px-Sagittal_MRI_scan_of_brain_of_patient_with_Chiari_malformation.jpg)

Arnold-Chiari malformation is a congenital hindbrain malformation where part of the cerebellum gets pulled/pushed downward through the foramen magnum.

Definitions:

* congenital = present from birth
* hindbrain = back part of the brain
* cerebellum = brain region for coordination/balance
* foramen magnum = big hole at the bottom of the skull where the spinal cord exits

---

### Core idea

The back of the skull is too crowded, so the cerebellum herniates downward.

Herniates = gets displaced through an opening.

So think:

> too little room in posterior fossa -> cerebellum moves down -> brainstem/CSF flow can get compressed

Posterior fossa = back-lower skull compartment that holds the cerebellum and brainstem.

CSF = cerebrospinal fluid, the fluid around the brain and spinal cord.

---

### Why symptoms happen

If the cerebellum/brainstem gets compressed:

* poor coordination/ataxia
* dizziness
* trouble swallowing
* abnormal breathing
* headache/neck pain

Ataxia = uncoordinated movement.

If CSF flow is blocked:

* hydrocephalus
* syringomyelia

Hydrocephalus = enlarged brain ventricles from too much CSF.

Syringomyelia = fluid-filled cavity inside the spinal cord.

---

### Step 1 association

The classic board association is:

* Arnold-Chiari type II
* lumbar myelomeningocele
* hydrocephalus

Myelomeningocele = spina bifida cystica where meninges and spinal cord protrude through a vertebral defect.

Meninges = coverings of the brain/spinal cord.

---

## One-line intuition

Arnold-Chiari is basically the cerebellum being crowded downward through the skull hole, which can squeeze the brainstem and block CSF flow.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Arnold-Chiari type II is strongly associated with myelomeningocele and hydrocephalus.

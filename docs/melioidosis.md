### Core idea

Melioidosis is an infection caused by **Burkholderia pseudomallei**.

Think:

* soil/water exposure
* tropical regions
* pneumonia
* abscesses
* sepsis

It is sometimes called **Whitmore disease**.

---

### Organism

**Burkholderia pseudomallei** is a:

* gram-negative rod
* oxidase-positive bacterium
* found in soil and freshwater
* intracellular pathogen, meaning it can survive inside host cells

It can show **bipolar staining**, meaning the two ends stain darker, giving a "safety pin" appearance.

---

### How people get it

Usually from contaminated soil or water through:

* skin inoculation, meaning bacteria enter through cuts or abrasions
* inhalation, meaning breathing it in
* ingestion, meaning swallowing contaminated water

This is why it is classically associated with:

* farmers
* rainy seasons
* Southeast Asia
* northern Australia

---

### What it causes

Melioidosis can look like many different infections, but the big Step 1 pattern is:

* pneumonia
* multiple abscesses
* septicemia, meaning bacteria in the blood causing systemic illness

Abscesses can occur in:

* liver
* spleen
* prostate
* skin/subcutaneous tissue
* brain, less commonly

---

### Why abscesses happen

Burkholderia pseudomallei can survive inside phagocytes, meaning immune cells that normally eat bacteria.

So instead of being cleared easily, it can:

* hide inside cells
* spread through blood
* seed multiple organs
* trigger pus-filled abscess formation

Intuition:

> It acts like a soil-water gram-negative bug that disseminates and makes abscesses everywhere.

---

### Risk factors

Most important risk factor:

* diabetes mellitus

Other risk factors:

* chronic kidney disease
* chronic lung disease
* alcoholism
* immunosuppression, meaning weakened immune function

Why diabetes matters:

* macrophages, meaning bacteria-eating immune cells, work less effectively
* intracellular bacteria become harder to clear
* risk of severe sepsis increases

---

### Treatment idea

Treatment usually has 2 phases:

* acute IV (intravenous) therapy, often ceftazidime or meropenem
* eradication therapy, often trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole

Why so long?

Because the organism can persist inside cells and relapse if not fully eradicated.

---

## One-line intuition

Melioidosis is **Burkholderia pseudomallei from soil/water causing pneumonia, sepsis, and multiple abscesses, especially in diabetics**.

---

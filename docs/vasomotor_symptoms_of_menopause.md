# Vasomotor symptoms of menopause

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d9/Symptoms_of_menopause_%28raster%29.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/TEWL_hot_flash_plot.svg/500px-TEWL_hot_flash_plot.svg.png)

Vasomotor symptoms of menopause = hot flashes and night sweats.

Break it down:

* vaso = blood vessels
* motor = movement/change
* vasomotor = changes in blood vessel tone

So these symptoms happen because blood vessels suddenly dilate (widen), causing:

* sudden heat
* flushing
* sweating
* sometimes palpitations (feeling your heartbeat)

---

### Core idea

In menopause, estrogen decreases.

Lower estrogen affects the hypothalamus (brain temperature control center).

The hypothalamus becomes overly sensitive to tiny changes in body temperature, so it falsely thinks:

> "We are overheating."

Then the body tries to dump heat by:

* dilating skin blood vessels
* increasing sweating

That causes the hot flash.

---

### Why night sweats happen

Night sweats are basically hot flashes during sleep.

The same hypothalamus problem happens, but because the person is asleep and under blankets, it can feel like waking up drenched in sweat.

---

### One-line intuition

Low estrogen makes the hypothalamic thermostat too sensitive, so the body randomly activates heat-loss responses.

---

## Pearl

The most effective treatment for menopausal vasomotor symptoms is estrogen therapy, but it is avoided or used carefully in patients with contraindications (reasons a drug should not be used), like estrogen-sensitive breast cancer or high clot risk.

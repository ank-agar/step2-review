## Hypertensive retinopathy

Hypertensive retinopathy = damage to the retina from chronic high blood pressure.

Retina = the light-sensing layer in the back of the eye.

Think:

> High pressure damages tiny retinal vessels, so the eye shows signs of systemic vascular damage.

---

### Core findings

High blood pressure causes:

* arteriolar narrowing = small retinal arteries get tight/narrow
* AV nicking = arteriovenous nicking, where a thickened artery compresses a vein at a crossing point
* flame hemorrhages = superficial retinal bleeding shaped like flames
* cotton wool spots = fluffy white retinal infarcts from ischemia
* hard exudates = lipid/protein leakage from damaged vessels
* papilledema = optic disc swelling, seen in severe malignant hypertension

---

### Why this happens

High blood pressure injures retinal arterioles.

Step-by-step:

* chronic pressure makes arteriolar walls thick and stiff
* stiff arteries narrow the lumen, so less blood gets through
* at artery-vein crossings, the stiff artery compresses the vein = AV nicking
* damaged endothelium leaks blood and proteins = hemorrhages + exudates
* severe ischemia causes cotton wool spots

So the retina becomes a visible window into blood vessel damage elsewhere in the body.

---

### Malignant hypertension clue

If you see:

* flame hemorrhages
* cotton wool spots
* papilledema

think severe hypertension with end-organ damage.

End-organ damage = injury to organs from high blood pressure, like retina, kidney, brain, or heart.

---

## One-line intuition

Hypertensive retinopathy is high blood pressure leaving "wear-and-tear marks" on the tiny vessels of the retina.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Papilledema in hypertensive retinopathy means malignant hypertension until proven otherwise.

Malignant hypertension = severely elevated blood pressure with acute end-organ damage.

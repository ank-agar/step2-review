### Core idea

Stasis dermatitis is skin inflammation caused by chronic venous insufficiency.

Chronic venous insufficiency means the leg veins are bad at pushing blood back up to the heart.

So blood/fluid pools in the lower legs.

Break it down:

* stasis = standing still / pooling
* dermatitis = skin inflammation

So:

> pooled venous blood causes an inflamed, itchy, scaly rash around the ankles/lower legs.

---

### Why it happens

Normally, leg vein valves keep blood moving upward toward the heart.

If those valves fail:

* blood falls backward due to gravity
* venous pressure increases
* fluid leaks into the tissue
* red blood cells leak out
* hemoglobin breaks down into hemosiderin, an iron pigment

That hemosiderin causes the classic:

* brown/rust-colored skin
* ankle/lower leg discoloration

The swelling also makes the skin inflamed and fragile, causing:

* itching
* scaling
* redness
* oozing/crusting
* ulcers in severe cases

---

### Classic location

Usually around the:

* medial ankle
* shin
* lower legs

Medial ankle is high yield because venous pressure is especially bad there.

---

### What it looks like

Think:

* older patient
* chronic leg swelling
* varicose veins
* itchy, scaly rash
* brown hyperpigmentation near ankles

It can be bilateral, which helps distinguish it from cellulitis.

Cellulitis is a bacterial skin infection and is usually more acute, painful, warm, and one-sided.

---

## One-line intuition

Bad leg vein valves let blood pool near the ankles, and that chronic pressure/leakage irritates the skin into a brown, itchy, scaly rash.

---

## Pearl

Stasis dermatitis can progress to venous ulcers, classically near the medial malleolus, which is the inner ankle bone.

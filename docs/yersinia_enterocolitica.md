# Yersinia enterocolitica

![Image](https://journals.plos.org/plospathogens/article/figure/image?id=10.1371/journal.ppat.1001147.g001&size=large)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/1049.jpg)

### Core idea

**Yersinia enterocolitica** is a **gram-negative rod/coccobacillus** (short rod-shaped bacterium) that causes **enterocolitis** (inflammation of the small intestine and colon), usually after eating contaminated food.

Most classic source:

* undercooked pork
* contaminated milk/water

---

### What it causes

It can cause:

* watery or bloody diarrhea
* fever
* abdominal pain
* **pseudoappendicitis** (appendicitis-like pain without true appendicitis)

Pseudoappendicitis happens because Yersinia likes the **terminal ileum** (last part of the small intestine) and **mesenteric lymph nodes** (lymph nodes in the intestinal mesentery), which are near the appendix.

So the patient gets:

* right lower quadrant pain
* fever
* inflamed lymph nodes

and it can look like appendicitis.

---

### Why it invades there

Yersinia can enter through **M cells** (microfold cells), which are specialized intestinal cells over **Peyer patches** (lymphoid tissue in the ileum).

Think:

* Peyer patches = immune sampling stations in the ileum
* M cells = doorway that samples gut contents
* Yersinia = uses that doorway to invade

That is why it has a strong association with:

* terminal ileitis
* mesenteric adenitis
* pseudoappendicitis

---

### Other Step 1 associations

Yersinia can trigger:

* **reactive arthritis** (joint inflammation after infection)
* **erythema nodosum** (painful red nodules, usually on the shins)

It is also **siderophilic** (iron-loving), so severe infection risk is higher in iron-overload states like **hemochromatosis** (excess body iron).

---

## One-line intuition

Yersinia enterocolitica is a pork-associated gram-negative enteric bug that invades Peyer patches in the terminal ileum, causing diarrhea plus appendicitis-like right lower quadrant pain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Yersinia enterocolitica grows in the cold, so it can survive/reproduce at refrigerator temperatures; this is why it is a classic cause of contaminated stored food or blood products.

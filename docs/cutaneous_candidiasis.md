Cutaneous candidiasis means a **Candida** yeast infection of the skin.

Most commonly it is caused by **Candida albicans**.

Break it apart:

* cutaneous = skin
* candidiasis = infection by Candida yeast

So:

> Candida overgrowth on the skin.

---

### Core idea

Candida likes warm, moist areas.

So it commonly shows up in skin folds:

* under breasts
* groin
* axillae (armpits)
* between fingers/toes
* diaper area

This skin-fold inflammation is called **intertrigo** (rash where two skin surfaces rub together).

---

### What it looks like

Classic findings:

* red, itchy, burning rash
* moist/macerated skin (soft, wet, broken-down skin)
* sharply irritated skin folds
* **satellite lesions** (small red papules or pustules near the main rash)

Satellite lesions are the big clue.

They are like little Candida “islands” spreading outward from the main red rash.

---

### Why it happens

Candida can normally live on skin and mucosa (wet body linings) without causing disease.

It causes infection when local defenses are weakened:

* moisture lets yeast grow
* friction damages the skin barrier
* antibiotics reduce competing bacteria
* diabetes gives Candida a better growth environment
* immunosuppression means weaker T-cell control

Intuition:

Skin fold + moisture + friction = damaged barrier, so Candida can overgrow.

---

### Diagnosis clue

If scraped and tested with **KOH** (potassium hydroxide), Candida shows:

* budding yeast
* pseudohyphae (chain-like elongated yeast forms)

Pseudohyphae are high-yield because they help separate Candida from just random skin irritation.

---

### Treatment idea

Treat with topical antifungals, like:

* nystatin
* clotrimazole
* miconazole

Also keep the area dry, because moisture is the setup.

---

## One-line intuition

Cutaneous candidiasis is Candida taking advantage of warm, wet, rubbed skin folds, causing a beefy red rash with satellite lesions.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Candida classically causes **pseudohyphae on KOH prep** and is associated with **satellite pustules** in skin folds.

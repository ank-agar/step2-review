MSSA (methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus) means:

* Staphylococcus aureus (a Gram-positive cocci bacteria in clusters)
* that is sensitive to methicillin/nafcillin/oxacillin-type beta-lactam antibiotics (cell-wall antibiotics)

So MSSA is basically:

> regular Staph aureus that beta-lactams can still kill.

---

### Core idea

Staph aureus (Staphylococcus aureus) is a pus-forming bacteria.

It commonly causes:

* skin abscesses
* cellulitis
* osteomyelitis (bone infection)
* septic arthritis (joint infection)
* endocarditis (infection of heart valves)
* pneumonia

The “MSSA” part tells you about antibiotic sensitivity, not a different organism.

---

### MSSA vs MRSA

* **MSSA (methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus):** normal PBPs (penicillin-binding proteins), so anti-staph beta-lactams can bind and work
* **MRSA (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus):** has mecA gene, which makes PBP2a (altered penicillin-binding protein), so many beta-lactams cannot bind well

PBP (penicillin-binding protein) is the enzyme that cross-links the bacterial cell wall.

Beta-lactams (penicillins/cephalosporins/carbapenems/monobactams) kill bacteria by blocking PBPs.

---

### Why MSSA is easier to treat

MSSA still has vulnerable PBPs.

So drugs like:

* nafcillin
* oxacillin
* cefazolin

can block cell-wall cross-linking.

No strong cell wall = bacteria lyses.

---

## One-line intuition

MSSA is Staph aureus whose cell-wall-building enzymes are still “grabbable” by beta-lactam antibiotics.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For serious MSSA (methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus) infections, beta-lactams like nafcillin/oxacillin/cefazolin are preferred over vancomycin because they kill MSSA better.

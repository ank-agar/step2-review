# Tay-Sachs disease

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f1/Autosomal_recessive_-_en.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0f/Tay-sachsUMich.jpg)

Tay-Sachs disease is an **autosomal recessive** (need 2 mutated copies) lysosomal storage disease caused by deficiency of **hexosaminidase A** (lysosomal enzyme that breaks down GM2 ganglioside).

The buildup is:

* **GM2 ganglioside** (a lipid found especially in neurons)

So the core problem is:

> no hexosaminidase A → GM2 ganglioside accumulates in neurons → progressive neurodegeneration

---

### Classic findings

* developmental delay/regression
* exaggerated startle response
* seizures
* hypotonia/weakness
* vision loss
* **cherry-red spot** on the macula (central retina)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Neurodegeneration:** neurons get swollen with stored GM2 ganglioside
* **Developmental regression:** the brain initially develops, then neurons progressively fail
* **Cherry-red spot:** retinal cells around the fovea (center of macula) get pale/swollen from lipid storage, so the normal red fovea stands out
* **No hepatosplenomegaly:** mainly a neuronal storage disease, unlike some other lysosomal storage diseases

---

## One-line intuition

Tay-Sachs = the brain cannot clear GM2 ganglioside, so neurons slowly fill with lipid and die.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Tay-Sachs is:

* **hexosaminidase A deficiency**
* **GM2 ganglioside accumulation**
* **cherry-red spot**
* **no hepatosplenomegaly**

Compare with **Niemann-Pick disease** (sphingomyelinase deficiency): cherry-red spot **plus hepatosplenomegaly**.

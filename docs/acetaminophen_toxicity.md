### Core idea

Acetaminophen toxicity means overdose of acetaminophen, also called APAP (N-acetyl-para-aminophenol), causes liver injury.

The dangerous molecule is not acetaminophen itself.

It is **NAPQI (N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine)**, a toxic metabolite made by CYP450 (cytochrome P450 liver enzymes), especially CYP2E1 (cytochrome P450 2E1).

---

### Normal metabolism

At normal doses, acetaminophen mostly goes through:

* glucuronidation
* sulfation

These make safe metabolites that leave in urine.

A small amount becomes NAPQI, but GSH (glutathione) neutralizes it.

So:

> Normal dose = tiny NAPQI + enough glutathione = safe.

---

### What happens in overdose

In overdose:

* glucuronidation and sulfation get saturated
* more drug is pushed into CYP450 metabolism
* more NAPQI is made
* glutathione gets depleted
* free NAPQI binds liver cell proteins
* hepatocytes (liver cells) undergo necrosis

The injury is especially in **zone 3**, meaning the centrilobular area around the central vein.

Why zone 3?

Because zone 3 has lots of CYP450 activity, so it makes more NAPQI.

---

### Symptoms/labs

Early symptoms can be vague:

* nausea
* vomiting
* abdominal pain
* feeling "fine" at first

Then liver injury develops:

* very high AST/ALT (aspartate aminotransferase/alanine aminotransferase)
* increased PT/INR (prothrombin time/international normalized ratio), meaning impaired clotting
* hepatic encephalopathy (confusion from liver failure) in severe cases

---

### Antidote

The antidote is **NAC (N-acetylcysteine)**.

NAC works because it replenishes glutathione.

So the logic is:

> NAC gives liver cells the raw material to remake glutathione, which detoxifies NAPQI.

Best if given early, but still useful later in severe toxicity.

---

## One-line intuition

Acetaminophen overdose overwhelms the safe liver pathways, makes too much NAPQI, burns through glutathione, and causes centrilobular liver necrosis.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 association: chronic alcohol use increases risk because alcohol induces CYP2E1 (cytochrome P450 2E1) and can lower glutathione stores, so more acetaminophen gets converted into toxic NAPQI.

## Myofascial pain syndrome

Myofascial pain syndrome is chronic muscle pain caused by trigger points.

Break it down:

* **myo-** = muscle
* **fascia** = connective tissue around muscles
* **syndrome** = group of symptoms that happen together

So:

> pain coming from irritated/tight spots in muscle and surrounding fascia.

The classic lesion is a **trigger point** (hyperirritable tender spot in a tight band of skeletal muscle).

When you press the trigger point, it can cause:

* local pain
* referred pain (pain felt somewhere else)
* muscle tightness
* decreased range of motion
* sometimes a local twitch response (brief muscle contraction when the spot is stimulated)

---

### Why does the pain happen?

Think of a trigger point like a tiny muscle cramp that gets stuck.

The cycle is:

* muscle overuse/trauma/posture stress
* muscle fibers stay contracted
* contracted muscle compresses small blood vessels
* less blood flow means less oxygen and less ATP (adenosine triphosphate, the cell's energy molecule)
* low ATP makes it harder for the muscle to relax
* the tight spot keeps irritating pain nerves

So it becomes a self-feeding loop:

**spasm -> tightness -> low blood flow -> waste buildup -> more spasm**

---

### Key feature

The big clue is **referred pain**.

Example:

* trigger point in upper trapezius (shoulder/neck muscle)
* patient feels headache or neck pain

So the painful area is not always the source.

---

### How to tell apart from fibromyalgia

Myofascial pain syndrome:

* regional pain
* trigger points
* referred pain pattern

Fibromyalgia:

* widespread pain
* fatigue/sleep issues often
* more central pain processing issue

One-line intuition:

**Myofascial pain = painful muscle knots that can refer pain elsewhere.**

---

## Pearl

Trigger points can mimic other diseases because referred pain may look like headache, radiculopathy (nerve root pain), tendonitis (tendon inflammation), or joint pain, but the neuro exam is usually normal.

### Core idea

Tinea corporis is **ringworm of the body**.

But key point:

> It is not a worm.

It is a **superficial dermatophyte fungal infection**.

Dermatophytes = fungi that eat **keratin**.

Keratin = the tough structural protein in:

* skin
* hair
* nails

So tinea infections like to live in the **stratum corneum**.

Stratum corneum = the dead, outer keratin layer of the epidermis.

---

### What it looks like

Classic finding:

* annular plaque
* scaly raised border
* central clearing
* itchy

Annular = ring-shaped.

Central clearing means the middle starts looking more normal while the fungus keeps spreading outward at the edge.

That is why it forms the classic “ring.”

---

### Why the ring happens

The fungus grows outward through keratin.

The immune system reacts most strongly at the **active advancing edge**, so the border becomes:

* red
* raised
* scaly

The center may partially heal, so it looks clearer.

One-line intuition:

> The fungus expands outward like a spreading circle, leaving a calmer center behind.

---

### Common organisms

Usually caused by dermatophytes such as:

* Trichophyton
* Microsporum
* Epidermophyton

These spread by contact with:

* people
* animals
* contaminated objects, like towels, clothing, mats, or bedding

---

### Diagnosis

Usually clinical, but you can confirm with **KOH prep**.

KOH prep = potassium hydroxide preparation.

KOH dissolves keratin from skin scrapings, leaving fungal elements visible under the microscope.

Classic finding:

* branching septate hyphae

Hyphae = long fungal filaments.

Septate = divided by internal walls.

---

### Treatment

Localized tinea corporis:

* topical antifungal, like terbinafine or clotrimazole

Extensive, recurrent, immunocompromised, or hair-follicle involvement:

* oral antifungal may be needed

Important:

Do **not** treat suspected ringworm with topical steroids alone.

Steroids reduce inflammation and can make the rash look less classic while the fungus keeps growing.

That is called **tinea incognito**.

Incognito = hidden/disguised.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, connect:

> Tinea corporis = annular scaly plaque with central clearing + KOH showing branching septate hyphae.

And remember:

* tinea corporis = body
* tinea capitis = scalp
* tinea pedis = feet
* tinea cruris = groin

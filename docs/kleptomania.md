# Kleptomania

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Mesolimbic_pathway.svg/1280px-Mesolimbic_pathway.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/25/Th%C3%A9odore_G%C3%A9ricault_-_Portrait_of_a_Kleptomaniac_-_WGA08636.jpg)

### Core idea

Kleptomania is an impulse-control disorder (inability to resist a sudden urge) where a person has recurrent urges to steal things that they usually do **not** need for personal use or money.

Key point:

* the stealing is driven by an internal urge
* not by poverty
* not by revenge
* not by hallucinations/delusions
* not mainly for the value of the item

---

### What happens emotionally

Classic pattern:

* rising tension before stealing
* pleasure/relief/gratification during or right after stealing
* guilt/shame afterward

So the act temporarily lowers internal tension, which reinforces the behavior.

---

### Why this happens

Think of it as a mismatch between:

* reward drive: dopamine pathways (reward/motivation signaling)
* impulse braking: prefrontal cortex (front brain area involved in judgment and inhibition)

If the urge system is strong and the brake system is weak, the person may steal even when they know it is wrong and risky.

---

### Not the same as ordinary theft

Ordinary theft is usually goal-directed:

* money
* useful object
* revenge
* antisocial behavior

Kleptomania is more like:

> "I know I should not do this, but the urge builds until stealing gives relief."

---

## One-line intuition

Kleptomania = pathologic stealing for **tension relief**, not for profit.

---

## Pearl

Kleptomania, pyromania (recurrent deliberate fire-setting), and intermittent explosive disorder (recurrent aggressive outbursts) are all Step 1-style examples of disruptive/impulse-control disorders.

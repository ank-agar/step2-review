# Bipolar disorder

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/19/Bipolar_mood_shifts.png)

![Image](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/sites/default/files/images/Bipolar-Disorder-Infographic-SMI.jpg)

Bipolar disorder is a mood disorder where a person has episodes of:

* mania or hypomania
* depression

Key idea:

> mood goes too high and too low.

---

### Mania

Mania = abnormally elevated/irritable mood + increased energy.

Classic findings:

* decreased need for sleep
* grandiosity
* pressured speech
* racing thoughts
* distractibility
* increased goal-directed activity
* risky behavior

Why?

The brain is in a high-energy, overactivated mood state, so the patient feels unusually powerful, fast, energized, and impulsive.

---

### Hypomania

Hypomania = milder mania.

Main difference:

* mania causes major impairment, psychosis (loss of touch with reality), or hospitalization
* hypomania does not

But hypomania is still abnormal for that person.

---

### Depression

Depressive episodes look like major depressive disorder:

* low mood
* low energy
* poor sleep or too much sleep
* anhedonia (loss of interest/pleasure)
* guilt/worthlessness
* poor concentration
* appetite changes
* suicidal ideation (thoughts of suicide)

---

### Bipolar I vs Bipolar II

**Bipolar I:**

* at least 1 manic episode
* depression often happens, but is not required for diagnosis

**Bipolar II:**

* hypomania + major depression
* no full mania

So:

> Mania = bipolar I.
> Hypomania + depression = bipolar II.

---

### Treatment idea

Use mood stabilizers such as:

* lithium
* valproate
* carbamazepine

Important:

* antidepressants alone can trigger mania

because they can push mood upward without stabilizing the manic side.

---

## One-line intuition

Bipolar disorder is not just “mood swings”; it is episodic abnormal mood regulation with pathologic highs and lows.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Lithium can cause:

* nephrogenic diabetes insipidus
* hypothyroidism
* Ebstein anomaly (downward displacement of the tricuspid valve in the heart) in pregnancy

Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus means the kidney cannot respond well to ADH (antidiuretic hormone), so the patient makes lots of dilute urine.

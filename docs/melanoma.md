Melanoma is a malignant tumor of melanocytes (pigment-producing cells that make melanin, the pigment that gives skin color).

Core idea:

* melanocytes normally sit in the basal layer of the epidermis (bottom layer of the outer skin)
* UV radiation (ultraviolet light) can damage DNA
* mutated melanocytes can become cancerous
* melanoma is dangerous because it invades early and can metastasize (spread to distant sites)

---

### What it looks like

Use **ABCDE**:

* **A = asymmetry:** one half does not match the other
* **B = border irregularity:** jagged, notched, blurry edges
* **C = color variation:** black, brown, red, blue, white mixed together
* **D = diameter:** classically >6 mm
* **E = evolving:** changing size, shape, color, bleeding, itching

The most important clue is **evolution** because a benign nevus (mole) usually stays stable.

---

### Why melanoma spreads easily

Melanocytes are near the dermis (deeper skin layer).

Once melanoma grows downward into the dermis, it can reach:

* blood vessels
* lymphatics (drainage vessels that carry immune fluid)

That is why depth of invasion matters so much.

---

### Step 1 associations

Common mutations:

* **BRAF** mutation, especially **V600E**
* **CDKN2A** mutation in familial melanoma

Risk factors:

* UV exposure
* fair skin
* many atypical nevi (abnormal moles)
* family history
* immunosuppression (weakened immune system)

---

## One-line intuition

Melanoma is a melanocyte cancer that looks like a changing ugly mole and is feared because it invades downward and spreads early.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Melanoma prognosis is strongly tied to **Breslow depth** (tumor thickness measured downward in millimeters), because deeper tumor means easier access to lymphatics and blood vessels.

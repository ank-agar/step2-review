# Right ventricular hypertrophy

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Right_Ventricular_hypertrophy.svg/960px-Right_Ventricular_hypertrophy.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK499876/bin/right-ventricular-hypertrophy.jpg)

Right ventricular hypertrophy (RVH) = thickening/enlargement of the right ventricle muscle.

The right ventricle (RV) normally pumps blood to the lungs, which is a low-pressure system.

So if the RV gets hypertrophied, think:

* something is making it pump against higher resistance
* most often pulmonary hypertension (high pressure in lung blood vessels)

---

### Core idea

The RV is like a muscle lifting heavier weight than usual.

More pressure to pump against → RV muscle gets thicker.

Common causes:

* pulmonary hypertension
* chronic lung disease, like chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD; chronic obstructive pulmonary disease)
* pulmonary valve stenosis (narrowing of the pulmonary valve)
* congenital heart disease, like Tetralogy of Fallot

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Right axis deviation on ECG (electrocardiogram):** the thick RV creates stronger electrical forces toward the right side
* **Tall R wave in V1:** V1 sits over the right side of the heart, so a bigger RV gives a bigger positive signal there
* **Deep S waves in V5/V6:** left-sided leads see the main electrical force moving away from them
* **Right heart failure symptoms:** if the RV cannot keep up, blood backs up into systemic veins

Systemic venous backup can cause:

* jugular venous distension (JVD; enlarged neck veins)
* peripheral edema
* hepatomegaly (enlarged liver)

---

### Important Step 1 association

Chronic lung disease can cause pulmonary hypertension, which then causes RVH.

This is called cor pulmonale (right heart failure caused by lung disease).

---

## One-line intuition

Right ventricular hypertrophy = the right ventricle bulks up because it is chronically pushing against high pulmonary pressure.

---

## Pearl 🔥

RVH on ECG classically gives **right axis deviation + tall R wave in V1**, because the enlarged RV pulls the heart’s electrical vector toward the right/anterior chest.

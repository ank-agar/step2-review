## Neonatal respiratory distress syndrome

![Image](https://clipart-library.com/2023/kisspng-pulmonary-alveolus-lung-pulmonary-compliance-surfa-naxin-5ae3df81b15361.9055492615248833297263.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.serc.carleton.edu/images/NAGTWorkshops/health/case_studies/labeled_diagram_lungsrespirato.v2.webp)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK560779/bin/RDS-xray__statpearls.jpg)

Neonatal respiratory distress syndrome is lung collapse in a newborn, usually a premature baby, because there is not enough surfactant (a substance that lowers surface tension inside alveoli).

Alveoli (tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens) naturally want to collapse because their inner surface is wet.

Surfactant is like anti-stick coating for alveoli:

* lowers surface tension
* keeps alveoli open
* makes lungs easier to inflate

So in neonatal RDS (respiratory distress syndrome):

* low surfactant
* alveoli collapse, called atelectasis (collapse of lung tissue)
* lungs become stiff, meaning decreased compliance (harder to expand)
* baby has to work harder to breathe
* less oxygen enters blood

---

### Why premature babies get it

Surfactant is made by type II pneumocytes (alveolar cells that produce surfactant).

These mature later in fetal development, so premature babies have immature lungs and low surfactant.

Think:

> Premature lungs = not enough type II pneumocyte function = not enough surfactant = collapsed alveoli.

---

### Classic findings

Baby shortly after birth may have:

* tachypnea (fast breathing)
* nasal flaring
* grunting
* intercostal retractions (skin pulling in between ribs)
* cyanosis (blue color from low oxygen)

Chest X-ray classically shows:

* ground-glass appearance
* air bronchograms (air-filled bronchi visible against collapsed/opaque alveoli)

---

### Why hyaline membranes form

When alveoli collapse and oxygen is low, the alveolar wall gets injured.

That injury causes leakage of protein-rich fluid and dead cell debris into the alveoli.

This forms hyaline membranes (pink protein/debris lining alveoli on histology), which further blocks gas exchange.

That is why neonatal RDS is also called hyaline membrane disease.

---

## One-line intuition

Neonatal RDS is premature lungs without enough surfactant, so the alveoli collapse and the baby cannot oxygenate well.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Antenatal corticosteroids (steroids given to the mother before delivery) increase fetal surfactant production and help prevent neonatal RDS.

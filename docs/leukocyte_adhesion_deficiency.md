## Leukocyte adhesion deficiency

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK53380/bin/fig7.1.gif)

![Image](https://bioart.niaid.nih.gov/api/bioarts/10/files/626654)

Leukocyte adhesion deficiency is an autosomal recessive immunodeficiency where neutrophils cannot stick to blood vessel walls and leave the bloodstream properly.

Most Step 1 questions mean **LAD-1 (leukocyte adhesion deficiency type 1)**:

* defective **CD18 (beta-2 integrin subunit)**
* impaired neutrophil adhesion to **ICAM (intercellular adhesion molecule)** on endothelium
* neutrophils stay trapped in blood instead of entering infected tissue

---

### Core idea

Normal neutrophil movement:

* rolling = selectins
* firm adhesion = integrins
* transmigration/diapedesis = moving through vessel wall

In LAD-1, the problem is:

> no firm adhesion.

So neutrophils can be high in the blood, but low at the infection site.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Recurrent bacterial/fungal infections:** neutrophils cannot reach tissue well
* **No pus:** pus is mostly dead neutrophils, but neutrophils cannot get into the tissue
* **Delayed umbilical cord separation:** neutrophils are needed to inflame/remodel the umbilical stump so it falls off
* **Poor wound healing:** neutrophils cannot migrate into damaged tissue
* **Marked leukocytosis/neutrophilia:** neutrophils build up in blood because they cannot exit

---

### One-line intuition

The neutrophils are present, but they cannot park and get out of the blood vessel.

---

## Pearl 🔥

LAD-1 = **CD18 integrin defect** with **delayed umbilical cord separation + no pus + high neutrophils in blood**.

## Bell palsy

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Bells_palsy_diagram.svg/330px-Bells_palsy_diagram.svg.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/9101.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482290/bin/Gray788.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Bellspalsy.JPG/500px-Bellspalsy.JPG)

Bell palsy is acute unilateral peripheral facial nerve palsy.

Break that down:

* acute = sudden onset
* unilateral = one-sided
* peripheral = lower motor neuron lesion, meaning the nerve itself is affected after it leaves the brainstem
* facial nerve = cranial nerve VII (7th cranial nerve)
* palsy = weakness/paralysis

So the core idea is:

> Bell palsy is sudden one-sided weakness of the whole face due to dysfunction of cranial nerve VII.

---

### What you see

Because cranial nerve VII controls muscles of facial expression, the affected side has:

* forehead weakness
* inability to fully close the eye
* flattened nasolabial fold (skin crease from nose to mouth)
* drooping corner of the mouth

Key point:

* the forehead is involved

That tells you it is a lower motor neuron lesion (damage to the facial nerve itself), not an upper motor neuron lesion (damage above the facial nerve nucleus, like a stroke).

---

### Why the whole face is weak

The upper face gets input from both brain hemispheres.

So in a stroke, the forehead is usually spared because the other hemisphere can still help activate it.

But in Bell palsy, the problem is after the facial nerve nucleus, so the entire ipsilateral face is weak.

Ipsilateral = same side.

---

### Cause

Most cases are idiopathic.

Idiopathic = no clear known cause.

But Step 1 often links it to inflammation/reactivation of HSV (herpes simplex virus), which causes swelling of cranial nerve VII.

Swollen nerve = compressed nerve = poor facial muscle activation.

---

### Extra symptoms

Depending on where cranial nerve VII is affected, you can also get:

* hyperacusis = sounds seem too loud, due to stapedius muscle weakness
* decreased lacrimation = decreased tear production
* decreased salivation = decreased saliva production
* loss of taste from anterior 2/3 of tongue

Anterior 2/3 = front two-thirds.

---

## One-line intuition

Bell palsy is like cranial nerve VII suddenly “turning off” on one side, so the entire same-side face droops, including the forehead.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Forehead involvement is the big Step 1 clue for lower motor neuron facial nerve palsy, like Bell palsy.

Forehead sparing points more toward an upper motor neuron lesion, like stroke.

# Normal-pressure hydrocephalus

### Core idea

Normal-pressure hydrocephalus is a type of **communicating hydrocephalus** where cerebrospinal fluid accumulates in the ventricles, but the measured cerebrospinal fluid pressure is normal or only mildly elevated.

* **CSF** = cerebrospinal fluid, the fluid around the brain and spinal cord
* **Ventricles** = fluid-filled spaces inside the brain
* **Communicating hydrocephalus** = CSF can still flow through the ventricular system, but it is not being reabsorbed well

So the main problem is not a blocked tube.

The main problem is:

> CSF is not being absorbed well, so the ventricles slowly enlarge.

---

### Classic triad

Think:

* **wet**
* **wobbly**
* **wacky**

Meaning:

* **urinary incontinence**
* **gait disturbance**
* **cognitive decline/dementia**

The gait problem is usually the earliest and most prominent finding.

Patients may have a magnetic, shuffling gait, like their feet are stuck to the floor.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The enlarged ventricles stretch and compress nearby brain tissue.

This especially affects white matter tracts near the ventricles.

That explains:

* **gait problems:** pressure on motor pathways controlling walking
* **urinary incontinence:** pressure on pathways controlling bladder inhibition
* **dementia:** disruption of frontal/subcortical circuits

So the ventricles are not just "big on imaging."

They are physically distorting nearby brain pathways.

---

### Why is the pressure "normal"?

The pressure is often normal when measured because this is usually a slow, chronic process.

The brain and ventricles gradually adapt to the extra CSF volume.

But even if the pressure reading is normal, the enlarged ventricles can still stretch surrounding tissue over time.

So:

> normal pressure does not mean normal brain mechanics.

---

### Diagnosis clue

Imaging shows **ventriculomegaly**.

* **Ventriculomegaly** = enlarged brain ventricles

A lumbar puncture may temporarily improve symptoms after CSF removal.

* **Lumbar puncture** = spinal tap, removing CSF from the lower back

If gait improves after removing CSF, that supports normal-pressure hydrocephalus and predicts benefit from shunting.

---

### Treatment

Treatment is usually a ventriculoperitoneal shunt.

* **Ventriculoperitoneal shunt** = tube that drains CSF from a brain ventricle into the peritoneal cavity of the abdomen
* **Peritoneal cavity** = space in the abdomen around the intestines

This reduces ventricular size and pressure on nearby brain tissue.

---

## One-line intuition

Normal-pressure hydrocephalus is **too much CSF trapped in enlarged ventricles, causing gait problems, urinary incontinence, and dementia even though the pressure reading may look normal.**

---

## Pearl

Normal-pressure hydrocephalus is a **reversible cause of dementia**, and the symptom most likely to improve after shunting is usually **gait**.

## Parkinson disease

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b0/Basal_ganglia_in_Parkinson%27s_disease.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19515.jpg)

### Core idea

Parkinson disease is a slowly progressive neurodegenerative disease where dopaminergic neurons (dopamine-releasing nerve cells) die in the substantia nigra pars compacta (part of the midbrain that helps control movement).

Less dopamine in the basal ganglia (deep brain movement-control nuclei) means the brain has trouble initiating smooth movement.

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* resting tremor
* rigidity
* bradykinesia (slow movement)
* postural instability (poor balance)

Other common clues:

* shuffling gait
* masked facies (reduced facial expression)
* micrographia (small handwriting)
* anosmia (loss of smell)
* constipation
* depression or sleep problems

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Dopamine normally helps movement by:

* stimulating the direct pathway, which promotes movement
* inhibiting the indirect pathway, which suppresses movement

In Parkinson disease:

* less dopamine = less direct pathway activity
* less dopamine = more indirect pathway activity

So the net effect is:

> too much braking on movement.

That is why patients become slow, stiff, and have difficulty starting movements.

---

### Pathology

The key microscopic finding is:

* Lewy bodies

Lewy bodies are intracellular inclusions (abnormal protein clumps inside cells) made of alpha-synuclein (a misfolded neuronal protein).

The affected substantia nigra also loses its dark pigment because those dopamine neurons contain neuromelanin (dark pigment in certain brain neurons).

---

### Treatment intuition

Main treatment:

* levodopa + carbidopa

Levodopa is a dopamine precursor (building block that can cross the blood-brain barrier).

Carbidopa blocks peripheral conversion of levodopa to dopamine, meaning:

* more levodopa reaches the brain
* fewer peripheral side effects like nausea and hypotension (low blood pressure)

---

## One-line intuition

Parkinson disease is loss of dopamine from the substantia nigra, causing the basal ganglia to over-brake movement.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

MPTP (a toxin contaminant in some synthetic opioids) can cause Parkinson-like symptoms because it selectively damages dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra.

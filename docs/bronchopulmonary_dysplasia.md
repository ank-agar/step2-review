# Bronchopulmonary dysplasia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/46/Alveolus_diagram.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/82/CXR_-_Bronchopulmonary_dysplasia.jpg)

Bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) is chronic lung disease in premature infants.

Break the word apart:

* broncho- = airways
* pulmonary = lungs
* dysplasia = abnormal development

So BPD basically means:

> abnormal lung development/injury in a premature baby.

---

### Core idea

Premature lungs are still developing, especially the alveoli (tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens).

Then the baby often needs:

* oxygen
* positive-pressure ventilation (machine-assisted breathing that pushes air into the lungs)

Those help the baby survive, but they can also injure fragile immature lungs.

Result:

* fewer alveoli
* larger/simple alveoli
* less surface area for gas exchange
* chronic need for oxygen

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Hypoxemia (low blood oxygen):** fewer functioning alveoli means less oxygen can enter blood
* **Tachypnea (fast breathing):** baby breathes faster to compensate for poor gas exchange
* **Retractions/increased work of breathing:** stiff or injured lungs take more effort to inflate
* **Pulmonary hypertension (high blood pressure in lung arteries):** abnormal lung vessels + chronic low oxygen cause pulmonary vasoconstriction

---

### Classic risk factors

Most associated with:

* prematurity
* low birth weight
* prolonged oxygen therapy
* mechanical ventilation
* neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (NRDS; surfactant deficiency in premature infants)

---

## One-line intuition

BPD is when a premature baby survives respiratory distress, but the immature lungs get stuck with simplified, injured alveoli that do not exchange oxygen well.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) is classically caused by oxygen toxicity + barotrauma (pressure injury from ventilation) in premature infants treated for neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (NRDS).

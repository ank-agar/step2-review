# Alopecia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Alopecia_areata.svg/1280px-Alopecia_areata.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537000/bin/AA.jpg)

Alopecia just means **hair loss**.

It is a description, not one single disease.

Common types:

* **Alopecia areata:** autoimmune patchy hair loss
* **Androgenic alopecia:** male/female pattern hair loss from androgen sensitivity
* **Telogen effluvium:** diffuse shedding after stress, illness, pregnancy, or drugs
* **Traction alopecia:** hair loss from chronic pulling/tension
* **Scarring alopecia:** follicle is destroyed, so regrowth is harder

---

### Core idea

Hair loss happens when the hair follicle either:

* stops cycling normally
* gets miniaturized
* gets attacked by inflammation
* gets physically damaged
* gets permanently scarred

So the key question is:

> Is the follicle still alive?

If yes, hair can often regrow.  
If no, hair loss is usually permanent.

---

### Alopecia areata

This is the classic **Step 1 autoimmune version**.

In alopecia areata, **T cells (T lymphocytes)** attack the hair follicle, especially around the bulb/root.

That causes:

* sudden round patches of hair loss
* smooth skin
* no scarring
* possible regrowth later

Why no scarring?

Because the follicle is stunned/attacked, but not necessarily destroyed.

---

### Why the hair falls out

Normally, the hair follicle cycles through growth and rest.

In alopecia areata:

* immune cells cluster around the follicle
* inflammation disrupts active hair growth
* the hair shaft weakens near the root
* the hair falls out in patches

This is why you can see **exclamation point hairs**:

* thicker at the tip
* thinner near the scalp

They look like a tiny exclamation mark because the base of the hair shaft is weakened.

---

### Androgenic alopecia

Androgenic alopecia is pattern baldness.

Mechanism:

* hair follicles are sensitive to **DHT (dihydrotestosterone)**
* DHT causes follicle miniaturization
* hairs become shorter, thinner, and lighter

So:

> alopecia areata = immune attack  
> androgenic alopecia = androgen-driven follicle miniaturization

---

## One-line intuition

Alopecia means hair loss; for Step 1, think **alopecia areata = autoimmune T-cell attack on hair follicles causing smooth, patchy, non-scarring hair loss**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Alopecia areata is associated with other autoimmune diseases, especially **autoimmune thyroid disease**, so patchy hair loss can point you toward an autoimmune tendency.

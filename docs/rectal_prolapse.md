### Core idea

Rectal prolapse = the rectum slips downward and can protrude out through the anus.

Rectum = the last part of the large intestine before stool exits.

Prolapse = an organ or tissue falls/slides out of its normal position.

So this is basically:

> the rectum telescopes downward and comes out through the anal opening.

---

### Why it happens

Usually the support structures holding the rectum in place get weak, especially:

* pelvic floor muscles
* anal sphincters (muscles that help keep the anus closed)
* connective tissue support

Then pressure from straining, constipation, childbirth, aging, chronic diarrhea, or chronic coughing can push the rectum downward.

---

### What it looks/feels like

Patients may have:

* red tissue protruding from the anus
* mucus leakage
* bleeding
* constipation
* feeling of incomplete emptying
* fecal incontinence (loss of stool control)

Why incontinence happens:

The prolapsed rectum stretches and weakens the anal sphincter, so the patient may lose the ability to hold stool in.

---

### Rectal prolapse vs hemorrhoids

Hemorrhoids = swollen veins around the anus/rectum.

Rectal prolapse = the rectal wall itself is coming down.

Key visual clue:

* rectal prolapse often has circular/concentric folds
* hemorrhoids often look like separate bulging cushions

This is why rectal prolapse can be mistaken for hemorrhoids, but they are not the same thing.

---

## One-line intuition

Rectal prolapse is like the rectum turning inside-out and sliding down through the anus because its support system is weak.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In children with rectal prolapse, think about cystic fibrosis because thick secretions and malabsorption can cause bulky stools, straining, and increased risk of prolapse.

## Benzodiazepine withdrawal

Benzodiazepine withdrawal = symptoms that happen when someone who is physically dependent on benzodiazepines suddenly lowers or stops them.

Benzodiazepines are drugs like:

* diazepam
* lorazepam
* alprazolam
* clonazepam

They normally increase GABA-A activity (gamma-aminobutyric acid type A receptor activity).

GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) is the main inhibitory neurotransmitter in the brain.

So:

> benzodiazepines press the brain's brake pedal.

---

### Core idea

With chronic benzodiazepine use, the brain adapts by becoming less sensitive to inhibition.

Then if the drug is removed suddenly:

* less GABA braking
* relatively too much excitation
* nervous system becomes hyperactive

That causes withdrawal.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Benzodiazepines increase chloride entry through GABA-A receptors.

Chloride makes neurons more negative inside, so they are harder to fire.

So benzodiazepines normally cause:

* sedation
* anxiolysis (reduced anxiety)
* anticonvulsant effect
* muscle relaxation

Withdrawal is the opposite:

* anxiety
* insomnia
* tremor
* sweating
* agitation
* palpitations
* seizures

Why seizures?

Because the brain lost inhibitory GABA tone, so neurons fire too easily.

---

### Big danger

Do not abruptly stop chronic benzodiazepines.

Withdrawal can be life-threatening because it can cause:

* seizures
* delirium
* severe autonomic instability (unstable heart rate, blood pressure, sweating, and temperature regulation)

This is similar in concept to alcohol withdrawal because alcohol also enhances GABA activity.

---

## One-line intuition

The brain got used to extra GABA braking, so when the brake is suddenly removed, the nervous system rebounds into overexcitation.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Benzodiazepine withdrawal is treated with a gradual taper, often using a long-acting benzodiazepine like diazepam, because long half-life drugs give smoother blood levels and less severe withdrawal.

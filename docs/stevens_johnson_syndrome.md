### Core idea

Stevens-Johnson syndrome, or **SJS (Stevens-Johnson syndrome)**, is a severe immune reaction where the skin and mucous membranes start dying and sloughing off.

Think:

* drug reaction
* fever
* painful rash
* mucosal erosions
* skin detachment

It is on the same spectrum as **TEN (toxic epidermal necrolysis)**.

---

### What is actually happening

Most cases are triggered by medications.

Classic triggers:

* sulfonamides
* allopurinol
* lamotrigine
* carbamazepine
* phenytoin
* NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs)

The drug or drug-metabolite gets presented to immune cells, especially **CD8+ T cells (cytotoxic T lymphocytes)**.

Those T cells attack keratinocytes, which are the main cells of the epidermis (outer skin layer).

So the skin cells undergo apoptosis, meaning programmed cell death.

Result:

* epidermis dies
* blisters form
* skin separates
* mucosal surfaces ulcerate

---

### Why mucosal involvement is so important

SJS loves mucous membranes, especially:

* mouth
* lips
* eyes
* genital mucosa

That is why the classic picture is:

* painful oral erosions
* conjunctivitis (inflammation of the conjunctiva of the eye)
* targetoid or dusky skin lesions
* skin tenderness

The skin is not just "rash-y" - it is dying and peeling.

---

### SJS vs TEN

This is based on **BSA (body surface area)** detached:

* **SJS:** less than 10% BSA
* **SJS/TEN overlap:** 10-30% BSA
* **TEN:** more than 30% BSA

Same disease spectrum, different severity.

---

### One-line intuition

SJS is basically a severe drug-triggered cytotoxic T-cell attack against skin and mucosal cells, causing the epidermis to die and peel off.

---

## Pearl 🔥

High-yield Step 1 association: **lamotrigine can cause Stevens-Johnson syndrome**, especially if titrated too quickly.

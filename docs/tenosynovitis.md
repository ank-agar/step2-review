# Tenosynovitis

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/3bf2/5965425/c4f67a8be762/cpcem-01-89-g001.jpg)

Tenosynovitis = inflammation of a tendon sheath.

Break it down:

* teno- = tendon
* synov- = synovium/synovial sheath, the smooth lining around some tendons
* -itis = inflammation

So:

> the slippery tunnel around a tendon gets inflamed.

---

### Core idea

A tendon is like a rope that lets muscle pull bone.

Some tendons run inside a sheath, which is like a lubricated sleeve that helps the tendon glide smoothly.

In tenosynovitis, that sleeve becomes:

* swollen
* painful
* tighter
* harder for the tendon to slide through

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Pain with movement:** the tendon is rubbing through an inflamed sheath
* **Swelling:** inflammation brings fluid and immune cells into the sheath
* **Stiffness/catching:** the swollen sheath narrows the tunnel, so the tendon does not glide smoothly
* **Warmth/redness sometimes:** more concerning for infectious tenosynovitis

---

### Common examples

* **De Quervain tenosynovitis:** thumb-side wrist pain from inflammation around thumb tendons
* **Trigger finger:** finger tendon catches because the sheath/pulley is too tight
* **Pyogenic flexor tenosynovitis:** bacterial infection in a finger flexor tendon sheath

Pyogenic = pus-forming.

Flexor = bends a joint.

---

### Important clinical clue

In infectious flexor tenosynovitis, think Kanavel signs:

* finger held in flexion
* fusiform swelling, meaning sausage-like swelling of the whole digit
* tenderness along the flexor tendon sheath
* pain with passive extension

Passive extension = someone else straightens the finger for the patient.

---

## One-line intuition

Tenosynovitis is when the tendon itself may be okay, but the lubricated sleeve around it is inflamed, so the tendon gets painful and stuck while sliding.

---

## Pearl 🔥

De Quervain tenosynovitis causes pain at the radial wrist, meaning the thumb side of the wrist, and is tested with the Finkelstein test.

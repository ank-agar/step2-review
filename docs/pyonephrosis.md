### Core idea

Pyonephrosis = pus trapped inside an obstructed kidney collecting system.

Break the word apart:

* pyo- = pus
* nephro- = kidney
* -osis = condition

So it literally means:

> pus in the kidney.

More specifically, it is infected hydronephrosis.

Hydronephrosis means dilation/swelling of the kidney collecting system because urine cannot drain.

---

### Why it happens

Usually you need 2 things:

* obstruction
* infection

Common obstruction causes:

* kidney stone
* tumor
* ureteral stricture (narrowing of the ureter, the tube from kidney to bladder)

Then bacteria infect the trapped urine.

Since the urine cannot drain, it becomes pus under pressure.

---

### Why this is dangerous

The infected pus is stuck, so antibiotics alone may not work well.

Why?

Because the kidney is basically a closed infected pocket.

That can cause:

* fever/chills
* flank pain (side/back pain near the kidney)
* sepsis (life-threatening body-wide inflammatory response to infection)
* kidney destruction

---

### Treatment intuition

You must drain it.

Treatment is usually:

* IV antibiotics (intravenous antibiotics, given through a vein)
* urgent drainage with nephrostomy tube or ureteral stent

Nephrostomy tube = tube placed through the back into the kidney to drain urine/pus.

Ureteral stent = tube placed inside the ureter to bypass the blockage.

---

## One-line intuition

Pyonephrosis is hydronephrosis that got infected, so the swollen blocked kidney fills with pus and needs urgent drainage.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Step 1-style distinction:

* pyelonephritis = infection of kidney tissue
* pyonephrosis = infected, obstructed collecting system filled with pus

The emergency part is the obstruction + infection combo.

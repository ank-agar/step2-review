### Core idea

A **sarcoma** is a malignant tumor from **mesenchymal tissue** (connective/support tissue).

So it comes from things like:

* bone
* cartilage
* muscle
* fat
* blood vessels
* fibrous tissue

Contrast:

* **carcinoma** = malignant tumor from epithelial cells (lining/surface cells)
* **sarcoma** = malignant tumor from connective/support tissue

---

### Why it can happen in many body parts

Connective tissue is everywhere.

So sarcomas can show up in:

* extremities
* trunk
* retroperitoneum
* bone
* deep soft tissue

That is why “sarcoma” is more of a category than one single disease.

---

### Common examples

* **Osteosarcoma** = malignant bone-forming tumor
* **Chondrosarcoma** = malignant cartilage-forming tumor
* **Liposarcoma** = malignant fat-forming tumor
* **Leiomyosarcoma** = malignant smooth muscle tumor
* **Rhabdomyosarcoma** = malignant skeletal muscle tumor
* **Angiosarcoma** = malignant blood vessel tumor

The name usually tells you the tissue origin.

Example:

* lipo- = fat
* sarcoma = malignant connective tissue tumor
* liposarcoma = malignant tumor of fat tissue

---

### Why sarcomas often spread through blood

Sarcomas arise from deep connective tissues, which are often close to blood vessels.

So they classically spread **hematogenously** (through the blood), especially to the lungs.

This is different from many carcinomas, which often spread first through lymphatics.

---

## One-line intuition

Sarcoma = cancer of the body’s “support tissues,” like bone, muscle, fat, and vessels.

---

## Pearl

High-yield Step 1: **sarcomas usually spread hematogenously to the lungs**, while many **carcinomas** commonly spread through lymphatics first.

### Core idea

Appendicitis = inflammation of the appendix, usually because the appendix lumen gets blocked.

The appendix is a small blind-ended pouch coming off the cecum (first part of the large intestine).

---

### Why it happens

Most common mechanism:

* lumen gets blocked
* bacteria overgrow
* pressure builds up
* venous drainage gets compressed
* ischemia (low blood flow) and inflammation happen
* appendix can perforate if untreated

Common things that can block it:

* fecalith (hardened stool stone)
* lymphoid hyperplasia (enlarged immune tissue), especially after viral infection
* rarely tumor or parasites

---

### Classic presentation

Pain often starts near the umbilicus (belly button), then migrates to the RLQ (right lower quadrant).

Why?

* early pain is visceral pain (organ pain), so it feels vague and midline
* later inflammation irritates the parietal peritoneum (pain-sensitive abdominal lining), so pain localizes sharply to the RLQ (right lower quadrant)

Other findings:

* fever
* anorexia (loss of appetite)
* nausea/vomiting
* leukocytosis (high white blood cell count)
* rebound tenderness from peritoneal irritation

---

### Anatomy landmark

Classic pain localizes near McBurney point:

* one-third of the way from the ASIS (anterior superior iliac spine, front hip bone point) to the umbilicus (belly button)

This is where the inflamed appendix often irritates the abdominal wall.

---

## One-line intuition

Appendicitis is a blocked appendix that swells like an infected closed tube, causing RLQ (right lower quadrant) pain and risk of rupture.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 clue: periumbilical pain migrating to the RLQ (right lower quadrant) = appendicitis until proven otherwise.

Obstruction by a fecalith can progress to perforation because pressure cuts off venous drainage first, then arterial blood flow, causing ischemic necrosis (tissue death from low blood flow).

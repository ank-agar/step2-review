# Chlamydial cystitis

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17736/N00670-H.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5e/Chlamydia.jpg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/PHIL_Images/6427/6427_lores.jpg)

Chlamydial cystitis = bladder/UTI-like symptoms caused by **Chlamydia trachomatis** infection.

But the Step 1 intuition is:

* it often is not a classic bladder infection
* it is usually **chlamydial urethritis** (urethra inflammation) or cervicitis (cervix inflammation) that feels like cystitis

**Cystitis** means bladder inflammation.

**Urethritis** means urethra inflammation.

**UTI** means urinary tract infection.

---

### Core idea

Chlamydia infects epithelial cells (lining cells), especially in the:

* urethra
* cervix
* genital tract

Inflammation there causes:

* dysuria (painful urination)
* urinary frequency
* urinary urgency
* sometimes suprapubic discomfort

So it can look like a normal bacterial UTI.

---

### Why it can be confusing

Classic bacterial cystitis is usually from **E. coli** (Escherichia coli), which grows on routine urine culture.

Chlamydia is different because it is an **obligate intracellular bacterium** (must live inside host cells).

So the patient may have:

* dysuria
* pyuria (white blood cells in urine)
* negative routine urine culture

That combo is called **sterile pyuria**:

> white blood cells are present, but routine bacterial culture is negative.

---

### Diagnosis

Best test:

* **NAAT** (nucleic acid amplification test) on urine or genital swab

NAAT detects chlamydial DNA/RNA (genetic material), so it can find organisms that routine culture misses.

---

### Treatment idea

Treat with antibiotics that work intracellularly, classically:

* doxycycline

Also treat sexual partners, because otherwise reinfection is common.

---

## One-line intuition

Chlamydial cystitis is basically “UTI symptoms, but think STI (sexually transmitted infection), especially if urine culture is negative.”

---

## Pearl 🔥

Chlamydia trachomatis is an obligate intracellular bacterium with:

* elementary body = infectious form
* reticulate body = replicating form

HY clue: dysuria + sterile pyuria in a sexually active patient should make you think **Chlamydia**.

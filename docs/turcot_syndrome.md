## Turcot syndrome

Turcot syndrome = inherited colon polyposis + primary brain tumor.

Break it down:

* colon polyposis = lots of polyps (abnormal growths from the colon lining)
* primary brain tumor = tumor that starts in the brain, not metastasis (spread from somewhere else)

---

### Core idea

It is basically a hereditary cancer syndrome where the same DNA-control problem increases tumor risk in:

* colon
* brain

So think:

> Turcot = tumors in the colon + tumors in the CNS (central nervous system, meaning brain/spinal cord).

---

### Two classic genetic patterns

There are 2 big Step 1 associations:

* **APC (adenomatous polyposis coli) mutation** → like **FAP (familial adenomatous polyposis)** → many colon adenomas + **medulloblastoma**
* **MMR (mismatch repair) gene mutation** → like **Lynch syndrome (hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer)** → fewer colon polyps/cancer + **glioblastoma**

MMR (mismatch repair) genes normally fix DNA copying mistakes.

APC (adenomatous polyposis coli) normally suppresses excess cell growth in the colon.

---

### Why tumors happen

If DNA repair or tumor suppression fails:

* DNA mutations accumulate
* cells lose growth control
* rapidly dividing tissues are especially vulnerable

That is why the colon is a big target: the colon lining constantly renews itself, so lots of cell division means lots of chances for mutations.

The brain tumor association depends on the mutation type:

* APC/FAP pattern → medulloblastoma
* MMR/Lynch-like pattern → glioblastoma

---

## One-line intuition

Turcot syndrome is when an inherited cancer mutation links **colon tumors** with **brain tumors**.

---

## HY pearl

For Step 1:

* **FAP (APC) + medulloblastoma**
* **Lynch/MMR + glioblastoma**

That pairing is the highest-yield way to remember Turcot syndrome.

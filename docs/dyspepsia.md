### Core idea

Dyspepsia means indigestion: discomfort coming from the upper GI tract (gastrointestinal tract), especially the stomach/duodenum (first part of the small intestine).

Classic symptoms:

* epigastric pain or burning
* early satiety (feeling full too quickly)
* postprandial fullness (fullness after eating)
* bloating
* nausea/belching

Epigastric = upper middle abdomen, right under the sternum.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Think: the stomach is either irritated, stretched, or sensing normal digestion as painful.

* **Burning/pain:** acid irritation, gastritis (stomach inflammation), ulcer disease, or visceral hypersensitivity
* **Full quickly:** impaired gastric accommodation, meaning the stomach fundus (upper stomach) does not relax/stretch well after food enters
* **Bloating/fullness:** delayed gastric emptying or abnormal motility (movement of food through the gut)
* **Nausea:** stomach stretch + abnormal gut-brain signaling

So dyspepsia is a symptom pattern, not one single disease.

---

### Big causes to know

Common causes include:

* functional dyspepsia = symptoms but no structural lesion found
* peptic ulcer disease = acid-related ulcer in stomach or duodenum
* gastritis = inflammation of stomach lining
* H. pylori (Helicobacter pylori) infection
* NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs), like ibuprofen/naproxen
* GERD (gastroesophageal reflux disease), which can overlap

Functional dyspepsia is basically:

> the stomach/duodenum looks normal, but the nerves and motility are acting abnormally.

---

### Red flags

Red flags suggest something more serious than simple functional dyspepsia:

* weight loss
* GI bleeding (gastrointestinal bleeding), melena (black tarry stool), or anemia
* progressive dysphagia (worsening trouble swallowing)
* persistent vomiting
* older age with new symptoms
* family history of upper GI cancer

These push you toward endoscopy (camera test of the upper GI tract).

---

## One-line intuition

Dyspepsia = upper stomach discomfort after eating, usually from acid/irritation, abnormal stomach movement, or hypersensitive gut nerves.

### Core idea

Streptococcal pharyngitis means **strep throat**:

* infection of the pharynx (back of the throat)
* usually involving the tonsils
* caused by **group A Streptococcus**, especially **Streptococcus pyogenes**

Pharyngitis = inflammation of the pharynx.

Group A Streptococcus = a group of bacteria classified by their cell wall carbohydrate.

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* fever
* sore throat
* tonsillar exudates (pus/white material on the tonsils)
* tender anterior cervical lymph nodes (painful lymph nodes in the front of the neck)
* no cough

No cough matters because cough points more toward a viral upper respiratory infection.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The bacteria attach to the throat/tonsillar mucosa (surface lining).

Your immune system responds with inflammation:

* **red throat:** increased blood flow
* **swollen tonsils:** tissue edema (fluid swelling)
* **exudates:** neutrophils (bacteria-fighting white blood cells) + dead cells + bacteria = pus
* **fever:** cytokines (immune signaling proteins) reset the hypothalamic temperature set point
* **tender lymph nodes:** local immune cells are reacting to bacterial antigens (immune targets)

So the white tonsillar patches are basically the immune system fighting bacteria locally.

---

### Microbiology

Streptococcus pyogenes is:

* gram-positive cocci in chains
* beta-hemolytic on blood agar
* bacitracin-sensitive
* PYR-positive

Beta-hemolytic means it completely lyses red blood cells on blood agar, making a clear zone.

PYR-positive means it tests positive for pyrrolidonyl arylamidase, a lab enzyme test.

---

### Big complications

Untreated strep throat can lead to:

* acute rheumatic fever
* post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis
* scarlet fever
* peritonsillar abscess

Acute rheumatic fever is autoimmune molecular mimicry:

* antibodies against strep M protein cross-react with heart/joint/brain/skin tissues

Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis means kidney inflammation after strep infection due to immune complex deposition.

Scarlet fever is from erythrogenic toxin, which causes fever + diffuse sandpaper-like rash + strawberry tongue.

---

### Treatment idea

Treat with penicillin or amoxicillin.

Why antibiotics matter:

* shorten symptoms a bit
* reduce transmission
* prevent acute rheumatic fever

Important nuance: antibiotics do **not reliably prevent post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis**.

---

## One-line intuition

Strep throat is a group A strep infection of the tonsils/pharynx that causes a pus-heavy immune response and matters because untreated infection can trigger autoimmune complications.

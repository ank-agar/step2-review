## Vitamin D deficiency

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK551616/bin/Vitamn__D__metabolism.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279023/bin/ca-phosphate-homeost-Image008.jpg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil///phil_images/20021118/22/PHIL_2435_lores.jpg)

Vitamin D deficiency means low vitamin D, especially low **25-hydroxyvitamin D** (the main blood form used to measure vitamin D stores).

Vitamin D normally helps the gut absorb:

* calcium
* phosphate

These minerals are needed to harden bone.

So if vitamin D is low:

* less calcium is absorbed from the intestine
* serum calcium can fall
* parathyroid hormone rises
* bone mineralization gets weak

Parathyroid hormone, or **PTH** (parathyroid hormone), is the hormone that tries to keep blood calcium normal.

---

### Why the bone findings happen

Bone has a protein framework called **osteoid** (unmineralized bone matrix).

Vitamin D deficiency causes poor mineralization of osteoid.

So the body can lay down bone framework, but it cannot harden it properly.

That causes:

* **rickets** in kids: soft growing bones, bowed legs, widened wrists
* **osteomalacia** in adults: soft bones, bone pain, fractures

Kids get deformities because their growth plates are still open.

Adults get bone pain/fractures because their bones are already formed but become poorly mineralized.

---

### Classic labs

Usually:

* low 25-hydroxyvitamin D
* low calcium or normal calcium
* low phosphate
* high PTH (parathyroid hormone)
* high alkaline phosphatase

Alkaline phosphatase is high because osteoblasts (bone-forming cells) are working hard trying to build/mineralize bone.

---

## One-line intuition

Vitamin D is the gut's calcium/phosphate absorption helper; without it, bone framework gets made but cannot harden well.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, vitamin D deficiency causes **secondary hyperparathyroidism**: low vitamin D → low calcium absorption → increased PTH (parathyroid hormone) → phosphate wasting in urine → low phosphate.

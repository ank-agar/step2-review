# Pharyngeal obstruction

![Image](https://content.healthwise.net/resources/14.8/en-us/media/medical/hw/acl2702_460x300.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/9701.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/69/Obstruction_ventilation_apn%C3%A9e_sommeil.svg)

Pharyngeal obstruction means blockage of the pharynx (the throat passage behind the nose and mouth).

The pharynx is shared by:

* air going to the larynx (voice box) and trachea (windpipe)
* food going to the esophagus (food tube)

So if the pharynx gets narrowed or blocked, airflow can drop.

---

### Core idea

Think:

> the throat tube gets too narrow for air to pass well.

This can be:

* partial obstruction = noisy or difficult breathing
* complete obstruction = no effective airflow

---

### Common causes

Pharyngeal obstruction can happen from:

* relaxed tongue falling backward during sleep
* enlarged tonsils or adenoids (lymph tissue in the throat/nasopharynx, the upper throat behind the nose)
* swelling from infection or allergy
* abscess (pus collection), like peritonsillar or retropharyngeal abscess
* tumor or foreign body

---

### Why the symptoms happen

If the pharynx narrows, air has to squeeze through a smaller space.

That causes:

* snoring/stertor (low-pitched snoring-type noisy breathing) from soft tissue vibration
* increased work of breathing because resistance rises sharply as radius decreases
* hypoxia (low oxygen) if airflow is severely reduced
* obstructive sleep apnea, or OSA (repeated airway blockage during sleep), if the airway keeps collapsing at night

Key physics intuition:

> small decrease in airway radius = big increase in resistance.

So even mild swelling can make breathing much harder.

---

## One-line intuition

Pharyngeal obstruction is basically a bottleneck in the throat, so air cannot move freely from the nose/mouth into the lungs.

---

## High-yield pearl 🔥

In OSA (obstructive sleep apnea), the problem is airway obstruction despite continued breathing effort; in central sleep apnea, the brainstem drive to breathe is reduced, so respiratory effort drops.

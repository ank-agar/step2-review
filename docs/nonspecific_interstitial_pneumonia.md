## Nonspecific interstitial pneumonia

Nonspecific interstitial pneumonia, or **NSIP (nonspecific interstitial pneumonia)**, is a type of **interstitial lung disease (lung disease affecting the tissue around alveoli/air sacs)**.

Core idea:

* inflammation and/or fibrosis (scarring) happens in the lung interstitium
* the interstitium = the thin tissue between alveoli (air sacs) and blood vessels
* this makes oxygen diffusion harder

So the patient gets:

* progressive dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* dry cough
* restrictive lung disease pattern
* decreased DLCO (diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide, a test of gas transfer)

---

### Why it causes restrictive lung disease

Fibrosis makes the lung stiff.

Stiff lung = harder to expand.

So:

* decreased compliance (stretchiness)
* decreased TLC (total lung capacity)
* decreased FVC (forced vital capacity)

Think:

> scarred lung cannot open up well.

---

### CT finding

NSIP often shows:

* ground-glass opacities
* reticular markings
* lower lobe predominance
* subpleural sparing

Ground-glass opacity means the lung looks hazy on CT, but vessels are still visible.

Why?

Because the interstitium is thickened by inflammation/fibrosis, but the alveoli are not completely filled with pus like in lobar pneumonia.

---

### Big association

NSIP is classically associated with connective tissue disease, especially:

* systemic sclerosis
* polymyositis/dermatomyositis
* rheumatoid arthritis

So if you see interstitial lung disease plus autoimmune clues, think NSIP.

---

### NSIP vs UIP

**UIP (usual interstitial pneumonia)** is the pattern seen in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.

Key difference:

* NSIP = more uniform injury
* UIP = patchy, older + newer areas of fibrosis together

That is why NSIP usually has a better prognosis than UIP.

Intuition:

> NSIP is a smoother, more evenly spread interstitial injury; UIP is a messy patchwork of progressive scarring.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 association: **NSIP + systemic sclerosis**.

Also remember: systemic sclerosis can cause pulmonary hypertension from vascular disease, but NSIP is the interstitial/fibrotic lung disease pattern.

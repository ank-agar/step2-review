Hyperparathyroidism = too much parathyroid hormone (PTH) from the parathyroid glands.

Parathyroid hormone (PTH) normally raises blood calcium.

So the core idea is:

> too much PTH = body keeps trying to raise calcium, even when it should not.

---

### What PTH does

PTH raises calcium by acting on:

* **Bone:** increases bone resorption, meaning bone is broken down to release calcium into blood
* **Kidney:** increases calcium reabsorption, meaning the kidney holds onto calcium
* **Kidney:** decreases phosphate reabsorption, meaning phosphate is peed out
* **Vitamin D:** increases active vitamin D, which increases calcium absorption from the gut

So labs often look like:

* high calcium
* low phosphate
* high or inappropriately normal PTH

"Inappropriately normal" means PTH should be low when calcium is high, but it is not.

---

### Why symptoms happen

High calcium causes the classic:

* **stones:** kidney stones from excess calcium in urine
* **bones:** bone pain/fractures because PTH is pulling calcium out of bone
* **groans:** constipation/abdominal pain because high calcium slows gut smooth muscle
* **psychiatric overtones:** confusion, fatigue, depression

The bone findings happen because PTH indirectly activates osteoclasts (bone-resorbing cells) through osteoblasts (bone-building cells) using RANKL (receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand).

Translation:

> PTH tells bone cells to call in bone-chewing cells.

---

### Primary vs secondary

**Primary hyperparathyroidism:**

* problem is in the parathyroid gland itself
* usually a parathyroid adenoma, which is a benign tumor
* PTH is high
* calcium is high
* phosphate is low

**Secondary hyperparathyroidism:**

* parathyroid is reacting to low calcium
* common causes: chronic kidney disease (CKD) or vitamin D deficiency
* PTH is high
* calcium is low or normal
* phosphate is often high in chronic kidney disease (CKD)

---

## One-line intuition

Hyperparathyroidism is the body acting like calcium is too low, so it keeps stealing calcium from bone and saving calcium in the kidney.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Primary hyperparathyroidism classically causes:

* increased calcium
* decreased phosphate
* increased PTH

because PTH tells the kidney: **keep calcium, dump phosphate.**

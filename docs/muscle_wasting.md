# Muscle wasting

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19477.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a3/Cancer-associated_cachexia_diagram.jpg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/PHIL_Images/26188/26188_lores.jpg)

Muscle wasting = loss of muscle mass.

Another word for it is **muscle atrophy**.

So the core idea is:

> muscle fibers shrink because the body is breaking down muscle protein faster than it is building it.

---

### Why it happens

Muscle size depends on:

* use/load on the muscle
* nerve stimulation
* nutrition
* hormones
* inflammation

If any of those are disrupted, the muscle can shrink.

Common causes:

* **Disuse:** bed rest, immobilization, sedentary state
* **Denervation:** loss of nerve supply to the muscle
* **Malnutrition:** not enough calories/protein
* **Cachexia:** wasting syndrome from chronic disease, especially cancer or chronic infection
* **Sarcopenia:** age-related muscle loss
* **Excess glucocorticoids:** steroid effect causing protein breakdown

---

### Why disuse causes wasting

Muscle is expensive tissue to maintain.

If you stop using it, the body thinks:

> “We do not need this much contractile protein.”

So protein synthesis decreases and protein breakdown increases.

Result:

* smaller muscle fibers
* weaker contraction
* reduced endurance

---

### Why denervation causes wasting

Nerves give muscles trophic signals, meaning survival/growth signals.

If the nerve input is lost:

* muscle is not activated
* trophic support is lost
* muscle fibers shrink

That is why lower motor neuron lesions can cause:

* weakness
* fasciculations, meaning visible muscle twitches
* muscle wasting

---

## One-line intuition

Muscle wasting means the muscle is not getting enough “build/maintain” signals, so it gets broken down and shrinks.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, **muscle wasting + fasciculations** points toward a **lower motor neuron** problem, because the muscle has lost direct nerve input.

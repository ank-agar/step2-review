## Juvenile laryngeal papillomatosis

Juvenile laryngeal papillomatosis is recurrent wart-like growths in the larynx (voice box) of a child.

It is also called recurrent respiratory papillomatosis, or RRP (recurrent respiratory papillomatosis).

---

### Core idea

HPV (human papillomavirus), usually types 6 and 11, infects the airway lining and causes benign papillomas (wart-like epithelial growths).

They most often grow on the vocal cords, so the classic first symptom is:

* hoarseness

If they get bigger, they can narrow the airway and cause:

* stridor (high-pitched noisy breathing)
* chronic cough
* respiratory distress

---

### Why this happens in kids

Juvenile cases are classically from vertical transmission (spread from mother to baby during birth), especially with maternal genital HPV (human papillomavirus) infection.

The virus stays in the local mucosa (surface lining), so even after surgery removes the visible papillomas, they can come back.

That is why “recurrent” is in recurrent respiratory papillomatosis.

---

### Why the voice changes

The vocal cords need smooth edges to vibrate cleanly.

Papillomas make the cord surface bumpy and heavier, so vibration becomes irregular.

Irregular vocal cord vibration = hoarse voice.

If papillomas physically block the glottis (opening between the vocal cords), air has trouble passing through:

* partial obstruction → stridor
* severe obstruction → respiratory distress

---

## One-line intuition

HPV makes wart-like growths on a child’s vocal cords, so the kid gets recurrent hoarseness and sometimes airway obstruction.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association: juvenile laryngeal papillomatosis is usually caused by low-risk HPV (human papillomavirus) types 6 and 11, the same types classically associated with condyloma acuminata (genital warts).

### Core idea

Factor V Leiden is an inherited mutation in factor V, a clotting factor.

It makes factor V resistant to being turned off by activated protein C.

Activated protein C, or APC (activated protein C), normally acts like a brake on clotting.

So in Factor V Leiden:

* factor V stays active too long
* thrombin production keeps going
* fibrin clot formation increases
* patient becomes hypercoagulable

Hypercoagulable means the blood is too likely to clot.

---

### What mutation is it?

Classic mutation:

* factor V gene mutation
* arginine replaced by glutamine at position 506
* often written as R506Q

Why that matters:

APC (activated protein C) normally cuts factor V at this site to inactivate it.

If the cut site is mutated, APC cannot shut factor V down well.

---

### Why clots happen

Normal:

* factor V helps factor Xa convert prothrombin to thrombin
* thrombin helps make fibrin
* fibrin stabilizes the clot
* APC turns factor V off so clotting does not run forever

Factor V Leiden:

* APC cannot inactivate factor V properly
* factor V keeps helping make thrombin
* more thrombin means more fibrin
* more fibrin means more venous clots

Think:

> broken clotting brake.

---

### What does it cause?

Main risk:

* DVT (deep vein thrombosis), usually in the leg
* PE (pulmonary embolism), when a clot travels to the lungs

It is more associated with venous clots than arterial clots.

Venous clots = slow-flow clots, like leg veins.

---

### Risk factors that make it worse

Factor V Leiden risk increases with:

* pregnancy
* estrogen-containing OCPs (oral contraceptive pills)
* smoking
* surgery
* immobilization
* long travel
* obesity
* malignancy

These all push the body toward clotting, so the broken APC brake becomes more clinically important.

---

### Inheritance

Usually autosomal dominant.

Autosomal dominant means one mutated copy can increase disease risk.

Homozygous patients have much higher risk than heterozygous patients.

Homozygous means two mutated copies.

Heterozygous means one mutated copy.

---

## One-line intuition

Factor V Leiden = factor V cannot be shut off by activated protein C, so clotting keeps going and venous clots become more likely.

---

## Pearl

Factor V Leiden causes APC resistance, but protein C level can be normal. The problem is not lack of protein C; the problem is that factor V does not respond to protein C.

# Dislocation

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/98/Anterior_Dislocation.jpeg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/34/Shoulder_dislocation%2C_anteroposterior_before_reduction.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ae/Dislocated_Finger_XRay.png)

Dislocation means a bone gets forced completely out of its normal joint position.

So instead of:

* bone sitting in socket
* joint surfaces touching normally

you get:

* bone displaced away from the socket
* joint surfaces no longer aligned
* pain + deformity + loss of movement

---

### Core idea

A joint is like a fitted hinge/socket.

In a dislocation, the bones are not broken necessarily, but the relationship between them is wrong.

Example:

* shoulder dislocation = humeral head (top of arm bone) leaves the glenoid fossa (shoulder socket)
* finger dislocation = one phalanx (finger bone) slips off the next phalanx
* hip dislocation = femoral head (ball of thigh bone) leaves the acetabulum (hip socket)

---

### Why it hurts so much

When the bone pops out of place, it stretches or tears nearby:

* ligaments = connective tissue that stabilizes joints
* joint capsule = fibrous covering around the joint
* muscles/tendons = tissues that move and stabilize the joint
* nerves/blood vessels sometimes

That stretching + pressure is why dislocations are very painful and the limb often looks visibly abnormal.

---

### Important complication

Always think:

* nerve injury
* blood vessel injury
* fracture

because the displaced bone can compress or tear nearby structures.

For example, anterior shoulder dislocation can injure the axillary nerve.

Axillary nerve injury causes:

* numbness over the lateral shoulder
* weak shoulder abduction, meaning trouble lifting the arm away from the body

---

### Treatment idea

Reduction means putting the bone back into normal alignment.

So:

* dislocation = bone out of place
* reduction = bone put back in place

You usually get imaging to check for fracture and confirm alignment.

---

## One-line intuition

A dislocation is when the joint surfaces lose contact because one bone gets shoved out of its normal seat.

---

## HY pearl

Anterior shoulder dislocation is the most common major joint dislocation and classically risks axillary nerve injury.

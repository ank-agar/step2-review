### Core idea

Esophageal varices are dilated veins in the lower esophagus (food pipe).

They happen because blood cannot easily pass through the liver, so pressure backs up in the portal venous system (veins that drain blood from the gut to the liver).

Most common cause:

* cirrhosis (scarred liver)

---

### Why cirrhosis causes them

Normally:

* gut blood goes through the portal vein
* portal vein goes into the liver
* liver processes the blood

In cirrhosis:

* the liver becomes scarred and stiff
* blood has trouble flowing through it
* portal pressure rises
* blood looks for alternate routes around the liver

One alternate route is through veins near the lower esophagus.

Those veins are not built to handle high pressure, so they balloon out into varices.

---

### Why they are dangerous

Varices are thin-walled, high-pressure veins.

So if they rupture, they can cause massive upper GI bleeding (upper gastrointestinal bleeding, meaning bleeding from the esophagus, stomach, or duodenum).

Classic presentation:

* hematemesis (vomiting blood)
* melena (black, tarry stool from digested blood)
* shock signs like low blood pressure and fast heart rate

---

### Treatment intuition

You can treat them in 2 big ways:

* lower portal pressure
* directly stop/avoid bleeding

Examples:

* nonselective beta blockers (meds like propranolol that lower portal pressure)
* endoscopic band ligation (using an endoscope, a camera tube, to place rubber bands around varices)
* octreotide (a drug that decreases blood flow into the portal system during acute bleeding)
* TIPS (transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt, a liver shunt that connects portal blood to systemic venous blood to lower pressure)

---

## One-line intuition

Cirrhosis creates a traffic jam in the liver, so blood detours through fragile esophageal veins until they swell and can burst.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Esophageal varices are a portosystemic anastomosis (connection between portal and systemic veins): left gastric vein from the portal system connects with esophageal veins draining to the azygos vein in the systemic system.

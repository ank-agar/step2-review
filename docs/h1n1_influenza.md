### Core idea

H1N1 influenza is a subtype of influenza A virus.

Break the name apart:

* H = hemagglutinin, the viral surface protein that helps the virus attach to respiratory epithelial cells
* N = neuraminidase, the viral surface enzyme that helps new viruses leave infected cells
* 1 and 1 = the specific versions of those proteins

So H1N1 basically means:

> influenza A virus with hemagglutinin type 1 and neuraminidase type 1.

---

### Why it causes flu symptoms

Influenza infects respiratory epithelial cells, meaning cells lining the nose, throat, and lungs.

That causes:

* fever
* myalgias (muscle aches)
* cough
* sore throat
* headache
* fatigue

The reason symptoms feel so systemic is that your immune response releases cytokines (immune signaling proteins), which make you feel feverish and achy.

---

### Why H and N matter

Hemagglutinin helps the virus get in.

Neuraminidase helps the virus get out.

Intuition:

* H = hooks onto host cells
* N = new viruses escape

That is why neuraminidase inhibitors, like oseltamivir, reduce viral release from infected cells.

---

### Important concept

Influenza A has a segmented negative-sense RNA genome.

Segmented means its genome is split into separate pieces.

This allows reassortment, meaning two flu viruses infect the same cell and swap RNA segments.

That can cause antigenic shift, meaning a major sudden change in viral antigens that can lead to pandemics.

---

## One-line intuition

H1N1 is influenza A named by its two key surface proteins: H helps attach, N helps release.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Influenza A causes pandemics because it undergoes antigenic shift from reassortment of its segmented RNA genome.

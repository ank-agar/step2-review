## Respiratory failure due to enteral feeding

### Core idea

Enteral feeding means giving nutrition through the GI tract (gastrointestinal tract), usually with an NG tube (nasogastric tube), G-tube (gastrostomy tube), or J-tube (jejunostomy tube).

Respiratory failure due to enteral feeding means the tube feeds somehow end up causing the lungs to fail at oxygenating blood.

Most classic mechanism:

* tube feeds reflux/regurgitate upward
* material gets aspirated (inhaled into the airway)
* lungs get inflamed or infected
* gas exchange gets worse
* patient develops hypoxemia (low blood oxygen)

So the simple version:

> tube feed goes toward the lungs instead of staying in the gut.

---

### Why the respiratory failure happens

The lungs are made for air, not formula/stomach contents.

If feed enters the airway, it can cause:

* aspiration pneumonitis (chemical lung inflammation from acidic or irritating material)
* aspiration pneumonia (lung infection after aspirated material brings bacteria)
* airway obstruction if thick material plugs bronchi (airway branches)
* ARDS (acute respiratory distress syndrome), severe diffuse lung inflammation with poor oxygenation

All of these reduce oxygen movement from alveoli (tiny air sacs) into blood.

That causes:

* low O2 (oxygen)
* shortness of breath
* increased work of breathing
* need for oxygen, intubation, or mechanical ventilation in severe cases

---

### Why enteral feeding increases aspiration risk

Tube feeding can increase risk because:

* the stomach may become overfilled
* the patient may have poor gastric emptying
* the patient may be lying flat
* the patient may have weak cough/gag reflexes
* the feeding tube can keep the lower esophageal sphincter (valve between esophagus and stomach) more open
* rarely, the tube can be misplaced into the airway

High-risk patients:

* stroke
* dementia
* altered mental status
* sedation
* vomiting
* intubation
* neurologic disease causing dysphagia (difficulty swallowing)

---

## One-line intuition

Enteral feeding is supposed to feed the gut, but if the feed is aspirated into the lungs, it can inflame/infect the alveoli and cause hypoxemic respiratory failure.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Aspiration pneumonia classically affects dependent lung regions:

* upright: lower lobes, especially right lower lobe
* supine: posterior upper lobes or superior lower lobes

Right side is common because the right main bronchus is wider, shorter, and more vertical.

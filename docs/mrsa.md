### Core idea

MRSA (methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus*) is *Staphylococcus aureus* (a gram-positive cocci bacteria in clusters) that became resistant to methicillin and most beta-lactam antibiotics (cell-wall antibiotics like penicillins and cephalosporins).

So it is not a totally different organism.

It is:

* *Staphylococcus aureus*
* but with a resistance mechanism
* so usual anti-staph beta-lactams do not work well

---

### Why it is resistant

MRSA usually has the **mecA gene**.

The mecA gene makes **PBP2a** (penicillin-binding protein 2a).

PBP (penicillin-binding protein) is the bacterial enzyme that builds/cross-links the cell wall.

Normally:

* beta-lactam antibiotic binds PBP
* PBP stops working
* cell wall becomes weak
* bacteria die

But in MRSA:

* mecA makes altered PBP2a
* beta-lactams bind poorly to PBP2a
* cell wall synthesis continues
* bacteria survive

That is the whole intuition: the drug is trying to hit the lock, but MRSA changed the lock.

---

### What it causes

Most commonly, MRSA causes skin and soft tissue infections (infections of skin, fat, or muscle), like:

* abscess (pus-filled pocket)
* cellulitis (spreading skin infection)
* boils
* infected wounds

More serious cases can cause:

* pneumonia (lung infection)
* bacteremia (bacteria in the blood)
* endocarditis (infection of heart valves)
* osteomyelitis (bone infection)
* sepsis (dangerous whole-body inflammatory response to infection)

---

### Community vs hospital MRSA

**CA-MRSA** (community-associated MRSA) often affects otherwise healthy people and classically causes skin abscesses.

**HA-MRSA** (healthcare-associated MRSA) is more associated with hospitals, nursing homes, surgery, catheters, and IV lines (intravenous lines).

---

## One-line intuition

MRSA is regular *Staphylococcus aureus* that picked up mecA, making a new PBP (penicillin-binding protein) that beta-lactam antibiotics cannot bind well.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: MRSA resistance is due to **altered PBP** from **mecA**, not beta-lactamase (enzyme that destroys beta-lactam antibiotics).

Classic treatment association: serious MRSA infections are treated with **vancomycin** (cell-wall antibiotic that binds D-Ala-D-Ala), because vancomycin does not need to bind PBP.

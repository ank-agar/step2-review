### Core idea

Ventricular free wall rupture = the outer wall of a ventricle tears open, usually after an MI (myocardial infarction, heart attack).

Most commonly, this is the LV (left ventricle) free wall.

So blood leaks out of the ventricle into the pericardial sac (the fibrous bag around the heart).

---

### Why it happens after MI

After an MI, dead myocardium (heart muscle) gets digested by macrophages (cleanup immune cells).

Around days 3-7, the wall is weak because:

* dead tissue is being broken down
* collagen scar has not formed yet
* the LV is still pumping at high pressure

So the weakened wall can tear.

---

### What does it cause?

Blood enters the pericardial space and causes cardiac tamponade (heart compression from fluid/blood around the heart).

That leads to:

* hypotension (low blood pressure)
* muffled heart sounds
* JVD (jugular venous distension, bulging neck veins)
* sudden PEA (pulseless electrical activity, electrical rhythm without a pulse)

Reasoning:

The heart is being squeezed from the outside, so it cannot fill properly during diastole (relaxation/filling phase). Less filling = less cardiac output (blood pumped forward).

---

### One-line intuition

After a full-thickness MI, the dead ventricular wall gets soft and tears, so blood fills the pericardial sac and rapidly compresses the heart.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Post-MI mechanical complications classically happen days 3-7:

* free wall rupture → tamponade + sudden death
* papillary muscle rupture → acute mitral regurgitation (leaky mitral valve)
* septal rupture → VSD (ventricular septal defect, hole between ventricles)

### Core idea

Gastric polyps are little growths that stick out from the stomach lining.

Break it down:

* gastric = stomach
* polyp = protruding mucosal growth

So:

> gastric polyp = a bump growing from the stomach mucosa.

Most are found incidentally during upper endoscopy (camera test looking at the esophagus, stomach, and duodenum).

---

### Why they form

The stomach lining can grow extra tissue when it is irritated or when growth signaling is abnormal.

Common causes/associations:

* chronic gastritis = long-term stomach inflammation
* Helicobacter pylori infection = spiral bacteria that can inflame the stomach
* PPI use = proton pump inhibitor use, acid-blocking meds like omeprazole
* FAP = familial adenomatous polyposis, an inherited APC mutation syndrome with many GI (gastrointestinal) polyps

Intuition:

* inflammation makes mucosa repair itself over and over
* repeated repair can create excess tissue
* excess tissue can bulge into the stomach lumen

---

### Main types

* Fundic gland polyps: usually benign, often linked to PPI use
* Hyperplastic polyps: linked to chronic inflammation, especially Helicobacter pylori
* Adenomatous polyps: true premalignant polyps, meaning they can progress toward cancer

The most important Step 1 idea:

> Adenomatous polyps are the ones you worry about most for gastric cancer risk.

---

### Cancer risk

Most gastric polyps are benign.

But risk goes up if the polyp has:

* adenomatous histology
* dysplasia (abnormal premalignant cell growth)
* large size
* background chronic atrophic gastritis (long-term inflammation causing thinning/loss of stomach glands)

---

## One-line intuition

Gastric polyps are stomach-lining overgrowths; most are harmless, but adenomas matter because they can be premalignant.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Hyperplastic gastric polyps are associated with chronic gastritis and Helicobacter pylori, while fundic gland polyps are classically associated with chronic PPI use.

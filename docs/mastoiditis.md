### Core idea

Mastoiditis is infection/inflammation of the mastoid air cells.

Mastoid air cells = air-filled spaces in the mastoid part of the temporal bone behind the ear.

Usually it happens when acute otitis media (middle ear infection) spreads backward into the mastoid air cells.

---

### Why the findings happen

Middle ear infection can spread through the aditus ad antrum (opening connecting the middle ear to the mastoid antrum).

Then pus/inflammation fills the mastoid air cells:

* ear pain
* fever
* tenderness/redness behind the ear
* ear protrudes outward
* conductive hearing loss sometimes

Conductive hearing loss = sound is blocked from reaching the inner ear normally, often due to fluid/pus in the middle ear.

---

### Why it can be dangerous

The mastoid is close to important structures:

* brain/meninges (brain coverings)
* sigmoid sinus (large venous drainage channel)
* facial nerve

So infection can spread and cause:

* meningitis
* brain abscess
* sigmoid sinus thrombosis
* facial nerve palsy

---

## One-line intuition

A middle ear infection spreads into the honeycomb-like bone behind the ear, filling those air spaces with pus.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Classic Step 1 clue: child with recent otitis media + fever + postauricular tenderness/swelling + protruding ear = mastoiditis.

Influenza = the flu.

It is a contagious respiratory infection caused by influenza viruses, especially:

* influenza A
* influenza B

These infect the respiratory epithelium (lining cells of the nose, throat, and lungs).

---

### Core idea

Influenza is an enveloped, segmented, negative-sense RNA (ribonucleic acid) virus.

The big Step 1 buzzwords:

* **orthomyxovirus**
* **enveloped**
* **segmented RNA genome**
* **negative-sense RNA**
* **replicates in the nucleus**

Most RNA viruses replicate in the cytoplasm, so influenza going to the nucleus is weird and high-yield.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Influenza damages respiratory epithelium and triggers a strong cytokine (immune signaling protein) response.

That gives:

* fever
* chills
* myalgias (muscle aches)
* headache
* fatigue
* dry cough
* sore throat

The “hit by a truck” feeling is mostly from cytokines, not just the virus sitting in the nose.

---

### Surface proteins

Influenza A is named by its two major surface proteins:

* **H = hemagglutinin**, helps the virus attach to respiratory cells
* **N = neuraminidase**, helps newly made viruses leave infected cells

So **H1N1** means:

* hemagglutinin type 1
* neuraminidase type 1

Intuition:

* H gets the virus **in**
* N gets the virus **out**

---

### Antigenic drift vs shift

**Antigenic drift** = small mutations over time.

This causes seasonal flu changes, so the vaccine needs updating.

**Antigenic shift** = big reassortment (gene segment swapping) between different influenza A strains.

This can cause pandemics because the immune system sees a much more “new-looking” virus.

Why shift happens:

Influenza has a segmented genome, so if two strains infect the same cell, they can swap RNA segments like mixed deck cards.

---

### Treatment/prevention

Treatment:

* oseltamivir
* zanamivir

These are neuraminidase inhibitors, meaning they block viral release from infected cells.

Prevention:

* annual influenza vaccine

Annual vaccine is needed because antigenic drift changes the viral antigens (immune-recognized surface proteins).

---

## One-line intuition

Influenza is a segmented RNA respiratory virus that constantly changes its surface proteins, causing abrupt fever, aches, cough, and yearly vaccine updates.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Influenza virus has **8 RNA segments**, and antigenic **shift** happens when those segments reassort, classically involving influenza A strains from humans, birds, and pigs.

## Tracheomalacia

![Image](https://www.chop.edu/sites/default/files/styles/original_320x_2x/public/tracheomalacia-composition-780x364.jpg?itok=mw1jwipf)

![Image](https://tracheostomyeducation.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Tracheomalacia-1.jpg)

### Core idea

Tracheomalacia is when the trachea (windpipe) is too floppy and collapses during breathing.

Basically:

* normal tracheal cartilage keeps the airway open
* weak cartilage lets the airway narrow/collapse
* airflow gets blocked, especially during expiration (breathing out)

---

### Why symptoms happen

The airway is not rigid enough, so pressure changes during breathing can make it cave in.

That causes:

* **expiratory stridor/wheeze:** airway collapses more when breathing out
* **barky cough:** turbulent airflow through a floppy airway
* **recurrent infections:** poor airway clearance traps mucus
* **worse with crying/coughing/feeding:** these increase airflow/pressure swings, so the airway collapses more

---

## One-line intuition

Tracheomalacia = floppy trachea, so the airway caves in when the baby tries to move air forcefully.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Tracheomalacia is associated with tracheoesophageal fistula (abnormal connection between trachea and esophagus) and can cause noisy breathing that worsens with feeding or crying.

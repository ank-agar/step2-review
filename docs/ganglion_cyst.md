### Core idea

A ganglion cyst is a benign (noncancerous), fluid-filled sac near a joint or tendon sheath (covering around a tendon).

Most common spot:

* wrist

But it can also show up on:

* fingers
* foot
* ankle

---

### What is inside it?

Thick, clear, jelly-like fluid.

Think:

> joint fluid leaks/bulges out and gets trapped in a little sac.

So it is not a tumor of nerve ganglia (clusters of nerve cell bodies), even though the name sounds like that.

---

### Why the bump changes size

The cyst connects to nearby joint/tendon tissue.

So with more movement or irritation:

* more fluid can collect
* the cyst can look bigger
* pressure can cause pain or tingling

If it presses on a nearby nerve:

* numbness/tingling can happen

If it presses mechanically near a joint:

* decreased range of motion can happen

---

### What you should think clinically

Usually:

* smooth
* round or oval
* soft or firm
* mobile under the skin
* often painless

Treatment is often just observation because many go away on their own.

If painful or limiting movement:

* aspiration (removing fluid with a needle)
* surgery if recurrent or severe

---

## One-line intuition

A ganglion cyst is basically a little balloon of jelly-like joint/tendon fluid bulging out near a joint.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Ganglion cysts are the most common soft tissue mass of the hand/wrist, and they are benign (noncancerous).

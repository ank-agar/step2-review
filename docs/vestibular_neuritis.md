# Vestibular neuritis

![Image](https://content.healthwise.net/resources/14.6/en-ca/media/medical/hw/acm5574_460x300.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fb/Vestibular_system%27s_semicircular_canal-_a_cross-section.jpg)

![Image](https://www.e-jyms.org/upload/thumbnails/yujm-2021-01228f1.jpg)

Vestibular neuritis is inflammation of the **vestibular nerve**, which is the balance part of **CN VIII (cranial nerve 8, the vestibulocochlear nerve)**.

Break it down:

* **vestibular** = balance system of the inner ear
* **neuritis** = nerve inflammation

So vestibular neuritis literally means:

> inflamed balance nerve.

### Core idea

One vestibular nerve suddenly stops sending normal balance signals to the brain.

The brain now gets mismatched input:

* normal signal from one inner ear
* weak/abnormal signal from the inflamed side
* visual system says "I am not moving"

That mismatch makes the patient feel like the room is spinning.

This spinning sensation is **vertigo (false sensation of movement or spinning)**.

### Classic presentation

Vestibular neuritis causes:

* sudden severe vertigo
* nausea/vomiting
* gait instability
* nystagmus (rhythmic involuntary eye movement)
* recent viral illness often

Very important:

* **no hearing loss**
* **no tinnitus (ringing in the ears)**

Why no hearing loss?

Because the problem is mainly the **vestibular branch (balance branch)**, not the **cochlear branch (hearing branch)** of CN VIII.

### Why nystagmus happens

The eyes are connected to the inner ear through the **vestibulo-ocular reflex (reflex that stabilizes gaze during head movement)**.

If one vestibular nerve is underactive, the brain interprets it like the head is turning, even when it is not.

So the eyes drift one way and quickly snap back the other way.

That snapping eye movement is nystagmus.

### Vestibular neuritis vs labyrinthitis

Think:

* **vestibular neuritis** = vertigo without hearing loss
* **labyrinthitis** = vertigo with hearing loss

Why?

The **labyrinth (inner ear structure containing balance and hearing organs)** includes both vestibular and cochlear components, so inflammation there can affect hearing too.

### One-line intuition

Vestibular neuritis is basically **one inflamed balance nerve lying to the brain**, so the brain thinks the body is spinning.

### HY Step 1 pearl

Acute prolonged vertigo + nausea + nystagmus **without hearing loss** points to **vestibular neuritis**. If there is hearing loss, think **labyrinthitis** or **Meniere disease (episodic vertigo with hearing loss, tinnitus, and ear fullness)** instead.

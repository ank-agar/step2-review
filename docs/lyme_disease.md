## Lyme disease

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/lyme/media/images/EM-rashes.jpg)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/lyme/media/images/Classic-Lyme-Disease-Rash.jpg)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/lyme/images/ticks-life-stages.jpg)

Lyme disease is a tick-borne infection caused by **Borrelia burgdorferi**, a **spirochete** (spiral-shaped bacterium).

It is transmitted by the **Ixodes tick** (blacklegged/deer tick).

Think:

* tick bite
* spirochete enters skin
* spreads outward locally first
* can later spread to joints, heart, and nervous system

---

### Classic early finding

**Erythema migrans** (expanding red rash) is the classic rash.

It can look like a **bull's-eye**, but it does **not always** have perfect central clearing.

Break the term apart:

* erythema = redness
* migrans = migrating/spreading

So:

> a red rash that expands outward from the bite site.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The bacteria initially multiply in the skin, causing:

* expanding rash
* fever
* headache
* fatigue
* muscle/joint aches

If untreated, the spirochete can disseminate (spread through the body), causing:

* **facial nerve palsy** (weakness of cranial nerve VII, the facial nerve)
* **meningitis** (inflammation of the meninges, the coverings of the brain/spinal cord)
* **AV block** (atrioventricular conduction block, slowed electrical conduction from atria to ventricles)
* migratory arthritis, especially large joints like the knee

---

### Diagnosis intuition

Early Lyme with classic erythema migrans is usually a clinical diagnosis.

Serology (blood antibody testing) can be falsely negative early because the immune system has not made enough antibody yet.

So if the rash is classic after tick exposure, do not wait for labs to become positive.

---

### Treatment

Common treatment is **doxycycline**.

Use alternatives like amoxicillin in some patients where doxycycline is avoided, such as young children or pregnancy, depending on the clinical situation.

---

## One-line intuition

Lyme disease is a Borrelia spirochete infection from an Ixodes tick that starts with an expanding rash and can later spread to the heart, nerves, and joints.

---

## Pearl 🔥

The Step 1 buzzword triad is:

* erythema migrans
* facial nerve palsy
* migratory arthritis

Also remember: Ixodes tick can transmit other infections too, especially **Babesia** (malaria-like parasite causing hemolytic anemia) and **Anaplasma** (intracellular bacterium infecting granulocytes).

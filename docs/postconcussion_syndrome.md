# Postconcussion syndrome

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/17143.jpg)

![Image](https://braininjurycanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Concussion-Signs-Symptoms.jpg)

![Image](https://braininjurycanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Concussion-coping.jpg)

Postconcussion syndrome, aka **PCS (postconcussion syndrome)**, is when symptoms continue after a concussion.

A concussion is a mild **TBI (traumatic brain injury)** where the brain is shaken, but there may be no obvious bleeding or structural damage on imaging.

Common symptoms:

* headache
* dizziness
* fatigue
* trouble concentrating
* memory issues
* irritability/anxiety
* sleep problems
* sensitivity to light/noise

---

### Core idea

The brain got “shaken,” and even after the initial injury, the brain’s normal signaling/recovery systems stay irritated.

So PCS is less about a big visible brain lesion and more about persistent brain dysfunction after mild trauma.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

After concussion, neurons can have:

* stretched axons
* altered ion flow
* increased energy demand
* reduced ability to regulate blood flow/metabolism

So the brain is working inefficiently.

That gives:

* **headache:** irritated pain pathways
* **dizziness/balance issues:** vestibular system dysfunction
  * vestibular system = inner ear + brain pathways that control balance
* **brain fog/concentration problems:** frontal lobe networks are not processing normally
* **sleep/mood symptoms:** autonomic and limbic system disruption
  * autonomic = automatic body functions like heart rate/sweating
  * limbic system = emotion/memory circuits

---

## One-line intuition

Postconcussion syndrome is the brain staying “glitchy” after a concussion, even when CT/MRI may look normal.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 point: concussion often has **normal CT (computed tomography)** because the problem is usually functional/microscopic, not a large bleed or mass lesion.

Also: avoid a second head injury before recovery because repeat concussion can rarely cause dangerous cerebral edema, especially in young athletes.

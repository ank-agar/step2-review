Dysmenorrhea means painful menstruation.

Break the word apart:

* dys- = painful/abnormal
* meno- = menses/menstruation
* -rrhea = flow

So:

> painful menstrual flow.

---

### Core idea

The uterus cramps too hard during menstruation, causing lower abdominal/pelvic pain.

The main mechanism in primary dysmenorrhea is:

* endometrial shedding releases prostaglandins
* prostaglandins make the myometrium contract
* myometrium = uterine smooth muscle layer
* strong contractions compress blood vessels
* less blood flow causes ischemic pain

Ischemia means low blood flow/low oxygen to tissue.

---

### Primary vs secondary

**Primary dysmenorrhea** = painful periods without an underlying pelvic disease.

Usually starts soon after menarche, meaning the first menstrual period.

**Secondary dysmenorrhea** = painful periods due to another condition, like:

* endometriosis
* adenomyosis
* fibroids
* pelvic inflammatory disease

Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) means infection/inflammation of the upper female reproductive tract.

---

### Why NSAIDs help

NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) inhibit COX (cyclooxygenase).

COX makes prostaglandins.

So:

less COX -> less prostaglandins -> less uterine contraction -> less cramping pain.

This is why ibuprofen/naproxen work best if taken early, before prostaglandins peak.

---

## One-line intuition

Dysmenorrhea is prostaglandin-driven uterine squeezing that temporarily cuts down uterine blood flow and causes crampy pain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Primary dysmenorrhea is treated first-line with NSAIDs because the problem is excess prostaglandin-mediated uterine contraction.

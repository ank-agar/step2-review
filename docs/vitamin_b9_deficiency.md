## Vitamin B9 deficiency

![Image](https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/images/a/a8/Folate_one-carbon_metabolism.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/51/Hypersegmented_neutrophil.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7e/Spina-bifida.jpg)

Vitamin B9 = folate.

So vitamin B9 deficiency means low folate, which mainly causes:

* megaloblastic anemia (big immature red blood cell precursors due to impaired DNA synthesis)
* glossitis (inflamed tongue)
* neural tube defects in pregnancy

---

### Core idea

Folate is needed to make DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid), especially thymidine and purines (DNA building blocks).

Fast-dividing cells get hit hardest:

* bone marrow
* GI (gastrointestinal) lining
* developing fetal neural tube

---

### Why the cells get big

Low folate → poor DNA synthesis.

The cytoplasm (inside body of the cell) keeps growing, but the nucleus cannot divide normally.

So the cell becomes:

* large
* immature-looking
* stuck before proper division

That is why you get macrocytosis (large red blood cells) and megaloblasts (large immature red blood cell precursors).

On smear, think:

* macro-ovalocytes (large oval red blood cells)
* hypersegmented neutrophils (neutrophils with too many nuclear lobes)

---

### Labs

Classic labs:

* increased MCV (mean corpuscular volume = red blood cell size)
* decreased folate
* increased homocysteine
* normal methylmalonic acid

Key comparison:

* B9 deficiency: increased homocysteine, normal methylmalonic acid
* B12 deficiency: increased homocysteine, increased methylmalonic acid

---

### Causes

Common causes:

* poor diet
* alcoholism
* pregnancy
* malabsorption (poor nutrient absorption)
* methotrexate, trimethoprim, phenytoin

Methotrexate and trimethoprim inhibit DHFR (dihydrofolate reductase), which prevents folate from being recycled into its active form.

---

### B9 vs B12

Both cause megaloblastic anemia.

But folate deficiency does **not** cause neurologic symptoms.

B12 deficiency can cause neurologic problems because B12 is needed for myelin (insulation around nerves).

So if a patient has numbness, tingling, gait issues, or loss of vibration sense, think B12 more than B9.

---

## One-line intuition

Folate is for DNA synthesis; without it, fast-dividing cells cannot divide, so blood cells become big and immature.

---

## HY pearl

Always rule out B12 deficiency before giving folate, because folate can fix the anemia but does not fix the neurologic damage from B12 deficiency.

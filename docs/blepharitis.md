### Core idea

Blepharitis = inflammation of the eyelid margin, especially around the eyelashes and meibomian glands (oil glands in the eyelid that help stabilize the tear film).

Break the word apart:

* blepharo- = eyelid
* -itis = inflammation

So blepharitis literally means:

> inflamed eyelid.

---

### What it looks/feels like

Classic findings:

* red, irritated eyelid edges
* crusting or flakes at the base of eyelashes
* burning, itching, tearing
* gritty “sand in the eye” feeling
* eyelids stuck together in the morning

---

### Why the findings happen

The eyelid margin gets chronically inflamed.

That inflammation causes:

* **crusting:** debris, oil, bacteria, and dead skin collect around lashes
* **gritty sensation:** irritated eyelid rubs against the ocular surface
* **dry eye symptoms:** meibomian glands (oil glands) do not release normal oil, so tears evaporate faster
* **morning stickiness:** crusts and secretions build up while the eyes are closed overnight

---

### Main types

**Anterior blepharitis** = front eyelid/lash area.

Common associations:

* Staphylococcus aureus (a common skin bacterium)
* seborrheic dermatitis (dandruff-like oily/flaky skin inflammation)

**Posterior blepharitis** = inner eyelid margin near the meibomian glands.

Common association:

* meibomian gland dysfunction, often with rosacea (chronic facial redness/inflammatory skin disease)

---

## One-line intuition

Blepharitis is basically “dandruff/inflammation of the eyelid margin,” and when the oil glands get clogged, the tears evaporate too fast and the eye feels dry and gritty.

---

## HY pearl

Blepharitis can predispose to chalazion (blocked meibomian gland causing a usually painless eyelid nodule) and hordeolum/stye (acute infected eyelid gland causing a painful red bump).

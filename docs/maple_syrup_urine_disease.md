## Maple syrup urine disease

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK22214/bin/msud.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557773/bin/Table__1__Characteristics__of__the__different__types__of__Maple__Syrup__urine__disease.jpg)

### Core idea

Maple syrup urine disease is an autosomal recessive (need 2 bad copies) metabolic disorder where the body cannot break down branched-chain amino acids.

Branched-chain amino acids are:

* leucine
* isoleucine
* valine

The enzyme problem is:

* deficient branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase complex

This enzyme normally helps break down leucine, isoleucine, and valine after they are converted into ketoacids (acidic breakdown products).

---

### Why it smells like maple syrup

Because the branched-chain amino acids and their ketoacid byproducts build up in blood and urine.

Those byproducts create the classic:

* sweet maple syrup smell in urine
* sometimes sweet smell in earwax

So the name is literal: toxic amino acid byproducts spill into urine and smell sweet.

---

### Why the baby gets neurologic symptoms

The buildup is toxic to the brain, especially leucine.

That causes:

* poor feeding
* vomiting
* lethargy
* abnormal movements
* seizures
* coma if severe/untreated

Reasoning:

If the enzyme is blocked, protein intake keeps adding more leucine/isoleucine/valine, but the body cannot clear them. They accumulate and injure the CNS (central nervous system), so the baby looks progressively encephalopathic (brain-dysfunction state).

---

### High-yield associations

Think:

* newborn with poor feeding
* vomiting/lethargy
* seizures or abnormal movements
* sweet-smelling urine
* metabolic acidosis/ketosis

Inheritance:

* autosomal recessive

Treatment idea:

* restrict branched-chain amino acids in diet
* give special formula
* manage acute metabolic crises aggressively

Some variants respond to thiamine, which is vitamin B1.

---

## One-line intuition

Maple syrup urine disease = inability to break down leucine, isoleucine, and valine, so toxic sweet-smelling ketoacids build up and damage the brain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, the key enzyme is branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase, and it uses thiamine pyrophosphate, which is vitamin B1-derived.

So if you see a thiamine-responsive version of MSUD (maple syrup urine disease), that makes biochemical sense because thiamine helps this enzyme complex work.

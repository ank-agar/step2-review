## Fever of unknown origin

![Image](https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/courses-images/wp-content/uploads/sites/1094/2016/11/10194119/OSC_Microbio_17_06_Hypothal1-1024x524.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/65d6/3884594/5a2244de6c3f/bmjopen2013003971f02.jpg)

Fever of unknown origin, or **FUO (fever of unknown origin)**, means:

* fever is real
* fever lasts a long time
* the cause is still not obvious after appropriate evaluation

Classic definition:

* temperature **>38.3 C / 101 F**
* for **>3 weeks**
* no diagnosis after a reasonable workup

---

### Core idea

FUO is not one disease.

It is a **diagnostic bucket** for prolonged fever where the usual quick causes have not shown themselves yet.

Think:

> The body is acting like something inflammatory is happening, but we have not found the source yet.

---

### Big cause categories

The main buckets are:

* **infection**
* **malignancy (cancer)**
* **autoimmune/inflammatory disease**
* **drug fever**
* **unknown even after workup**

Important examples:

* infection: tuberculosis, abscess, endocarditis
* malignancy: lymphoma, renal cell carcinoma
* autoimmune/inflammatory: adult Still disease, giant cell arteritis, lupus
* drugs: antibiotics, anticonvulsants, allopurinol

---

### Why fever happens

Fever is usually cytokine-driven.

Immune cells release:

* **IL-1 (interleukin-1)**
* **IL-6 (interleukin-6)**
* **TNF-alpha (tumor necrosis factor-alpha)**

These increase **PGE2 (prostaglandin E2)** in the hypothalamus, which raises the body's temperature set point.

So the body feels "cold" at the old normal temperature and responds with:

* chills
* shivering
* vasoconstriction

That is why fever often comes with chills.

---

### Why the cause can be hard to find

Some causes hide well.

For example:

* **endocarditis** can cause persistent fever with subtle heart findings
* **tuberculosis** can be extrapulmonary, meaning outside the lungs
* **abscesses** can be deep in the abdomen or pelvis
* **lymphoma** can cause fever from cytokine release before obvious mass symptoms
* **giant cell arteritis** can present mostly as fever in older adults

So FUO is often solved by careful history, repeated physical exams, cultures, inflammatory markers, imaging, and sometimes biopsy.

---

## One-line intuition

FUO means a long fever is being driven by some hidden inflammatory signal, most often infection, cancer, autoimmune disease, or a drug.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

**Lymphoma** is a classic malignancy associated with FUO because tumor/immune cells can release cytokines like **IL-1, IL-6, and TNF-alpha**, which increase **PGE2** and reset the hypothalamic thermostat upward.

# Diabetic retinopathy

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/17150.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9f/Fundus_Proliferative_retinopathy_EDA01.JPG)

Diabetic retinopathy means damage to the retina (light-sensitive layer in the back of the eye) from chronic diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar disease).

Core idea:

High glucose damages tiny retinal blood vessels, so they become:

* weak
* leaky
* blocked
* fragile

That causes retinal bleeding, fluid leakage, ischemia (low oxygen from poor blood flow), and eventually abnormal new blood vessel growth.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Microaneurysms:** tiny weakened vessel outpouchings from vessel wall damage
* **Hemorrhages:** damaged vessels leak blood
* **Hard exudates:** leaked lipid/protein deposits from leaky vessels
* **Cotton-wool spots:** small retinal infarcts (areas of tissue death from ischemia)
* **Macular edema:** fluid leaks into the macula (central retina for sharp vision), causing blurry central vision

---

### Nonproliferative vs proliferative

**NPDR (nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy):**

* vessel leakage and blockage
* microaneurysms, hemorrhages, exudates, cotton-wool spots
* no new vessel growth yet

**PDR (proliferative diabetic retinopathy):**

* ischemic retina releases VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor, a signal that grows blood vessels)
* new abnormal vessels grow
* these vessels are fragile, so they bleed easily

That is why PDR can cause vitreous hemorrhage (bleeding into the gel inside the eye) and tractional retinal detachment (scar tissue pulls the retina off).

---

## One-line intuition

Diabetes slowly injures retinal capillaries, and the retina responds by leaking, bleeding, and eventually growing bad fragile vessels.

---

## Pearl 🔥

The biggest Step 1 distinction:

* **NPDR:** microaneurysms + hemorrhages
* **PDR:** neovascularization (new abnormal blood vessels) due to VEGF from retinal ischemia

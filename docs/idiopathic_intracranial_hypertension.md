# Idiopathic intracranial hypertension

### Core idea

Idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH) means:

* idiopathic = unknown cause
* intracranial = inside the skull
* hypertension = high pressure

So IIH is **high intracranial pressure (ICP; pressure inside the skull) without a mass, tumor, hydrocephalus (too much cerebrospinal fluid in ventricles), infection, or other clear cause**.

It used to be called **pseudotumor cerebri**:

* pseudo = false
* tumor = mass
* cerebri = brain

because it can act like a brain tumor by causing headache + papilledema, but there is no actual tumor.

---

### What is actually high-pressure?

The pressure is from **CSF (cerebrospinal fluid; fluid around the brain and spinal cord)**.

Normally, CSF is made, circulates around the brain/spinal cord, and gets reabsorbed.

In IIH, the pressure in that CSF space is too high, even though imaging does not show a mass blocking things.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

High CSF pressure pushes on structures inside the skull:

* **Headache:** pressure stretches pain-sensitive meninges (brain coverings)
* **Papilledema:** high pressure travels along the optic nerve sheath and makes the optic disc swell
* **Transient visual obscurations:** brief vision dimming/blackouts from pressure on the optic nerve
* **Pulsatile tinnitus:** whooshing sound from pressure/venous flow changes near the ear
* **CN VI palsy:** abducens nerve (cranial nerve 6; lateral rectus muscle nerve) gets stretched → horizontal diplopia (double vision)

The optic nerve is basically an extension of the CNS (central nervous system), so pressure around the brain can be transmitted to the back of the eye.

---

### Classic patient

Think:

* obese woman
* childbearing age
* headache
* papilledema
* normal brain imaging
* elevated opening pressure on lumbar puncture

Lumbar puncture (LP; spinal tap) shows:

* high opening pressure
* normal CSF composition

That matters because infection or malignancy would usually change the CSF contents.

---

### Drugs associated with it

Important associations:

* tetracyclines
* vitamin A excess
* retinoids, like isotretinoin

These can cause a secondary pseudotumor cerebri picture.

---

## One-line intuition

IIH is like the brain is under too much CSF pressure, so the pressure backs up into the optic nerves and causes papilledema, even though there is no tumor.

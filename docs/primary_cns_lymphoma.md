# Primary CNS lymphoma

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/24519.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK545145/bin/CNS__lymphoma.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7a/Primary_central_nervous_system_B-cell_non-Hodgkin_lymphoma.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ae/Primary_CNS_lymphoma_-_high_mag.jpg)

Primary CNS lymphoma (primary central nervous system lymphoma) is a lymphoma (cancer of lymphocytes, which are immune cells) that starts in the CNS (central nervous system: brain, spinal cord, eyes, and cerebrospinal fluid).

Most cases are diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (aggressive cancer of mature B lymphocytes).

---

### Core idea

It is basically:

* a B-cell lymphoma
* living inside the brain/CNS
* without lymphoma starting somewhere else in the body

So "primary" means:

> the CNS is the original/main site, not spread from systemic lymphoma.

---

### Why it causes neuro symptoms

The tumor grows inside tight CNS space, so it can cause:

* headache from increased pressure
* seizures from cortical irritation
* focal neurologic deficits (weakness, vision changes, cranial nerve problems) from local brain involvement
* altered mental status if deeper brain structures are affected

It often likes deep/periventricular areas (near the ventricles, the CSF-filled spaces in the brain), because malignant lymphocytes commonly track around blood vessels.

---

### Classic associations

Think of it especially in immunocompromised patients (patients with weakened immune systems), like:

* AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome)
* transplant patients on immunosuppression

In immunocompromised patients, it is often associated with EBV (Epstein-Barr virus).

---

### Imaging intuition

On MRI (magnetic resonance imaging), primary CNS lymphoma often appears as an enhancing brain mass, commonly deep or periventricular.

"Enhancing" means it lights up with contrast because the tumor disrupts the blood-brain barrier (tight blood vessel barrier that normally protects the brain).

---

### Step 1 contrast: toxoplasmosis

In an AIDS patient with brain lesions:

* toxoplasmosis = usually multiple ring-enhancing lesions, often basal ganglia
* primary CNS lymphoma = often solitary enhancing lesion, often periventricular

Not perfect in real life, but very testable.

---

## One-line intuition

Primary CNS lymphoma is an aggressive B-cell lymphoma trapped inside the brain/CNS, often in immunocompromised patients, causing mass-effect neurologic symptoms.

---

## HY pearl

Primary CNS lymphoma in AIDS is classically EBV-associated and often presents as an enhancing periventricular brain lesion.

# Uterine inversion

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615337/bin/ch10f67.jpg)

![Image](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/-/scassets/images/org/health/articles/22326-uterine-inversion)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615337/bin/ch10f67.gif)

Uterine inversion = the uterus turns inside out, usually right after delivery.

Think:

> the fundus gets pulled downward through the cervix.

Fundus = the top part of the uterus.

Cervix = the lower opening of the uterus into the vagina.

---

### Core idea

Normally after delivery, the uterus should contract and shrink down.

In uterine inversion, the top of the uterus collapses inward and can get pulled down:

* into the uterine cavity
* through the cervix
* into or outside the vagina

So instead of the uterus being a firm ball in the abdomen, it may feel absent on abdominal exam.

---

### Why it happens

Classic setup:

* placenta is still attached
* someone pulls on the umbilical cord
* uterus is relaxed/atonic

Umbilical cord traction = pulling on the cord to deliver the placenta.

Uterine atony = the uterus is not contracting well.

If the placenta is still stuck to the fundus, pulling the cord can drag the fundus downward.

It is like pulling the inside of a sock outward.

---

### Major risks

High-yield risk factors:

* excessive cord traction
* fundal pressure before placental separation
* placenta accreta
* retained placenta
* uterine atony
* short umbilical cord

Placenta accreta = placenta attaches too deeply into the uterine wall.

Retained placenta = placenta does not come out normally after delivery.

---

### Presentation

Uterine inversion is an obstetric emergency.

Key findings:

* postpartum hemorrhage
* severe pelvic/abdominal pain
* shock
* smooth round mass in the vagina
* absent uterine fundus on abdominal exam

Postpartum hemorrhage = heavy bleeding after delivery.

Shock = poor tissue perfusion, often from severe blood loss.

---

### Why shock can be dramatic

There are 2 reasons:

* hemorrhage: the uterus cannot clamp down well, so bleeding continues
* vagal response: sudden stretching/pulling of pelvic structures can trigger bradycardia and hypotension

Bradycardia = low heart rate.

Hypotension = low blood pressure.

So the patient may look much more unstable than expected.

---

### Treatment intuition

First move:

* push the uterus back into place manually

Then:

* give uterotonics to make the uterus contract

Uterotonics = drugs that make the uterus contract, like oxytocin.

Important logic:

Do not give uterotonics before replacing the uterus, because a contracted cervix/uterus can trap the inverted uterus and make replacement harder.

---

## One-line intuition

Uterine inversion is the postpartum uterus getting pulled inside out, causing massive bleeding and shock.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Uterine inversion is classically associated with excessive traction on the umbilical cord before the placenta has separated, especially with placenta accreta.

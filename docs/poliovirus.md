# Poliovirus

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bc/Polio_spinal_diagram-en.svg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/PHIL_Images/22498/22498_lores.jpg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/PHIL_Images/1837/1837_lores.jpg)

Poliovirus is a small **non-enveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus**.

Break that down:

* **non-enveloped** = no lipid outer coat, so it is tougher in the environment/GI tract
* **positive-sense RNA** = its RNA can act like mRNA (messenger RNA) and be translated directly by host ribosomes
* **GI tract** = gastrointestinal tract

It is in the:

* Picornaviridae family
* Enterovirus genus

---

### Core idea

Poliovirus enters through the mouth, replicates in the GI tract, and in a small number of cases spreads to the CNS (central nervous system), where it destroys lower motor neurons.

Most infections are asymptomatic.

But if it reaches the spinal cord:

* it attacks anterior horn cells
* anterior horn cells = lower motor neurons in the ventral spinal cord
* lower motor neuron damage causes flaccid paralysis

---

### Why paralysis happens

Anterior horn cells are the motor neurons that directly send signals to muscles.

So if poliovirus destroys them:

* no motor signal reaches muscle
* muscle becomes weak/floppy
* reflexes decrease
* muscle can atrophy (shrink) over time

That is why polio causes **asymmetric flaccid paralysis**.

Asymmetric = one side/limb can be worse than the other.

Flaccid = limp/low tone.

---

### Transmission

Classically spreads by the fecal-oral route.

Meaning:

* virus is shed in stool
* contaminated hands/food/water reach the mouth
* virus infects the GI tract

This is why sanitation and vaccination are so important.

---

### Vaccines

Two important vaccine types:

* IPV (inactivated polio vaccine): killed virus, injected
* OPV (oral polio vaccine): live attenuated virus, oral

Live attenuated = weakened but still living.

Big Step 1 idea:

* OPV gives strong gut immunity because it replicates in the intestine
* IPV is safer because it cannot revert to virulence

Revert to virulence = mutate back toward a disease-causing form.

---

## One-line intuition

Poliovirus is a tough fecal-oral RNA virus that usually stays in the gut, but if it reaches the spinal anterior horn, it kills lower motor neurons and causes flaccid paralysis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Polio causes **lower motor neuron signs**:

* flaccid paralysis
* decreased reflexes
* muscle atrophy
* fasciculations sometimes

because it destroys **anterior horn cells** in the spinal cord.

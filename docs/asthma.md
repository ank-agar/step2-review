## Asthma

![Image](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/sites/default/files/inline-images/NHLBI_Fig04_final02%20English.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19375.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19321.jpg)

Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disease of the airways where the bronchi become:

* hyperreactive
* narrowed
* mucus-filled
* intermittently obstructed

So the core problem is:

> airways overreact to triggers and clamp down.

---

### What happens in an asthma attack

Three big things narrow the airway:

* **bronchoconstriction:** smooth muscle around the airway tightens
* **airway edema:** the airway wall swells from inflammation
* **mucus plugging:** extra mucus blocks the lumen

Lumen = the hollow inside space of a tube.

So air has trouble moving through, especially when breathing out.

That causes:

* wheezing
* shortness of breath
* cough
* chest tightness

---

### Why wheezing happens

Wheezing is just air moving through a narrowed tube.

Like blowing through a tight straw:

* normal airway = quiet airflow
* narrowed airway = high-pitched turbulent airflow

---

### Allergic asthma mechanism

Classic allergic asthma is driven by a **type I hypersensitivity reaction**.

Type I hypersensitivity = immediate allergy reaction mediated by **IgE (immunoglobulin E)**.

Basic sequence:

* allergen enters airway
* **Th2 cells (T helper 2 cells)** activate
* Th2 cells release **IL-4 (interleukin-4)**, **IL-5 (interleukin-5)**, and **IL-13 (interleukin-13)**
* IL-4 increases IgE production
* IL-5 recruits eosinophils
* IL-13 increases mucus production

So asthma is not just “tight airways.”

It is:

> tight airways + inflamed airways + mucus.

---

### Early vs late phase

**Early phase:**

* happens quickly
* mast cells release histamine/leukotrienes
* causes bronchoconstriction

**Late phase:**

* happens hours later
* eosinophils cause ongoing inflammation
* causes edema, mucus, and airway damage

This is why rescue inhalers help symptoms fast, but steroids are needed to calm the inflammation.

---

### Treatment intuition

**Albuterol** is a **SABA (short-acting beta-2 agonist)**.

It stimulates beta-2 receptors on bronchial smooth muscle.

Beta-2 activation relaxes airway smooth muscle, so:

> albuterol opens the airway quickly.

**Inhaled corticosteroids** reduce airway inflammation, so:

> steroids prevent attacks; albuterol treats acute bronchoconstriction.

---

### Step 1 pathology buzzwords

Asthma can show:

* eosinophils
* Curschmann spirals, which are spiral-shaped mucus plugs
* Charcot-Leyden crystals, which come from eosinophil breakdown
* thickened basement membrane
* smooth muscle hypertrophy, meaning enlarged airway muscle
* goblet cell hyperplasia, meaning increased mucus-producing cells

These all fit the same theme:

> chronic inflammation makes the airway thicker, twitchier, and more mucus-producing.

---

## One-line intuition

Asthma is reversible airway narrowing from hyperreactive, inflamed, mucus-producing bronchi.

---

## Pearl

Asthma is classically **obstructive**, so spirometry shows decreased **FEV1/FVC (forced expiratory volume in 1 second / forced vital capacity)**, and it improves after a bronchodilator.

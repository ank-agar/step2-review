## Hemolytic disease of the newborn

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/2913.jpg)

Hemolytic disease of the newborn is when maternal antibodies cross the placenta and destroy fetal/newborn RBCs (red blood cells).

Most classic cause:

* Rh incompatibility: Rh-negative mom + Rh-positive fetus

Also common:

* ABO incompatibility: usually type O mom + type A or B baby

---

### Core idea

Mom's immune system sees the baby's RBC antigen (surface marker) as foreign.

Then mom makes IgG (immunoglobulin G) antibodies.

IgG can cross the placenta, so it enters the fetus and attacks fetal RBCs.

Result:

* hemolysis = RBC destruction
* anemia = low RBC count
* jaundice = yellow skin from bilirubin buildup

---

### Why jaundice happens

RBCs contain hemoglobin.

When RBCs are destroyed:

* hemoglobin gets broken down
* bilirubin is produced
* bilirubin builds up after birth
* baby becomes jaundiced

So the yellow color is basically from RBC breakdown products.

---

### Why Rh disease is usually worse

In the first pregnancy, fetal blood often mixes with maternal blood during delivery.

That sensitizes the mom, meaning she makes anti-D antibodies against Rh-positive RBCs.

In a later pregnancy with another Rh-positive fetus:

* maternal anti-D IgG crosses placenta
* binds fetal RBCs
* causes hemolysis

That is why the first Rh-positive baby is often okay, but the next Rh-positive baby can be severely affected.

---

### Main findings

Baby can have:

* jaundice
* anemia
* hepatosplenomegaly (enlarged liver and spleen)
* positive direct Coombs test
* severe cases: hydrops fetalis (massive fetal edema from heart failure)

Direct Coombs test checks whether antibodies are already stuck to the baby's RBCs.

---

## One-line intuition

Mom's IgG crosses the placenta and tags the baby's RBCs for destruction.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Rhogam is anti-D immune globulin given to an Rh-negative mom to prevent sensitization.

It binds fetal Rh-positive RBCs before mom's immune system can react, so she does not make her own long-lasting anti-D IgG.

### Core idea

A pituitary adenoma is a benign tumor of the pituitary gland.

Break it down:

* pituitary = hormone gland under the brain
* adenoma = benign glandular tumor

So:

> benign tumor arising from hormone-producing pituitary cells.

---

### Why it causes symptoms

It can cause problems in 2 big ways:

* **Hormone secretion:** the tumor makes too much of a pituitary hormone
* **Mass effect:** the tumor grows and compresses nearby structures

The key nearby structure is the **optic chiasm** (where optic nerve fibers cross).

Since the pituitary sits right below the optic chiasm, an enlarging adenoma can push upward and cause:

* bitemporal hemianopia (loss of the outer visual fields in both eyes)

Why bitemporal?

* nasal retinal fibers cross in the optic chiasm
* nasal retina sees the temporal/peripheral visual field
* compression of crossing fibers = loss of temporal fields in both eyes

---

### Hormone patterns

Functional pituitary adenomas can secrete:

* prolactin -> galactorrhea (milk production), amenorrhea (no periods), infertility
* GH (growth hormone) -> acromegaly in adults
* ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone) -> Cushing disease

Nonfunctional adenomas do not secrete a useful hormone, so they often present later from mass effect:

* headache
* visual field loss
* hypopituitarism (low pituitary hormone output)

---

### Micro vs macro

* microadenoma = less than 10 mm
* macroadenoma = 10 mm or larger

Macroadenomas are more likely to compress the optic chiasm because they are bigger.

---

## One-line intuition

A pituitary adenoma is a usually benign hormone-gland tumor that either makes too much hormone or grows upward and hits the optic chiasm.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Most common functional pituitary adenoma = prolactinoma, and first-line treatment is a dopamine agonist like cabergoline or bromocriptine because dopamine inhibits prolactin release.

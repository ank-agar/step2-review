### Core idea

Circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorder means the body's internal clock is misaligned with the desired sleep schedule.

Circadian rhythm = about-24-hour body rhythm.

Sleep-wake = when you naturally feel sleepy vs awake.

So the main problem is not always "can this person sleep?"

It is:

> their body wants sleep at the wrong time.

---

### Main controller

The master clock is the **SCN (suprachiasmatic nucleus)** in the hypothalamus.

Light enters the eye and tells the SCN what time of day it is.

Then the SCN helps regulate:

* melatonin (sleep-promoting hormone from the pineal gland)
* body temperature
* alertness
* sleep timing

---

### Why the symptoms happen

If the clock is shifted late:

* the patient cannot fall asleep until very late
* waking up for school/work feels terrible
* this is delayed sleep-wake phase disorder

If the clock is shifted early:

* the patient gets sleepy very early
* wakes up very early
* this is advanced sleep-wake phase disorder

If the clock is not synchronized to 24 hours:

* sleep time drifts later and later each day
* classically seen in totally blind patients because light cannot entrain the SCN

Entraining = resetting/synchronizing a rhythm to an outside cue.

---

## One-line intuition

The sleep machinery works, but the body's clock is set to the wrong time zone.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Light and melatonin shift the clock in opposite timing-dependent ways:

* morning bright light usually shifts sleep earlier
* evening bright light shifts sleep later
* melatonin can help shift the rhythm, especially in delayed sleep phase or non-24-hour sleep-wake disorder

HY association: totally blind patient + progressively drifting sleep schedule = non-24-hour sleep-wake disorder.

# Nonaccidental trauma

Nonaccidental trauma means injury caused intentionally, usually in a child.

In medicine, this is basically:

* physical child abuse
* injury that does not match the history
* injury that does not match the child’s developmental ability

Example:

* a 2-month-old with a femur fracture
* a non-walking infant with bruises
* multiple fractures at different healing stages
* subdural hematoma (blood under the dura mater, which is the outer brain covering) plus retinal hemorrhages (bleeding in the retina of the eye)

---

### Core idea

Kids do get hurt accidentally, but accidental injuries usually make sense.

Nonaccidental trauma is suspected when the injury pattern is too severe, too unusual, or too inconsistent with the story.

So the key question is:

> Could this child realistically have caused this injury accidentally?

If no, think nonaccidental trauma.

---

### Classic clues

Highly suspicious findings include:

* bruising on torso, ears, or neck
* patterned bruises, like hand marks, grab marks, loop marks, or belt marks
* any bruising in a very young infant
* posterior rib fractures
* classic metaphyseal lesions (fractures at the growth plate region of long bones)
* multiple fractures in different stages of healing
* abusive head trauma (head injury from inflicted trauma, classically shaking or impact)
* retinal hemorrhages
* subdural hematoma

---

### Why these findings happen

Infants are vulnerable because:

* their heads are large compared with their bodies
* their neck muscles are weak
* their brains move more inside the skull

So violent shaking or impact can tear:

* bridging veins → subdural hematoma
* retinal vessels → retinal hemorrhages
* fragile growing bone near the metaphysis → classic metaphyseal lesion

Metaphysis means the flared end of a long bone near the growth plate.

---

### Important screening phrase

TEN-4-FACESp:

* T = torso
* E = ears
* N = neck
* 4 = any bruise anywhere in an infant 4 months or younger
* FACES = frenulum, angle of jaw, cheeks, eyelids, subconjunctivae
* p = patterned bruising

Frenulum means the small fold of tissue connecting the lip or tongue to the gums/mouth floor.

Subconjunctivae means the area under the conjunctiva, which is the thin clear covering over the white part of the eye.

---

## One-line intuition

Nonaccidental trauma is when the injury pattern tells you the child was hurt by someone, not by a normal accident.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, the most classic bone finding for child abuse is a posterior rib fracture or classic metaphyseal lesion, and the classic head findings are subdural hematoma plus retinal hemorrhages.

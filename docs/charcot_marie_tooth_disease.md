Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease is an inherited peripheral neuropathy.

Peripheral neuropathy = damage to peripheral nerves, meaning nerves outside the brain and spinal cord.

It affects:

* motor nerves = nerves that tell muscles to move
* sensory nerves = nerves that carry sensation back to the brain

So patients get:

* distal muscle weakness
* muscle wasting
* decreased sensation
* decreased reflexes
* foot deformities

Distal means farther from the center of the body, like feet/hands.

---

### Core idea

The nerves going to the feet and hands slowly stop working well.

Because the longest nerves are affected first, symptoms usually start in the:

* feet
* lower legs

then can later involve:

* hands
* forearms

---

### Why the findings happen

There are 2 big mechanisms:

* **Demyelinating type:** myelin is damaged
* **Axonal type:** the axon itself is damaged

Myelin = fatty insulation around nerves that helps signals travel fast.

Axon = long nerve fiber that carries the electrical signal.

If myelin is damaged:

* signal becomes slow
* nerve conduction velocity decreases

If the axon is damaged:

* signal strength decreases
* muscles do not get enough stimulation

That causes:

* weakness
* muscle atrophy
* foot drop
* high arches/pes cavus
* hammer toes

Pes cavus = abnormally high foot arch.

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

* kid/young adult
* family history
* progressive distal leg weakness
* high-arched feet
* foot drop
* decreased sensation
* reduced deep tendon reflexes

Foot drop happens because weak ankle dorsiflexors cannot lift the front of the foot.

Dorsiflexion = lifting the foot upward toward the shin.

---

## One-line intuition

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease is like inherited "bad wiring" of peripheral nerves, so the longest nerves to the feet fail first.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association: **CMT1A** is the most common subtype and is due to **PMP22 duplication**.

PMP22 = peripheral myelin protein 22, a myelin protein important for Schwann cells.

Schwann cells = cells that make myelin in the peripheral nervous system.

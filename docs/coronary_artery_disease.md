Coronary artery disease (CAD) = narrowing/blockage of the coronary arteries (arteries that supply blood to the heart muscle).

Most commonly, this happens from atherosclerosis (plaque buildup inside an artery).

---

### Core idea

The heart muscle needs oxygen-rich blood.

In CAD:

* plaque builds up in coronary arteries
* the lumen (inside opening of the vessel) gets smaller
* less blood reaches myocardium (heart muscle)
* the heart becomes ischemic (low oxygen from low blood flow)

So CAD is basically:

> clogged heart arteries causing low oxygen delivery to heart muscle.

---

### Why symptoms happen

When the heart works harder, like during exercise or stress, it needs more oxygen.

But narrowed coronary arteries cannot increase blood flow enough.

That causes:

* chest pain/pressure = angina (chest pain from myocardial ischemia)
* shortness of breath
* fatigue
* sometimes silent ischemia, especially in diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar disease)

If a plaque ruptures, a thrombus (blood clot) can suddenly form and fully block the artery.

That can cause myocardial infarction (MI, heart attack), meaning heart muscle dies from prolonged ischemia.

---

### Stable vs unstable

Stable angina:

* predictable chest pain with exertion
* improves with rest or nitroglycerin (vasodilator drug that reduces cardiac workload)

Unstable angina:

* chest pain at rest or worsening/new chest pain
* suggests plaque rupture with partial blockage
* high risk for MI (myocardial infarction)

---

### Why plaque forms

Endothelial injury (damage to the inner blood vessel lining) lets LDL (low-density lipoprotein, "bad cholesterol") enter the vessel wall.

Macrophages (immune cells that eat debris) eat LDL and become foam cells.

Over time:

* fatty streak forms
* fibrous cap forms
* lumen narrows
* plaque may rupture and clot

---

## One-line intuition

CAD is like slowly narrowing the fuel line to the heart, so the heart cannot get enough oxygen when demand goes up.

---

## Pearl 🔥

The LAD (left anterior descending artery) is the most commonly occluded coronary artery and supplies the anterior wall of the left ventricle plus the anterior 2/3 of the interventricular septum.

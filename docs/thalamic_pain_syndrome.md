# Thalamic pain syndrome

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK604196/bin/CPSPS__Pathways.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8f/Major_structures_involved_in_the_Ascending_network.png)

Thalamic pain syndrome is chronic neuropathic pain (pain from damaged nerves or pain pathways) after injury to the thalamus, classically after a thalamic stroke.

The thalamus is basically the brain's sensory relay station.

So normally:

* body pain/temp signals travel up the spinothalamic tract
* they synapse in the thalamus
* then they go to the somatosensory cortex, where the brain consciously localizes sensation

Break it down:

* spino- = spinal cord
* thalamic = thalamus
* tract = pathway

So the spinothalamic tract is the pain/temperature pathway from spinal cord to thalamus.

---

### Core idea

In thalamic pain syndrome, the sensory relay is damaged.

At first, the patient may have:

* numbness
* reduced pain/temperature sensation
* decreased sensation on the opposite side of the body

Then later, the damaged pathway becomes abnormally active and the patient develops severe pain.

That pain can feel like:

* burning
* aching
* shooting
* freezing
* electric
* hypersensitive to touch

---

### Why does a thalamic stroke cause pain?

The thalamus normally helps filter and organize sensory input.

If a stroke injures the ventral posterolateral nucleus (VPL nucleus, the thalamic relay for body sensation) or nearby sensory pathways, the brain can misinterpret signals.

So instead of "normal touch" or "normal temperature," the damaged central pathway may generate pain even without tissue injury.

That's why this is called central pain: the problem is in the CNS (central nervous system), not in the skin/muscle/peripheral nerves.

---

### Classic pattern

Thalamic lesion on one side causes pain on the opposite side of the body.

Example:

* right thalamic stroke
* left-sided burning pain or hypersensitivity

Why opposite side?

Because pain/temp fibers cross in the spinal cord before ascending to the thalamus.

---

### One-line intuition

Thalamic pain syndrome = a damaged sensory relay after stroke starts sending distorted pain signals.

---

### HY Step 1 pearl

Thalamic pain syndrome is also called Dejerine-Roussy syndrome and is classically due to a thalamic stroke affecting the VPL nucleus (ventral posterolateral nucleus), causing contralateral sensory loss followed by chronic burning pain.

### Core idea

Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis means a blood clot forms in the dural venous sinuses.

Break it down:

* cerebral = brain
* venous = vein side of circulation
* sinus = large venous drainage channel in the dura mater (outer meningeal layer)
* thrombosis = clot formation

So this is basically:

> a clot blocking the brain's venous drainage system.

---

### What normally happens

Brain blood flow normally goes:

* arteries bring blood into brain tissue
* veins drain used blood out
* dural venous sinuses collect that venous blood
* blood exits through the internal jugular vein

If a venous sinus gets blocked, blood can enter the brain, but it has trouble leaving.

---

### Why symptoms happen

The key problem is venous backup.

That causes:

* increased intracranial pressure (pressure inside the skull)
* headache
* papilledema (optic disc swelling from increased intracranial pressure)
* seizures
* focal neurologic deficits (localized brain findings, like weakness or aphasia)
* venous infarction (brain injury from blocked venous drainage)
* hemorrhage sometimes, because backed-up veins can leak/rupture

Important intuition:

Arterial stroke is like no blood can get in.

Venous sinus thrombosis is like blood gets in, but cannot drain out, so pressure backs up.

---

### Causes/risk factors

Think hypercoagulable states (conditions that make blood clot more easily), especially:

* pregnancy/postpartum state
* oral contraceptive pills
* inherited thrombophilias (inherited clotting tendencies)
* malignancy
* dehydration
* head/neck infection, like mastoiditis or sinusitis
* antiphospholipid syndrome (autoimmune clotting disorder)

---

### Diagnosis

Best test idea:

* MRV (magnetic resonance venography)
* CTV (computed tomography venography)

These look specifically at venous drainage.

Regular CT (computed tomography) can miss it, especially early.

---

### Treatment

Main treatment is anticoagulation (blood-thinning therapy), usually heparin acutely.

This can feel weird because there may be hemorrhage, but the primary problem is still a clot causing venous backup.

So anticoagulation prevents the clot from growing and helps restore drainage.

---

## One-line intuition

Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis is a clot in the brain's drainage veins, causing pressure backup, headache, seizures, and sometimes hemorrhagic venous infarction.

---

## Pearl

CVST (cerebral venous sinus thrombosis) can mimic idiopathic intracranial hypertension (increased intracranial pressure without a mass), because both can present with headache + papilledema, but CVST needs venous imaging like MRV (magnetic resonance venography) or CTV (computed tomography venography).

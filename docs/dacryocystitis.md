# Dacryocystitis

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/23190.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470565/bin/dacryoadenitis__and__dacryocystitis.jpg)

![Image](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/mmanual-ssq1ci05/media/home/images/a/c/u/acute_dacryocystitis_with_fistula_high.jpg?sc_lang=en-us&mw=2048)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/63/Dacryocystitis2017.png)

Dacryocystitis = infection/inflammation of the lacrimal sac (tear sac).

Break the word apart:

* dacryo- = tears
* cyst = sac
* -itis = inflammation

So literally:

> inflammation of the tear sac.

---

### Core idea

Tears normally drain:

* eye surface
* puncta (tiny tear-drain openings on the eyelids)
* canaliculi (small drainage channels)
* lacrimal sac (tear sac near the inner corner of the eye)
* nasolacrimal duct (tear duct draining into the nose)

In dacryocystitis, the nasolacrimal duct is usually blocked.

That blockage makes tears sit in the lacrimal sac.

Stagnant fluid = bacteria can grow there.

---

### What it looks like

Classically:

* pain
* redness
* swelling near the medial canthus (inner corner of the eye)
* tearing/epiphora (excess tears overflowing onto the face)
* sometimes pus from the punctum if you press on the lacrimal sac

Why medial canthus?

Because that is where the lacrimal sac sits, right beside the nose.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Tearing:** tears cannot drain into the nose
* **Swelling:** infected lacrimal sac fills with fluid/pus
* **Redness/pain:** local inflammation
* **Pus:** bacteria grow in trapped tear fluid

Simple intuition:

> A clogged tear drain turns the tear sac into a little infected pocket.

---

### Common bugs

Common causes include:

* Staphylococcus aureus
* Streptococcus pneumoniae

These are typical skin/respiratory-type bacteria that can infect stagnant fluid.

---

### Treatment idea

Acute cases are treated with:

* antibiotics
* warm compresses
* drainage if abscess forms

Recurrent/chronic cases may need dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR; surgery that creates a new drainage pathway from the lacrimal sac into the nose).

---

## One-line intuition

Dacryocystitis is a blocked tear duct causing infected backup in the tear sac at the inner corner of the eye.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Differentiate from hordeolum (stye): hordeolum is usually on the eyelid margin/lash line, while dacryocystitis is a tender swollen bump at the medial canthus (inner corner near the nose).

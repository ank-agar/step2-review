### Core idea

An endometrial polyp is a benign overgrowth of endometrium (inner lining of the uterus) that protrudes into the uterine cavity.

Think:

> a little finger-like or mushroom-like bump growing from the uterine lining.

It can be:

* pedunculated, meaning attached by a stalk
* sessile, meaning attached by a broad base

---

### What it is made of

Endometrial polyps contain:

* endometrial glands
* endometrial stroma (supportive connective tissue)
* blood vessels

That blood vessel part matters because the polyp can bleed irregularly.

---

### Why it causes bleeding

The polyp sticks into the uterine cavity and has fragile surface tissue + its own blood supply.

So it can cause:

* abnormal uterine bleeding
* heavy menstrual bleeding
* intermenstrual bleeding, meaning bleeding between periods
* postmenopausal bleeding, meaning bleeding after menopause

Menopause means no menstrual periods for 12 months because ovarian estrogen production has dropped.

---

### Why infertility can happen

If the polyp is in the uterine cavity, it can physically interfere with implantation.

Implantation = the embryo attaching to the endometrium.

So the issue is basically:

> the embryo is trying to attach to a smooth uterine lining, but a polyp creates an irregular bump in the cavity.

---

### Diagnosis and treatment idea

Ultrasound may show an echogenic mass in the endometrial cavity.

Echogenic means bright on ultrasound.

A better test is often saline infusion sonohysterography, where saline (salt water) is put into the uterus to outline the cavity.

Definitive diagnosis and treatment is hysteroscopy with polypectomy.

Hysteroscopy = camera inside the uterus.

Polypectomy = removal of the polyp.

---

## One-line intuition

An endometrial polyp is a benign bump of uterine lining that protrudes into the cavity, so it can bleed irregularly or interfere with implantation.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: endometrial polyps are a classic cause of abnormal uterine bleeding, especially intermenstrual bleeding or postmenopausal bleeding.

In a postmenopausal patient, bleeding is always important because endometrial cancer must be ruled out, even though many polyps are benign.

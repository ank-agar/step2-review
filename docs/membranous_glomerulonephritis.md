# Membranous glomerulonephritis

Membranous glomerulonephritis, also called membranous nephropathy, is a nephrotic syndrome (kidney disease pattern with heavy protein loss in urine, low blood albumin, edema, and hyperlipidemia).

Core idea:

* immune complexes deposit under podocytes
* this thickens the glomerular basement membrane
* the filtration barrier gets damaged
* protein leaks into urine

Break the key phrase apart:

* glomerulo- = glomerulus, the kidney filtering tuft
* nephritis = kidney inflammation
* membranous = thickened membrane, especially the glomerular basement membrane

The classic location is:

* subepithelial deposits

Subepithelial means under the epithelial cell layer. In the glomerulus, that means under podocytes (specialized filtration cells) on the urine side of the glomerular basement membrane.

Why proteinuria happens:

* podocytes normally help keep protein in the blood
* immune complexes injure podocytes
* damaged podocytes lose their foot processes
* the filter becomes leaky
* albumin (main blood protein) spills into urine

Classic biopsy findings:

* diffuse capillary wall thickening on light microscopy
* granular IgG (immunoglobulin G) and C3 (complement protein 3) on immunofluorescence
* spike and dome appearance from immune deposits plus new glomerular basement membrane growth

Intuition:

The kidney filter wall gets coated with immune junk on the outside, then the basement membrane grows around it, making the wall thick and leaky.

Important causes:

* primary membranous nephropathy: often anti-PLA2R (anti-phospholipase A2 receptor) antibodies
* secondary membranous nephropathy: SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus), hepatitis B, hepatitis C, malignancy, or NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs)

## One-line intuition

Membranous glomerulonephritis = immune complexes stuck beneath podocytes causing a thick, leaky glomerular basement membrane.

## Pearl 🔥

Membranous nephropathy is the most common primary cause of nephrotic syndrome in white adults, and Step 1 loves the phrase **subepithelial deposits with spike and dome appearance**.

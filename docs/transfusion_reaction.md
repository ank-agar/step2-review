## Transfusion reaction

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3e/Main_symptoms_of_acute_hemolytic_reaction.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f8/Transfusion_reaction_algorithm.jpg)

A transfusion reaction is a bad response to a blood transfusion.

Core idea:

* the patient receives blood product
* the immune system or circulatory system reacts badly
* symptoms happen during or shortly after transfusion

The big Step 1 version is **acute hemolytic transfusion reaction**.

Acute hemolytic transfusion reaction means:

> recipient antibodies destroy donor RBCs (red blood cells).

Usually this is from **ABO incompatibility** (A/B/O blood type mismatch).

---

### Why it happens

If a type O patient gets type A blood:

* type O patient already has anti-A antibodies
* those antibodies bind donor A RBCs (red blood cells)
* complement gets activated
* donor RBCs lyse inside blood vessels

So the problem is not slow antibody formation.

It is:

> preformed antibodies attacking the wrong donor blood immediately.

That is why it can happen within minutes.

---

### Classic findings

Think sudden immune attack during transfusion:

* fever/chills
* flank or back pain
* hypotension (low blood pressure)
* hemoglobinuria (hemoglobin in urine, classically dark/red urine)
* kidney injury
* DIC (disseminated intravascular coagulation, widespread clotting that uses up clotting factors)

Why kidney injury?

Destroyed RBCs (red blood cells) release free hemoglobin, which can damage renal tubules.

Why DIC?

Massive complement/inflammation activates coagulation, so the body starts clotting everywhere and then burns through clotting factors.

---

### Other transfusion reactions to keep separate

**Febrile nonhemolytic reaction**

* fever after transfusion
* due to cytokines (inflammatory signaling proteins) or antibodies against donor leukocytes (white blood cells)
* no RBC (red blood cell) destruction

**Allergic reaction**

* urticaria (hives), itching
* reaction to donor plasma proteins

**Anaphylactic reaction**

* severe allergy with hypotension/airway symptoms
* classically in IgA deficiency (immunoglobulin A deficiency) due to anti-IgA antibodies

**TRALI (transfusion-related acute lung injury)**

* acute hypoxemia (low blood oxygen) and pulmonary edema (fluid in lungs)
* donor antibodies activate recipient neutrophils (innate immune cells) in lung vessels

**TACO (transfusion-associated circulatory overload)**

* too much volume too fast
* pulmonary edema with hypertension (high blood pressure) and volume overload

---

## One-line intuition

Transfusion reaction = the transfused blood product triggers an immune or volume-related problem; acute hemolytic reaction is the scary ABO mismatch where preformed antibodies rapidly destroy donor RBCs (red blood cells).

---

## HY pearl

For acute hemolytic transfusion reaction, the first step is **stop the transfusion immediately**, then maintain IV (intravenous) access with normal saline and send blood/bag testing, including DAT (direct antiglobulin test, also called direct Coombs test).

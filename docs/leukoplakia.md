### Core idea

Leukoplakia literally means:

* leuko- = white
* -plakia = plaque

So leukoplakia = a **white patch/plaque in the mouth that cannot be scraped off and cannot be explained by another diagnosis**.

Most commonly it happens from chronic irritation, especially:

* tobacco
* alcohol
* rough teeth/dentures

---

### Why it looks white

The oral mucosa (lining of the mouth) responds to chronic irritation by making extra keratin.

Keratin = tough protective protein, like what skin/nails use.

More keratin on the mucosal surface = thick white plaque.

That is why leukoplakia looks white and stuck-on instead of wiping away.

---

### Why we care

Leukoplakia is a **potentially premalignant lesion**.

Premalignant = not cancer yet, but it has increased risk of becoming cancer.

The feared progression is:

* chronic irritation
* epithelial dysplasia
* squamous cell carcinoma

Epithelial dysplasia = abnormal growth/maturation of epithelial cells (surface-lining cells).

Squamous cell carcinoma = malignant cancer from squamous epithelial cells.

---

### Key distinction

If a white oral plaque **scrapes off**, think candidiasis (Candida fungal infection).

If it **does not scrape off**, think leukoplakia.

---

## One-line intuition

Leukoplakia is the mouth lining “toughening up” from chronic irritation, forming a white keratin plaque that matters because it can hide or progress to dysplasia/cancer.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Oral hairy leukoplakia is different: it is caused by EBV (Epstein-Barr virus) and is classically seen in immunosuppression, especially HIV (human immunodeficiency virus). It appears as white, hairy-looking plaques on the lateral tongue that do not scrape off.

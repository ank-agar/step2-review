# Immune thrombocytopenic purpura

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/bfef/7920457/111b58dbf178/jcm-10-00789-g001.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/c873/5937051/657568ecaf15/fimmu-09-00880-g001.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/13/Petechiaesmall.jpg)

Immune thrombocytopenic purpura, or **ITP (immune thrombocytopenic purpura)**, is an autoimmune disease where the body makes antibodies against platelets.

Platelets = little blood fragments that help form clots.

So the core problem is:

* immune system attacks platelets
* spleen removes those antibody-coated platelets
* platelet count drops
* patient bleeds into skin/mucosa

---

### Break down the name

* **immune** = caused by the immune system
* **thrombocytopenic** = low platelets
  * thrombocyte = platelet
  * -penia = low
* **purpura** = purple bleeding spots under the skin

So ITP literally means:

> immune-caused low platelets leading to purple skin bleeding.

---

### Why the bleeding happens

Platelets normally plug small vessel injuries.

In ITP:

* IgG (immunoglobulin G antibody) binds platelet surface proteins, classically GPIIb/IIIa (glycoprotein IIb/IIIa)
* splenic macrophages (immune cells in the spleen that eat tagged cells) recognize the IgG-coated platelets
* macrophages destroy the platelets

That gives isolated thrombocytopenia, meaning:

* low platelets
* normal red blood cells
* normal white blood cells

Because the issue is platelet destruction, not full bone marrow failure.

---

### Classic symptoms

Low platelets cause mucocutaneous bleeding, meaning bleeding from skin and mucous membranes.

Examples:

* petechiae (tiny pinpoint red-purple bleeding spots)
* purpura (larger purple skin spots)
* easy bruising
* gum bleeding
* nosebleeds
* heavy menstrual bleeding

This is platelet-type bleeding, so it is usually superficial: skin, gums, nose, mucosa.

---

### Why it is not like hemophilia

Hemophilia is a coagulation factor problem.

Coagulation factors stabilize clots deeper in tissues, so hemophilia causes:

* deep muscle bleeding
* hemarthrosis (bleeding into joints)

ITP is a platelet problem, so it causes:

* petechiae
* purpura
* mucosal bleeding

One-line distinction:

> Platelet problems bleed on surfaces; coagulation factor problems bleed deep.

---

### Labs

Typical ITP labs:

* decreased platelet count
* increased bleeding time
* normal PT (prothrombin time)
* normal PTT (partial thromboplastin time)

Why PT/PTT are normal:

PT and PTT test coagulation factors, not platelet number.

So in ITP, the clotting-factor pathways are fine. The problem is that there are not enough platelets to start a good platelet plug.

---

### Acute vs chronic

In children:

* often acute
* often follows a viral infection
* usually self-limited

In adults:

* more often chronic
* classically associated with young women
* can be associated with autoimmune disease like SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus)

---

## One-line intuition

ITP is when IgG tags platelets for splenic destruction, so platelets drop and the patient gets petechiae, purpura, and mucosal bleeding.

---

## Pearl

ITP is a **type II hypersensitivity reaction** because antibodies bind directly to a cell surface target, the platelet, leading to destruction by macrophages.

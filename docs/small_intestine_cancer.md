Small intestine cancer = malignant growth in the small bowel, which includes:

* duodenum
* jejunum
* ileum

Most common major types:

* adenocarcinoma (gland-forming cancer)
* neuroendocrine tumor (hormone-producing tumor, formerly often called carcinoid)
* lymphoma (cancer of lymphocytes, which are immune cells)
* GIST (gastrointestinal stromal tumor, a tumor from GI tract support cells)

### Core idea

The small intestine is where digestion and nutrient absorption happen, so a tumor there can cause:

* obstruction
* bleeding
* abdominal pain
* weight loss
* anemia (low red blood cells or hemoglobin)

### Why symptoms happen

* **Obstruction:** tumor narrows the bowel lumen (inside tube space), so food/stool cannot pass easily
* **Bleeding:** fragile tumor vessels ulcerate into the lumen
* **Iron deficiency anemia:** slow chronic GI (gastrointestinal) blood loss drains iron over time
* **Weight loss:** cancer uses energy and can reduce absorption/appetite

### Risk associations

Think chronic irritation or inherited cancer risk:

* Crohn disease (chronic inflammatory bowel disease), especially ileum
* celiac disease (immune reaction to gluten causing small bowel villous damage)
* FAP (familial adenomatous polyposis, inherited APC mutation causing many polyps)
* Lynch syndrome (inherited DNA mismatch repair defect)

### One-line intuition

Small intestine cancer is rare, but when it shows up, think: “tumor growing inside a long narrow absorption tube,” so it can bleed, block, or cause unexplained weight loss/anemia.

### Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1: Ileal neuroendocrine tumors can make serotonin, but carcinoid syndrome usually requires liver metastases because the liver normally breaks down serotonin from the gut before it reaches systemic blood.

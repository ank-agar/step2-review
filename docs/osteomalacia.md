### Core idea

Osteomalacia = soft bones in adults due to poor mineralization of osteoid.

Osteoid = the unmineralized collagen/protein matrix that osteoblasts lay down before calcium and phosphate harden it.

So the problem is not that the body cannot make bone matrix.

The problem is:

* osteoblasts make osteoid
* but there is not enough mineralization
* so bone becomes soft, weak, and painful

---

### Most common cause

Most commonly due to vitamin D deficiency.

Vitamin D normally helps absorb:

* calcium
* phosphate

from the gut.

Calcium and phosphate are needed to form hydroxyapatite, which is the hard mineral part of bone.

So:

low vitamin D -> low calcium/phosphate absorption -> poor hydroxyapatite formation -> unmineralized osteoid builds up -> soft bone

---

### Why symptoms happen

* **Bone pain:** soft, poorly mineralized bone is stressed more easily
* **Fractures/pseudofractures:** weak bone develops small stress injuries
* **Proximal muscle weakness:** vitamin D deficiency can also impair muscle function
* **Waddling gait:** hip/pelvic pain + proximal muscle weakness

Pseudofractures are also called Looser zones.

Looser zones = thin radiolucent lines on X-ray that look like incomplete fractures.

Radiolucent means darker on X-ray because fewer X-rays are blocked.

---

### Osteomalacia vs osteoporosis

Osteomalacia:

* normal amount of osteoid can be made
* mineralization is defective
* bone is soft

Osteoporosis:

* mineralization is normal
* total bone mass is decreased
* bone is porous/brittle

One-line difference:

Osteomalacia = bad mineralization.

Osteoporosis = not enough bone.

---

### Adults vs kids

In adults:

* defective mineralization = osteomalacia

In kids:

* defective mineralization = rickets

Why different?

Kids still have open growth plates, so poor mineralization causes growth plate problems and bone deformities like bowed legs.

Adults have closed growth plates, so the main issue is soft painful bone and fractures.

---

### Labs to know

Classic vitamin D deficiency osteomalacia:

* low vitamin D
* low or normal calcium
* low phosphate
* high PTH (parathyroid hormone)
* high alkaline phosphatase

Why high PTH?

Low calcium stimulates the parathyroid glands to release PTH.

PTH tries to restore serum calcium by:

* increasing bone resorption
* increasing kidney calcium reabsorption
* increasing phosphate wasting in urine

That phosphate wasting makes mineralization even harder.

---

## One-line intuition

Osteomalacia is when osteoblasts lay down the bone scaffold, but the body cannot add enough calcium/phosphate mineral, so the bone stays soft.

---

## HY pearl

Osteomalacia has increased unmineralized osteoid, while osteoporosis has normally mineralized bone with decreased bone mass.

# Secondary adrenal insufficiency

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK278953/bin/congn-adren-hyprplsa-Image002.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK448184/bin/HPA__Axis.jpg)

### Core idea

Secondary adrenal insufficiency means the adrenal glands are not making enough cortisol because the pituitary is not sending enough ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone).

Normal pathway:

* hypothalamus releases CRH (corticotropin-releasing hormone)
* pituitary releases ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone)
* adrenal cortex releases cortisol

In secondary adrenal insufficiency:

* pituitary problem
* low ACTH
* low cortisol

So the adrenal gland is under-stimulated, not primarily destroyed.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Fatigue/weakness:** low cortisol means poor stress response and low energy availability
* **Hypotension can happen:** cortisol helps blood vessels respond to catecholamines (epinephrine/norepinephrine)
* **Hyponatremia:** low cortisol increases ADH (antidiuretic hormone), so the body holds extra water and dilutes sodium
* **No hyperpigmentation:** ACTH is low, so there is not excess melanocyte stimulation
* **Usually no hyperkalemia:** aldosterone is mostly controlled by RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system), not ACTH

---

### Primary vs secondary

Primary adrenal insufficiency = adrenal gland problem.

* low cortisol
* high ACTH
* often low aldosterone
* hyperpigmentation
* hyperkalemia

Secondary adrenal insufficiency = pituitary ACTH problem.

* low cortisol
* low ACTH
* aldosterone usually preserved
* no hyperpigmentation
* no major hyperkalemia

---

## One-line intuition

The adrenal gland can still work, but the pituitary is not telling it to make cortisol.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Secondary adrenal insufficiency has **low ACTH + low cortisol**, while primary adrenal insufficiency has **high ACTH + low cortisol**.

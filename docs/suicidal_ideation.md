### Core idea

Suicidal ideation means thoughts about ending one’s own life.

It can be:

* passive: “I wish I would not wake up”
* active: “I want to kill myself”
* active with plan/intent: specific plan + desire to act

The more specific the plan and the stronger the intent (commitment to act), the higher the risk.

---

### Why it happens

Usually it comes from overwhelming psychological pain plus impaired coping.

Common associations:

* major depressive disorder (persistent low mood and/or loss of interest)
* bipolar disorder (mood disorder with mania/hypomania and depression)
* schizophrenia (psychotic disorder with hallucinations, delusions, or disorganized thinking)
* substance use disorder (harmful/recurrent use of alcohol or drugs)
* PTSD (post-traumatic stress disorder)
* chronic pain or serious medical illness
* recent loss, trauma, isolation, or humiliation

Reasoning:

When hopelessness + distress + access to lethal means combine, suicidal thoughts can shift from passive thoughts to active planning.

---

### Risk assessment

Always ask directly about:

* ideation: thoughts of suicide
* intent: desire/commitment to act
* plan: how/when/where
* means: access to the method
* prior attempts: strongest single risk factor
* protective factors: family, reasons for living, religion, treatment connection

Directly asking does not “put the idea in their head.”

It actually helps risk-stratify and can make the patient feel less alone.

---

### Clinical safety

If a patient has active suicidal ideation with plan/intent, this is an emergency.

Management:

* do not leave the patient alone
* remove access to lethal means
* urgent psychiatric evaluation
* consider inpatient hospitalization

In the US, 988 is the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

---

## One-line intuition

Suicidal ideation is not just “sad thoughts”; it is a risk-spectrum from passive death wishes to active intent with a plan.

---

## Pearl 🔥

The strongest risk factor for future suicide attempt/completion is a prior suicide attempt.

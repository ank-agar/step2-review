## Cervical cancer

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/gynecologic-cancer/media/images/gyn-diagram-16x9-1.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5b/Transformation_zone_types.svg/960px-Transformation_zone_types.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/21/Ca_in_situ%2C_cervix_2.jpg)

Cervical cancer is cancer that starts in the cervix.

The cervix is the lower narrow part of the uterus that opens into the vagina.

Most cervical cancers are caused by persistent high-risk HPV (human papillomavirus) infection.

---

### Core idea

HPV infects cervical epithelial cells, especially near the transformation zone.

The transformation zone is where:

* squamous cells meet glandular cells
* cells are actively changing/metaplastic
* most cervical dysplasia and cervical cancer begins

Dysplasia means abnormal cell growth.

So the usual sequence is:

HPV infection -> cervical dysplasia -> carcinoma in situ -> invasive cervical cancer

Carcinoma in situ means cancer-like cells are still above the basement membrane and have not invaded deeper tissue.

---

### Why HPV causes cancer

High-risk HPV types, especially HPV 16 and HPV 18, make viral proteins:

* E6
* E7

These knock out tumor suppressors:

* E6 inhibits p53
* E7 inhibits Rb (retinoblastoma protein)

p53 normally says: "DNA is damaged, stop or die."

Rb normally says: "Do not enter S phase yet."

So if HPV disables both, the cell keeps dividing despite DNA damage.

That is why persistent HPV can push cells toward cancer.

---

### Classic presentation

Cervical cancer can be asymptomatic early.

When symptomatic, think:

* postcoital bleeding
* abnormal vaginal bleeding
* watery or bloody discharge
* pelvic pain in advanced disease

Postcoital means after sex.

---

### Screening

Pap smear checks for abnormal cervical cells.

HPV testing checks for high-risk HPV DNA.

Screening matters because cervical cancer often has a long precancer phase, so you can catch it before invasion.

---

## One-line intuition

Cervical cancer is usually HPV-driven cancer from the transformation zone of the cervix, where viral E6 and E7 disable p53 and Rb so abnormal cells keep dividing.

# Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/Wpw2_%28CardioNetworks_ECGpedia%29.svg/960px-Wpw2_%28CardioNetworks_ECGpedia%29.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a5/Wolff-Parkinson-White_syndrome_12_lead_EKG.png)

### Core idea

Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome is a heart conduction disorder where there is an extra electrical pathway between the atria and ventricles.

That extra pathway is called the **Bundle of Kent**.

So instead of electricity going:

* SA node (sinoatrial node) → AV node (atrioventricular node) → His-Purkinje system

some electricity takes a shortcut:

* atria → accessory pathway → ventricles

---

### Why the findings happen

Normally, the AV node (atrioventricular node) slows conduction a little.

In WPW (Wolff-Parkinson-White), the accessory pathway bypasses that AV nodal delay.

So the ventricles get activated too early:

* **pre-excitation** = ventricles are depolarized earlier than normal
* **short PR interval** = signal reaches ventricles too fast
* **delta wave** = slurred early upstroke of the QRS complex because part of the ventricle is activated early and slowly through myocardium
* **wide QRS complex** = early accessory-pathway activation + normal His-Purkinje activation mix together

---

### Classic ECG findings

Think:

* short PR interval
* delta wave
* widened QRS complex

Delta wave = the key visual clue.

---

### Why tachycardia happens

The accessory pathway can create a reentry loop.

Reentry means the electrical signal keeps circling around instead of stopping.

That can cause AVRT (atrioventricular reentrant tachycardia), a type of SVT (supraventricular tachycardia).

So patients can have:

* palpitations
* dizziness/syncope (fainting)
* episodic very fast heart rate

---

## One-line intuition

WPW is a shortcut wire in the heart, so the ventricles get excited too early and can get trapped in a fast reentry loop.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Avoid AV nodal blockers in atrial fibrillation with WPW because blocking the AV node can force conduction down the accessory pathway and trigger dangerous ventricular rhythms.

### Core idea

Hypertensive nephropathy means kidney damage from chronic high blood pressure.

Break it down:

* hypertensive = due to high blood pressure
* nephro- = kidney
* -pathy = disease

So it literally means:

> high blood pressure damaging the kidneys.

---

### What is getting damaged?

Mainly the small kidney blood vessels, especially:

* afferent arterioles (small vessels bringing blood into the glomerulus)
* small renal arteries
* glomeruli (kidney filtering tufts)

Chronic pressure makes these vessels thicken and narrow.

Less blood gets through, so the kidney tissue becomes ischemic (low oxygen from low blood flow).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Decreased GFR (glomerular filtration rate):** narrowed arterioles reduce blood flow into glomeruli
* **Proteinuria (protein in urine):** damaged glomeruli leak some protein, usually not massive
* **Small, shrunken kidneys:** long-term ischemia causes scarring and nephron loss
* **CKD (chronic kidney disease):** progressive nephron loss reduces filtering ability over time

The key mechanism is:

> high pressure -> arteriole wall thickening -> narrowed lumen -> ischemia -> scarring.

---

### Pathology words to know

In long-standing, "benign" hypertension:

* **hyaline arteriolosclerosis** = pink hyaline protein thickening in arteriole walls
* causes narrowed lumens and chronic ischemia

In severe malignant hypertension:

* **hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis** = concentric "onion-skin" thickening of arterioles
* **fibrinoid necrosis** = vessel wall damage with fibrin-like material

Malignant hypertension is more acute and dangerous.

---

## One-line intuition

High blood pressure slowly squeezes and scars the kidney's tiny blood vessels, so the filters lose blood supply and the kidney shrinks over time.

---

## HY pearl

Hypertensive nephropathy classically causes **hyaline arteriolosclerosis**, while malignant hypertension causes **hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis with onion-skinning**.

### Core idea

Panic disorder = recurrent, unexpected panic attacks + persistent fear/behavior change because of the attacks.

A panic attack is a sudden surge of intense fear with physical symptoms like:

* palpitations
* sweating
* trembling
* shortness of breath
* chest pain
* dizziness
* fear of dying or losing control

Important distinction:

* **panic attack** = one episode
* **panic disorder** = repeated unexpected attacks + worrying about more attacks or avoiding things because of them

---

### Why it feels so physical

The body incorrectly flips on the sympathetic nervous system, meaning the fight-or-flight system.

So the body acts like there is danger even when there is no real external threat.

That causes:

* **racing heart:** adrenaline increases heart rate
* **shortness of breath:** breathing speeds up
* **chest tightness:** muscle tension + fast breathing
* **dizziness/tingling:** hyperventilation lowers carbon dioxide in the blood
* **sweating/trembling:** sympathetic activation

That is why panic can feel like a heart attack even though the core problem is anxiety circuitry.

---

### Why it becomes a disorder

The patient starts fearing the panic attack itself.

Example:

* heart races
* patient thinks, "I am dying"
* fear increases
* sympathetic output increases more
* symptoms get worse

So panic disorder is basically a positive feedback loop:

> body sensation -> catastrophic interpretation -> more fear -> more body symptoms

---

### Classic presentation

Usually:

* sudden episodes of intense fear
* peak within minutes
* no clear trigger sometimes
* repeated ER visits for chest pain/palpitations
* normal cardiac workup
* avoidance of places where attacks happened before

If avoidance becomes fear of being trapped somewhere difficult to escape, that is **agoraphobia** (fear of places where escape/help may feel difficult).

---

### Treatment

First-line long-term treatment:

* **CBT** (cognitive behavioral therapy, therapy that changes fear-based thoughts/behaviors)
* **SSRI** (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor, antidepressant that increases serotonin signaling)
* **SNRI** (serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor, antidepressant that increases serotonin and norepinephrine signaling)

Benzodiazepines can reduce symptoms quickly, but they are not ideal long-term because of dependence and sedation risk.

---

## One-line intuition

Panic disorder is when the brain repeatedly hits the body's fight-or-flight alarm button, and then the patient becomes afraid of the alarm itself.

---

## Pearl

Before diagnosing panic disorder, rule out medical/substance causes of panic-like symptoms, especially hyperthyroidism, stimulant use, arrhythmias, and pheochromocytoma.

### Core idea

Nephrotic syndrome = damage to the glomerular filtration barrier (kidney filter), causing massive protein loss in urine.

Classic findings:

* proteinuria (protein in urine), usually >3.5 g/day
* hypoalbuminemia (low albumin in blood)
* edema (swelling)
* hyperlipidemia (high lipids/fats in blood)
* lipiduria (fat in urine)

---

### Why the findings happen

The glomerulus (kidney capillary filter) normally keeps big proteins like albumin in the blood.

When podocytes (filter cells with foot processes) are damaged:

* albumin leaks into urine
* blood albumin falls
* oncotic pressure (protein-pulling force that keeps water inside vessels) falls
* water leaves blood vessels and enters tissues
* edema happens

So the main chain is:

> leaky glomerulus -> proteinuria -> hypoalbuminemia -> low oncotic pressure -> edema

---

### Why hyperlipidemia happens

The liver senses low blood protein and tries to compensate by making more proteins.

But while doing that, it also makes more lipoproteins (fat-carrying particles), causing:

* hyperlipidemia (high blood lipids)
* lipiduria (fat in urine)

Classic urine clue:

* fatty casts
* oval fat bodies
* "Maltese cross" appearance under polarized light

---

### Nephrotic vs nephritic intuition

Nephrotic syndrome = mostly protein leak.

Nephritic syndrome = mostly inflammation/bleeding.

So:

* nephrotic -> lots of proteinuria, edema
* nephritic -> hematuria (blood in urine), RBC casts (red blood cell casts), hypertension

---

### Common Step 1 causes

Children:

* minimal change disease = most common
* podocyte foot process effacement (flattening/fusion of podocyte feet)
* often after infection/vaccine
* responds to steroids

Adults:

* focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, or FSGS (scarring of some glomeruli and part of each affected glomerulus)
* membranous nephropathy (thickened glomerular basement membrane from immune deposits)
* diabetic nephropathy (kidney damage from diabetes)

---

## One-line intuition

The kidney filter becomes too leaky, so albumin spills into urine, blood loses its water-holding force, and the patient swells up.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Nephrotic syndrome causes a hypercoagulable state (increased clotting tendency) because antithrombin III (natural anticoagulant protein) is lost in the urine, increasing risk of renal vein thrombosis.

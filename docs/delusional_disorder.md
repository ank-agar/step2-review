Delusional disorder = having one or more **delusions** (fixed false beliefs held despite clear evidence against them) for **at least 1 month**.

Key idea:

* the belief is false
* the belief is fixed
* the person usually functions pretty normally outside that belief
* there are no prominent hallucinations (perceptions without real external stimuli), disorganized speech, or negative symptoms (loss of normal function like flat affect or low motivation)

Common types:

* **Persecutory:** “Someone is spying on me / trying to hurt me”
* **Grandiose:** “I have special powers or importance”
* **Erotomanic:** “This famous or high-status person is in love with me”
* **Jealous:** “My partner is cheating,” despite no evidence
* **Somatic:** “Something is wrong with my body,” despite medical reassurance

### Why it happens

The brain assigns too much meaning or threat to something neutral.

So a normal event:

* someone laughs nearby
* a car parks outside
* a news headline appears

gets interpreted as personally important:

* “They are laughing at me”
* “That car is watching me”
* “The headline is a secret message”

That false interpretation becomes locked in as a delusion.

### How it differs from schizophrenia

Delusional disorder is mainly:

* delusions
* relatively preserved functioning
* less bizarre overall behavior

Schizophrenia usually has more global psychosis (loss of contact with reality), such as:

* hallucinations
* disorganized speech
* disorganized behavior
* negative symptoms
* major functional decline

## One-line intuition

Delusional disorder is when one false belief becomes fixed, but the rest of the person’s thinking and functioning can look surprisingly normal.

## HY Step 1 pearl

Delusions are **positive symptoms** (extra abnormal experiences/thoughts added on), and antipsychotics often work by blocking dopamine D2 receptors (dopamine type 2 receptors).

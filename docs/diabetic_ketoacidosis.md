# Diabetic ketoacidosis

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279087/bin/mgmt-t1t2diab-child-Image001.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/18/Ketonuria.jpg)

Diabetic ketoacidosis, or DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis), is an acute emergency where:

* insulin is too low
* glucose is high
* ketones are high
* blood becomes acidic

Core idea:

> No insulin = body thinks it is starving, even though glucose is high in the blood.

So the body starts breaking down fat for fuel.

---

### Why ketones form

Insulin normally tells the body:

* put glucose into cells
* stop breaking down fat
* stop making extra glucose in the liver

In DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis), insulin is absent or very low, so:

* cells cannot use glucose well
* fat gets broken down into free fatty acids (fat breakdown products)
* the liver converts free fatty acids into ketones (acidic fuel molecules)

The major ketone in DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis) is beta-hydroxybutyrate (a ketone body that is strongly associated with DKA severity).

---

### Why glucose is high

Even though blood glucose is already high, the liver keeps making more glucose because low insulin makes the body think:

> "We are starving. Release more fuel."

Also, stress hormones increase:

* glucagon (hormone that raises blood glucose)
* cortisol (stress steroid hormone)
* catecholamines (epinephrine-like stress hormones)
* growth hormone (hormone that can oppose insulin)

These hormones push glucose even higher.

---

### Why patients get dehydrated

High blood glucose spills into urine.

Glucose pulls water with it by osmotic diuresis (increased urination because solute pulls water into urine).

So patients get:

* polyuria (excessive urination)
* polydipsia (excessive thirst)
* dehydration
* tachycardia (fast heart rate)
* low blood pressure if severe

---

### Why they breathe weird

Ketones are acids, so DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis) causes anion gap metabolic acidosis (acidic blood due to extra unmeasured acids).

The body compensates by breathing fast and deep to blow off carbon dioxide, or CO2 (carbon dioxide).

This is called Kussmaul respirations (deep, labored breathing due to metabolic acidosis).

Intuition:

> The lungs cannot remove ketones, but they can remove CO2, which helps raise pH.

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* high glucose
* ketones in blood/urine
* metabolic acidosis
* dehydration
* nausea/vomiting
* abdominal pain
* fruity breath from acetone (a volatile ketone)
* Kussmaul respirations (deep, labored breathing due to metabolic acidosis)
* altered mental status if severe

---

### Potassium trap

Serum potassium can look normal or high, but total body potassium is low.

Why?

* acidosis shifts potassium out of cells into blood
* lack of insulin also keeps potassium out of cells
* osmotic diuresis makes the patient pee out potassium

So once you give insulin, potassium moves back into cells and serum potassium can crash.

That is why potassium must be checked before giving insulin.

---

## One-line intuition

DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis) is what happens when low insulin makes the body burn fat uncontrollably, producing acidic ketones while glucose and dehydration both worsen.

---

## Pearl 🔥

DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis) is classically:

* hyperglycemia (high blood glucose)
* ketosis (high ketones)
* anion gap metabolic acidosis (acidic blood from unmeasured acids)

But SGLT2 inhibitors (sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors, diabetes drugs that make you pee out glucose) can cause euglycemic DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis with normal or only mildly elevated glucose).

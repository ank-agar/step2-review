Group A Streptococcus, aka **GAS (group A Streptococcus)**, usually means **Streptococcus pyogenes**.

It is:

* **gram-positive cocci** = purple, round bacteria
* **in chains** = “strep” grows like beads on a string
* **beta-hemolytic** = completely lyses RBCs (red blood cells) on blood agar
* **bacitracin-sensitive** = classic lab clue

---

### What it causes

Think:

* **pharyngitis** = strep throat
* **impetigo** = honey-crusted skin infection
* **erysipelas/cellulitis** = skin infection
* **scarlet fever** = sandpaper rash after strep throat
* **necrotizing fasciitis** = deep, rapidly spreading tissue infection
* **streptococcal toxic shock syndrome** = shock from toxin-driven inflammation

---

### Why the findings happen

GAS makes a bunch of virulence factors (bacterial tools that help it cause disease):

* **M protein:** blocks phagocytosis (immune cell eating bacteria)
* **streptolysin O/S:** lyse RBCs, causing beta-hemolysis
* **streptococcal pyrogenic exotoxins:** act as superantigens (toxins that massively activate T cells)
* **hyaluronidase:** helps spread through connective tissue

So the intuition is:

> GAS sticks to tissue, avoids immune killing, releases toxins, and can spread aggressively.

---

### Big post-infection complications

After GAS infection, the immune system can cause damage even when the bacteria are gone:

* **acute rheumatic fever:** after strep throat
* **post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis:** after strep throat or skin infection

Why?

In **acute rheumatic fever**, antibodies against GAS cross-react with human tissue due to **molecular mimicry** (bacterial antigens look similar to host antigens).

That is why rheumatic fever can affect:

* heart
* joints
* brain
* skin

---

## One-line intuition

Group A Strep is **S. pyogenes**, a beta-hemolytic gram-positive chain-forming coccus that causes throat/skin infections and can trigger immune complications afterward.

---

## Pearl 🔥

**Acute rheumatic fever follows strep throat, not impetigo**, but **post-strep glomerulonephritis can follow either throat or skin infection**.

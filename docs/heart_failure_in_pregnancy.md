## Heart failure in pregnancy

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18082.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/17/Peripartum_cardiomyopathy.png)

### Core idea

Heart failure in pregnancy means the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body’s needs during pregnancy or shortly after delivery.

The classic pregnancy-specific cause is **peripartum cardiomyopathy** (heart muscle weakness that develops near the end of pregnancy or in the months after delivery).

---

### Why pregnancy can trigger it

Pregnancy is basically a stress test for the heart:

* blood volume increases
* heart rate increases
* cardiac output (amount of blood pumped by the heart per minute) increases
* delivery and postpartum fluid shifts suddenly increase preload (blood returning to the heart)

So if the heart is already weak, or becomes weak from peripartum cardiomyopathy, it cannot keep up.

---

### What happens

If the left ventricle (main pumping chamber to the body) weakens:

* blood backs up into the lungs
* pulmonary edema (fluid in the lungs) can develop
* patient gets dyspnea (shortness of breath), orthopnea (shortness of breath lying flat), cough, and crackles

If the right side backs up:

* leg edema
* jugular venous distension/JVD (bulging neck veins from high venous pressure)
* liver congestion

---

### Key clues

Concerning symptoms in late pregnancy or postpartum:

* shortness of breath out of proportion to normal pregnancy
* orthopnea
* paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea/PND (waking up gasping for air)
* pulmonary edema
* S3 heart sound
* elevated BNP (B-type natriuretic peptide, a marker of heart stretch)

Diagnosis is usually with echocardiogram (heart ultrasound), showing reduced ejection fraction (percent of blood the left ventricle pumps out each beat), often less than 45% in peripartum cardiomyopathy.

---

## One-line intuition

Pregnancy increases the heart’s workload; heart failure happens when the heart cannot match that extra demand, so fluid backs up into the lungs and body.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Avoid ACE inhibitors (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors) and ARBs (angiotensin receptor blockers) during pregnancy because they are teratogenic (harmful to fetal development), but they can often be used postpartum if clinically appropriate.

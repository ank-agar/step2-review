# Verruca vulgaris

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19709.jpg)

![Image](https://dermnetnz.org/assets/Uploads/viral/viral-wart-15.jpg)

![Image](https://dermnetnz.org/assets/Uploads/pathology/e/verruca-vulgaris-figure-1.jpg)

Verruca vulgaris = common wart.

It is a benign epidermal growth caused by HPV (human papillomavirus), especially HPV types 2 and 4.

Break it down:

* verruca = wart
* vulgaris = common

So:

> verruca vulgaris = common wart.

---

### Core idea

HPV infects keratinocytes (epidermal skin cells that make keratin).

This makes the epidermis grow too much, causing:

* rough raised papule
* hyperkeratosis (thickened keratin layer)
* papillomatosis (finger-like/warty surface projections)
* tiny black dots sometimes, which are thrombosed capillaries (clotted tiny blood vessels)

---

### Why it looks rough

HPV pushes infected skin cells to proliferate.

More epidermal cells + more keratin = thick, rough, cauliflower-like surface.

That is why common warts feel hard and gritty rather than soft or fluid-filled.

---

### Histology clue

Classically shows:

* hyperkeratosis (thick stratum corneum)
* acanthosis (thickened epidermis)
* papillomatosis (wavy/finger-like surface)
* koilocytes (HPV-infected squamous cells with perinuclear halos)

Koilocyte = squamous cell altered by HPV.

---

## One-line intuition

Verruca vulgaris is HPV making epidermal cells overgrow into a rough little keratin bump.

---

## HY pearl

On Step 1, HPV causes koilocytosis (perinuclear clearing in squamous cells), but verruca vulgaris is usually from low-risk cutaneous HPV types, not the high-risk cervical cancer types like HPV 16 and 18.

## Disorders of sexual differentiation

Disorders of sexual differentiation, now often called DSDs (disorders of sex development), are conditions where chromosomal sex, gonadal sex, internal reproductive organs, external genitalia, or hormone signaling do not line up in the usual male/female pathway.

Think of sex development as a stepwise pathway:

* **Chromosomes:** usually 46,XX or 46,XY
* **Gonads:** ovaries or testes
* **Internal ducts:** Mullerian ducts become uterus/fallopian tubes; Wolffian ducts become epididymis/vas deferens
* **External genitalia:** develop based mainly on androgen effect

---

### Core idea

DSD = something disrupts the normal chain:

> chromosomes -> gonads -> hormones -> ducts/genitalia

So the key question is:

**Where in the chain did the mismatch happen?**

---

### High-yield mechanisms

**46,XX DSD**

Usually means ovaries are present, but there is too much androgen exposure.

Classic cause:

* congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH = enzyme defect in adrenal steroid synthesis)

Why:

* excess adrenal androgens masculinize external genitalia
* internal female organs are usually present because there are no testes making AMH (anti-Mullerian hormone)

---

**46,XY DSD**

Usually means testes are present or were supposed to form, but the body cannot make or respond to male hormones normally.

Examples:

* androgen insensitivity syndrome (AIS = androgen receptor defect)
* 5-alpha-reductase deficiency (cannot convert testosterone to DHT)

Why:

* AMH from testes removes Mullerian structures, so no uterus
* poor androgen or DHT effect means external genitalia may be female-appearing or ambiguous

DHT (dihydrotestosterone) is the stronger androgen needed for male external genitalia.

---

### Why the findings happen

If **SRY** (sex-determining region Y gene) works:

* gonads become testes
* Sertoli cells make AMH
* AMH makes Mullerian ducts regress

If **Leydig cells** make testosterone:

* Wolffian ducts become male internal structures

If testosterone becomes **DHT**:

* external genitalia masculinize

So:

* **No AMH** -> uterus/fallopian tubes stay
* **No testosterone effect** -> Wolffian ducts do not develop well
* **No DHT effect** -> external genitalia do not masculinize well

---

## One-line intuition

DSDs happen when the body's chromosomal, gonadal, hormone, or receptor signals give mixed instructions during reproductive development.

---

## Pearl

For Step 1, separate **AIS** from **5-alpha-reductase deficiency**:

* AIS: androgen receptor does not work -> scant pubic/axillary hair
* 5-alpha-reductase deficiency: receptor works, DHT is low -> virilization can increase at puberty when testosterone rises

## Reactive attachment disorder

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/Ways_of_attachment.svg/500px-Ways_of_attachment.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.medicpresents.com/infographicsimages/5s62_Reactive_Attachment_Disorder_Infographic.jpg)

![Image](https://liahonaacademy.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Reactive-Attachment-Disorder-RAD-Infographic.png)

Reactive attachment disorder, or **RAD (reactive attachment disorder)**, is a childhood disorder where a child does **not form normal emotional attachment** to caregivers after severe early neglect.

The core idea:

* the child needed comfort/safety
* the caregiver did not reliably provide it
* so the child learns: “people are not a safe source of comfort”

So instead of running to a caregiver when scared or hurt, the child is:

* emotionally withdrawn
* minimally responsive to comfort
* socially/affectively flat
* less likely to seek help from adults

---

### Why it happens

Attachment normally forms when a baby repeatedly gets:

* food
* warmth
* soothing
* protection
* consistent caregiving

If those are missing, the child’s brain does not build the normal “caregiver = safety” association.

That is why the child may look detached or unusually independent:

> not because they are fine, but because they learned that seeking comfort does not work.

---

### Classic risk factors

Think severe early caregiving disruption:

* neglect
* abuse
* institutional care/orphanage care
* repeated foster home changes
* caregiver absence or severe inconsistency

---

### Important distinction

RAD (reactive attachment disorder):

* emotionally withdrawn
* does **not** seek comfort much
* does **not** respond much when comforted

DSED (disinhibited social engagement disorder):

* overly friendly with strangers
* poor boundaries
* may wander off with unfamiliar adults

Both can come from severe neglect, but RAD is the **withdrawn/inhibited** one.

---

## One-line intuition

Reactive attachment disorder is what happens when early neglect teaches a child that caregivers are not reliable sources of safety.

---

## Pearl

RAD (reactive attachment disorder) is associated with **grossly inadequate care**, and treatment centers on a stable, nurturing caregiver relationship, not just medication.

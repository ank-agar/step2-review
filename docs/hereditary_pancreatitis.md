### Core idea

Hereditary pancreatitis is repeated pancreatitis (inflammation of the pancreas) caused by inherited gene mutations.

Most classically:

* **PRSS1** (cationic trypsinogen gene)

This is usually **autosomal dominant** (one mutated copy can cause disease).

---

### What goes wrong

The pancreas normally makes digestive enzymes as inactive forms so it does not digest itself.

Key enzyme:

* **trypsinogen** = inactive precursor
* **trypsin** = active protein-digesting enzyme

In hereditary pancreatitis, mutations make trypsin activity too easy to turn on or too hard to shut off.

So the pancreas gets injured by its own digestive enzymes.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Recurrent epigastric pain:** repeated pancreatic inflammation
* **Pain radiating to the back:** pancreas sits retroperitoneally (behind the peritoneal cavity), near posterior abdominal nerves
* **Chronic pancreatitis:** repeated injury causes fibrosis (scarring)
* **Steatorrhea:** fatty stools from loss of pancreatic lipase (fat-digesting enzyme)
* **Diabetes mellitus:** islet cell damage decreases insulin production

---

### One-line intuition

The pancreas is born with a broken “enzyme safety lock,” so trypsin activates too early and the pancreas starts digesting itself.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Hereditary pancreatitis greatly increases risk of **pancreatic adenocarcinoma** (pancreatic gland-forming cancer), especially with chronic inflammation and smoking.

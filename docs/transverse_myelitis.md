# Transverse myelitis

Transverse myelitis is inflammation of the spinal cord.

Break it down:

* transverse = across
* myelitis = inflammation of the spinal cord

So the idea is:

> inflammation damages a cross-section of the spinal cord.

That inflammation can damage myelin (the insulating covering around nerves), so signals cannot travel normally between the brain and body.

---

### Core symptoms

Because the spinal cord carries motor, sensory, and autonomic signals, transverse myelitis can cause:

* weakness or paralysis below the lesion
* numbness, tingling, or pain
* a sensory level (a clear body level below which sensation changes)
* bladder or bowel dysfunction

The lesion level matters:

* cervical cord = arms and legs can be affected
* thoracic cord = legs and trunk can be affected

---

### Why it happens

The spinal cord is like a cable carrying signals up and down.

Inflammation damages that cable, so:

* motor tracts affected -> weakness
* sensory tracts affected -> numbness/tingling/pain
* autonomic tracts affected -> urinary retention, incontinence, bowel issues

The word “transverse” is important because the inflammation often affects both sides of the cord, so symptoms are often bilateral.

---

### Causes/associations

Transverse myelitis can be:

* idiopathic (no clear cause)
* postinfectious
* autoimmune
* associated with multiple sclerosis (MS, a demyelinating disease of the central nervous system)
* associated with neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD, autoimmune demyelination often involving optic nerves and spinal cord)
* associated with myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein antibody disease (MOGAD, autoimmune demyelinating disease involving MOG antibodies)

---

## One-line intuition

Transverse myelitis is an inflamed spinal cord segment that blocks signals going up and down, causing weakness, sensory loss, and bladder/bowel problems below that level.

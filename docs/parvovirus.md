Parvovirus usually means **parvovirus B19**, a small **single-stranded DNA virus (DNA virus with one DNA strand)**.

Classic disease:

* **erythema infectiosum (fifth disease)**
* "slapped cheek" rash in kids
* lacy rash on trunk/arms/legs

---

### Core idea

Parvovirus B19 loves **erythroid precursors (immature red blood cell-making cells)** in bone marrow.

So it temporarily shuts down red blood cell production.

Most healthy people are fine because red blood cells live about 120 days, so a brief pause is not a big deal.

But if someone already destroys red blood cells quickly, like in **sickle cell disease (inherited hemoglobin disorder causing sickled red blood cells)**, that pause can cause a sudden severe anemia.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Slapped cheek rash:** immune response after infection
* **Lacy body rash:** immune complex-type skin reaction
* **Aplastic crisis:** bone marrow stops making red blood cells temporarily
* **Low reticulocytes (young red blood cells):** marrow is not producing new red blood cells
* **Fetal hydrops (severe fetal edema):** fetal anemia can become severe if infection occurs during pregnancy

---

### Important associations

Think:

* kid with red cheeks = fifth disease
* sickle cell patient + sudden anemia + low reticulocytes = parvovirus B19 aplastic crisis
* pregnant patient exposure = risk of fetal anemia/hydrops
* adult with joint pain = parvovirus can mimic rheumatoid arthritis

---

## One-line intuition

Parvovirus B19 is a "red blood cell factory shutdown" virus that usually causes a rash in kids, but can be dangerous when the patient depends on constant red blood cell production.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Parvovirus B19 causes **aplastic crisis**, meaning low red blood cells with **low reticulocytes**, because it infects erythroid precursors in bone marrow.

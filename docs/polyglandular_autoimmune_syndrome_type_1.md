# Polyglandular autoimmune syndrome type 1

Polyglandular autoimmune syndrome type 1 is an inherited autoimmune disease where the immune system attacks multiple endocrine glands.

Endocrine glands = hormone-secreting glands.

It is also called:

* APS-1 (autoimmune polyglandular syndrome type 1)
* APECED (autoimmune polyendocrinopathy-candidiasis-ectodermal dystrophy)

Break down APECED:

* autoimmune = immune system attacks self
* polyendocrinopathy = many endocrine glands are affected
* candidiasis = Candida fungal infection
* ectodermal dystrophy = abnormal skin/nails/teeth/hair development

---

### Classic triad

Think:

* chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis
* hypoparathyroidism
* Addison disease

Mucocutaneous = mucous membranes + skin.

Hypoparathyroidism = low parathyroid hormone, causing low calcium.

Addison disease = primary adrenal insufficiency, meaning the adrenal glands fail to make enough cortisol and aldosterone.

---

### Why it happens

Cause:

* AIRE gene mutation
* autosomal recessive inheritance

AIRE (autoimmune regulator) helps the thymus teach T cells what “self” looks like.

Normally:

* thymus shows self-antigens to developing T cells
* dangerous self-reactive T cells get deleted

In APS-1:

* defective AIRE means poor self-antigen presentation
* self-reactive T cells survive
* they attack endocrine organs

So the immune system basically fails its “do not attack self” training.

---

### Why Candida infections happen

Patients get chronic Candida infections because AIRE defects are associated with impaired antifungal immune pathways, especially involving IL-17 (interleukin-17), which helps defend mucosal surfaces from fungi.

So:

* weak mucosal antifungal defense → chronic Candida
* autoimmune gland destruction → endocrine failure

---

## One-line intuition

APS-1 is an AIRE mutation disease where bad T-cell education causes autoimmune destruction of endocrine glands plus chronic Candida infections.

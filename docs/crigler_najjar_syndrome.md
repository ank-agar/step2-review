### Core idea

Crigler-Najjar syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder where the liver cannot conjugate bilirubin well.

Conjugation = making bilirubin water-soluble so it can leave through bile.

The defective enzyme is:

* UGT1A1 (uridine diphosphate glucuronosyltransferase 1A1)

So unconjugated bilirubin builds up.

---

### Why jaundice happens

Normally:

* RBCs (red blood cells) break down heme
* heme becomes unconjugated bilirubin
* unconjugated bilirubin goes to liver
* UGT1A1 conjugates it
* conjugated bilirubin exits in bile

In Crigler-Najjar:

* UGT1A1 is absent or very low
* bilirubin stays unconjugated
* unconjugated bilirubin is lipid-soluble
* it deposits in tissues → jaundice

---

### Big danger

Unconjugated bilirubin can cross the BBB (blood-brain barrier), especially in newborns.

That can cause:

* kernicterus

Kernicterus = bilirubin deposition in the brain, classically basal ganglia, causing neurologic damage.

---

### Type I vs Type II

**Type I**

* no UGT1A1 activity
* severe neonatal unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia
* high risk of kernicterus
* does not respond to phenobarbital
* treated with phototherapy; liver transplant can be definitive

**Type II**

* decreased UGT1A1 activity
* milder
* lower bilirubin than type I
* responds to phenobarbital

Phenobarbital helps because it induces UGT1A1 enzyme activity.

---

## One-line intuition

Crigler-Najjar is like the liver missing the bilirubin "water-solubilizing step," so unconjugated bilirubin piles up and can poison the brain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Crigler-Najjar = unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia with normal liver enzymes, because the problem is bilirubin conjugation, not hepatocyte destruction.

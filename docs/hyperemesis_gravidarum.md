## Hyperemesis gravidarum

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Morning_sickness.svg/1280px-Morning_sickness.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.netterimages.com/images/vpv/000/000/032/32692-0550x0475.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c1/Molar_pregnancy.jpg)

Hyperemesis gravidarum is **severe nausea and vomiting during pregnancy**.

It is basically **morning sickness taken to a dangerous level**.

Instead of mild nausea, the patient can develop:

* weight loss
* dehydration
* ketonuria (ketones in the urine from starvation/fat breakdown)
* electrolyte abnormalities, especially hypokalemia (low potassium)

---

### Why it happens

The exact cause is not perfectly one thing, but it is associated with high pregnancy hormone signaling, especially:

* beta-hCG (beta-human chorionic gonadotropin; pregnancy hormone made by placenta)
* GDF15 (growth differentiation factor 15; a hormone strongly linked to nausea/vomiting in pregnancy)

High beta-hCG is why hyperemesis is classically worse with:

* multiple gestation (twins/triplets)
* molar pregnancy (abnormal placental tissue growth)

More placental tissue = more beta-hCG = more nausea/vomiting tendency.

---

### Why ketones show up

If the patient keeps vomiting and cannot eat:

* body runs out of easy glucose (sugar fuel)
* body starts breaking down fat
* fat breakdown makes ketones

So ketonuria means:

> this is not just nausea; she is starving/dehydrated enough to switch into fat-burning mode.

---

### Classic clues

Think hyperemesis gravidarum when pregnancy vomiting causes:

* dehydration
* weight loss
* ketonuria (ketones in urine)
* low potassium from vomiting

---

## One-line intuition

Hyperemesis gravidarum = pregnancy vomiting so severe that the patient becomes dehydrated, loses weight, and starts making ketones.

---

## Pearl 🔥

If a pregnant patient has **very severe vomiting + very high beta-hCG**, think about **molar pregnancy** or **multiple gestation** as the cause.

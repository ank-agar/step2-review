## Tinnitus

![Image](https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/images/1/1e/Auditory_neural_pathway.jpg)

![Image](https://ucient.wpenginepowered.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Tinnitus-Ear-Nose-and-Throat-Infographic.jpg)

Tinnitus = hearing sound when there is no external sound.

Usually described as:

* ringing
* buzzing
* hissing
* clicking
* roaring

Core idea:

> the auditory system is creating a “phantom sound.”

---

### Why it happens

Most commonly, there is damage somewhere in the hearing pathway, especially:

* cochlear hair cells
* auditory nerve
* auditory cortex

Cochlear hair cells = sensory cells in the cochlea (inner ear hearing organ) that convert sound vibrations into nerve signals.

If hair cells are damaged, the brain gets less normal sound input.

So the brain may “turn up the gain,” meaning it becomes more sensitive and starts interpreting abnormal/random nerve activity as sound.

That is why tinnitus often comes with sensorineural hearing loss (hearing loss from inner ear or nerve damage).

---

### Common causes

* noise exposure
* presbycusis (age-related hearing loss)
* ototoxic drugs (ear-toxic drugs), especially aminoglycosides
* Meniere disease
* earwax impaction
* temporomandibular joint problems, also called TMJ (jaw joint) problems

---

### Pulsatile tinnitus

Pulsatile tinnitus = tinnitus that beats with the heartbeat.

This is different because it suggests a vascular cause.

Vascular = related to blood vessels.

Think:

* carotid artery stenosis (narrowing)
* arteriovenous malformation, or AVM (abnormal artery-to-vein connection)
* glomus tumor/paraganglioma (neuroendocrine tumor near the middle ear)

---

## One-line intuition

Tinnitus is like the brain “hearing static” because the ear/nerve pathway is damaged or sending abnormal signals.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, aminoglycosides can cause ototoxicity (ear toxicity), leading to both tinnitus and sensorineural hearing loss.

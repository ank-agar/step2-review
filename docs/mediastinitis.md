### Core idea

Mediastinitis = inflammation/infection of the mediastinum (the central chest space between the lungs).

The mediastinum contains important structures like:

* heart
* great vessels
* trachea (windpipe)
* esophagus (food pipe)
* lymph nodes

So if this space gets infected, it can become very dangerous quickly.

---

### Common causes

Big ones to know:

* esophageal perforation (hole/tear in the esophagus)
* post-sternotomy infection (infection after opening the sternum during cardiac surgery)
* spread from deep neck/oral infection

Descending necrotizing mediastinitis means:

* descending = starts higher up, usually mouth/neck, then moves downward
* necrotizing = tissue-destroying
* mediastinitis = infection/inflammation of the mediastinum

So it is a neck/oral infection spreading down into the central chest.

---

### Why it is so dangerous

The mediastinum is a deep space with loose connective tissue.

That means infection can spread fast and cause:

* abscess (pocket of pus)
* sepsis (body-wide inflammatory response to infection)
* compression/involvement of heart, vessels, airway, or esophagus

Intuition:

> An infection in the "middle compartment" of the chest is dangerous because all the vital plumbing and airways live there.

---

### Imaging clue

CT (computed tomography) can show:

* widened mediastinum
* gas bubbles in mediastinal tissue
* fluid collections/abscess
* pleural effusion (fluid around the lung)

Gas in the mediastinum after esophageal rupture is a big clue because air and bacteria can leak out of the esophagus into that central chest space.

---

## One-line intuition

Mediastinitis is a deep central-chest infection, often from esophageal rupture, surgery, or neck infection spreading downward.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY cause after vomiting: Boerhaave syndrome (full-thickness esophageal rupture) can cause mediastinitis because esophageal contents leak into the mediastinum.

## Gastroparesis

### Core idea

Gastroparesis means delayed stomach emptying without a mechanical blockage.

Break the word apart:

* gastro- = stomach
* paresis = weakness/partial paralysis

So:

> the stomach is too weak or poorly coordinated to push food into the small intestine normally.

---

### Why it happens

Normally, stomach smooth muscle contracts in coordinated waves to move food through the pylorus (exit of the stomach) into the duodenum (first part of the small intestine).

In gastroparesis, that movement is slowed because of impaired:

* vagus nerve function
* enteric nervous system (gut nervous system)
* stomach smooth muscle contraction

The classic cause is diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar disease), because chronic hyperglycemia (high blood glucose) damages autonomic nerves, especially the vagus nerve.

---

### Symptoms

Food sits in the stomach too long, so patients get:

* early satiety = feeling full quickly
* nausea/vomiting
* bloating
* abdominal pain
* weight loss
* poor glucose control in diabetes mellitus

Why glucose becomes unpredictable:

* insulin may start working before food actually leaves the stomach
* then food absorbs later than expected
* so blood sugar can swing low first, then high later

---

### Diagnosis

Best test is gastric emptying scintigraphy.

Scintigraphy means imaging using a small radioactive tracer.

Patient eats a labeled meal, then imaging tracks how much food remains in the stomach over time.

Delayed emptying = too much food still retained in the stomach after several hours.

---

## One-line intuition

Gastroparesis is a “stomach pump problem”: food is not blocked, the stomach just cannot push it forward well.

---

## Pearl 🔥

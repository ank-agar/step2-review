### Core idea

An **umbilical hernia** is when abdominal contents push through a weak spot at the **umbilical ring** (the opening in the abdominal wall where the umbilical cord used to pass).

So the belly button bulges outward.

Usually the thing poking through is:

* fat
* omentum (fatty apron-like tissue in the abdomen)
* sometimes bowel

---

### Why it happens

In babies, the umbilical ring is supposed to close after birth.

If it does not close completely:

* there is a small abdominal wall defect
* pressure from crying/coughing/straining pushes tissue outward
* you see a soft bulge at the belly button

That is why it often becomes more obvious when the baby cries.

---

### Reducible vs dangerous

Most umbilical hernias are **reducible** (can be pushed back in) and painless.

More concerning terms:

* **incarcerated hernia** = stuck outside, cannot be pushed back in
* **strangulated hernia** = blood supply is cut off

Strangulation is dangerous because bowel without blood supply can become ischemic (low oxygen from poor blood flow) and die.

---

### Important management idea

In children, many umbilical hernias close on their own.

Surgery is more likely if it:

* persists past early childhood
* is large
* causes symptoms
* becomes incarcerated or strangulated

---

## One-line intuition

The belly button is a natural weak spot, and if that opening does not seal fully, abdominal contents can bulge through it.

---

## High-yield pearl

Umbilical hernias in infants are common and often close spontaneously, but **incarceration** or **strangulation** turns it into an urgent surgical problem.

### Core idea

Hypothermia = abnormally low core body temperature, classically:

* **below 35 deg C**
* **below 95 deg F**

The body is losing heat faster than it can make heat.

---

### What the body tries to do

The hypothalamus (brain temperature-control center) senses low temperature and triggers:

* **peripheral vasoconstriction** = blood vessels in skin/limbs constrict to keep warm blood near the core
* **shivering** = skeletal muscle rapidly contracts to generate heat
* **sympathetic activation** = increased catecholamines (fight-or-flight chemicals), so early hypothermia can cause tachycardia (fast heart rate) and hypertension (high blood pressure)

So early on, the body is basically saying:

> keep heat in the core + make more heat with muscle activity.

---

### Why symptoms happen

As temperature keeps dropping:

* **confusion/slurred speech:** cold slows CNS (central nervous system) function
* **clumsiness/ataxia:** brain and muscle function slow down
* **bradycardia:** cold slows cardiac pacemaker activity
* **slow breathing:** respiratory drive decreases
* **shivering stops in severe hypothermia:** the body runs out of energy and the CNS becomes too depressed to keep driving shivering

That last point is important:

* shivering = mild/moderate hypothermia
* no shivering + altered mental status = more severe and dangerous

---

### Step 1 association

Classic ECG (electrocardiogram, heart rhythm tracing) finding:

* **Osborn J wave**

This is a positive deflection at the J point (junction between QRS complex and ST segment on ECG).

Think:

> cold heart cells repolarize abnormally, so you get a little extra hump after the QRS complex.

---

## One-line intuition

Hypothermia is when the body's heat-conservation system gets overwhelmed, so the brain, muscles, lungs, and heart all start slowing down.

---

## HY pearl

In hypothermia, the myocardium (heart muscle) becomes very irritable, so handle the patient gently because rough movement can trigger ventricular fibrillation (VF, chaotic life-threatening ventricular rhythm).

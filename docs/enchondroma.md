### Core idea

An enchondroma is a benign cartilage tumor inside bone.

Break the word apart:

* en- = inside
* chondro = cartilage
* -oma = tumor

So:

> cartilage tumor growing inside the bone.

It usually grows in the medullary cavity, which is the inner marrow space of bone.

---

### Why it looks the way it does

Cartilage normally belongs at joint surfaces and growth plates, not sitting as a mass in the middle of bone.

So when cartilage cells grow inside bone, they can:

* make a lytic lesion, meaning an area where bone looks eaten out/darker on x-ray
* thin the cortex, meaning thin the hard outer shell of bone
* cause pathologic fracture, meaning fracture through weakened abnormal bone
* show rings-and-arcs calcifications, meaning curved cartilage-type calcium deposits

The key intuition:

The tumor is benign, but it replaces strong bone with cartilage, so the bone can become weaker.

---

### Common location

Most classic Step 1 association:

* small bones of the hand
* sometimes femur, tibia, or humerus

It is often found incidentally, meaning discovered by accident on imaging done for another reason.

---

### Associations

Multiple enchondromas can be seen in:

* Ollier disease, which is multiple enchondromas
* Maffucci syndrome, which is multiple enchondromas plus hemangiomas (benign blood vessel tumors)

Maffucci syndrome has a higher risk of malignant transformation, meaning becoming cancerous.

---

## One-line intuition

An enchondroma is benign cartilage stuck inside bone, usually in the hand, that can weaken the bone and cause fractures.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 distinction:

* Enchondroma = cartilage tumor inside the bone
* Osteochondroma = cartilage-capped bony outgrowth on the surface of bone

Inside = enchondroma.
Outside/projection = osteochondroma.

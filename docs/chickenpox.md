# Chickenpox

Chickenpox = primary infection with varicella-zoster virus, or **VZV (varicella-zoster virus)**.

It is a highly contagious viral infection that causes:

* fever
* malaise (general tired/sick feeling)
* itchy vesicular rash

Vesicular means “small fluid-filled blisters.”

---

### Core idea

Chickenpox is the **first time** your body gets infected with VZV.

Later, the same virus can hide in nerves and reactivate as:

* shingles, also called herpes zoster

So:

* first infection = chickenpox
* reactivation = shingles

---

### Why the rash looks weird

Classic chickenpox has lesions in **different stages at the same time**:

* red macules (flat red spots)
* papules (raised bumps)
* vesicles (fluid-filled blisters)
* crusted scabs

This happens because new lesions keep appearing in waves over several days.

That is why chickenpox is often described as:

> “dew drops on a rose petal”

Meaning: clear vesicles sitting on a red base.

---

### Where it starts

The rash usually starts on:

* face
* scalp
* trunk

Then it spreads outward to the arms and legs.

This is called a **centripetal distribution**.

Centripetal = more concentrated toward the center of the body.

---

### Why shingles can happen later

VZV is a herpesvirus.

Herpesviruses can become latent.

Latent means “sleeping/inactive inside the body.”

After chickenpox, VZV hides in dorsal root ganglia.

Dorsal root ganglia = sensory nerve cell clusters near the spinal cord.

Years later, if cell-mediated immunity weakens, the virus can reactivate and travel down a nerve to the skin, causing shingles.

Cell-mediated immunity = T-cell immune defense.

---

## One-line intuition

Chickenpox is the first VZV infection: a contagious itchy blister rash with lesions in multiple stages, and the virus can later sleep in nerves and return as shingles.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Chickenpox is a **DNA virus** infection because VZV is a herpesvirus, and all herpesviruses are double-stranded DNA viruses.

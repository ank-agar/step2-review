## Flat foot

Flat foot = **pes planus**.

It means the **medial longitudinal arch** (inner arch of the foot) is low, collapsed, or absent, so more of the sole touches the ground.

---

### Core idea

Normally, the foot has an arch that acts like a spring:

* absorbs shock
* distributes body weight
* helps push off during walking

In flat foot, that arch flattens.

So instead of weight being nicely distributed through an arched structure, the foot rolls inward and the inner sole bears more contact/pressure.

---

### Why the findings happen

The arch is supported by:

* bones
* ligaments
* plantar fascia
* posterior tibial tendon

If these supports are weak, stretched, or underdeveloped, the arch drops.

That can cause:

* **overpronation** = foot rolls inward too much
* heel may angle outward
* ankle/foot pain with walking
* knee, hip, or back discomfort from altered biomechanics

---

### Flexible vs rigid

**Flexible flat foot**:

* arch appears when not standing
* arch disappears when weight-bearing
* common in kids
* often benign

**Rigid flat foot**:

* arch is absent even when not weight-bearing
* more concerning
* can be due to tarsal coalition

**Tarsal coalition** = abnormal fusion/connection between foot bones.

---

## One-line intuition

Flat foot is basically a collapsed inner foot arch, so the foot loses some of its normal “spring” and rolls inward more when weight-bearing.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY association: **posterior tibial tendon dysfunction** is a common cause of acquired flat foot in adults because the posterior tibial tendon normally helps hold up the medial longitudinal arch.

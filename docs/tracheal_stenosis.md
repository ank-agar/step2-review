# Tracheal stenosis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2e/LarynxTracheaandbronchi.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/ff/Subglottic_stenosis_03.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f6/Subglottic_stenosis_%28X-ray%29.jpg)

Tracheal stenosis means narrowing of the trachea (windpipe).

So instead of air flowing through a wide tube:

* the airway lumen (inside opening of a tube) gets smaller
* airflow resistance increases
* the patient has trouble moving air, especially during exertion

---

### Core idea

Think:

> scarred or abnormally formed windpipe = tight airway tube.

The trachea is supposed to be a stiff, open tube held by cartilage rings. If part of it becomes narrowed, air has to squeeze through a smaller space.

---

### Why symptoms happen

* **Dyspnea (shortness of breath):** less air can pass through the narrowed trachea
* **Stridor (high-pitched noisy breathing):** turbulent air moving through a tight upper airway
* **Wheezing:** can mimic asthma because both involve noisy airflow obstruction
* **Recurrent infections/secretions:** narrowed airway makes it harder to clear mucus

Reasoning:

Airway resistance goes way up when radius goes down. So even a small decrease in tracheal diameter can cause a big increase in breathing effort.

---

### Common causes

Most HY acquired cause:

* prolonged intubation (breathing tube placement)

Why?

The tube or cuff can injure tracheal mucosa (inner lining), causing:

* ischemia (low blood flow)
* inflammation
* scar formation
* fixed narrowing

Other causes:

* tracheostomy (surgical airway opening in the neck)
* inhalation injury
* trauma
* congenital stenosis (born with a narrow trachea)
* autoimmune/inflammatory disease, like granulomatosis with polyangiitis (small-vessel vasculitis, meaning inflamed blood vessels)

---

### Diagnosis

Best direct test:

* bronchoscopy (camera inserted into the airways)

Other helpful tests:

* CT (computed tomography) neck/chest
* X-ray sometimes
* spirometry (breathing test), which can show fixed upper airway obstruction

---

## One-line intuition

Tracheal stenosis is basically a narrowed windpipe, usually from scarring after airway injury, making air squeeze through a tight tube.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Post-intubation tracheal stenosis can be misdiagnosed as asthma because both can cause wheezing, but tracheal stenosis often has **stridor** and does not improve much with bronchodilators (medications that open small airways).

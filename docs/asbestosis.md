## Asbestosis

Asbestosis is a chronic interstitial lung disease caused by inhaling asbestos fibers.

Interstitial means the lung tissue between the air sacs gets damaged.

The core idea:

* asbestos fibers get inhaled
* they lodge deep in the lungs
* macrophages (immune cleanup cells) try to eat them
* the fibers are hard to clear
* chronic inflammation leads to fibrosis (scarring)

So asbestosis is basically:

> asbestos fibers stuck in the lungs causing progressive lung scarring.

---

### Why symptoms happen

Fibrosis makes the lungs stiff.

Stiff lungs cannot expand well, so you get a restrictive lung disease pattern.

Restrictive means:

* decreased total lung capacity
* decreased forced vital capacity
* trouble taking a deep breath

Symptoms:

* progressive dyspnea (shortness of breath), especially with exertion
* dry cough
* bibasilar crackles

Bibasilar means at both lung bases.

---

### Classic findings

Asbestosis mainly affects the lower lung fields.

Imaging can show:

* lower-lobe interstitial fibrosis
* pleural plaques
* calcified pleural plaques

Pleura means the lining around the lungs.

Pleural plaques are scars/thickened areas of pleura from asbestos exposure.

---

### Pathology clue

Histology can show asbestos bodies, also called ferruginous bodies.

Ferruginous means iron-containing/rust-colored.

They look like golden-brown, beaded rods because asbestos fibers get coated with iron-protein material.

---

## One-line intuition

Asbestosis = inhaled asbestos fibers cause chronic inflammation and scarring of the lower lungs, making the lungs stiff.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Asbestos exposure is strongly associated with:

* asbestosis
* bronchogenic carcinoma (lung cancer)
* malignant mesothelioma
* pleural plaques

HY Step 1 point: smoking greatly increases the risk of bronchogenic carcinoma with asbestos exposure, but not mesothelioma.

# Viral encephalitis

![Image](https://journals.plos.org/plospathogens/article/figure/image?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.ppat.1002004.g001&size=large)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d5/Hsv_encephalitis.jpg)

Viral encephalitis = inflammation of the brain parenchyma (functional brain tissue) caused by a virus.

Break it down:

* encephalo- = brain
* -itis = inflammation

So viral encephalitis literally means:

> a virus gets into/irritates the brain tissue and causes brain inflammation.

---

### Core idea

This is different from meningitis.

* meningitis = inflammation of meninges (protective coverings around brain/spinal cord)
* encephalitis = inflammation of brain tissue itself

Because the actual brain tissue is inflamed, you get **brain dysfunction**, not just headache/neck stiffness.

That means:

* confusion
* altered mental status
* seizures
* focal neurologic deficits (weakness, speech problems, cranial nerve findings, etc.)
* fever/headache

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Viruses can injure neurons (nerve cells) directly and also trigger immune inflammation.

That causes:

* **fever:** immune response to infection
* **headache:** inflammation + swelling inside the skull
* **confusion/behavior change:** inflamed cortex/limbic system disrupts thinking and memory
* **seizures:** irritated neurons fire abnormally
* **focal deficits:** certain brain regions stop working normally

One-line intuition:

> Meningitis irritates the covering; encephalitis irritates the computer itself.

---

### High-yield causes

Important viral causes include:

* HSV-1 (herpes simplex virus type 1)
* arboviruses (arthropod-borne viruses spread by mosquitoes/ticks), like West Nile virus
* enteroviruses
* varicella-zoster virus
* rabies virus

HSV-1 is the classic Step 1 cause to know because it likes the temporal lobes.

Temporal lobe involvement can cause:

* personality changes
* memory problems
* seizures
* abnormal smell/taste auras sometimes

---

### Diagnosis clue

CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) usually shows a viral pattern:

* lymphocytic pleocytosis (increased lymphocytes, a type of white blood cell)
* normal glucose
* mildly increased protein

For suspected HSV encephalitis:

* PCR (polymerase chain reaction) of CSF detects HSV DNA
* MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) often shows temporal lobe abnormalities

---

### Treatment intuition

If HSV encephalitis is possible, give IV (intravenous) acyclovir immediately.

Reason:

* HSV encephalitis can be rapidly fatal
* acyclovir blocks viral DNA replication
* waiting for confirmatory testing can cost brain tissue

---

## Pearl

HSV-1 encephalitis classically affects the temporal lobes and can cause fever + altered mental status + seizures. Treat empirically with IV acyclovir.

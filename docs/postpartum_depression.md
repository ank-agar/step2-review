### Core idea

Postpartum depression is **major depression after childbirth**.

It is more than normal “baby blues.”

Key distinction:

* **Baby blues:** mild mood swings/crying/anxiety, usually resolves within 2 weeks
* **Postpartum depression:** more severe symptoms lasting >2 weeks and interfering with daily life

---

### What it looks like

Symptoms are basically major depressive disorder symptoms in the postpartum period:

* depressed mood
* loss of interest or pleasure
* guilt or worthlessness
* low energy
* sleep/appetite changes
* poor concentration
* anxiety or panic
* trouble bonding with the baby
* thoughts of harming self or baby

If there are thoughts of self-harm, harming the baby, hallucinations, delusions, or severe confusion, that is an emergency.

---

### Why it happens

After delivery, **estrogen and progesterone drop rapidly**.

That hormone crash can affect mood circuits in the brain, especially in someone who is already vulnerable.

Risk factors:

* prior depression
* prior postpartum depression
* family history of depression
* poor social support
* stressful life events
* sleep deprivation
* pregnancy or delivery complications

So the intuition is:

> childbirth creates a huge biologic + emotional + sleep-stress shift, and in some patients that shift triggers a true depressive episode.

---

### Treatment

Treatment can include:

* psychotherapy (talk therapy)
* CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy)
* IPT (interpersonal therapy)
* SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors)
* support with sleep, childcare, and social stressors

For severe postpartum depression, there are also postpartum-specific neurosteroid medications:

* brexanolone
* zuranolone

---

## One-line intuition

Postpartum depression is **major depression triggered around childbirth**, not “just being tired” or “normal new mom emotions.”

---

## Pearl

Postpartum psychosis is the dangerous one:

* hallucinations
* delusions
* mania
* confusion
* risk of harm to baby/self

It usually starts soon after delivery and requires emergency psychiatric care.

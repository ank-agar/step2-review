### Core idea

A language disorder is a problem with understanding or using language.

Language = words + grammar + meaning.

So this can affect:

* understanding spoken words
* producing words/sentences
* reading
* writing
* vocabulary
* grammar

---

### Simple distinction

Do not confuse:

* **Language disorder:** problem with words/meaning/grammar
* **Speech disorder:** problem physically producing sounds clearly

Example:

* Language disorder: child cannot organize words into a sentence
* Speech disorder: child knows the sentence but cannot pronounce the sounds correctly

---

### Why it happens

Language depends on brain networks that connect:

* **Wernicke area:** language comprehension
* **Broca area:** language production
* **arcuate fasciculus:** fiber tract connecting Wernicke area to Broca area

So if the brain has trouble processing meaning or building sentences, the person may struggle to understand or express language.

---

### In children

Developmental language disorder means the child has persistent language difficulty not explained by:

* hearing loss
* intellectual disability
* autism spectrum disorder
* lack of language exposure

It can show up as:

* late talking
* limited vocabulary
* short/simple sentences
* trouble following directions
* difficulty telling a story

---

### In adults

An acquired language disorder is often called **aphasia** (loss or impairment of language ability after brain injury).

Classic causes:

* stroke
* traumatic brain injury
* brain tumor
* neurodegenerative disease

High-yield aphasia examples:

* **Broca aphasia:** understands, but speech is slow/effortful
* **Wernicke aphasia:** fluent speech, but poor comprehension

---

## One-line intuition

Language disorder = the brain has trouble turning thoughts into words, or turning words into meaning.

---

## Pearl

Most language function is in the dominant hemisphere, usually the left hemisphere, so a left middle cerebral artery stroke can cause aphasia.

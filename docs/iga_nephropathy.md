### Core idea

IgA nephropathy is a glomerulonephritis (inflammation of the kidney glomeruli, which are the blood-filtering units) caused by IgA (immunoglobulin A) immune complex deposits in the mesangium (supportive central area of the glomerulus).

Also called Berger disease.

---

### Why it causes blood in urine

Think:

* IgA deposits in glomerular mesangium
* deposits trigger inflammation
* inflamed glomerular capillaries leak RBCs (red blood cells)
* patient gets hematuria (blood in urine)

So the big symptom is:

* episodic hematuria
* often after URI (upper respiratory infection)

---

### Classic timing

IgA nephropathy causes hematuria **within days** of a URI (upper respiratory infection).

This is why it is called:

* synpharyngitic hematuria

Meaning:

* hematuria happens at the same time as, or very soon after, pharyngitis (sore throat infection).

---

### Why URI triggers it

IgA (immunoglobulin A) is important in mucosal immunity (immune defense at surfaces like respiratory and GI tracts).

So after a mucosal infection, the body makes more IgA.

In IgA nephropathy, abnormal IgA forms immune complexes that get trapped in the glomerular mesangium.

That is why respiratory or GI (gastrointestinal) infections can trigger episodes.

---

### Biopsy finding

Key finding:

* mesangial IgA deposits

On immunofluorescence (antibody-based staining under microscope), you see granular IgA in the mesangium.

---

## One-line intuition

After a mucosal infection, abnormal IgA gets stuck in the glomerular mesangium, inflames the filter, and causes blood to leak into urine.

---

## Pearl 🔥

IgA nephropathy = hematuria **days after URI**.

Post-strep glomerulonephritis = hematuria **weeks after strep infection** with low C3 (complement component 3).

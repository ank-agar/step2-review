## Uterovaginal prolapse

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b7/Uterine_Prolapse.png)

Uterovaginal prolapse = the uterus + upper vagina descend downward into the vaginal canal because the pelvic support structures weaken.

Break it down:

* utero- = uterus
* vaginal = vagina
* prolapse = falling/slipping out of normal position

So the core idea is:

> the uterus and vaginal apex lose support and start dropping down.

### Why it happens

The uterus is normally held up by:

* pelvic floor muscles
* uterosacral ligaments (ligaments from uterus/cervix to sacrum)
* cardinal ligaments (major lateral support ligaments of cervix/uterus)
* connective tissue/fascia (supportive tissue sheets)

If these weaken, the uterus follows gravity downward.

Common risk factors:

* vaginal childbirth
* aging/menopause
* chronic increased intra-abdominal pressure (pressure inside the abdomen), like chronic cough, constipation, obesity, or heavy lifting

### Symptoms

Patients may feel:

* pelvic pressure/heaviness
* sensation of “something falling out”
* visible or palpable vaginal bulge
* urinary symptoms, like frequency, retention, or incontinence (loss of urine control)
* bowel symptoms if rectocele (rectum bulging into vagina) is also present

### Intuition

Think of the pelvic floor like a hammock holding up the uterus, bladder, and rectum.

If the hammock stretches out or tears, the uterus can sag into the vagina.

---

## One-line intuition

Uterovaginal prolapse is basically pelvic floor failure causing the uterus and upper vagina to drop downward.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Uterovaginal prolapse is a type of pelvic organ prolapse, and it often coexists with cystocele (bladder bulging into vagina) or rectocele (rectum bulging into vagina).

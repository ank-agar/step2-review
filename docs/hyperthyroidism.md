### Core idea

Hyperthyroidism = the thyroid gland makes too much thyroid hormone.

Main thyroid hormones:

* T3 (triiodothyronine)
* T4 (thyroxine)

These hormones increase metabolic activity, so too much thyroid hormone makes the body feel like it is running too fast.

---

### Classic findings

Think: **high metabolism + high sympathetic tone**.

* weight loss despite increased appetite
* heat intolerance
* sweating
* tremor
* anxiety/irritability
* tachycardia (fast heart rate)
* palpitations (feeling your heartbeat)
* diarrhea or frequent bowel movements
* menstrual irregularities

Why?

Thyroid hormone increases beta-adrenergic receptor expression, meaning tissues become more sensitive to catecholamines (epinephrine/norepinephrine-like sympathetic signals).

So the patient looks “adrenergic”:

* shaky
* sweaty
* anxious
* fast heart rate

---

### Labs

Usually:

* increased T3/T4
* decreased TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone)

Why is TSH low?

The pituitary senses there is already too much thyroid hormone, so it stops releasing TSH.

So:

> high thyroid hormone tells the brain: “stop stimulating the thyroid.”

---

### Most important cause

The classic Step 1 cause is **Graves disease**.

Graves disease is autoimmune:

* TSI (thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin) binds the TSH receptor
* this mimics TSH
* thyroid keeps making T3/T4

So even though true TSH is low, the thyroid is still being stimulated by antibody.

---

### Graves-specific findings

Graves can cause:

* diffuse goiter (enlarged thyroid gland)
* ophthalmopathy (eye disease)
* exophthalmos (protruding eyes)
* pretibial myxedema (thickened skin over the shins)

Why the eye findings?

Autoimmune inflammation affects tissue behind the eye, causing swelling and pushing the eye forward.

---

## One-line intuition

Hyperthyroidism is the body’s metabolism getting turned up too high because excess T3/T4 makes organs act overactive and catecholamine-sensitive.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Graves disease has low TSH but high thyroid activity because TSI (thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin) bypasses the pituitary and directly stimulates the TSH receptor.

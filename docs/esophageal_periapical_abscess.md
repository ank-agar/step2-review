This phrase is probably a wording mix-up.

**Periapical abscess** means an abscess around the apex, or tip, of a tooth root.

The esophagus does not really have a “periapical” area like a tooth does.

So this likely means one of these:

* **esophageal abscess** = pus collection in the esophageal wall
* **periesophageal abscess** = pus collection around the esophagus
* dental periapical abscess spreading into deep neck spaces, then potentially toward the esophagus/mediastinum

---

### Core idea

An abscess is a walled-off pocket of pus from infection.

Near the esophagus, that is dangerous because the esophagus sits in the neck/chest near the:

* trachea
* mediastinum, meaning the central chest space between the lungs
* major blood vessels

So infection can spread fast.

---

### Why symptoms happen

* **Dysphagia** = trouble swallowing, because swelling narrows the esophageal lumen
* **Odynophagia** = painful swallowing, because inflamed tissue gets stretched when food passes
* **Fever** = systemic inflammatory response to infection
* **Chest/neck pain** = the esophagus runs through the neck and mediastinum
* **Wall thickening on CT (computed tomography)** = inflamed, swollen esophageal tissue

---

### Common causes

Think: bacteria get into or around the esophagus after a barrier breaks.

Examples:

* fish bone or other sharp foreign body
* endoscopy injury
* esophageal perforation, meaning a tear through the esophageal wall
* spread from a retropharyngeal abscess, meaning an abscess behind the pharynx/throat
* rarely, spread from severe dental infection

---

## One-line intuition

It is basically pus near/in the swallowing tube, usually after bacteria sneak through injured mucosa.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Esophageal perforation can cause **mediastinitis** (infection/inflammation of the central chest space), which is a life-threatening emergency.

Classic Step 1 association: **Boerhaave syndrome** = full-thickness esophageal rupture after forceful vomiting.

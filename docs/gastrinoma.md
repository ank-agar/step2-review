## Gastrinoma

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17863/N00977-H.jpg)

![Image](https://epomedicine.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/gastrinoma-triangle.jpg)

### Core idea

A gastrinoma is a gastrin-secreting neuroendocrine tumor (tumor from hormone-releasing nerve/endocrine-type cells).

Gastrin = hormone that tells stomach parietal cells (acid-secreting stomach cells) to make acid.

So gastrinoma causes:

* increased gastrin
* increased stomach acid
* recurrent/severe peptic ulcers
* diarrhea

This syndrome is called ZES (Zollinger-Ellison syndrome).

---

### Where it usually is

Most gastrinomas are in the:

* duodenum (first part of small intestine)
* pancreas

Classic location = gastrinoma triangle.

This is why the diagram matters: most tumors hide near the pancreas + duodenum + bile duct region.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Recurrent ulcers:** too much gastrin causes too much acid, so the stomach/duodenum lining gets injured repeatedly
* **Ulcers that do not respond normally:** the acid drive is coming from a tumor, so acid production keeps getting pushed
* **Diarrhea:** excess acid enters the small intestine, damages mucosa, and inactivates pancreatic enzymes (digestive enzymes from the pancreas), causing malabsorption
* **Steatorrhea sometimes:** fat is not digested well, so stools can become greasy/fatty

---

### Classic clue

Think gastrinoma when you see:

* multiple peptic ulcers
* ulcers beyond the duodenal bulb
* ulcer disease without H. pylori (Helicobacter pylori bacteria) or NSAID (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug) use
* high fasting gastrin
* diarrhea with ulcer symptoms

---

## One-line intuition

A gastrinoma is basically a tumor pressing the “make acid” button nonstop.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Gastrinoma is associated with MEN1 (multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1): parathyroid tumors, pituitary tumors, and pancreatic endocrine tumors.

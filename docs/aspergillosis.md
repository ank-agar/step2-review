### Core idea

Aspergillosis is disease caused by **Aspergillus**, a mold whose spores are inhaled into the lungs.

Most healthy people inhale Aspergillus spores and are fine.

It becomes important when the patient has:

* neutropenia (low neutrophils, which are infection-fighting white blood cells)
* transplant immunosuppression
* chronic steroids
* asthma
* cystic fibrosis
* old lung cavities, like after tuberculosis

---

### Main forms

Think of Aspergillus as causing 3 big patterns:

* **Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis**, or ABPA (allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis): allergic reaction in asthma/cystic fibrosis
* **Aspergilloma:** fungus ball sitting inside a pre-existing lung cavity
* **Invasive aspergillosis:** fungus invades lung tissue and blood vessels in immunocompromised patients

---

### Why the findings happen

Aspergillus likes the airways because we inhale the spores.

In ABPA:

* the fungus triggers a hypersensitivity reaction
* this causes wheezing, mucus plugging, and bronchiectasis (permanent airway dilation)
* labs often show high IgE (immunoglobulin E, an allergy-associated antibody) and eosinophilia (increased eosinophils, allergy/parasite-associated white blood cells)

In aspergilloma:

* the fungus colonizes an old cavity
* it forms a mobile fungal ball
* it can cause hemoptysis (coughing up blood) because it irritates nearby vessels

In invasive aspergillosis:

* hyphae (branching fungal filaments) invade blood vessels
* vessel invasion causes thrombosis (clotting), infarction (tissue death from loss of blood flow), and hemorrhage (bleeding)
* this is why CT (computed tomography) can show a halo sign, meaning a lung nodule surrounded by hemorrhage

---

### Microscopy clue

Aspergillus has:

* septate hyphae
* acute-angle branching, classically around 45 degrees

Compare that to Mucor/Rhizopus, which has broad nonseptate hyphae with wide-angle branching.

---

## One-line intuition

Aspergillus is an inhaled mold that is harmless in most people, allergic in asthmatics/cystic fibrosis patients, a fungus ball in old cavities, and angioinvasive in immunocompromised patients.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Invasive aspergillosis is classically seen in neutropenic patients and is treated with **voriconazole**; the HY histology clue is **septate hyphae branching at 45 degrees**.

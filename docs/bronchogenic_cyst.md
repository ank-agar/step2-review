## Bronchogenic cyst

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK536973/bin/bronchogenic__cyst.jpg)

![Image](https://obgynkey.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/A321246_1_En_52_Fig2_HTML.jpg)

### Core idea

A bronchogenic cyst is a congenital cyst (fluid-filled sac present from birth) that comes from abnormal budding of the primitive foregut.

Foregut = embryologic tube that helps form the airway and upper GI (gastrointestinal) tract.

So the intuition is:

> a little piece of airway tissue buds off during development and becomes a blind pouch filled with mucus/fluid.

---

### Where it usually is

Most commonly near the tracheobronchial tree:

* mediastinum (middle chest space between the lungs)
* near the trachea (windpipe)
* near main bronchi (large airways)
* sometimes inside the lung

This is why it can compress nearby structures.

---

### Why symptoms happen

Many are asymptomatic.

If large, they can cause:

* cough
* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* wheezing/stridor (noisy breathing from airway narrowing)
* dysphagia (difficulty swallowing)
* recurrent infections

Why?

Because the cyst can press on the airway or esophagus (food tube), or get infected.

---

### Histology clue

Bronchogenic cysts are lined by respiratory epithelium:

* ciliated pseudostratified columnar epithelium
* may have cartilage
* may have smooth muscle
* may have mucous glands

That makes sense because it is basically misplaced airway tissue.

---

## One-line intuition

A bronchogenic cyst is an embryologic airway bud that got pinched off and turned into a mucus-filled cyst near the trachea/bronchi.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association: bronchogenic cysts are foregut duplication-type lesions, so think congenital + respiratory epithelium + mediastinal mass near the carina (where the trachea splits into the main bronchi).

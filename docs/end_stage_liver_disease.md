End-stage liver disease = very advanced chronic liver disease where the liver can no longer do its normal jobs well.

Usually this means decompensated cirrhosis.

Break that down:

* cirrhosis = chronic liver scarring with regenerative nodules (clumps of hepatocytes trying to regrow)
* decompensated = the organ cannot keep up anymore

So:

> end-stage liver disease is a scarred, distorted liver that is failing at blood flow, protein-making, bile handling, and detox.

---

### Core idea

Think:

> chronic liver injury → fibrosis (scar tissue) → portal hypertension + liver failure

Portal hypertension = high pressure in the portal venous system, which normally drains blood from the gut to the liver.

The 2 big problem buckets are:

* blocked blood flow through the liver
* loss of liver function

---

### Why the findings happen

Scar tissue blocks blood flow through the liver.

That causes portal hypertension, leading to:

* ascites (fluid in the abdomen)
* esophageal varices (dilated esophageal veins that can rupture and bleed)
* splenomegaly (enlarged spleen)
* thrombocytopenia (low platelets) because the enlarged spleen traps platelets

The liver also loses synthetic function (ability to make important proteins), leading to:

* low albumin (main blood protein that keeps fluid inside vessels) → edema/ascites
* low clotting factors → easy bleeding
* increased PT/INR (prothrombin time/international normalized ratio; blood clotting tests)

The liver also loses detox function, so ammonia can build up.

That causes hepatic encephalopathy:

* hepatic = liver-related
* encephalopathy = brain dysfunction

So hepatic encephalopathy = confusion/asterixis from liver failure toxins affecting the brain.

---

### Classic decompensation signs

End-stage liver disease often shows up as:

* ascites
* jaundice (yellow skin/eyes from bilirubin buildup)
* variceal bleeding
* hepatic encephalopathy
* hepatorenal syndrome (kidney failure due to severe liver disease and abnormal blood flow signaling)

Jaundice happens because the liver cannot process and excrete bilirubin well.

Bleeding happens because the liver cannot make clotting factors well, and portal hypertension can create fragile varices.

---

### Common causes

Common causes include:

* chronic hepatitis B or C (long-term viral liver infection)
* alcohol use disorder
* MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis; fatty liver inflammation from metabolic disease)
* autoimmune hepatitis
* chronic bile duct disease

Different starting causes, same final pathway:

> repeated injury → scarring → cirrhosis → decompensation

---

## One-line intuition

End-stage liver disease is when chronic liver scarring gets so severe that blood backs up before the liver and the liver cannot make proteins, clear toxins, or handle bilirubin.

---

## Pearl 🔥

MELD (Model for End-Stage Liver Disease) estimates severity and helps prioritize liver transplant.

It uses labs reflecting liver/kidney failure, especially:

* bilirubin
* INR (international normalized ratio; clotting test)
* creatinine (kidney function marker)
* sodium

### Core idea

Pigmented nevi are benign moles made of melanocytes.

Melanocytes are pigment-producing cells in the epidermis (outer skin layer).

So the basic idea is:

> a local cluster of melanocytes makes extra melanin, so you see a brown/black spot.

Melanin is the pigment that gives skin, hair, and moles their color.

---

### Why they are pigmented

The nevus cells are melanocytes or melanocyte-like cells.

They produce melanin, and melanin is brown-black.

So:

* more melanocytes = more pigment-producing cells
* more melanin = darker lesion
* deeper pigment can sometimes look blue/gray because of light scattering through skin

That is why some nevi are tan, brown, black, or bluish.

---

### Types by location

Pathology classifies melanocytic nevi by where the nevus cells sit:

* **Junctional nevus:** melanocytes at the dermoepidermal junction, meaning the border between epidermis and dermis
* **Compound nevus:** melanocytes at the junction and in the dermis
* **Intradermal nevus:** melanocytes only in the dermis

Clinical intuition:

* junctional = usually flat and darker
* compound = raised center with pigment
* intradermal = often dome-shaped and lighter/flesh-colored

---

### Benign vs concerning

Most pigmented nevi are benign.

But melanoma (malignant cancer of melanocytes) can mimic a mole, so changing or atypical lesions matter.

Use ABCDE:

* **A:** asymmetry
* **B:** border irregularity
* **C:** color variation
* **D:** diameter greater than 6 mm
* **E:** evolution, meaning changing over time

The most important warning sign is evolution because benign moles are usually stable.

---

## One-line intuition

A pigmented nevus is a benign cluster of melanocytes that makes melanin, creating a mole.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

A giant congenital melanocytic nevus increases melanoma risk because it contains a large population of melanocytes that can undergo malignant transformation.

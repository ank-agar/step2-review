## Hiatal hernia

### Core idea

A hiatal hernia is when part of the stomach pushes upward through the esophageal hiatus.

Esophageal hiatus = the normal hole in the diaphragm where the esophagus passes through.

Diaphragm = muscle that separates the chest from the abdomen and helps with breathing.

So instead of the stomach staying fully below the diaphragm, part of it slips into the chest.

---

### Why reflux happens

Normally, the lower esophageal sphincter helps stop acid from coming back up.

Lower esophageal sphincter = LES = muscular valve between the esophagus and stomach.

In a hiatal hernia:

* the stomach/LES can move above the diaphragm
* the diaphragm no longer reinforces the LES as well
* acid can reflux upward more easily

That causes GERD symptoms.

GERD = gastroesophageal reflux disease = chronic acid reflux into the esophagus.

Symptoms can include:

* heartburn
* regurgitation
* chest discomfort
* dysphagia sometimes

Dysphagia = trouble swallowing.

---

### Main types

**Sliding hiatal hernia**

The gastroesophageal junction slides upward with the stomach.

Gastroesophageal junction = GE junction = where the esophagus meets the stomach.

This is the most common type and is strongly associated with GERD.

**Paraesophageal hernia**

Part of the stomach herniates next to the esophagus, but the GE junction may stay lower.

This is less common but more concerning because the stomach can get trapped.

---

## One-line intuition

A hiatal hernia is basically the stomach sneaking up through the diaphragm, weakening the anti-reflux barrier.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Hiatal hernia predisposes to GERD because the LES loses help from the diaphragm.

Think:

* LES = main anti-reflux valve
* diaphragm = extra external squeeze/support
* hiatal hernia = support is disrupted, so acid reflux becomes easier

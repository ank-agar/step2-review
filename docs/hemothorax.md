## Hemothorax

Hemothorax = blood in the pleural space (the potential space between the lung and chest wall).

Break it down:

* hemo- = blood
* thorax = chest

So it literally means:

> blood filling the chest cavity around the lung.

---

### Core idea

The blood sits outside the lung, not inside the airways.

That blood compresses the lung from the outside, so the lung cannot expand well.

This causes:

* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* pleuritic chest pain (sharp pain worse with breathing)
* decreased breath sounds on the affected side
* dullness to percussion (a dull tapping sound because fluid/blood is dense)
* shock if severe, because the patient is losing blood into the chest

---

### Why it happens

Most commonly:

* chest trauma
* rib fracture tearing an intercostal vessel (blood vessel running between ribs)
* penetrating injury
* lung or great vessel injury

The key mechanism:

* bleeding enters the pleural cavity
* blood takes up space
* lung gets compressed
* ventilation drops on that side

---

### Imaging intuition

On chest x-ray (CXR; chest radiograph), hemothorax often looks like a pleural effusion (fluid in the pleural space):

* white opacity at the lung base
* blunted costophrenic angle (loss of the sharp angle where diaphragm meets chest wall)
* large cases can whiten out most of one hemithorax (one side of the chest)

Why white?

Because blood is denser than air, so it blocks more x-rays and appears radiopaque (white).

---

### Treatment idea

Usually treat with a chest tube.

Chest tube = tube placed into the pleural space to drain blood/air so the lung can re-expand.

If bleeding is massive or continues, the patient may need surgery to stop the source.

---

## One-line intuition

Hemothorax is internal bleeding into the pleural space that compresses the lung and can cause both respiratory distress and hemorrhagic shock (shock from blood loss).

---

## Pearl

Hemothorax gives **dullness to percussion**, while pneumothorax (air in the pleural space) gives **hyperresonance to percussion** because blood is dense and air is extra resonant.

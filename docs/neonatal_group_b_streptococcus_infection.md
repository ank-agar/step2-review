## Neonatal group B streptococcus infection

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Group_B_Strep.gif/330px-Group_B_Strep.gif)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/group-b-strep/media/images/StrepB.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Streptococcus_group_b.JPG/1280px-Streptococcus_group_b.JPG)

Neonatal group B streptococcus infection is a serious newborn infection caused by **Streptococcus agalactiae**.

GBS (group B streptococcus) commonly colonizes the maternal:

* vagina
* rectum/GI tract (gastrointestinal tract)

Most moms are asymptomatic, but the baby can get exposed during delivery.

---

### Core idea

GBS is a major cause of neonatal:

* sepsis (life-threatening systemic infection)
* pneumonia (lung infection)
* meningitis (infection of the meninges, the coverings of the brain and spinal cord)

Think:

> colonized birth canal -> newborn exposure -> bloodstream/lung/meningeal infection

---

### Early vs late onset

**Early-onset disease**:

* first week of life
* usually acquired from mom during labor/delivery
* classically causes sepsis and pneumonia

**Late-onset disease**:

* after the first week, up to about 3 months
* often causes bacteremia (bacteria in blood) or meningitis

---

### Why babies get so sick

GBS has a capsule, which helps it avoid phagocytosis (immune-cell eating).

Newborns have immature immune systems, especially weaker opsonization (tagging bacteria so immune cells can eat them), so the bacteria can spread quickly.

That is why symptoms can be vague but dangerous:

* poor feeding
* fever or low temperature
* lethargy
* respiratory distress
* irritability
* seizures if meningitis occurs

---

### Prevention

Pregnant patients are screened for GBS colonization late in pregnancy.

If positive, they get intrapartum antibiotics (antibiotics during labor), usually penicillin.

This lowers bacterial load in the birth canal, so the baby is less likely to be exposed.

---

## One-line intuition

GBS is usually harmless colonization in mom, but in a newborn it can become rapidly invasive because the baby's immune system cannot contain encapsulated bacteria well.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Streptococcus agalactiae is **CAMP test positive**, **beta-hemolytic**, and classically causes neonatal sepsis, pneumonia, and meningitis.

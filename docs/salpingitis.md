# Salpingitis

![Image](https://www.mayoclinic.org/-/media/kcms/gbs/patient-consumer/images/2013/08/26/10/24/mcdc7_pelvic_inflammatory_disease_jpg.jpg)

![Image](https://training.seer.cancer.gov/images/anatomy/reproductive/reproductive_female.jpg)

![Image](https://pathologyapps.com/histology/109-salpingitis-fallopian-tube.jpg)

Salpingitis = inflammation of the fallopian tube.

Usually this is from an ascending infection, meaning bacteria move upward from:

* vagina
* cervix
* uterus
* then fallopian tubes

Most commonly it is part of PID (pelvic inflammatory disease).

Common causes:

* Chlamydia trachomatis
* Neisseria gonorrhoeae
* mixed vaginal flora

---

### Core idea

The fallopian tube gets infected and inflamed.

That inflammation can cause:

* swollen tube
* pus in the tube
* scarring
* adhesions, meaning abnormal fibrous bands that make tissues stick together

---

### Why infertility and ectopic pregnancy happen

The fallopian tube is supposed to move the egg toward the uterus using:

* cilia, which are tiny hair-like projections
* smooth muscle contractions

Inflammation damages the tube lining and cilia.

So the egg or embryo can get stuck.

That leads to:

* infertility because sperm and egg may not meet
* ectopic pregnancy because the embryo may implant in the tube instead of the uterus

---

### Symptoms

Think PID-type symptoms:

* lower abdominal or pelvic pain
* fever
* cervical motion tenderness, meaning pain when the cervix is moved on pelvic exam
* abnormal vaginal discharge
* dyspareunia, meaning painful sex

---

## One-line intuition

Salpingitis is basically PID hitting the fallopian tubes, and the big danger is tubal scarring.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association: salpingitis can cause ectopic pregnancy because tubal scarring blocks embryo transport through the fallopian tube.

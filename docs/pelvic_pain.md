Pelvic pain = pain felt in the lower abdomen/pelvis, where the bladder, uterus, ovaries, fallopian tubes, bowel, pelvic floor muscles, and nerves all sit close together.

So the key idea is:

> Pelvic pain is a location, not a diagnosis.

You have to ask: which organ system is irritated?

---

### Big buckets

Common causes include:

* gynecologic: ectopic pregnancy, ovarian torsion, endometriosis, ovarian cyst, PID (pelvic inflammatory disease)
* urinary: UTI (urinary tract infection), kidney stone, interstitial cystitis (chronic bladder pain syndrome)
* GI (gastrointestinal): appendicitis, IBS (irritable bowel syndrome), constipation, diverticulitis
* musculoskeletal/nerve: pelvic floor spasm, hernia, nerve irritation

---

### Why pain happens

Pain usually comes from:

* **inflammation:** infected or irritated tissue releases pain signals
* **stretching/distention:** cysts, bowel gas, urinary obstruction, or swelling stretch organs
* **ischemia:** low blood flow, classically ovarian torsion
* **bleeding:** ectopic pregnancy rupture or hemorrhagic ovarian cyst irritates the peritoneum (abdominal lining)
* **muscle spasm:** pelvic floor muscles stay contracted and cause deep aching pain

---

### High-yield dangerous causes

In reproductive-age patients, always think:

* **ectopic pregnancy:** pregnancy outside the uterus, classically fallopian tube
* **ovarian torsion:** ovary twists on its blood supply
* **PID (pelvic inflammatory disease):** ascending infection of uterus/fallopian tubes/ovaries
* **appendicitis:** inflamed appendix can present as lower pelvic pain

These matter because they can become emergencies.

---

### Intuition

Pelvic organs are packed into a small space, so different diseases can feel similar.

That is why pelvic pain questions often use clues:

* missed period or positive pregnancy test -> ectopic pregnancy
* sudden severe unilateral pain -> ovarian torsion or ruptured cyst
* fever + cervical motion tenderness -> PID (pelvic inflammatory disease)
* cyclic pain with periods -> endometriosis
* dysuria (painful urination) + frequency -> UTI (urinary tract infection)

---

## One-line intuition

Pelvic pain means something in the lower pelvis is inflamed, stretched, ischemic, bleeding, or spasming.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In any reproductive-age patient with pelvic pain, the first test is usually a pregnancy test, because ectopic pregnancy is the dangerous diagnosis you do not want to miss.

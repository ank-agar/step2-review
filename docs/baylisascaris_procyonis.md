# Baylisascaris procyonis

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/PHIL_Images/3381/3381_lores.jpg)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/baylisascaris/media/images/2024/05/baylisascaris-egg-300x169-1.png)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/dpdx/baylisascariasis/images/5/Baylisascaris_GA1adults.jpg)

Baylisascaris procyonis is the raccoon roundworm.

It is a nematode (roundworm) that normally lives in raccoons, but humans can accidentally get infected by swallowing eggs from raccoon feces.

---

### Core idea

Raccoons are the normal host.

Humans are accidental hosts.

So in humans, the larvae do not mature normally in the gut. Instead, they migrate through tissues and can damage:

* brain
* eye
* liver/lungs/other organs

This is called larva migrans (larvae migrating through tissues).

---

### Why it is dangerous

After a person ingests embryonated eggs (eggs containing larvae):

* eggs hatch in the intestine
* larvae enter the bloodstream/tissues
* larvae migrate randomly
* inflammation + tissue damage occurs

The scary form is neural larva migrans (larvae migrating through the nervous system), which can cause:

* encephalitis (brain inflammation)
* seizures
* neurologic deficits
* coma/death in severe cases

It can also cause ocular larva migrans (larvae migrating through the eye), leading to vision problems or blindness.

---

### Key clue

Think:

* child
* raccoon exposure or contaminated soil
* eosinophilia (high eosinophils, allergy/parasite-fighting white blood cells)
* neurologic symptoms or eye findings

Eosinophils show up because this is a helminth (worm parasite), and helminths classically trigger eosinophils.

---

## One-line intuition

Baylisascaris procyonis is raccoon roundworm: harmless-ish in raccoons, but in humans the larvae get lost and migrate into organs, especially brain and eye.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Raccoon roundworm is a classic cause of severe neural larva migrans, while Toxocara canis/cati (dog/cat roundworm) more classically causes visceral or ocular larva migrans.

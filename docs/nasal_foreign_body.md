# Nasal foreign body

![Image](https://dgnvxbcc3-res.cloudinary.com/image/upload/v1773106473/Journals/Automation/AFP/2007/1015/p1185-f2-gif.gif)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/9657.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b3/Anatomy_of_the_human_nasal_cavity.png)

Nasal foreign body = an object stuck inside the nose.

This is most common in young children who put small things into a nostril, like:

* beads
* toy parts
* paper
* food
* pebbles
* button batteries

---

### Core idea

The object usually gets stuck in the front part of the nasal cavity, often near the:

* inferior turbinate
* nasal septum

Inferior turbinate = a curved shelf of bone/mucosa (moist lining) on the side wall of the nose.

Nasal septum = the wall between the two nostrils.

So the object sits in a narrow space and blocks drainage/airflow on one side.

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

* child
* unilateral nasal discharge
* foul smell
* nasal obstruction
* sometimes epistaxis

Unilateral = one-sided.

Epistaxis = nosebleed.

The high-yield clue is foul-smelling discharge from only one nostril.

---

### Why the discharge smells bad

The foreign body irritates the nasal mucosa and traps secretions.

Then bacteria grow in the stagnant mucus.

So you get:

* inflammation
* purulent discharge
* bad odor

Purulent = pus-like.

---

### Important danger

Button batteries and paired magnets are emergencies.

Button batteries can leak caustic material and generate an electrical current, causing:

* mucosal burns
* tissue necrosis
* nasal septal perforation

Necrosis = tissue death.

Perforation = a hole.

---

### Management intuition

If the object is visible and easy to reach, it can often be removed with direct visualization.

Tools/techniques may include:

* forceps
* suction
* hook/curette
* positive pressure technique

ENT (ear, nose, and throat) help is needed if it is deep, hard to see, impacted, dangerous, or removal attempts fail.

Do not keep blindly poking at it, because that can push it backward into the nasopharynx and create an aspiration risk.

Nasopharynx = back of the nose/throat area.

Aspiration = inhaling something into the airway/lungs.

---

## One-line intuition

A nasal foreign body is usually a small object stuck in one nostril of a child, causing one-sided foul-smelling discharge because it blocks drainage and irritates the nasal mucosa.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Unilateral foul-smelling nasal discharge in a child = nasal foreign body until proven otherwise.

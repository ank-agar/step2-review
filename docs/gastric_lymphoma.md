### Core idea

Gastric lymphoma = lymphoma (cancer of lymphocytes, which are immune cells) that involves the stomach.

Most important Step 1 version:

* **gastric MALT lymphoma**

MALT = mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue, meaning immune tissue sitting near mucosal surfaces like the stomach lining.

---

### Big association

Gastric MALT lymphoma is classically linked to:

* **H. pylori** = Helicobacter pylori, a bacteria that chronically infects the stomach

Why?

H. pylori causes chronic inflammation in the stomach. That keeps stimulating B cells (antibody-producing lymphocytes). Over time, repeated B-cell stimulation can lead to a malignant B-cell clone.

So the logic is:

> chronic H. pylori infection → chronic lymphoid stimulation → MALT lymphoma

---

### How it presents

Symptoms can look vague, like:

* epigastric pain (upper central abdominal pain)
* nausea
* weight loss
* early satiety (feeling full quickly)
* GI bleeding (gastrointestinal bleeding) sometimes

It can look like an ulcer or gastric cancer on endoscopy, so biopsy is needed.

---

### Why antibiotics can treat a cancer here

This is the weird HY point.

Early gastric MALT lymphoma can regress after H. pylori eradication therapy because the tumor may still depend on chronic antigen stimulation.

Antigen = something the immune system recognizes and reacts to.

So removing H. pylori removes the immune stimulus feeding the lymphoma.

---

### Important pathology idea

MALT lymphoma is usually a low-grade B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

Non-Hodgkin lymphoma = lymphoma that is not Hodgkin lymphoma and usually lacks Reed-Sternberg cells.

Histology may show:

* small malignant B cells
* lymphoepithelial lesions

Lymphoepithelial lesion = lymphoma cells invading and damaging epithelial glands.

---

## One-line intuition

Gastric lymphoma is usually a stomach B-cell lymphoma caused by long-term H. pylori-driven immune stimulation.

---

## Pearl 🔥

If gastric MALT lymphoma has **t(11;18)** translocation (chromosomal swap between chromosomes 11 and 18), it is less likely to respond to H. pylori eradication alone.

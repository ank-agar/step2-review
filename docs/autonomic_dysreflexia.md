## Autonomic dysreflexia

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK613069/bin/ch9nsa-Image044.jpg)

### Core idea

Autonomic dysreflexia is a sudden, dangerous sympathetic nervous system overreaction after spinal cord injury, usually at or above **T6**.

Autonomic = automatic body functions  
Dysreflexia = abnormal reflex response

So basically:

> a trigger below the spinal cord injury causes massive vasoconstriction and severe hypertension.

Hypertension means high blood pressure.

---

### Classic setup

Patient with spinal cord injury at or above **T6** gets:

* severe headache
* very high blood pressure
* sweating/flushing above the lesion
* cold, pale skin below the lesion
* bradycardia

Bradycardia means slow heart rate.

---

### Why it happens

A painful or irritating stimulus happens **below** the spinal cord lesion, most commonly:

* full bladder
* kinked Foley catheter
* constipation/fecal impaction
* skin irritation

The body sends pain signals upward, but the spinal cord injury blocks proper communication with the brain.

So the spinal cord reflexively activates the sympathetic nervous system below the lesion:

* blood vessels constrict
* blood pressure shoots up

The brain senses the high blood pressure and tries to lower it through the parasympathetic nervous system.

But those calming signals cannot pass below the spinal cord lesion.

So you get:

* parasympathetic effects above the lesion: flushing, sweating, bradycardia
* sympathetic effects below the lesion: vasoconstriction, pale/cold skin, hypertension

---

### Why T6 matters

T6 is important because the splanchnic vascular bed is around this level.

Splanchnic means blood vessels supplying abdominal organs.

That vascular bed holds a lot of blood, so if it constricts hard, blood pressure rises dramatically.

---

## One-line intuition

The lower body is stuck in uncontrolled "fight or flight," while the brain can only calm the body above the spinal cord injury.

---

## Pearl

Autonomic dysreflexia is a medical emergency: in a patient with spinal cord injury at or above T6, sudden headache + hypertension should make you immediately think **full bladder or bowel impaction** until proven otherwise.

### Core idea

Venous insufficiency means the leg veins cannot return blood back to the heart efficiently.

Usually this is because the venous valves become incompetent.

Incompetent valve = valve does not close properly.

So instead of blood moving upward toward the heart:

* blood falls backward
* blood pools in the legs
* venous pressure increases

This is why it is also called chronic venous stasis.

Stasis = standing still / pooling.

---

### Why the findings happen

Normal leg veins fight gravity using:

* one-way valves
* calf muscle pump

When you walk, calf muscles squeeze deep veins and push blood upward.

But if valves are damaged, blood refluxes.

Reflux = backward flow.

That chronic pooling causes:

* leg heaviness/aching
* edema (swelling from fluid leaving vessels)
* varicose veins (dilated, tortuous superficial veins)
* skin darkening from hemosiderin
* venous ulcers near the medial malleolus

Hemosiderin = iron-storage pigment left behind after red blood cells leak out and break down.

Medial malleolus = inner ankle bone.

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

* older patient
* obesity, pregnancy, prolonged standing, or prior deep vein thrombosis
* worse at end of day
* better with leg elevation
* brown discoloration around ankles
* shallow ulcer near medial malleolus

Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) = clot in a deep vein.

Why DVT matters:

* clot damages venous valves
* damaged valves allow reflux
* reflux causes chronic venous hypertension

Venous hypertension = increased pressure inside veins.

---

### Venous vs arterial ulcer

Venous ulcer:

* medial malleolus
* shallow, irregular border
* swollen leg
* brown skin changes
* pulses usually present

Arterial ulcer:

* toes/lateral ankle/pressure points
* punched-out appearance
* painful
* cold limb
* weak pulses

---

## One-line intuition

Venous insufficiency is basically leaky leg vein valves causing blood to pool downward, which creates swelling, brown skin changes, varicose veins, and medial ankle ulcers.

---

## HY pearl

Venous stasis ulcers classically occur at the medial malleolus, while arterial ulcers are more often on the toes or lateral malleolus and are associated with weak pulses.

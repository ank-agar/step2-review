# Secondary enuresis

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17752/N00693-H_thumbnail.jpg)

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17770/N00715-H_thumbnail.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/30/Urinary_system.svg)

Secondary enuresis = a child starts wetting again after previously being dry for at least 6 months.

Enuresis means involuntary urination, usually at an age where bladder control is expected.

So:

* primary enuresis = never achieved a long dry period
* secondary enuresis = was dry, then regressed

---

### Core idea

Think:

> “Something new happened.”

The child already had bladder control, so secondary enuresis makes you look for a trigger.

Common triggers:

* UTI (urinary tract infection)
* constipation
* diabetes mellitus
* diabetes insipidus
* psychosocial stress
* sleep problems
* seizure disorder, less commonly

---

### Why the findings happen

* **UTI (urinary tract infection):** bladder irritation causes urgency/frequency, so the child may leak urine
* **Constipation:** stool-filled rectum presses on the bladder, lowering bladder capacity
* **Diabetes mellitus:** hyperglycemia (high blood glucose) causes osmotic diuresis (glucose pulls water into urine), so urine volume increases
* **Diabetes insipidus:** low ADH (antidiuretic hormone) effect causes very dilute, high-volume urine
* **Stress:** can disrupt sleep/arousal and learned bladder control

---

### What to screen for

Ask about:

* dysuria (painful urination)
* urinary frequency
* polydipsia (excessive thirst)
* polyuria (excessive urination)
* constipation
* new stressors at home/school
* neurologic symptoms

Basic workup often starts with:

* urinalysis
* urine culture if UTI (urinary tract infection) symptoms are present
* glucose testing if diabetes symptoms are present

---

## One-line intuition

Secondary enuresis means the child had bladder control, lost it, and now you should look for a new medical or psychosocial trigger.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Secondary enuresis + polyuria (excessive urination) + polydipsia (excessive thirst) = think diabetes mellitus until proven otherwise.

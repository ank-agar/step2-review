# Vitamin E deficiency

![Image](https://pub.mdpi-res.com/molecules/molecules-26-05385/article_deploy/html/images/molecules-26-05385-g001.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Vitamin-E-from-xtal-3D-bs-17.png/1280px-Vitamin-E-from-xtal-3D-bs-17.png)

Vitamin E deficiency = low vitamin E, which is a fat-soluble antioxidant.

Antioxidant means it protects cells from free radicals (reactive molecules that damage membranes).

Since vitamin E is fat-soluble, deficiency usually happens with fat malabsorption, like:

* cystic fibrosis
* chronic cholestasis (poor bile flow)
* abetalipoproteinemia (inability to make apolipoprotein B-containing lipoproteins)
* pancreatic insufficiency

---

### Core idea

Vitamin E protects lipid membranes.

So when vitamin E is low:

* red blood cell membranes become fragile → hemolytic anemia
* neurons get oxidative membrane damage → neuropathy, ataxia, loss of vibration/position sense
* retina can be damaged → retinopathy

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Hemolytic anemia:** red blood cells get oxidative membrane injury and rupture
* **Ataxia:** damage to cerebellar/spinal sensory pathways causes poor coordination
* **Loss of vibration/position sense:** dorsal columns are affected
* **Hyporeflexia:** peripheral nerves are affected
* **Retinopathy:** retinal cells are vulnerable to oxidative damage

Ataxia means uncoordinated movement.

Retinopathy means disease of the retina, the light-sensing layer in the back of the eye.

---

## One-line intuition

Vitamin E is the lipid membrane bodyguard; without it, red blood cells and nerves get oxidatively damaged.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Vitamin E deficiency can look neuro-wise like vitamin B12 deficiency, but vitamin E deficiency causes **hemolytic anemia**, while vitamin B12 deficiency causes **megaloblastic anemia**.

### Core idea

A contracture is fixed tightening/shortening of tissue that limits movement of a joint or body part.

The tightened tissue can be:

* muscle
* tendon
* ligament
* skin
* joint capsule

So:

> the tissue becomes too tight and stiff to let the joint move normally.

---

### Why movement gets limited

Normally, soft tissue has some elasticity, meaning it can stretch and recoil.

In a contracture, tissue becomes shortened and fibrotic.

Fibrotic means replaced by scar-like collagen.

Scar-like collagen is stiff, so it does not stretch well.

That is why the joint gets stuck in a partially bent or abnormal position.

---

### Common causes

Contractures can happen after:

* burns, because scarred skin tightens
* prolonged immobilization, because tissue adapts to the shortened position
* neurologic disease, because chronic spasticity keeps muscles tight
* ischemia, meaning low blood flow, because damaged muscle can scar down
* Dupuytren contracture, where palmar fascia in the hand thickens and pulls fingers into flexion

Flexion = bending a joint.

---

### Key examples

Burn contracture:

* skin scars and tightens
* nearby joint cannot fully extend

Dupuytren contracture:

* palmar fascia thickens
* ring finger and little finger are often pulled into flexion

Volkmann ischemic contracture:

* forearm flexor muscles lose blood supply
* muscles become fibrotic
* hand/wrist can become claw-like

---

### Contracture vs contraction

Contraction is normal active muscle shortening.

Contracture is pathologic fixed shortening.

So contraction is something a muscle does.

Contracture is something tissue becomes.

---

## One-line intuition

A contracture is when soft tissue scars or shortens so much that a joint gets physically stuck and loses range of motion.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Volkmann ischemic contracture is classically due to forearm compartment syndrome after a supracondylar humerus fracture, causing ischemic injury to the forearm flexor muscles.

Opioid intoxication means too much opioid effect in the body.

Opioids are drugs that activate opioid receptors (pain-control receptors), especially mu receptors (main opioid receptors responsible for analgesia, euphoria, respiratory depression, and miosis).

Classic triad:

* respiratory depression
* CNS depression
* pinpoint pupils

CNS depression means central nervous system depression, so the brain is slowed down:

* sleepy
* confused
* hard to wake up
* coma in severe cases

Respiratory depression means breathing becomes too slow or shallow.

This is the dangerous part.

---

### Why the findings happen

Opioids activate mu receptors in the brainstem.

The brainstem normally keeps automatic breathing going.

So if opioids inhibit those brainstem breathing signals:

* respiratory rate drops
* CO2 (carbon dioxide) rises
* O2 (oxygen) falls
* patient becomes hypoxic

Hypoxic means low oxygen.

That is why opioid overdose kills by stopping breathing, not mainly by directly stopping the heart.

---

### Why pinpoint pupils happen

Opioids cause miosis.

Miosis means constricted pupils.

Think:

> Opioids make the brain sleepy and the pupils tiny.

Pinpoint pupils are especially helpful because many sedative overdoses cause coma, but opioids classically add very small pupils.

---

### Treatment

Give naloxone.

Naloxone is an opioid receptor antagonist, meaning it blocks opioid receptors.

So it knocks opioids off the receptor and reverses:

* respiratory depression
* sedation
* coma

But naloxone can wear off before the opioid does, so symptoms can return.

---

## One-line intuition

Opioid intoxication is basically: mu receptor activation makes the brainstem stop driving breathing, while also causing coma and pinpoint pupils.

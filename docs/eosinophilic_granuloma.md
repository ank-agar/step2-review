# Eosinophilic granuloma

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/49c4/3781243/b2ead4c75aea/13244_2013_271_Fig2_HTML.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/1448.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK559038/bin/histio.jpg)

Eosinophilic granuloma is the localized bone form of Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH = abnormal clonal proliferation of Langerhans cells).

Think:

* Langerhans cells pile up in bone
* eosinophils (allergy/parasite-type white blood cells) come with them
* the bone gets eaten away, creating a lytic lesion (punched-out bone defect)

Common sites:

* skull
* ribs
* vertebrae
* long bones

Classic presentation:

* child or young adult
* bone pain or swelling
* skull X-ray with a punched-out lytic lesion

---

### Why the bone lesion happens

The abnormal Langerhans cells form a granuloma (organized inflammatory cell collection).

That cell collection activates local inflammation and bone breakdown, so the X-ray looks like a clean hole in the bone.

---

### Path buzzwords

Langerhans cells are positive for:

* CD1a (cluster of differentiation 1a)
* S100
* CD207/langerin

Electron microscopy can show Birbeck granules, which look like tennis rackets.

---

## One-line intuition

Eosinophilic granuloma is basically a localized Langerhans cell tumor-like inflammatory pileup that punches a hole in bone.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Langerhans cell histiocytosis is associated with Birbeck granules, which are tennis-racket-shaped on electron microscopy.

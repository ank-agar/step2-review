### Core idea

Gastroenteritis is inflammation of the stomach and intestines.

Break the word apart:

* gastro- = stomach
* enter- = intestine
* -itis = inflammation

So:

> stomach + intestine inflammation.

Usually this causes:

* diarrhea
* vomiting
* nausea
* abdominal cramps
* sometimes fever

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The infection irritates the intestinal lining.

That causes:

* **diarrhea:** inflamed intestines cannot absorb water well, so water stays in stool
* **vomiting:** stomach/intestine irritation activates nausea-vomiting reflexes
* **cramps:** intestines squeeze harder to move irritating contents along
* **dehydration:** fluid is lost through vomiting + diarrhea

Viruses are the most common cause, especially norovirus.

Other causes include:

* bacteria, like Salmonella or Campylobacter
* parasites, like Giardia
* toxins from contaminated food

---

### Important distinction

Most watery gastroenteritis is treated with fluids, not antibiotics.

But bloody diarrhea suggests invasive/inflammatory disease, where organisms damage the bowel lining.

Think:

* Shigella
* Salmonella
* Campylobacter
* enterohemorrhagic E. coli (EHEC, a toxin-producing E. coli that can cause bloody diarrhea)

---

## One-line intuition

Gastroenteritis is an irritated, inflamed gut that dumps water out as diarrhea and may trigger vomiting.

---

## Pearl 🔥

The biggest danger in acute gastroenteritis is dehydration, especially in infants and older adults.

Oral rehydration solution (ORS, a glucose-salt fluid used to replace water and electrolytes) works because glucose and sodium are absorbed together in the small intestine, and water follows them.

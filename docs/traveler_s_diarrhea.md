# Traveler's diarrhea

Traveler's diarrhea is acute diarrhea that happens during or shortly after travel, usually from eating/drinking contaminated food or water.

Most classic Step 1 cause:

* ETEC (enterotoxigenic Escherichia coli)

Key idea:

* watery diarrhea
* abdominal cramps
* usually little/no fever
* usually no blood

Why watery?

ETEC does not need to invade and destroy the bowel wall.

Instead, it makes toxins that tell intestinal cells to secrete salt into the gut lumen.

Where salt goes, water follows.

So:

* toxin increases salt secretion
* water follows into the intestine
* stool becomes watery

The two toxin pathways:

* LT (heat-labile toxin) increases cAMP (cyclic adenosine monophosphate)
* ST (heat-stable toxin) increases cGMP (cyclic guanosine monophosphate)

Both lead to more fluid secretion into the intestine.

Think:

* labile = heat labile = like cholera toxin = cAMP
* stable = heat stable = cGMP

Treatment is mainly:

* fluids
* ORS (oral rehydration solution), especially if dehydrated
* sometimes antibiotics for severe cases

ORS works because sodium-glucose cotransport still works.

Meaning: glucose helps pull Na+ (sodium) into intestinal cells, and water follows with it.

So even while toxins are pushing water into the gut, ORS helps the body pull water back in.

---

## One-line intuition

Traveler's diarrhea is usually toxin-mediated watery diarrhea from contaminated food/water, classically ETEC, where salt gets secreted into the gut and water follows.

---

## HY pearl

ETEC causes traveler's diarrhea by LT increasing cAMP and ST increasing cGMP, while EHEC (enterohemorrhagic Escherichia coli) causes bloody diarrhea and HUS (hemolytic uremic syndrome) via Shiga-like toxin.

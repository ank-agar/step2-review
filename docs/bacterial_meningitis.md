Bacterial meningitis is infection/inflammation of the meninges (protective layers around the brain and spinal cord) caused by bacteria.

Classic presentation:

* fever
* headache
* neck stiffness
* photophobia (light sensitivity)
* altered mental status (confusion/decreased alertness)

---

### Core idea

Bacteria enter the CSF (cerebrospinal fluid), which is the fluid around the brain/spinal cord.

The immune system reacts strongly, especially with PMNs (polymorphonuclear neutrophils).

That inflammation causes:

* swollen meninges
* increased ICP (intracranial pressure)
* irritated pain-sensitive coverings of the brain

So the patient gets headache, stiff neck, fever, and mental status changes.

---

### Why the CSF findings happen

In bacterial meningitis, CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) usually shows:

* high neutrophils because bacteria trigger acute inflammation
* low glucose because bacteria + immune cells consume glucose
* high protein because inflammation makes the barrier leaky
* high opening pressure because inflammation/swelling increases pressure

One-line memory:

> Bacteria eat sugar and recruit neutrophils.

---

### Common causes by age

Neonate (newborn):

* Group B Strep (Streptococcus agalactiae)
* E. coli (Escherichia coli)
* Listeria monocytogenes

Child/young adult:

* Neisseria meningitidis
* Streptococcus pneumoniae

Older adult:

* Streptococcus pneumoniae
* Listeria monocytogenes

---

### Treatment idea

This is an emergency.

Give empiric antibiotics (broad antibiotics before the exact organism is known) quickly.

Often also give dexamethasone (steroid) to reduce inflammatory damage, especially classically with pneumococcal meningitis.

---

## One-line intuition

Bacteria get into the CSF (cerebrospinal fluid), the meninges become inflamed and swollen, and that causes fever + headache + stiff neck + confusion.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Listeria monocytogenes is important in neonates, older adults, pregnant patients, and immunocompromised patients, and it requires ampicillin coverage.

### Core idea

Cerebral infarction = brain tissue death from loss of blood supply.

Break it down:

* cerebral = brain
* infarction = tissue death from ischemia (low blood flow/oxygen)

So this is basically:

> a heart attack of the brain.

Most of the time, this means an ischemic stroke (stroke from blocked blood flow).

---

### What causes it?

Usually a blood vessel feeding the brain gets blocked by:

* thrombus = clot formed in that vessel
* embolus = clot/debris that traveled from somewhere else
* atherosclerosis = cholesterol plaque narrowing/blocking an artery

Once blood flow stops:

* less oxygen reaches neurons
* ATP (cell energy) drops
* ion pumps fail
* cells swell and die

That dead area is the infarct.

---

### Why symptoms are sudden

Neurons need constant oxygen and glucose.

So when an artery gets blocked, the downstream brain region stops working quickly.

Symptoms depend on which brain area is hit:

* motor cortex infarct -> weakness
* speech area infarct -> aphasia (language problem)
* visual pathway infarct -> vision loss
* cerebellar infarct -> ataxia (poor coordination)

---

### Important imaging idea

On CT (computed tomography), an infarct often becomes hypodense, meaning darker than normal brain tissue.

Why?

Dead/ischemic tissue develops edema (swelling from water), and water looks darker on CT.

---

## One-line intuition

A cerebral infarction is when a blocked brain artery starves part of the brain until that tissue dies.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Ischemic stroke causes liquefactive necrosis in the brain.

Translation: dead brain tissue gets digested into liquid-like material because the brain has lots of enzymes/lipids and little structural support.

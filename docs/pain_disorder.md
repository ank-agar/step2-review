### Core idea

Pain disorder is an old psychiatric diagnosis where the main symptom is:

* persistent pain
* causing distress or impairment
* strongly influenced by psychological factors

In modern terms, this is usually folded into **somatic symptom disorder with predominant pain**.

Somatic symptom disorder means a person has real physical symptoms plus excessive thoughts, anxiety, or behaviors about those symptoms.

---

### Key point

The pain is **not fake**.

The issue is that the brain's pain-processing system becomes overly amplified.

So the patient may have:

* severe pain
* repeated doctor visits
* high anxiety about the pain
* disability out of proportion to objective findings

---

### Why the pain happens

Pain is not just tissue damage.

Pain perception depends on:

* nociceptors (pain-sensing nerves)
* spinal cord pathways
* thalamus
* somatosensory cortex
* limbic system (emotion-processing brain system)
* descending modulation from the brain

So anxiety, depression, trauma, and attention to symptoms can increase pain signaling.

Basically:

> the volume knob on pain gets turned up.

---

### Test-style distinction

Pain disorder / somatic symptom disorder:

* symptom is pain or another physical complaint
* patient is genuinely distressed
* not intentionally produced

Malingering:

* symptoms are intentionally faked for external gain, like money, drugs, or avoiding work

Factitious disorder:

* symptoms are intentionally produced to take on the sick role

---

## One-line intuition

Pain disorder is real pain where psychological stress and abnormal pain processing amplify the pain experience.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, pain disorder is **not intentional**; if the patient is consciously faking symptoms for an obvious reward, think **malingering** instead.

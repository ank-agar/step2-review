## Nasopharyngitis

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/1118.jpg)

Nasopharyngitis = inflammation of the nasopharynx.

Break it down:

* naso- = nose
* pharyng- = pharynx (throat)
* -itis = inflammation

So it basically means:

> inflammation of the nose + upper throat area.

In everyday language, this is usually the **common cold**.

---

### Core idea

A virus infects the upper airway mucosa (moist lining), especially around the nose and nasopharynx (upper throat behind the nose).

That causes:

* runny nose
* nasal congestion
* sneezing
* sore throat
* cough from postnasal drip
* mild fever sometimes

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Runny nose:** inflamed mucosa makes extra mucus
* **Congestion:** swollen mucosa narrows the nasal passages
* **Sore throat:** inflammation extends into the pharynx (throat)
* **Cough:** mucus drips down the back of the throat and irritates it

---

### Important Step 1 distinction

Nasopharyngitis is usually **viral**, so antibiotics do not help.

If you see high fever, tonsillar exudates (white pus-like material), tender anterior cervical lymph nodes (front neck lymph nodes), and no cough, think more about **group A Streptococcus pharyngitis** instead.

---

## One-line intuition

Nasopharyngitis is a viral “cold” that inflames the nose + back of the throat, so you get mucus, congestion, sneezing, and sore throat.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

The most common cause of the common cold/nasopharyngitis is **rhinovirus**, a positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus.

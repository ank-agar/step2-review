Hepatitis B can cause glomerular disease because HBV (hepatitis B virus) antigens can trigger immune complex deposition in the glomerulus.

Glomerulus = the kidney's tiny filtering ball of capillaries.

Immune complex = antigen + antibody stuck together.

So the idea is:

* HBV antigens circulate in blood
* antibodies bind them
* antigen-antibody complexes deposit in the glomerular basement membrane
* complement gets activated
* the filtration barrier gets damaged
* protein leaks into urine

Most classic association:

* membranous nephropathy

Membranous nephropathy = thickening of the glomerular basement membrane due to immune complex deposits.

---

### Why it causes proteinuria

The glomerular filtration barrier normally keeps albumin (major blood protein) inside the blood.

In HBV-associated membranous nephropathy:

* immune complexes sit near podocytes
* podocytes are filtration cells with foot processes
* podocyte injury makes the filter leaky
* albumin spills into urine

That gives nephrotic syndrome.

Nephrotic syndrome = heavy proteinuria, low serum albumin, edema, and hyperlipidemia.

---

### What you may see clinically

* edema
* frothy urine from proteinuria
* hypoalbuminemia (low blood albumin)
* hyperlipidemia (high blood lipids)
* positive hepatitis B serologies

Serology = blood testing for infection markers.

---

### Other HBV kidney association

HBV can also be associated with MPGN (membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis).

MPGN = immune-complex kidney disease with mesangial proliferation and glomerular basement membrane splitting.

But for Step 1, the main buzzword link is:

> Hepatitis B -> membranous nephropathy -> nephrotic syndrome

---

## One-line intuition

Hepatitis B can leave immune-complex "gunk" in the kidney filter, making the glomerular membrane thick and leaky to protein.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Membranous nephropathy has subepithelial immune deposits and can show a "spike and dome" appearance on electron microscopy/silver stain.

Subepithelial = between the glomerular basement membrane and podocytes.

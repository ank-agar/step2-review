## Benign positional vertigo

![Image](https://vestibular.org/wp-content/webp-express/webp-images/doc-root/wp-content/uploads/2012/03/inner-ear-anatomy_graphic-only.jpg.webp)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Epley.svg/960px-Epley.svg.png)

Benign positional vertigo usually means **BPPV (benign paroxysmal positional vertigo)**.

Break it down:

* **Benign** = not cancer / not life-threatening
* **Paroxysmal** = sudden brief attacks
* **Positional** = triggered by head position changes
* **Vertigo** = false spinning sensation

### Core idea

BPPV happens when **otoconia (tiny calcium carbonate crystals)** break loose from the **utricle (inner ear gravity-sensing chamber)** and fall into a **semicircular canal (inner ear rotation-sensing tube)**.

Most commonly, they fall into the **posterior semicircular canal**.

### Why dizziness happens

Normally:

* semicircular canals sense head rotation using fluid movement
* otoconia stay in the utricle and help sense gravity/linear movement

In BPPV:

* loose crystals move when the head changes position
* this drags fluid in the canal
* the brain gets a false signal saying "you are spinning"

So the patient feels intense vertigo even though they are not actually rotating.

### Classic presentation

Brief episodes of spinning triggered by:

* rolling over in bed
* looking up
* bending down
* getting out of bed

Usually lasts **seconds to less than 1 minute**.

### Key exam finding

The **Dix-Hallpike maneuver (positional test for BPPV)** can trigger vertigo and **nystagmus (rhythmic involuntary eye movement)**.

Why nystagmus happens:

* inner ear falsely says the head is moving
* eyes reflexively move as if the head were rotating

### Treatment

The **Epley maneuver (canalith repositioning maneuver)** uses gravity to move the loose crystals out of the semicircular canal and back into the utricle.

### One-line intuition

BPPV is basically **inner-ear crystals in the wrong tube**, so head movement makes the brain think the room is spinning.

### HY Step 1 pearl

BPPV causes **episodic positional vertigo without hearing loss**. Hearing loss or tinnitus (ringing in the ears) points more toward inner ear diseases like **Meniere disease**, not classic BPPV.

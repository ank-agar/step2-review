## Ophthalmia neonatorum

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1e/Schematic_diagram_of_the_human_eye_en.svg)

![Image](https://eyewiki-images.s3.us-east-va.perf.cloud.ovh.us/d/d9/Ophthalmia_neonatorum.jpg)

Ophthalmia neonatorum = neonatal conjunctivitis (inflammation of the conjunctiva, the thin membrane covering the white of the eye and inside of the eyelids) occurring in the first 28 days of life.

Basically:

* newborn eye redness
* eyelid swelling
* watery or purulent discharge

Most Step 1-relevant causes:

* chemical irritation from eye prophylaxis: within 6-24 hours
* Neisseria gonorrhoeae: 2-5 days
* Chlamydia trachomatis: 5-14 days

---

### Why it happens

During vaginal delivery, the baby’s eyes can contact infected cervical/vaginal secretions.

The conjunctiva is exposed and delicate, so organisms can stick there and trigger inflammation:

* vessels dilate -> red eye
* immune cells enter -> swelling
* neutrophils/pus build up -> purulent discharge

Gonorrhea is especially dangerous because it can invade fast and damage the cornea (clear front surface of the eye), causing ulceration and blindness.

---

### Key distinction

Gonococcal ophthalmia:

* very purulent
* early onset, usually 2-5 days
* can rapidly destroy cornea
* treat with ceftriaxone or cefotaxime

Chlamydial ophthalmia:

* later onset, usually 5-14 days
* can be associated with neonatal pneumonia
* treat with oral erythromycin or azithromycin

Topical therapy alone is not enough for chlamydia because the organism can also be in the nasopharynx (nose/throat area) and lungs.

---

## One-line intuition

Ophthalmia neonatorum is a newborn’s conjunctiva getting inflamed after exposure to birth canal organisms, classically gonorrhea early and chlamydia later.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Erythromycin eye ointment at birth prevents gonococcal ophthalmia, but it does **not** reliably prevent chlamydial conjunctivitis.

### Core idea

Penile cancer is a malignant tumor of the penis, most commonly:

* **squamous cell carcinoma** = cancer from squamous epithelial cells, which are flat surface-lining cells

It usually starts on the:

* glans = head of the penis
* foreskin
* shaft skin

---

### Why it happens

The big Step 1 association is:

* **HPV** = human papillomavirus

Especially high-risk HPV types like:

* HPV 16
* HPV 18

HPV makes viral proteins:

* **E6** inhibits p53, a tumor suppressor that normally promotes DNA repair/apoptosis (programmed cell death)
* **E7** inhibits Rb (retinoblastoma protein), a tumor suppressor that normally blocks excess cell cycle progression

So the intuition is:

> HPV removes the cell's brakes, so squamous cells can proliferate into cancer.

---

### Risk factors

Common risk factors:

* HPV infection
* smoking
* phimosis = tight foreskin that cannot retract
* poor hygiene/chronic inflammation
* uncircumcised status, especially when associated with chronic irritation or smegma buildup
* older age

Why chronic inflammation matters:

* repeated irritation causes repeated epithelial repair
* more cell turnover means more chances for DNA mutations
* over time, this can progress to dysplasia (abnormal growth) and then carcinoma (invasive cancer)

---

### Presentation

Think:

* painless penile ulcer
* mass or growth on glans/foreskin
* bleeding or discharge
* nonhealing lesion
* enlarged inguinal lymph nodes

**Inguinal lymph nodes** = groin lymph nodes.

Why groin nodes?

The penis drains lymph mainly to superficial/deep inguinal lymph nodes, so penile cancer often spreads there first.

---

## One-line intuition

Penile cancer is usually HPV-associated squamous cell carcinoma of the glans or foreskin that spreads first to inguinal lymph nodes.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 pearl: HPV-related cancers often involve **E6 degrading p53** and **E7 inhibiting Rb**, removing two major tumor suppressor "brakes" on the cell cycle.

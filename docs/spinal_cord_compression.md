# Spinal cord compression

![Image](https://media.springernature.com/full/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-021-89848-3/MediaObjects/41598_2021_89848_Fig2_HTML.jpg)

### Core idea

Spinal cord compression = something is physically squeezing the spinal cord.

Common causes:

* herniated disc
* tumor/metastasis
* epidural abscess (infection in the epidural space, the space outside the dura mater)
* vertebral fracture
* spinal stenosis (narrowing of the spinal canal)

The key danger is that the cord carries motor, sensory, and autonomic signals, so compression can cause deficits below the lesion.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Weakness below the lesion:** corticospinal tract (motor pathway from brain to spinal cord) gets compressed
* **Sensory loss below the lesion:** ascending sensory tracts get compressed
* **Hyperreflexia/spasticity:** upper motor neuron (brain/spinal cord motor pathway) damage removes inhibitory control
* **Bowel/bladder dysfunction:** autonomic pathways to pelvic organs get disrupted
* **Back pain:** mass effect irritates vertebrae, meninges (protective coverings of the central nervous system), or nerve roots

---

### Big clinical clue

Think spinal cord compression when you see:

* back pain
* weakness
* sensory level
* urinary retention/incontinence
* hyperreflexia

Especially if the patient has cancer, fever, IV drug use, trauma, or new neurologic deficits.

---

## One-line intuition

The spinal cord is like a major cable inside a bony tunnel; if a disc, tumor, abscess, or fracture squeezes it, signals below that level start failing.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Spinal cord compression is an emergency because prolonged pressure can cause irreversible myelopathy (spinal cord dysfunction).

MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) is the best test to evaluate suspected cord compression.

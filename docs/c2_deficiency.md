### Core idea

C2 deficiency means low or absent complement component 2, a protein used in the classical complement pathway and lectin complement pathway.

Complement is a blood protein system that helps:

* opsonize bacteria, meaning coat them so phagocytes can eat them
* lyse microbes, meaning punch holes in them
* clear immune complexes, meaning remove antigen-antibody clumps

---

### What pathway is broken?

C2 helps form C3 convertase in the classical pathway:

* C4b + C2a = C3 convertase
* C3 convertase cleaves C3 into C3a and C3b

So if C2 is missing:

* less C3b opsonization
* worse clearance of encapsulated bacteria
* worse immune complex cleanup

Encapsulated bacteria are bacteria with a slippery capsule that makes them harder for immune cells to grab.

---

### Classic associations

C2 deficiency causes increased risk of:

* recurrent sinopulmonary infections, meaning repeated sinus and lung infections
* pneumonia
* meningitis, meaning infection of the meninges, the coverings around the brain and spinal cord
* sepsis, meaning bloodstream infection with dangerous whole-body inflammation

Common organisms:

* Streptococcus pneumoniae
* Haemophilus influenzae
* Neisseria meningitidis

These are encapsulated organisms, so they depend heavily on opsonization.

---

### Autoimmune association

C2 deficiency is also associated with SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus).

Why?

Normally, early classical complement proteins help clear immune complexes.

If immune complexes are not cleared well, they can deposit in tissues and trigger inflammation, which looks lupus-like.

So the intuition is:

> less complement cleanup = more immune complex disease.

---

### Lab intuition

CH50 (total classical complement activity) is low because the classical pathway is broken.

AH50 (alternative complement activity) is usually normal because the alternative pathway does not require C2.

---

## One-line intuition

C2 deficiency = broken early classical complement pathway, causing encapsulated bacterial infections plus lupus-like autoimmunity from poor immune complex clearance.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Early classical complement deficiencies, especially C1q, C2, and C4, are associated with SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus), while terminal complement deficiencies C5-C9 are classically associated with recurrent Neisseria infections.

# Autoimmune hepatitis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a6/Schematic_diagram_of_the_liver_structure.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bd/Autoimmune_hepatitis_-_high_mag.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/de/Jaundice_eye.jpg)

Autoimmune hepatitis is when the immune system attacks hepatocytes (liver cells), causing chronic hepatitis (liver inflammation).

Core idea:

* autoimmune = immune system attacks self
* hepatitis = inflammation of the liver

So autoimmune hepatitis = your immune system is treating liver cells like foreign cells.

---

### Why the liver gets damaged

Normally, T cells (immune cells that kill infected or abnormal cells) should ignore your own hepatocytes.

In autoimmune hepatitis, immune tolerance (the immune system's ability to not attack self) breaks down.

Then immune cells attack the liver, causing:

* hepatocyte injury
* inflammation
* elevated AST/ALT (aspartate aminotransferase/alanine aminotransferase; liver injury enzymes)
* possible fibrosis (scar tissue)
* possible cirrhosis (severe liver scarring with distorted architecture)

---

### Classic clues

Think:

* young or middle-aged female
* fatigue
* jaundice (yellow skin/eyes from bilirubin buildup)
* RUQ pain (right upper quadrant pain)
* high AST/ALT
* high IgG (immunoglobulin G; antibody type)
* positive ANA (antinuclear antibody) or anti-smooth muscle antibody

ANA (antinuclear antibody) means antibodies against nuclear material.

Anti-smooth muscle antibody means antibodies against smooth muscle-associated proteins.

---

### Biopsy finding

Classic biopsy:

* interface hepatitis
* plasma cells

Interface hepatitis means inflammation at the border between the portal tract (area with portal vein, hepatic artery, and bile duct branches) and liver lobule (functional liver tissue unit).

Plasma cells are antibody-producing B cells, which makes sense because this is an immune/autoantibody-driven disease.

---

### Treatment

Treat by calming the immune system:

* corticosteroids, like prednisone
* azathioprine (immunosuppressant drug that decreases lymphocyte proliferation)

Why this works:

The problem is immune attack, so suppressing immune activity reduces hepatocyte damage.

---

## One-line intuition

Autoimmune hepatitis is basically "self-reactive immune cells attacking hepatocytes," causing liver inflammation that can progress to cirrhosis if untreated.

---

## Pearl

Autoimmune hepatitis is associated with other autoimmune diseases, especially thyroid disease, type 1 diabetes mellitus (autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells), and celiac disease (autoimmune reaction to gluten causing small bowel villous atrophy).

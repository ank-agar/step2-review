# Pelvic cellulitis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/da/Purulent_anterior_parametritis_Wellcome_L0062139.jpg)

Pelvic cellulitis = infection/inflammation of the pelvic connective tissue.

Most commonly, this means **parametritis**:

* para- = around
* metrium = uterus

So parametritis means:

> infection of the tissue around the uterus.

This tissue is near the cervix, uterus, broad ligament, and pelvic sidewall.

---

### Core idea

Think:

> bacteria spread from the uterus/cervix into the surrounding pelvic soft tissue.

It is not “cellulitis” like leg skin cellulitis.

Here, **cellulitis** means infection of deeper connective tissue in the pelvis.

---

### Common causes

Pelvic cellulitis can happen after:

* childbirth, especially complicated delivery
* C-section (cesarean section)
* hysterectomy (surgical removal of the uterus)
* pelvic surgery
* PID (pelvic inflammatory disease = infection of the upper female reproductive tract)
* spread from endometritis (infection of the uterine lining)

Usually it is **polymicrobial**, meaning multiple bacteria are involved.

Common groups:

* anaerobes (bacteria that grow without oxygen)
* gram-negative rods
* streptococci

---

### Symptoms

Typical findings:

* fever
* lower abdominal/pelvic pain
* pelvic tenderness
* uterine or cervical motion tenderness
* leukocytosis (high white blood cell count)

If an abscess forms:

* persistent fever despite antibiotics
* focal pelvic mass or collection

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Fever:** immune system reacting to bacterial infection
* **Pelvic pain:** infected soft tissue becomes swollen and inflamed
* **Tender cervix/uterus:** moving infected pelvic tissues hurts
* **Abscess:** pus can wall off into a localized collection

---

## One-line intuition

Pelvic cellulitis is basically infection spreading into the soft tissue around the uterus, usually after childbirth, surgery, or PID.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

Postpartum fever + uterine/pelvic tenderness after C-section should make you think of postpartum endometritis, which can spread into pelvic cellulitis/parametritis if the infection extends beyond the uterine lining.

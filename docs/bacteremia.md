### Core idea

Bacteremia means **bacteria in the blood**.

Blood is normally sterile, so bacteria floating around in it is abnormal.

It can be:

* **transient** = brief, cleared quickly
* **persistent** = keeps happening because bacteria keep entering blood
* **clinically dangerous** if it triggers sepsis

Sepsis means infection causing a systemic inflammatory response with organ dysfunction.

So:

> bacteremia = bacteria are in the blood  
> sepsis = the body is getting very sick from infection

---

### Why it happens

Bacteria can enter the bloodstream from:

* pneumonia
* urinary tract infection
* skin infection/cellulitis
* IV catheter infection
* endocarditis
* dental procedures or brushing teeth, usually transient

The key idea:

> a local infection spills bacteria into the blood.

---

### Why bacteremia matters

Once bacteria are in blood, they can travel anywhere.

That can cause:

* fever/chills
* hypotension from sepsis
* metastatic infection, meaning infection seeded somewhere else
* endocarditis, meaning infection of heart valves
* osteomyelitis, meaning bone infection

Reasoning:

Blood is the highway, so bacteria in blood can spread to distant organs.

---

### Diagnosis

Diagnosed with **blood cultures**, meaning blood is placed into culture bottles to see if bacteria grow.

Important Step 1 idea:

* true bacteremia often grows the same organism in multiple blood culture bottles
* contamination often grows skin flora in only one bottle

Skin flora means normal bacteria living on the skin.

---

## One-line intuition

Bacteremia is bacteria getting into the bloodstream; the danger is that blood can spread bacteria everywhere and trigger sepsis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

**Staphylococcus aureus bacteremia is never ignored** because it can seed heart valves and cause infective endocarditis.

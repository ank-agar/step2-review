# Serum sickness

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/Henoch-Sch%C3%B6nlein_nephritis_IgA_immunostaining.jpg/500px-Henoch-Sch%C3%B6nlein_nephritis_IgA_immunostaining.jpg)

Serum sickness is a **type III hypersensitivity reaction**.

Type III hypersensitivity means:

* antigen-antibody complexes form in the blood
* they deposit in tissues
* they activate complement
* complement brings in neutrophils
* neutrophils cause inflammation and tissue damage

Antigen means the thing the immune system reacts to.

Antibody means the immune protein that binds antigen.

Complement means a set of immune proteins that amplify inflammation.

---

### Core idea

The body sees a foreign protein/drug as antigen, makes antibodies against it, and the antigen-antibody complexes get stuck in places like:

* skin
* joints
* kidneys
* small blood vessels

That is why the classic triad is:

* fever
* rash
* arthralgia, meaning joint pain

Sometimes there is lymphadenopathy, meaning enlarged lymph nodes, and proteinuria, meaning protein in the urine.

---

### Why it is delayed

Serum sickness usually appears about **1-2 weeks after exposure**.

Why?

Because the immune system needs time to:

* recognize the antigen
* make antibodies
* form immune complexes
* deposit those complexes into tissues

So it is not immediate like type I hypersensitivity, which is IgE-mediated allergy.

IgE means immunoglobulin E.

---

### Common triggers

Classically:

* antiserum
* antivenom
* antitoxins

Modern triggers can include:

* monoclonal antibodies
* antibiotics, especially in serum sickness-like reactions

Monoclonal antibodies are lab-made antibodies used as drugs.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Rash:** immune complexes deposit in small skin vessels, causing vasculitis
* **Joint pain:** immune complexes deposit near synovium, meaning the joint lining
* **Kidney findings:** immune complexes can deposit in glomeruli, meaning kidney filtering units
* **Low complement:** complement gets consumed while reacting to immune complexes

Vasculitis means inflammation of blood vessels.

---

## One-line intuition

Serum sickness is when circulating antigen-antibody clumps get stuck in tissues and trigger complement-driven inflammation.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember:

**Type III hypersensitivity = immune complex deposition.**

Classic examples:

* serum sickness
* Arthus reaction
* SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus)
* post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis

SLE means systemic lupus erythematosus.

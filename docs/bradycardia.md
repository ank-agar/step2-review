### Core idea

Bradycardia = slow heart rate.

Usually this means:

* heart rate less than 60 beats/minute in adults

But clinically, the key question is:

* is the patient symptomatic or unstable?

because some healthy people, especially athletes, normally have low resting heart rates.

---

### Why it happens

The heart rate is mainly set by the SA node (sinoatrial node), the heart's natural pacemaker.

Bradycardia can happen if:

* the SA node fires too slowly
* conduction through the AV node (atrioventricular node) is blocked
* vagal tone is increased
* drugs slow the heart

Common causes:

* beta blockers
* calcium channel blockers
* digoxin
* hypothyroidism
* increased intracranial pressure
* inferior myocardial infarction

Myocardial infarction = heart attack.

---

### Symptoms

If the heart beats too slowly, cardiac output drops.

Cardiac output = heart rate x stroke volume.

So low heart rate can cause:

* dizziness
* syncope (fainting)
* fatigue
* hypotension (low blood pressure)
* confusion

Reasoning:

slow heart rate -> less blood pumped per minute -> less brain perfusion -> dizziness/syncope.

---

### ECG idea

In sinus bradycardia:

* every QRS complex has a normal P wave before it
* rhythm is regular
* rate is slow

So the rhythm is normal in shape, just too slow.

QRS complex = ventricular depolarization, meaning the ventricles are electrically activated.

---

## One-line intuition

Bradycardia is the heart's pacemaker/conduction system running too slowly, so blood delivery can fall if the rate is too low.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Symptomatic bradycardia is treated first with atropine, which blocks parasympathetic/vagal input to the SA node and AV node, increasing heart rate.

## Pulmonary hypertension

Pulmonary hypertension means high blood pressure in the pulmonary arteries (arteries carrying blood from the right ventricle to the lungs).

Core idea:

* the lung blood vessels become narrowed, stiff, blocked, or damaged
* blood has a harder time moving through the lungs
* the right ventricle must pump harder
* chronic strain can cause right ventricular hypertrophy and right-sided heart failure

---

### Why symptoms happen

* **Dyspnea (shortness of breath):** less blood gets through lungs efficiently, so oxygen delivery drops with exertion
* **Fatigue:** low effective cardiac output (amount of blood pumped per minute)
* **Syncope (fainting):** the right heart cannot increase output enough during exertion
* **Chest pain:** strained right ventricle has increased oxygen demand
* **Peripheral edema:** right-sided heart failure backs blood up into systemic veins

---

### Causes to think about

Big buckets:

* left heart disease
* chronic lung disease or hypoxia (low oxygen)
* chronic thromboembolic disease (old clots blocking pulmonary arteries)
* idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension (unknown cause pulmonary artery disease)

Idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension is classically associated with:

* BMPR2 mutation (bone morphogenetic protein receptor type 2 mutation)
* young women
* plexiform lesions on pathology

---

## One-line intuition

Pulmonary hypertension is like putting a tight kink in the pipes between the right heart and lungs, so the right ventricle has to push against high resistance.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On Step 1, pulmonary hypertension causes right ventricular hypertrophy because the right ventricle is pumping against increased afterload (pressure resistance).

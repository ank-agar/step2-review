### Core idea

Lofgren syndrome is an acute form of sarcoidosis.

Sarcoidosis = noncaseating granulomas (organized immune cell collections that do **not** have cheesy necrosis) in different organs.

Classic Lofgren syndrome:

* erythema nodosum (tender red nodules, usually on the shins)
* bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy (enlarged lymph nodes at both lung hila, where vessels/bronchi enter the lungs)
* ankle arthritis/periarthritis (joint or tissue-around-joint inflammation)
* often fever

---

### Why the findings happen

Sarcoidosis is basically an overactive T-cell immune reaction.

That immune reaction forms granulomas in the chest lymph nodes, especially hilar lymph nodes.

So on chest x-ray you get:

* bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy

The same inflammatory immune activity can also trigger:

* erythema nodosum = inflammation of subcutaneous fat (fat under the skin)
* ankle swelling/pain = inflammation around joints
* fever = systemic inflammation

---

### Big clinical clue

If a young adult has:

* painful red shin bumps
* swollen/painful ankles
* bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy on chest x-ray

think:

> acute sarcoidosis = Lofgren syndrome.

---

## One-line intuition

Lofgren syndrome is the "acute sarcoid flare" that shows up as shin nodules + ankle pain + big hilar lymph nodes.

---

## Pearl

Lofgren syndrome has a good prognosis and often resolves spontaneously, unlike chronic sarcoidosis which can cause progressive lung fibrosis.

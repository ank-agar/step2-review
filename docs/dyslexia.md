![Image](https://education.purdue.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/guiding-principals.jpg)

Dyslexia is a learning disorder where a person has trouble with:

* accurate reading
* fluent reading
* spelling
* decoding words

even though intelligence is normal.

---

### Core idea

Dyslexia is mainly a problem with **phonological processing** (processing speech sounds).

That means the brain has trouble connecting:

* letters on the page
* to sounds
* to words

So the issue is usually not that the eyes “see letters wrong.”

The bigger issue is:

> the brain has trouble mapping written symbols onto speech sounds.

---

### Why reading becomes hard

To read a new word, the brain has to break it into sounds.

Example:

* cat = /k/ + /a/ + /t/

In dyslexia, that sound-mapping step is inefficient.

So the person may:

* read slowly
* guess words from context
* struggle with nonsense words
* spell poorly
* mix up similar-looking letters sometimes

The letter reversals can happen, but that is not the whole disease.

---

### Brain intuition

Reading uses a network involving:

* frontal areas = effortful sound decoding
* parietal areas = connecting letters, sounds, and meaning
* occipito-temporal areas = fast visual word recognition

In dyslexia, the back-of-brain reading network is often less efficient, so the person has to rely more on slow, effortful decoding.

---

## One-line intuition

Dyslexia is when the brain struggles to turn letters into speech sounds quickly and accurately.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

Dyslexia is a **specific learning disorder** with impaired reading despite normal intelligence; think **phonological processing deficit**, not primary vision problem.

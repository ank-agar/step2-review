### Core idea

An epidermal cyst, also called an epidermoid cyst or epidermal inclusion cyst, is a benign skin cyst filled with keratin (the structural protein found in skin, hair, and nails).

Think:

> skin cells get trapped under the skin, keep making keratin, and form a little sac.

---

### Why it forms

Normally, epidermal cells (surface skin cells) shed outward.

In an epidermal cyst, those cells end up deeper in the dermis (skin layer under the epidermis), often from:

* blocked hair follicle
* acne-related follicle injury
* trauma pushing epidermal cells downward

Since the trapped cells still act like epidermis, they keep producing keratin inside a closed sac.

That is why the contents can look thick, white-yellow, and "cheesy."

---

### Classic appearance

Usually:

* slow-growing, round, mobile bump under the skin
* often on face, neck, upper back, or trunk
* may have a central punctum (tiny dark opening)
* usually painless unless inflamed, infected, or ruptured

---

### Important correction

People often call this a sebaceous cyst, but that is technically wrong.

Sebaceous = related to sebum (oily material from sebaceous glands).

Epidermal cysts are filled mostly with keratin, not sebum.

---

### Why rupture causes inflammation

Keratin is fine when trapped inside the cyst wall.

But if the cyst ruptures, keratin spills into the dermis, and the immune system treats it like foreign material.

So you get:

* redness
* swelling
* tenderness
* possible abscess (pus-filled infection pocket)

---

## One-line intuition

An epidermal cyst is basically a tiny buried pocket of epidermis that keeps shedding keratin inward instead of outward.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Histology shows a cyst lined by stratified squamous epithelium (layered flat epithelial cells) with a granular layer and laminated keratin inside.

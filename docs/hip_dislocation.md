## Hip dislocation

![Image](https://upload.orthobullets.com/topic/1035/images/dislocate1.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.orthobullets.com/topic/1035/images/r%20hip%20fracture%20dislocation.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.orthobullets.com/topic/1035/images/CT%20-%20Posterior%20hip%20dislocation_moved.jpg)

Hip dislocation = the femoral head (ball of the thigh bone) pops out of the acetabulum (hip socket).

Most traumatic hip dislocations are:

* posterior
* caused by dashboard injury
* associated with high-energy trauma

Dashboard injury means:

> knee hits the dashboard → force travels up the femur → femoral head is driven backward out of the socket.

---

### Classic positioning

Posterior hip dislocation presents with the leg:

* flexed
* adducted
* internally rotated

Think:

> posterior = leg points inward.

Anterior hip dislocation is usually:

* abducted
* externally rotated

---

### Why it matters

The big complications are:

* sciatic nerve injury
* acetabular fracture
* femoral head fracture
* AVN (avascular necrosis, bone death from loss of blood supply)

Why AVN happens:

The femoral head gets much of its blood from branches of the MCFA (medial circumflex femoral artery). A dislocation can stretch or tear those vessels, so the femoral head loses blood flow.

That is why hip dislocation is reduced urgently.

---

### Imaging

X-ray shows the femoral head no longer sitting normally in the acetabulum.

CT (computed tomography) is often done after reduction to look for:

* loose bone/cartilage fragments
* acetabular fracture
* femoral head fracture

---

## One-line intuition

Hip dislocation is a high-force injury where the femoral head gets knocked out of the acetabulum, usually backward after a dashboard-type blow.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Posterior hip dislocation is classically associated with injury to the sciatic nerve, especially the common fibular/peroneal division, causing weak dorsiflexion (lifting the foot upward).

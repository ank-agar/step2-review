### Core idea

Meckel diverticulum is a congenital outpouching of the ileum (last part of the small intestine).

It happens because the vitelline duct (embryologic connection between the midgut and yolk sac) fails to fully disappear.

So basically:

> a leftover embryologic tube becomes a small pouch on the small bowel.

---

### Why it matters

Meckel diverticulum can contain ectopic tissue (normal tissue in the wrong place), especially:

* gastric mucosa (stomach lining)
* pancreatic tissue

The gastric mucosa can secrete acid.

That acid can ulcerate nearby ileal mucosa, causing:

* painless rectal bleeding
* melena (black tarry stool from digested blood)
* iron deficiency anemia

---

### Classic Step 1 rule of 2s

Meckel diverticulum is classically:

* present in 2% of people
* 2 inches long
* within 2 feet of the ileocecal valve (junction between ileum and cecum)
* 2 times more common in males
* often presents before age 2
* may contain 2 ectopic tissues: gastric and pancreatic

---

### Why the scan works

A Meckel scan uses technetium-99m pertechnetate (radioactive tracer used in nuclear imaging).

It lights up ectopic gastric mucosa because gastric mucosa takes up the tracer.

So if a child has painless lower GI bleeding (gastrointestinal bleeding), think:

> ectopic stomach tissue in a Meckel diverticulum is making acid and causing bleeding.

---

## One-line intuition

Meckel diverticulum is a leftover fetal gut connection that can act like a tiny acid-secreting stomach pouch in the ileum.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Meckel diverticulum is a **true diverticulum**, meaning it contains all layers of the bowel wall.

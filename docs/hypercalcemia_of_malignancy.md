### Core idea

Hypercalcemia of malignancy = high blood calcium caused by cancer.

Most commonly, tumor cells secrete **PTHrP (parathyroid hormone-related peptide)**.

PTHrP acts like **PTH (parathyroid hormone)**, so it tells the body:

* break down bone
* keep calcium in the blood
* dump phosphate in the urine

So serum calcium goes up.

---

### Why calcium goes up

Main mechanisms:

* **PTHrP secretion:** tumor makes a PTH-like hormone
* **Osteolytic metastases:** cancer in bone activates osteoclasts, which are bone-resorbing cells
* **Calcitriol production:** some lymphomas make too much active vitamin D, increasing gut calcium absorption

Most Step 1 classic mechanism:

> squamous cell carcinoma of the lung secretes PTHrP.

---

### Lab pattern

Usually:

* high calcium
* low PTH
* high PTHrP
* low phosphate

Why low PTH?

Because the blood calcium is already high, so the parathyroid glands get suppressed.

Important intuition:

* primary hyperparathyroidism = high calcium + high PTH
* hypercalcemia of malignancy = high calcium + low PTH

---

### Why the symptoms happen

High calcium makes nerves and muscles less excitable.

So patients can get:

* constipation
* abdominal pain
* kidney stones
* polyuria, meaning peeing a lot
* dehydration
* confusion
* weakness
* shortened QT interval on ECG (electrocardiogram)

One-line memory:

> bones, stones, groans, psychiatric overtones.

---

### Important cancer associations

PTHrP-mediated hypercalcemia is classically seen with:

* squamous cell carcinoma of lung
* renal cell carcinoma
* breast cancer
* ovarian cancer
* adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma

Multiple myeloma can also cause hypercalcemia, but more by bone destruction.

---

## One-line intuition

The cancer either releases a fake-PTH signal or destroys bone directly, so calcium leaves bone and stays in the blood.

---

## Pearl 🔥

If a cancer patient has **high calcium + low PTH**, think **PTHrP (parathyroid hormone-related peptide)** until proven otherwise, especially with squamous cell carcinoma of the lung.

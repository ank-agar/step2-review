### Core idea

Pyloric stenosis is narrowing of the pylorus (the muscular outlet from the stomach to the duodenum, which is the first part of the small intestine).

Usually on Step 1, this means infantile hypertrophic pyloric stenosis:

* hypertrophic = enlarged/thickened
* pyloric = pylorus
* stenosis = narrowing

So the pyloric muscle gets too thick and blocks food from leaving the stomach.

---

### Classic presentation

Infant around 2-8 weeks old with:

* projectile nonbilious vomiting
* hungry after vomiting
* dehydration
* weight loss or poor weight gain
* palpable olive-like mass in the RUQ (right upper quadrant)

Nonbilious means no bile in the vomit.

Why nonbilious?

Because the blockage is before the duodenum gets bile from the biliary tree. So milk cannot leave the stomach, but bile also cannot reflux back up into the stomach.

---

### Why the electrolyte findings happen

Vomiting loses stomach acid, which is mostly:

* HCl (hydrochloric acid)
* chloride
* hydrogen ions

So pyloric stenosis causes:

* hypochloremia = low chloride
* hypokalemia = low potassium
* metabolic alkalosis = blood is too basic because acid is lost

Why hypokalemia?

Vomiting causes volume depletion, so RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system) turns on. Aldosterone makes the kidney retain sodium/water but waste potassium and hydrogen ions.

So the baby loses even more potassium and becomes more alkalotic.

---

### Diagnosis

Best initial test is ultrasound.

Classic ultrasound findings:

* thickened pyloric muscle
* elongated pyloric channel
* target/donut sign on transverse view

The ultrasound images above show the thick pylorus in long view and the donut/target appearance in transverse view.

---

### Treatment

First stabilize:

* IV fluids (intravenous fluids)
* correct electrolytes

Then definitive treatment:

* pyloromyotomy

Pyloromyotomy means cutting/splitting the overgrown pyloric muscle so the stomach outlet opens again.

---

## One-line intuition

The stomach outlet becomes a thick muscular bottleneck, so milk shoots back out as projectile nonbilious vomiting.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY association: pyloric stenosis is classically seen in firstborn males and can be associated with macrolide exposure, especially erythromycin.

## Pterygium

![Image](https://assets.clevelandclinic.org/transform/ff6e19a5-79e4-4015-bb07-6be38d2dea08/pinguecula-pterygium-1372441755?io=transform%3Afit%2Cwidth%3A780)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK558907/bin/pterygium.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/61/Large_Pterygium.jpg)

Pterygium is a benign, wing-shaped growth of conjunctiva (thin clear membrane over the white part of the eye) that grows onto the cornea (clear front surface of the eye).

Think:

* conjunctiva starts on the white of the eye
* then creeps inward over the cornea
* usually from the nasal side, meaning the side closer to the nose

---

### Core idea

It is basically sun/irritation-induced scar-like tissue growing across the eye surface.

Common risk factors:

* UV (ultraviolet) light exposure
* wind
* dust
* dry environments

That is why it is called surfer's eye.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Red, fleshy triangle:** fibrovascular tissue (fibrous tissue plus blood vessels) is growing over the surface
* **Foreign body sensation/irritation:** the raised tissue disrupts the smooth tear film
* **Blurry vision:** if it grows onto the cornea, it can block light or distort the corneal shape
* **Astigmatism:** uneven corneal curvature causes light to focus unevenly

So the big issue is not cancer.

The issue is:

> tissue growing onto the clear visual window of the eye.

---

### Pinguecula vs pterygium

Pinguecula = yellow bump on conjunctiva only.

Pterygium = grows onto the cornea.

One-line distinction:

> Pinguecula stays on the white part; pterygium invades the clear cornea.

---

## One-line intuition

A pterygium is a sun-damaged, wing-shaped conjunctival growth that crawls onto the cornea and can distort vision.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Pterygium is associated with chronic UV (ultraviolet) exposure and can cause astigmatism by pulling/distorting the cornea.

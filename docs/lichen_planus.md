Lichen planus is an inflammatory autoimmune-like condition where T cells attack the skin or mucosal epithelium.

Main sites:

* skin
* mouth
* nails
* scalp/hair follicles
* genital mucosa

Classic skin description:

* purple
* polygonal
* planar/flat-topped
* pruritic (itchy)
* papules/plaques

This is often remembered as the **6 Ps**.

---

### Core idea

The immune system attacks basal keratinocytes (bottom-layer skin/mucosal cells).

So you get:

* inflammation at the dermal-epidermal junction
* damage to the basal layer
* itchy, flat-topped purple papules
* white lacy lines called Wickham striae

Wickham striae are the high-yield clue.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Purple color:** inflammation plus pigment changes in the skin
* **Flat-topped papules:** epidermis thickens in a broad, plateau-like way
* **Itching:** inflammatory cytokines irritate cutaneous nerves
* **Wickham striae:** focal thickening of the granular layer creates fine white lacy lines
* **Oral lesions:** the same immune attack happens on mucosal epithelium, especially buccal mucosa

Histology shows:

* sawtooth rete ridges
* band-like lymphocytic infiltrate
* basal cell degeneration
* Civatte bodies, which are apoptotic keratinocytes (dying skin/mucosal cells)

---

### Associations

Can be associated with:

* hepatitis C virus, especially in some populations
* drug-induced lichenoid reactions
* oral lesions that can rarely have malignant transformation risk if chronic/erosive

Lichenoid means "lichen planus-like."

---

## One-line intuition

Lichen planus is a T-cell attack on the basal layer of skin/mucosa, causing itchy purple flat-topped papules and lacy white Wickham striae.

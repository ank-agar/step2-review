### Core idea

**Anaplasma** is a tick-borne intracellular bacterium.

For Step 1, the main one is:

* **Anaplasma phagocytophilum**

It causes **human granulocytic anaplasmosis**.

Break that down:

* granulocytic = involving granulocytes, especially neutrophils
* neutrophils = white blood cells that fight bacteria
* anaplasmosis = disease caused by Anaplasma

So the key idea is:

> Anaplasma infects neutrophils and forms little intracellular clusters called morulae.

---

### How it spreads

Spread by **Ixodes ticks**:

* Ixodes scapularis = blacklegged/deer tick
* Ixodes pacificus = western blacklegged tick

These are the same ticks that can also transmit:

* Lyme disease
* Babesia

So coinfection can happen.

---

### What you see clinically

Anaplasmosis usually causes a nonspecific flu-like illness:

* fever
* headache
* malaise
* myalgias (muscle aches)

Rash is uncommon.

This helps separate it from some other tick-borne infections where rash is more classic.

---

### Why the labs happen

Common labs:

* leukopenia = low white blood cells
* thrombocytopenia = low platelets
* elevated transaminases = elevated liver enzymes

Why?

Anaplasma lives inside immune cells, especially neutrophils, so it messes with white blood cell function and count. The systemic infection/inflammation also stresses the liver, giving elevated liver enzymes.

---

### Blood smear clue

On peripheral blood smear, you may see:

* **morulae**

Morulae are little clusters of bacteria inside cells.

Key association:

* Anaplasma = morulae in granulocytes/neutrophils
* Ehrlichia = classically morulae in monocytes

Monocytes are another type of white blood cell.

---

### Treatment

Treat with:

* doxycycline

Doxycycline is the go-to antibiotic for many tick-borne intracellular bacteria.

---

## One-line intuition

Anaplasma is a deer-tick-transmitted intracellular bacterium that hides inside neutrophils and causes fever plus low white cells, low platelets, and elevated liver enzymes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

**Anaplasma phagocytophilum infects granulocytes**, while **Ehrlichia chaffeensis infects monocytes**.

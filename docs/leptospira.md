### Core idea

Leptospira is a thin, spiral-shaped bacterium called a spirochete (corkscrew-shaped bacterium).

It causes leptospirosis, a zoonotic infection (infection transmitted from animals to humans).

Classic source:

* animal urine, especially rats
* contaminated freshwater, mud, or soil
* entry through cuts in skin or mucous membranes (wet surfaces like eyes, nose, mouth)

---

### What it does

Leptospira spreads through the blood, then can injure:

* kidneys
* liver
* blood vessels
* meninges (brain and spinal cord coverings)

This is why symptoms can look very broad:

* fever
* headache
* calf muscle pain
* conjunctival suffusion (red eyes without pus)
* aseptic meningitis (meningitis without typical bacterial pus)
* jaundice (yellow skin/eyes from bilirubin buildup)
* renal failure (kidney failure)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Calf pain:** inflammation and injury in muscle
* **Red eyes:** vascular irritation causes conjunctival suffusion
* **Jaundice:** liver dysfunction causes bilirubin buildup
* **Kidney injury:** Leptospira likes the renal tubules (kidney tubes), causing tubulointerstitial nephritis (inflammation around kidney tubules)
* **Meningitis:** spread into CSF (cerebrospinal fluid, fluid around brain/spinal cord) can irritate meninges

---

### Severe form

Severe leptospirosis is called Weil disease.

Classic triad:

* jaundice
* renal failure
* hemorrhage (bleeding)

Think:

> Weil = worst leptospirosis.

---

### Treatment

Usually:

* doxycycline

Severe disease:

* IV penicillin G or ceftriaxone

IV means intravenous (given into a vein).

---

## One-line intuition

Leptospira is a corkscrew bacterium from animal urine in freshwater that twists into the body and hits liver + kidney + vessels.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Leptospira is classically associated with rat urine exposure after flooding or freshwater exposure, and the Step 1 clue is calf tenderness + conjunctival suffusion.

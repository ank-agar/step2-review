## Porphyria

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/Heme_synthesis.svg/3840px-Heme_synthesis.svg.png?utm_campaign=index&utm_content=thumbnail&utm_source=commons.wikimedia.org)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/2573.jpg)

Porphyria = disorders of heme synthesis.

Heme = the iron-containing molecule used in:

* hemoglobin
* myoglobin
* cytochrome P450 enzymes

The core idea:

> one enzyme in the heme-making pathway is deficient, so toxic intermediates build up before that blocked step.

---

### Why symptoms happen

Different intermediates cause different problems.

If early precursors build up, especially ALA (aminolevulinic acid) and PBG (porphobilinogen):

* abdominal pain
* neuropathy
* psychiatric symptoms
* dark urine

This is the classic acute intermittent porphyria pattern.

Why neuro symptoms?

ALA (aminolevulinic acid) is neurotoxic, so it irritates nerves and the CNS (central nervous system).

---

If later porphyrins build up:

* photosensitivity
* fragile skin
* blisters on sun-exposed areas

Why skin findings?

Porphyrins absorb light, especially sunlight, then generate reactive oxygen species, which damage skin.

So:

> porphyrins + light = oxidative skin injury.

---

### Classic Step 1 associations

Acute intermittent porphyria:

* porphobilinogen deaminase deficiency
* abdominal pain
* neuropsychiatric symptoms
* no photosensitivity

Porphyria cutanea tarda:

* uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase deficiency
* blistering photosensitivity
* associated with hepatitis C, alcohol, and iron overload

---

## One-line intuition

Porphyria is a blocked heme assembly line: backup of early intermediates hurts nerves, backup of later porphyrins hurts sun-exposed skin.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Acute intermittent porphyria attacks can be triggered by CYP450 (cytochrome P450) inducers like barbiturates, alcohol, and some anti-seizure drugs because they increase hepatic heme demand.

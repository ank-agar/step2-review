# Meningioma

Meningioma is a tumor that arises from the meninges.

Meninges are the protective coverings around the brain and spinal cord:

* dura mater
* arachnoid mater
* pia mater

Most meningiomas arise from arachnoid cap cells, which are cells related to the arachnoid layer.

So the key idea is:

> tumor from the brain covering, not from the brain tissue itself.

---

### Core idea

Meningiomas are usually:

* benign
* slow-growing
* extra-axial
* attached to dura

Extra-axial means outside the brain tissue itself.

So on imaging, they often look like a mass sitting on the edge of the brain and pushing inward.

---

### Why symptoms happen

The tumor usually causes symptoms by compression.

It takes up space inside the skull, so it can press on nearby brain tissue or cranial nerves.

That can cause:

* headache
* seizures
* focal neurologic deficits, meaning weakness, numbness, vision changes, or other symptoms depending on tumor location

Why location matters:

* near frontal lobe: personality or thinking changes
* near motor cortex: weakness
* near optic nerve: vision problems
* near spinal cord: weakness or sensory changes

---

### Classic imaging clue

Meningioma often has a dural tail.

Dural tail means contrast enhancement extending along the dura mater next to the tumor.

Intuition:

> because the tumor grows from/against the dura, the dura around it can light up too.

---

### Pathology clue

Classic histology shows:

* whorled cell pattern
* psammoma bodies

Psammoma bodies are round, laminated calcifications.

Think:

> meningioma cells swirl around, then calcium deposits can form in the middle.

---

### Important association

Meningiomas are associated with NF2 (neurofibromatosis type 2).

NF2 is a tumor suppressor gene syndrome classically associated with:

* bilateral vestibular schwannomas
* meningiomas
* ependymomas

---

## One-line intuition

Meningioma is a usually benign, slow-growing tumor from the meninges that compresses the brain from the outside.

---

## Pearl 🔥

USMLE loves:

> meningioma = extra-axial dural-based mass + whorls + psammoma bodies.

And if the stem mentions bilateral vestibular schwannomas plus meningiomas, think NF2.

# Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/N1_N2_NMDA_receptor.svg/1280px-N1_N2_NMDA_receptor.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/14/Extreme_delta_brush_in_anti-NMDAr_encephalitis.png)

### Core idea

Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis is autoimmune brain inflammation.

Break it down:

* **anti-** = against
* **NMDA receptor** = N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor, a glutamate receptor important for memory, learning, and normal brain signaling
* **encephalitis** = inflammation of the brain

So:

> The immune system makes antibodies against NMDA receptors in the brain.

Antibodies are immune proteins that normally attack infections, but here they attack the patient’s own NMDA receptors.

---

### What actually happens

The antibodies bind NMDA receptors on neurons.

This causes the receptors to get pulled inside the neuron and removed from the cell surface.

So the brain does not lose huge chunks of tissue at first.

Instead, it loses normal receptor signaling.

That is why the symptoms can look very psychiatric:

* psychosis
* agitation
* hallucinations
* confusion
* memory problems

Then it can progress to more neurologic/autonomic findings:

* seizures
* abnormal movements
* catatonia, meaning decreased movement/responsiveness
* autonomic instability, meaning unstable heart rate, blood pressure, temperature, or breathing

---

### Why it can be confused with psych disease

NMDA receptors help regulate excitatory brain signaling.

If NMDA receptor function drops, the brain can act like it is in a drug-induced psychotic state.

This is similar to how PCP (phencyclidine) and ketamine can cause hallucinations because they block NMDA receptors.

So the intuition is:

> Antibodies functionally “block/remove” NMDA receptors, so the brain becomes inflamed and electrically/behaviorally unstable.

---

### Classic association

Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis is classically associated with ovarian teratoma.

A teratoma is a germ cell tumor that can contain many tissue types, sometimes even neural tissue.

Why that matters:

* teratoma may contain NMDA-receptor-like neural tissue
* immune system reacts to that tissue
* antibodies cross-react with NMDA receptors in the brain

So the tumor can accidentally teach the immune system to attack the brain.

---

### Diagnosis

Key test:

* CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) anti-NMDA receptor antibodies

Other supportive findings:

* EEG (electroencephalogram) abnormalities, sometimes “extreme delta brush”
* MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) can be normal
* CSF may show inflammation
* screen for tumor, especially ovarian teratoma in young women

---

### Treatment

Main idea:

* remove the trigger if present
* calm down/remove the antibodies

Treatment can include:

* tumor removal if teratoma is found
* steroids
* IVIG (intravenous immunoglobulin)
* plasmapheresis, meaning filtering antibodies out of the blood
* rituximab, a drug that targets B cells, if severe/refractory

---

## One-line intuition

Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis is when antibodies remove/block NMDA receptors in the brain, causing a mix of psychosis, seizures, abnormal movements, and autonomic instability.

---

## HY pearl

Young woman + new psychosis + seizures/abnormal movements/autonomic instability = think anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis, and look for an ovarian teratoma.

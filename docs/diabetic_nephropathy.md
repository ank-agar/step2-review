## Diabetic nephropathy

Diabetic nephropathy = kidney damage from long-standing diabetes mellitus.

It mainly injures the **glomerulus** (the tiny capillary tuft that filters blood).

Classic path findings:

* thickened glomerular basement membrane
* mesangial expansion
* nodular glomerulosclerosis
* Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules

Glomerular basement membrane = the filtration barrier under glomerular capillaries.

Mesangium = supportive tissue between glomerular capillary loops.

Glomerulosclerosis = scarring/hardening of the glomerulus.

---

### Core idea

Too much glucose damages small blood vessels.

In the kidney, that means the glomerular filter gets:

* thicker
* leakier
* scarred

So albumin (a major blood protein) starts leaking into urine.

---

### Why proteinuria happens

High glucose causes:

* nonenzymatic glycation (sugar sticking to proteins without an enzyme)
* oxidative stress
* increased growth factors
* hyaline arteriolosclerosis (pink protein-like thickening of small arteries/arterioles)

These damage the filtration barrier:

* endothelial cells (inner capillary lining)
* glomerular basement membrane
* podocytes (cells with foot processes that wrap glomerular capillaries)

Once podocytes and the basement membrane are damaged, protein slips through into urine.

---

### Why GFR changes

Early diabetes can cause glomerular hyperfiltration.

GFR (glomerular filtration rate) = how much blood the kidneys filter per minute.

Why hyperfiltration happens:

* more glucose gets filtered
* proximal tubule reabsorbs more glucose + sodium
* less sodium reaches the macula densa (salt-sensing cells near the glomerulus)
* kidney thinks flow is too low
* afferent arteriole dilates
* glomerular pressure rises

At first: high pressure = high filtration.

Over time: high pressure damages the glomerulus, causing scarring and falling GFR.

---

### Classic Step 1 association

Diabetic nephropathy is a major cause of:

* nephrotic syndrome
* chronic kidney disease
* end-stage renal disease

Nephrotic syndrome = heavy protein loss in urine, low blood albumin, edema, and hyperlipidemia (high blood lipids).

End-stage renal disease = kidney failure severe enough to need dialysis or transplant.

---

### Histology buzzword

Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules = round mesangial nodules from nodular glomerulosclerosis in diabetic nephropathy.

Intuition:

> diabetes makes the glomerulus leak protein first, then scar down over time.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Diabetes causes **hyaline arteriolosclerosis of both afferent and efferent arterioles**.

That is high-yield because hypertension usually affects mainly the afferent arteriole, but diabetes affects both.

# Renal dysplasia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d5/Multicystic_Dysplastic_kidney_%28cropped%29.png)

![Image](https://www.wikidoc.org/images/0/00/Multicystic_dysplastic_kidney_002.jpg)

Renal dysplasia means the kidney developed abnormally in utero, so the kidney tissue is disorganized and does not form normal filtering units.

Break it down:

* renal = kidney
* dysplasia = abnormal development/growth

So renal dysplasia = abnormally formed kidney.

### Core idea

Normally, the kidney forms when the ureteric bud talks to the metanephric mesenchyme.

* ureteric bud = embryologic structure that forms collecting system
* metanephric mesenchyme = embryologic tissue that forms nephrons
* nephron = functional filtering unit of the kidney

If this interaction is abnormal, the kidney becomes malformed.

Instead of organized nephrons, you can get:

* cysts
* primitive ducts
* abnormal kidney tissue
* sometimes cartilage

That is why the kidney may be poorly functional or nonfunctional.

### Why cysts happen

The developing kidney is supposed to make connected tubules that drain urine.

If development is disorganized, urine flow/drainage is abnormal, so parts of the kidney balloon into cysts.

Think:

> bad kidney blueprint -> bad tubule formation -> cystic, nonfunctional kidney

### Important associations

Renal dysplasia is often linked with abnormal urine flow, especially:

* urinary tract obstruction
* vesicoureteral reflux

Vesicoureteral reflux means urine flows backward from the bladder up toward the ureters/kidneys.

Why this matters:

* obstruction/reflux can disrupt kidney development
* abnormal anatomy can predispose to UTIs (urinary tract infections)
* reduced nephron number can lead to hypertension or CKD (chronic kidney disease)

### Multicystic dysplastic kidney

Multicystic dysplastic kidney is a classic form where the kidney is basically replaced by multiple cysts and does not work well.

If one kidney is affected:

* often compatible with life
* other kidney may compensate

If both kidneys are severely affected:

* low fetal urine output
* oligohydramnios
* Potter sequence

Oligohydramnios means low amniotic fluid. Potter sequence means fetal compression and lung hypoplasia due to low amniotic fluid.

### One-line intuition

Renal dysplasia is a kidney that was built wrong during development, so it has disorganized tissue/cysts instead of normal filtering architecture.

### HY pearl

Bilateral severe renal dysplasia can cause oligohydramnios because fetal urine is a major source of amniotic fluid after the first trimester.

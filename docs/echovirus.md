Echovirus is a type of **enterovirus** (virus that mainly enters through the gastrointestinal tract).

Break the name apart:

* ECHO = enteric cytopathic human orphan
* enteric = gut-related
* cytopathic = damages cells
* orphan = originally not clearly linked to a specific disease

So echovirus is basically:

> a gut-entering RNA (ribonucleic acid) virus that often causes mild illness, but can also cause viral meningitis.

---

### Core idea

Echovirus is a **nonenveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus**.

That means:

* **nonenveloped:** no lipid envelope, so it is tougher in the environment and can survive stomach acid better
* **positive-sense RNA:** its RNA can act like mRNA (messenger RNA), so host ribosomes can translate it quickly
* **enterovirus:** spreads mainly fecal-oral, usually through contaminated hands, water, or surfaces

---

### What it causes

Most infections are mild or asymptomatic (no symptoms), but classic presentations include:

* fever
* rash/exanthem (widespread skin rash)
* upper respiratory symptoms
* diarrhea
* aseptic meningitis

Aseptic meningitis means meningitis (inflammation of the meninges, the coverings around the brain and spinal cord) where routine bacterial cultures are negative.

---

### Why meningitis happens

The virus first replicates in the gut and lymphoid tissue (immune tissue), then can enter the blood.

From blood, it can spread to the CNS (central nervous system), especially the meninges.

That meningeal inflammation causes:

* headache
* fever
* neck stiffness
* photophobia (light sensitivity)

CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) usually shows a viral pattern:

* lymphocytes
* normal glucose
* mildly increased protein

---

## One-line intuition

Echovirus is a fecal-oral enterovirus that usually causes a mild viral illness, but on exams think **aseptic meningitis + rash**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, echovirus is one of the classic causes of **aseptic meningitis**, especially in children.

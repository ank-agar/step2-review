## Celiac disease

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/24377.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441900/bin/CD__pathology.jpg)

![Image](https://dermnetnz.org/assets/collection/Dermatitis-herpetiformis/dermatitis-herpetiformis-0005.jpg)

Celiac disease is an autoimmune disease where gluten triggers immune damage to the small intestine.

Gluten = protein found in:

* wheat
* barley
* rye

Autoimmune = the immune system attacks the body’s own tissue.

---

### Core idea

In celiac disease:

* gluten gets eaten
* immune system reacts abnormally
* small intestinal villi get damaged
* nutrient absorption decreases

Villi = tiny finger-like projections in the small intestine that increase surface area for absorption.

So the key pathology is:

> villous atrophy

Villous atrophy = flattening/loss of villi.

---

### Why symptoms happen

The small intestine normally absorbs nutrients through villi.

If villi flatten:

* less surface area
* less absorption
* more malabsorption

Malabsorption = poor absorption of nutrients from the gut.

This causes:

* diarrhea
* weight loss
* bloating
* fatigue
* iron deficiency anemia
* vitamin deficiency

Iron deficiency happens because the proximal small intestine is important for iron absorption.

---

### Mechanism

Gluten contains gliadin.

Gliadin = gluten protein fragment that triggers the immune reaction in celiac disease.

Tissue transglutaminase modifies gliadin, making it more immunogenic.

Immunogenic = more likely to trigger an immune response.

Then T cells attack the small intestinal mucosa.

Mucosa = inner lining of the gut.

This leads to:

* villous atrophy
* crypt hyperplasia
* intraepithelial lymphocytes

Crypt hyperplasia = enlarged/deeper intestinal glands from increased regeneration.

Intraepithelial lymphocytes = immune cells sitting within the intestinal lining.

---

### Diagnosis clues

Blood tests often show:

* anti-tissue transglutaminase IgA
* anti-endomysial IgA

IgA = immunoglobulin A, an antibody type common in mucosal surfaces like the gut.

Important: also check total IgA because IgA deficiency can make these tests falsely negative.

Biopsy shows:

* flattened villi
* crypt hyperplasia
* increased intraepithelial lymphocytes

---

### Important association

Celiac disease can cause dermatitis herpetiformis.

Dermatitis herpetiformis = intensely itchy blistering rash associated with celiac disease.

Classic location:

* elbows
* knees
* buttocks
* extensor surfaces

Extensor surfaces = outer surfaces of joints, like elbows and knees.

---

## One-line intuition

Celiac disease is gluten-triggered autoimmune flattening of small intestinal villi, so the gut loses absorptive surface area.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association:

* celiac disease is strongly linked to **HLA-DQ2** and **HLA-DQ8**

HLA = human leukocyte antigen, a molecule that presents antigens to immune cells.

Also remember:

* treatment is strict gluten-free diet
* increased risk of enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma

Enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma = aggressive intestinal T-cell cancer associated with longstanding celiac disease.

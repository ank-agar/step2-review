## Splenic infarct

A **splenic infarct** is death of splenic tissue because part of the spleen loses blood supply.

Break it down:

* splenic = related to the spleen
* infarct = tissue death from ischemia (low blood flow/oxygen)

So:

> a splenic infarct is basically a "spleen stroke."

---

### Core idea

The spleen gets blood from the splenic artery.

If a clot blocks the splenic artery or one of its branches, that downstream area cannot get oxygen, so it becomes ischemic and dies.

Because splenic artery branches supply wedge-like territories, the infarct often looks **wedge-shaped** on CT (computed tomography).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Left upper quadrant pain:** the spleen sits in the LUQ (left upper quadrant)
* **Pain radiating to the left shoulder:** diaphragmatic irritation can refer pain through the phrenic nerve
* **Fever sometimes:** necrotic tissue can trigger inflammation
* **Wedge-shaped hypodensity on CT:** the blocked vessel supplies a triangular/wedge-shaped region

---

### Common causes

Think "something blocks splenic blood flow":

* atrial fibrillation (irregular heart rhythm causing emboli, meaning traveling clots)
* infective endocarditis (heart valve infection that can send septic emboli, meaning infected clots)
* sickle cell disease
* hypercoagulable states (conditions that make blood clot too easily)
* trauma or splenic artery injury

---

## One-line intuition

A splenic infarct is a clot cutting off blood to part of the spleen, causing a painful wedge-shaped area of dead tissue.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In sickle cell disease, repeated splenic infarcts can cause **autosplenectomy** (the spleen scars down and becomes functionally absent), increasing risk of infections by encapsulated organisms like *Streptococcus pneumoniae*.

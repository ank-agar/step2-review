### Core idea

Sinusitis is inflammation of the paranasal sinuses (air-filled spaces around the nasal cavity).

It is also called rhinosinusitis because the nose is usually inflamed too:

* rhino = nose
* sinus = air-filled cavity
* -itis = inflammation

So sinusitis basically means:

> swollen sinus lining + blocked drainage + trapped mucus.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The sinuses normally make mucus that drains into the nose.

In a URI (upper respiratory infection), allergy, or irritation:

* sinus mucosa (lining) swells
* sinus ostia (small drainage openings) get blocked
* mucus cannot drain
* pressure builds up
* bacteria can grow in the trapped mucus

That gives:

* nasal congestion
* thick nasal discharge
* facial pain/pressure
* headache
* worse pain when bending forward
* decreased smell

The bending-forward pain happens because inflamed, mucus-filled sinuses shift pressure when the head changes position.

---

### Common sinuses involved

* Maxillary sinuses: cheek pain, upper tooth pain
* Frontal sinuses: forehead pain
* Ethmoid sinuses: pain between the eyes
* Sphenoid sinuses: deeper headache, sometimes behind the eyes

---

### Acute bacterial causes

Most acute sinusitis starts viral, but classic bacterial causes are:

* Streptococcus pneumoniae
* Haemophilus influenzae
* Moraxella catarrhalis

Think: the same common bugs as otitis media (middle ear infection), because both involve blocked drainage after a URI (upper respiratory infection).

---

## One-line intuition

Sinusitis is like a clogged, swollen mucus room in the face: once the exit is blocked, pressure and infection can build up.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

Chronic/recurrent sinusitis can suggest impaired mucociliary clearance (poor movement of mucus by cilia), especially in cystic fibrosis or Kartagener syndrome.

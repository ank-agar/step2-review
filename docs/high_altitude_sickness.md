### Core idea

High-altitude sickness = symptoms from going high up too fast, before the body adapts to low oxygen.

At high altitude:

* barometric pressure drops
* inspired PO2 (partial pressure of oxygen you breathe in) drops
* arterial O2 (oxygen) drops

So the body gets hypoxemia, meaning low oxygen in the blood.

---

### Main types

Altitude illness has 3 big acute forms:

* AMS (acute mountain sickness): headache, nausea, dizziness, fatigue, poor sleep
* HAPE (high-altitude pulmonary edema): fluid in the lungs
* HACE (high-altitude cerebral edema): fluid/swelling in the brain

Think of them as:

* AMS = mild brain symptoms from low oxygen
* HAPE = lung edema
* HACE = brain edema

---

### Why symptoms happen

Low oxygen triggers hyperventilation, meaning breathing faster/deeper.

This helps bring in more O2, but it also blows off CO2 (carbon dioxide), causing respiratory alkalosis (blood becomes too basic from low CO2).

The brain also responds to hypoxia (low tissue oxygen) by vasodilating, meaning blood vessels widen to bring more oxygen.

But more blood flow can increase pressure and leakiness:

* brain vessel dilation can contribute to headache and cerebral edema
* lung vessel constriction can increase pulmonary artery pressure
* high lung pressure can push fluid into alveoli (air sacs)

That is why severe altitude illness becomes edema.

---

### HAPE

HAPE (high-altitude pulmonary edema) is noncardiogenic pulmonary edema, meaning lung fluid not caused by left-sided heart failure.

Mechanism:

* low oxygen causes pulmonary vasoconstriction (lung blood vessels tighten)
* this raises pulmonary artery pressure
* capillaries get stressed and leak
* fluid enters alveoli

Clinical clues:

* dyspnea (shortness of breath), especially at rest
* cough
* crackles on lung exam
* low O2 saturation
* pink frothy sputum in severe cases

---

### HACE

HACE (high-altitude cerebral edema) is brain swelling from severe altitude illness.

Clinical clues:

* confusion
* ataxia (unsteady walking)
* severe headache
* vomiting
* altered mental status

Ataxia at altitude is a huge red flag.

---

### Treatment idea

Best treatment:

* descend
* give O2 (oxygen)

Helpful meds:

* acetazolamide helps prevent/treat AMS by causing bicarbonate diuresis (peeing out bicarbonate), which creates mild metabolic acidosis and makes you breathe more
* dexamethasone helps HACE by reducing brain edema
* nifedipine can help HAPE by lowering pulmonary artery pressure

---

## One-line intuition

High altitude means low inspired oxygen; if you ascend too fast, the brain and lungs respond with pressure/leakiness, causing headache, nausea, pulmonary edema, or cerebral edema.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Acetazolamide works by inhibiting carbonic anhydrase in the proximal tubule, causing bicarbonate loss in urine; this mild metabolic acidosis stimulates ventilation, helping acclimatization.

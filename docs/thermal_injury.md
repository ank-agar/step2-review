## Thermal injury

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bd/Burn_Degree_Diagram.png)

![Image](https://chemm.hhs.gov/chemmimages/burn_BSA.gif)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9b/Major-2nd-degree-burn.jpg)

Thermal injury = tissue damage caused by heat or cold.

For Step 1, this usually means a **burn** from:

* flame
* hot liquid/steam
* hot surface
* radiation heat

The core idea:

> heat denatures proteins and damages cell membranes, so cells die.

---

### Burn depth

Think of burns by how deep the heat goes:

* **Superficial burn:** epidermis only
* **Partial-thickness burn:** epidermis + part of dermis
* **Full-thickness burn:** epidermis + entire dermis destroyed

Definitions:

* **Epidermis:** outer skin layer
* **Dermis:** deeper skin layer with nerves, blood vessels, sweat glands, and hair follicles
* **Full-thickness:** injury goes through the full skin thickness

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Pain:** heat injures exposed nerve endings
* **Blisters:** epidermis separates from dermis and fills with fluid
* **Edema:** inflammation makes capillaries leaky
* **Dehydration/shock in large burns:** plasma leaks out of vessels into tissues
* **Painless deep burns:** nerves are destroyed, so the patient may feel less pain despite worse injury

So the weird rule is:

> less pain can mean a deeper, more dangerous burn.

---

### Why big burns are dangerous

Skin normally prevents:

* water loss
* infection
* heat loss

When a large area is burned, the patient can get:

* hypovolemia (low circulating blood volume)
* infection/sepsis
* hypothermia

**TBSA** (total body surface area) matters because bigger burn area = more fluid loss.

---

## One-line intuition

Thermal injury is heat cooking tissue: proteins denature, cells die, vessels leak, and deeper burns destroy nerves and skin barrier.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Large burns cause hypovolemic shock from massive capillary leak, and initial resuscitation commonly uses the **Parkland formula**:

* 4 mL lactated Ringer solution x body weight in kg x %TBSA burned
* give half in the first 8 hours
* give the other half over the next 16 hours

**Lactated Ringer solution** = isotonic IV fluid used for resuscitation.

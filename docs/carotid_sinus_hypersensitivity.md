## Carotid sinus hypersensitivity

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK559059/bin/CHS.jpg)

![Image](https://anatomytool.org/sites/default/files/16795-257.jpg)

![Image](https://images.medicinenet.com/images/slideshow/low_blood_pressure_s6_baroreceptors.jpg?output-quality=70&resize=350px%3A%2A)

### Core idea

Carotid sinus hypersensitivity means the **carotid sinus baroreceptors** overreact to pressure.

Carotid sinus = pressure-sensing area at the split of the common carotid artery into the internal and external carotid arteries.

Baroreceptors = pressure/stretch receptors.

So if something presses on the neck, the body thinks:

> blood pressure is too high

and it responds by dropping heart rate and blood pressure too much.

---

### Why syncope happens

Normal carotid sinus reflex:

* carotid sinus senses high pressure/stretch
* sends signal through CN IX (glossopharyngeal nerve)
* medulla increases parasympathetic/vagal output
* medulla decreases sympathetic output
* heart rate drops
* blood vessels dilate

In carotid sinus hypersensitivity, this reflex is exaggerated.

So small triggers can cause:

* bradycardia (slow heart rate)
* hypotension (low blood pressure)
* cerebral hypoperfusion (low blood flow to the brain)
* syncope (fainting)

Reasoning:

overactive baroreceptor reflex -> too much vagal tone + too little sympathetic tone -> low cardiac output/blood pressure -> brain gets less blood -> fainting.

---

### Classic triggers

Think neck pressure or neck movement:

* shaving
* tight collar
* turning the head
* neck mass
* carotid sinus massage

This is most common in older adults.

---

### Types

* **Cardioinhibitory** = mainly bradycardia/asystole (heart pauses)
* **Vasodepressor** = mainly hypotension from vasodilation
* **Mixed** = both happen

Cardioinhibitory = the "heart is inhibited."

Vasodepressor = the "vessels depress the pressure."

---

## One-line intuition

Carotid sinus hypersensitivity is an overactive neck pressure sensor that falsely thinks BP (blood pressure) is too high, so it slows the heart and drops BP until the patient faints.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Carotid sinus afferents travel by CN IX (glossopharyngeal nerve), while aortic arch baroreceptor afferents travel by CN X (vagus nerve).

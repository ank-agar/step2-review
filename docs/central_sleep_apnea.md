# Central sleep apnea

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/53/Block_diagram_of_the_control_of_the_respiratory_system.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/24/CentralApnea.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d9/Cheyne-Stokes_respiration_no_lang.svg)

Central sleep apnea is when breathing repeatedly stops during sleep because the brain does not send enough signal to the respiratory muscles.

So the problem is not a blocked airway.

It is a failure of drive from the CNS (central nervous system).

Break it down:

* central = brain/brainstem control problem
* sleep = happens during sleep
* apnea = temporary stopping of breathing

---

### Core idea

Normally:

brainstem respiratory centers → phrenic nerve → diaphragm contracts → air moves in

In central sleep apnea:

brainstem respiratory drive drops → diaphragm does not try to breathe → no airflow

That is the key difference from obstructive sleep apnea.

---

### Central vs obstructive

Central sleep apnea:

* no airflow
* no respiratory effort
* airway is not the main problem

Obstructive sleep apnea:

* no airflow
* respiratory effort is still present
* airway is blocked/collapsed

Intuition:

* obstructive = body is trying to breathe, but air cannot get through
* central = body temporarily stops trying to breathe

---

### Why it happens

Central sleep apnea can happen when the breathing-control system becomes unstable or suppressed.

Common associations:

* heart failure
* stroke
* opioid use
* high altitude
* brainstem lesions

Why opioids matter:

Opioids depress the CNS (central nervous system), including brainstem respiratory centers, so the brain becomes less responsive to rising CO2 (carbon dioxide).

---

### Cheyne-Stokes breathing

Cheyne-Stokes breathing is a waxing-and-waning breathing pattern:

* breaths gradually get deeper/faster
* then gradually get shallower
* then apnea happens
* then the cycle repeats

This is classically associated with heart failure.

Why?

In heart failure, circulation is slower, so blood gas information reaches the brain late. The brain overcorrects, then undercorrects, causing unstable breathing cycles.

---

## One-line intuition

Central sleep apnea is a brainstem signaling problem: the airway may be open, but the brain temporarily stops telling the diaphragm to breathe.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On polysomnography (sleep study), central sleep apnea shows absent airflow **and** absent chest/abdominal effort; obstructive sleep apnea shows absent airflow but continued effort.

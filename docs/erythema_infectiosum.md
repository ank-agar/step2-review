Erythema infectiosum = **fifth disease**, a childhood viral rash caused by **parvovirus B19**.

Classic presentation:

* mild fever/viral prodrome first
* then bright red **"slapped cheek"** rash
* then lacy, reticular rash on trunk/arms/legs

Reticular = net-like or lacy.

---

### Core idea

Parvovirus B19 is a **nonenveloped single-stranded DNA virus**.

DNA = deoxyribonucleic acid.

It likes to infect **erythroid precursors** in bone marrow.

Erythroid precursors = immature cells that are trying to become RBCs (red blood cells).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Slapped cheek rash:** immune reaction after the virus has already spread
* **Lacy body rash:** immune complexes in the skin create a net-like pattern
* **Mild fever/runny nose first:** early viral phase
* **Arthralgia in adults:** immune-complex joint inflammation

Arthralgia = joint pain.

Important: once the rash appears, the child is usually **less contagious**, because the rash is mainly immune-mediated.

---

### Why Step 1 cares

Parvovirus B19 temporarily shuts down RBC production.

Usually that is fine, because normal RBCs live about 120 days.

But in chronic hemolytic anemia, RBCs are already being destroyed fast, so even a brief pause in RBC production can cause:

* **aplastic crisis**
* severe anemia
* low reticulocytes

Reticulocytes = young RBCs recently released from bone marrow.

Classic risk group:

* sickle cell disease

---

### Pregnancy association

If parvovirus B19 infects a fetus, it can destroy fetal RBC precursors and cause severe fetal anemia.

Severe fetal anemia can lead to:

* high-output heart failure
* hydrops fetalis

Hydrops fetalis = abnormal fluid accumulation in fetal compartments due to severe fetal illness.

---

## One-line intuition

Parvovirus B19 causes a slapped-cheek childhood rash, but the high-yield danger is that it stops RBC production.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Parvovirus B19 binds **P antigen** on erythroid precursors, so remember:

**Parvo B19 punches the bone marrow's RBC factory.**

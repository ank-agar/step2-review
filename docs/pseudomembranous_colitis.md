### Core idea

Pseudomembranous colitis is severe inflammation of the colon, usually from **Clostridioides difficile** infection.

Break it down:

* pseudo- = false
* membranous = membrane-like layer
* colitis = colon inflammation

So it means:

> inflamed colon with false membrane-like plaques stuck on top of the mucosa.

Those yellow-white plaques are made of:

* dead epithelial cells
* fibrin
* mucus
* neutrophils
* inflammatory debris

---

### Why it happens

Classic setup:

* patient takes antibiotics
* normal gut flora gets wiped out
* C. diff overgrows
* C. diff releases toxins A and B
* toxins damage colon mucosa
* damaged mucosa forms pseudomembranes

Normal gut bacteria usually keep C. diff controlled.

Antibiotics remove that competition, so C. diff gets room to expand.

---

### Symptoms

Think:

* watery diarrhea
* abdominal cramps
* fever
* leukocytosis
* recent antibiotic use

High-risk antibiotics:

* clindamycin
* ampicillin/amoxicillin
* cephalosporins
* fluoroquinolones

---

### Why the diarrhea happens

C. diff toxins injure colon cells and trigger inflammation.

That causes:

* less water absorption by the colon
* more fluid secretion into the lumen
* mucosal damage

So stool becomes watery and inflammatory.

---

### Histology clue

The classic microscopic finding is a **volcano lesion**:

* damaged crypts erupt upward
* neutrophils and debris spill out
* pseudomembrane forms on the surface

Intuition:

> the crypt is like a tiny volcano pushing inflammatory debris onto the colon surface.

---

## One-line intuition

Antibiotics clear out normal gut bacteria, C. diff overgrows, toxins burn the colon lining, and yellow-white pseudomembranes form.

---

## Pearl 🔥

C. diff is **spore-forming**, so alcohol hand sanitizer does not reliably kill it; use soap and water plus contact precautions.

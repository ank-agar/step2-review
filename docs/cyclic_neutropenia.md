# Cyclic neutropenia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1e/Cycling_absolute_neutrophil_count_in_cyclic_neutropenia.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/69/Hematopoiesis_%28human%29_diagram.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/45/Aphthous_ulcer.jpg)

Cyclic neutropenia is a disorder where neutrophils (bacterial-fighting white blood cells) drop very low in repeating cycles, classically about every 21 days.

During the low point, the patient is more prone to:

* fever
* recurrent infections
* mouth ulcers
* gingivitis (gum inflammation)
* pharyngitis (throat inflammation)

---

### Core idea

Neutrophils are made in the bone marrow.

In cyclic neutropenia, neutrophil production is unstable, so the ANC (absolute neutrophil count, the number of neutrophils in blood) rises and falls rhythmically.

When ANC drops:

* less bacterial defense
* mucosal surfaces like the mouth get infected/inflamed more easily
* symptoms flare

Then the marrow recovers, ANC rises again, and symptoms improve.

---

### Why it happens

Most classic cases are due to ELANE mutation.

ELANE (elastase, neutrophil expressed gene) codes for neutrophil elastase, an enzyme inside neutrophils.

If ELANE is mutated, neutrophil precursors (immature developing neutrophils) do not mature normally in the bone marrow.

So think:

> bad neutrophil maturation → neutrophils periodically crash → infections come in waves

---

### How to recognize it

The key clue is recurrent symptoms on a schedule:

* every ~3 weeks
* fever + oral ulcers
* low ANC during attacks

Diagnosis is usually made by serial CBCs (complete blood counts) showing repeated ANC dips.

---

## One-line intuition

Cyclic neutropenia = the neutrophil factory works in waves, so bacterial defense crashes every few weeks.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association: cyclic neutropenia is classically linked to ELANE mutation and treated with G-CSF (granulocyte colony-stimulating factor), which stimulates bone marrow to make more neutrophils.

### Core idea

Hemochromatosis is **iron overload**.

The body absorbs too much iron from the gut, then stores it in organs like:

* liver
* pancreas
* heart
* skin
* joints

Too much iron is toxic because iron can create **free radicals** (reactive molecules that damage cells).

---

### Most common cause

Classic hereditary hemochromatosis is usually due to an **HFE gene mutation** (hereditary hemochromatosis gene mutation), especially **C282Y**.

It is usually:

* autosomal recessive
* more common in people of Northern European ancestry

---

### Why iron builds up

Normally, the liver makes **hepcidin** (a hormone that decreases iron absorption).

Think:

* high iron -> more hepcidin -> less gut iron absorption

In hemochromatosis:

* HFE mutation causes low/inappropriately low hepcidin
* low hepcidin means the gut keeps absorbing iron
* iron slowly piles up in tissues

So the body acts like it is iron-deficient even though it is actually overloaded.

---

### Classic findings

Iron deposition causes:

* **cirrhosis** (scarring of the liver)
* **diabetes mellitus** (high blood sugar from pancreatic damage)
* **bronze skin** from skin iron/melanin changes
* cardiomyopathy (heart muscle disease)
* arthropathy (joint disease), especially MCP joints (metacarpophalangeal joints/knuckles)
* hypogonadism (low sex hormone function)

Classic phrase:

> Bronze diabetes

because pancreas damage causes diabetes, and skin darkening causes a bronze color.

---

### Diagnosis clues

Labs often show:

* increased serum iron
* increased ferritin (iron storage protein)
* increased transferrin saturation (percentage of iron-binding protein filled with iron)
* decreased TIBC (total iron-binding capacity)

Why decreased TIBC?

**TIBC reflects transferrin availability.** When the body already has too much iron, it does not need to make lots of extra transferrin to go catch more iron.

---

### Treatment

Main treatment:

* phlebotomy (removing blood)

Why does blood removal help?

Red blood cells contain lots of iron inside hemoglobin (oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells). Removing blood forces the body to use stored iron to make new red blood cells.

---

## One-line intuition

Hemochromatosis = the gut absorbs too much iron because hepcidin regulation fails, so iron deposits in organs and slowly damages them.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 pearl: hemochromatosis causes **increased ferritin + increased transferrin saturation**, while anemia of chronic disease causes **increased ferritin but decreased serum iron** because iron is trapped in storage.

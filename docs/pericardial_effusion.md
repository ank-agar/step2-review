## Pericardial effusion

![Image](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/-/scassets/images/org/health/articles/17351-pericardial-effusion)

![Image](https://us-southeast-1.linodeobjects.com/wikem-images/images/Pericardial_Effusion.png)

Pericardial effusion = extra fluid in the pericardial space.

Break it down:

* pericardium = sac around the heart
* effusion = abnormal fluid collection

So:

> fluid builds up around the heart, inside the sac that surrounds it.

---

### Core idea

Normally, there is a tiny amount of fluid around the heart so it can move smoothly.

In pericardial effusion, there is too much fluid, so the heart is sitting in a fluid-filled sac.

If enough fluid builds up, it can squeeze the heart from the outside.

---

### Why symptoms happen

The heart needs to fill with blood during diastole (relaxation/filling phase).

If fluid compresses the heart:

* ventricles cannot fill well
* less blood gets pumped out
* blood backs up into veins
* patient can get dyspnea (shortness of breath), hypotension (low blood pressure), and JVD (jugular venous distension)

This severe compression is called cardiac tamponade (life-threatening compression of the heart by fluid).

---

### Classic findings

Pericardial effusion can show:

* muffled heart sounds because fluid blocks sound transmission
* low-voltage ECG (electrocardiogram) because fluid dampens electrical signal detection
* enlarged "water bottle" heart on CXR (chest X-ray) if the effusion is large
* anechoic space on echo (black fluid space on echocardiogram/heart ultrasound)

---

### High-yield tamponade association

Beck triad:

* hypotension (low blood pressure)
* JVD (jugular venous distension)
* muffled heart sounds

Reason:

Fluid squeezes the heart, so it cannot fill or pump well.

---

## One-line intuition

Pericardial effusion is fluid trapped around the heart; tamponade is when that fluid squeezes the heart so badly that it cannot fill.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Electrical alternans (beat-to-beat changing QRS amplitude on ECG) happens because the heart is literally swinging in the fluid-filled pericardial sac.

## Systemic inflammatory response syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ad/Sepsis_Steps.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/38/Acute_phase_response.png)

Systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) means the whole body is showing signs of inflammation.

It can be caused by:

* infection
* trauma
* burns
* pancreatitis
* ischemia (low blood flow)
* major surgery

So SIRS does **not automatically mean infection**.

---

### Diagnostic idea

Classically, SIRS is diagnosed when there are **2 or more** of:

* temperature >38 C or <36 C
* heart rate >90/min
* respiratory rate >20/min or PaCO2 (partial pressure of carbon dioxide in arterial blood) <32 mmHg
* WBC (white blood cell) count >12,000/mm3, <4,000/mm3, or >10% bands (immature neutrophils)

---

### Why the findings happen

Big trigger, like infection or tissue injury, activates immune cells.

Those immune cells release cytokines (immune signaling proteins), especially:

* IL-1 (interleukin-1)
* IL-6 (interleukin-6)
* TNF-alpha (tumor necrosis factor-alpha)

These cytokines tell the body:

> something dangerous is happening everywhere.

So the body responds with:

* fever from hypothalamus activation
* tachycardia (fast heart rate) to maintain blood flow
* tachypnea (fast breathing) from stress/acidosis compensation
* abnormal WBC (white blood cell) count from bone marrow immune activation

---

### SIRS vs sepsis

SIRS = systemic inflammation.

Sepsis = life-threatening organ dysfunction from a dysregulated response to infection.

Old-school Step 1 intuition:

* SIRS + infection = sepsis
* SIRS + hypotension/shock picture = worry about septic shock if infection is the cause

---

## One-line intuition

SIRS is the body hitting the full-body inflammatory panic button, even if the trigger is not an infection.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Acute pancreatitis can cause SIRS without infection because pancreatic enzymes injure tissue, triggering cytokine release and systemic inflammation.

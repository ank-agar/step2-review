## Traumatic aortic dissection

Traumatic aortic dissection = a tear in the inner layer of the aorta after trauma, usually from rapid deceleration.

Most common scenario:

* high-speed motor vehicle crash
* fall from height
* sudden blunt chest trauma

The key mechanism is:

* the body suddenly stops
* the heart/aorta keep moving briefly
* fixed parts of the aorta get stretched and sheared
* the intima (inner vessel layer) tears

Blood then enters the aortic wall and splits the layers apart.

This creates:

* true lumen = the normal blood channel
* false lumen = the new abnormal channel inside the aortic wall
* intimal flap = the torn inner layer separating the true and false lumens

---

### Most tested location

Traumatic aortic injury classically occurs at the aortic isthmus.

The aortic isthmus is near the ligamentum arteriosum, which anchors the aorta.

Why there?

* mobile ascending aorta moves during sudden deceleration
* descending aorta is relatively fixed
* the junction between mobile and fixed parts gets maximum shear stress

So think:

> sudden stop + fixed aortic isthmus = tearing injury.

---

### What you might see

Classic imaging clue:

* widened mediastinum on chest X-ray

CT angiography (computed tomography angiography) may show:

* intimal flap
* pseudoaneurysm
* mediastinal hematoma
* irregular aortic contour

Pseudoaneurysm = blood contained by the outer vessel wall/tissue, not all normal vessel layers.

---

## One-line intuition

Trauma makes the aorta get yanked at its fixed point, tearing the inner wall and letting blood tunnel into a false channel.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, traumatic aortic rupture/dissection after rapid deceleration most commonly occurs at the aortic isthmus, near the ligamentum arteriosum, and can present with widened mediastinum.

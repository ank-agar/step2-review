### Core idea

A prolactinoma is a benign pituitary adenoma (gland tumor) that secretes too much prolactin (milk-production hormone).

So the main problem is:

* too much prolactin
* suppression of GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone)
* low LH/FSH (luteinizing hormone/follicle-stimulating hormone)
* low sex hormones

---

### Why the symptoms happen

High prolactin tells the body:

> “You are in a breastfeeding-like state.”

That shuts down reproduction.

In females:

* amenorrhea (no periods)
* infertility
* galactorrhea (milk discharge not due to breastfeeding)

In males:

* decreased libido
* erectile dysfunction
* infertility
* sometimes gynecomastia (male breast tissue growth)

---

### Mass effect

If the tumor gets big, it can compress nearby structures.

Most important:

* optic chiasm (where optic nerve fibers cross)

This causes:

* bitemporal hemianopia (loss of outer visual fields in both eyes)

Why?

The crossing nasal retinal fibers carry temporal visual fields, so compression at the optic chiasm knocks out the temporal fields.

---

### Diagnosis

Think:

* high serum prolactin
* pituitary MRI (magnetic resonance imaging)

Also rule out other causes of high prolactin:

* pregnancy
* hypothyroidism
* antipsychotics
* metoclopramide
* kidney disease

---

### Treatment

First-line treatment is dopamine agonists (drugs that activate dopamine receptors):

* cabergoline
* bromocriptine

Why dopamine?

Dopamine normally inhibits prolactin release from the anterior pituitary.

So dopamine agonist = decreased prolactin + tumor shrinkage.

---

## One-line intuition

A prolactinoma is a prolactin-secreting pituitary tumor that makes the body act “postpartum,” shutting down GnRH and causing infertility/galactorrhea.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Prolactinoma is treated medically first with dopamine agonists, unlike many other pituitary adenomas where surgery is often first-line.

## Nipple discharge

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6a/Breast_anatomy.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/17077.jpg)

### Core idea

Nipple discharge = fluid coming out of the nipple.

The big test-taking split is:

* **Physiologic discharge** = usually benign/normal-ish
* **Pathologic discharge** = concerning for duct lesion or cancer

---

### Physiologic discharge

Usually:

* bilateral
* from multiple ducts
* milky, green, or yellow
* happens only when the nipple is squeezed/stimulated

Common causes:

* pregnancy or breastfeeding
* galactorrhea (milk production not due to breastfeeding)
* high prolactin (hormone from the anterior pituitary that stimulates milk production)
* hypothyroidism (low thyroid hormone)
* medications like antipsychotics

Why hypothyroidism can cause this:

Low thyroid hormone makes the hypothalamus release more TRH (thyrotropin-releasing hormone), and TRH increases both TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone) and prolactin.

More prolactin = more milk-like discharge.

---

### Pathologic discharge

More concerning if it is:

* unilateral
* spontaneous
* bloody or clear/serous
* from one duct
* associated with a mass or skin/nipple changes

Classic causes:

* intraductal papilloma = benign growth inside a breast duct
* duct ectasia = dilated/inflamed duct
* breast cancer, including DCIS (ductal carcinoma in situ, cancer cells confined within ducts)

---

### High-yield cause

**Intraductal papilloma** is the classic benign cause of:

* unilateral
* spontaneous
* bloody nipple discharge

Why it bleeds:

The papilloma grows inside the duct and has a small fibrovascular stalk (connective tissue + blood vessels), so it can irritate/bleed into the duct.

---

## One-line intuition

Milky bilateral discharge makes you think hormones/prolactin; unilateral spontaneous bloody discharge makes you think a duct lesion until proven otherwise.

---

## Pearl

In a nonpregnant patient with milky nipple discharge, check prolactin and TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone), because hypothyroidism can raise TRH (thyrotropin-releasing hormone), which increases prolactin.

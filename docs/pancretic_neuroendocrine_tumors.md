# Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors

Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors, or **pNETs (pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors)**, are tumors that come from the **endocrine hormone-producing cells** of the pancreas.

Think:

* regular pancreatic adenocarcinoma = exocrine duct cells
* pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor = endocrine islet-type cells

So these are basically tumors of the pancreas cells that normally make hormones like:

* insulin
* glucagon
* gastrin
* somatostatin

---

### Core idea

These tumors can be:

* **functional:** they secrete hormones that cause symptoms
* **nonfunctional:** they do not cause an obvious hormone syndrome

Nonfunctional tumors are often found later because they just act like a mass:

* abdominal pain
* weight loss
* jaundice if near the bile duct
* liver metastases

---

### Functional examples

If the tumor secretes a hormone, the symptoms match that hormone.

* **Insulinoma:** too much insulin → hypoglycemia (low blood glucose)
* **Gastrinoma:** too much gastrin → Zollinger-Ellison syndrome (gastrin-secreting tumor causing severe acid production and peptic ulcers)
* **Glucagonoma:** too much glucagon → diabetes mellitus (high blood glucose) + necrolytic migratory erythema (migrating red blistering rash)
* **VIPoma:** too much VIP (vasoactive intestinal peptide) → watery diarrhea, hypokalemia (low potassium), achlorhydria (low stomach acid)
* **Somatostatinoma:** too much somatostatin → diabetes mellitus, gallstones, steatorrhea (fatty stools)

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The pancreas has endocrine cells that dump hormones directly into blood.

So if one of those cells becomes a tumor and keeps secreting hormone:

* insulin drives glucose into cells → low blood glucose
* gastrin stimulates acid secretion → ulcers/diarrhea
* glucagon raises blood glucose → diabetes-like picture
* VIP increases intestinal secretion → watery diarrhea

The tumor is not just a lump. It can behave like a hormone factory.

---

### Pathology clue

On histology, neuroendocrine tumors classically have:

* nests/trabeculae of uniform cells
* “salt-and-pepper” chromatin
* positive chromogranin A and synaptophysin

**Chromogranin A** and **synaptophysin** are neuroendocrine markers, meaning they help prove the tumor came from neuroendocrine cells.

---

### Important associations

pNETs can be sporadic, but also associate with:

* MEN1 (multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1)
* VHL (von Hippel-Lindau disease)

MEN1 is especially high-yield because it involves:

* pituitary tumors
* parathyroid tumors
* pancreatic endocrine tumors

---

## One-line intuition

A pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor is an islet-cell-type tumor that may either sit quietly as a pancreatic mass or overproduce a hormone and create a very specific clinical syndrome.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, if you see **recurrent severe peptic ulcers + diarrhea**, think **gastrinoma**, and remember it can be part of **MEN1 (multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1)**.

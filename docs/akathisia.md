## Akathisia

Akathisia = **subjective inner restlessness + objective need to move**.

Think:

* patient cannot sit still
* pacing
* rocking
* shifting weight
* repeatedly crossing/uncrossing legs

The key is that it feels like an **internal motor discomfort**, not just normal anxiety.

---

### Why it happens

Most classic Step 1 cause:

* antipsychotics, especially high-potency typical antipsychotics

Antipsychotics block **D2 receptors (dopamine type 2 receptors)** in the **nigrostriatal pathway (dopamine pathway from substantia nigra to striatum involved in movement control)**.

So:

* less dopamine signaling in basal ganglia movement circuits
* abnormal motor drive
* patient feels an unbearable urge to move

This is why akathisia is an **EPS (extrapyramidal symptom; drug-induced movement side effect from basal ganglia/nigrostriatal dysfunction)**.

---

### How to recognize it

Akathisia can look like:

* agitation
* anxiety
* worsening psychosis

But the clue is:

> "I feel like I have to keep moving."

So do not confuse it with pure anxiety. Anxiety is mental worry/fear; akathisia is more like the body has an internal "move now" signal stuck on.

---

### Treatment idea

Common treatments:

* lower dose or switch offending drug
* propranolol, a beta blocker
* benzodiazepines (GABA-enhancing sedatives) sometimes

Anticholinergics help more with **acute dystonia (sustained muscle contraction)** and **drug-induced parkinsonism**, but propranolol is more classic for akathisia.

---

## One-line intuition

Akathisia is when dopamine blockade in movement circuits makes the patient feel internally restless and physically unable to stay still.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY distinction:

* **Acute dystonia:** hours-days, muscle spasms, treat with benztropine/diphenhydramine
* **Akathisia:** days-weeks, inner restlessness, treat with propranolol
* **Tardive dyskinesia:** months-years, choreiform mouth/tongue movements

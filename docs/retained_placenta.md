### Core idea

Retained placenta means the placenta stays inside the uterus (womb) after the baby is delivered.

Normally:

* baby comes out
* uterus contracts
* placenta separates from the uterine wall
* placenta is expelled

In retained placenta, that last placental step fails.

---

### Why it matters

The uterus needs to clamp down after delivery to squeeze shut the blood vessels where the placenta was attached.

If placental tissue is still inside:

* the uterus cannot contract well
* blood vessels stay more open
* risk of postpartum hemorrhage (heavy bleeding after delivery) increases
* retained tissue can become infected later

So the danger is not just "placenta is still there."

The real danger is:

> the uterus cannot fully close down the placental bleeding site.

---

### Main types

* **Placenta adherens:** weak uterine contractions fail to separate/expel the placenta
* **Trapped placenta:** placenta separates, but gets stuck behind a closing cervix (opening of the uterus)
* **Placenta accreta:** placenta is abnormally attached into the myometrium (muscular wall of the uterus)

Placenta accreta is the scary one because pulling it off can cause massive bleeding.

---

### Why accreta causes retention

Normally, there is a clean separation layer between placenta and uterus.

In placenta accreta, that layer is defective, so placental villi (finger-like placental projections) attach too deeply.

So instead of peeling off cleanly, the placenta is stuck into the uterine wall.

That is why accreta is associated with:

* retained placenta
* severe postpartum hemorrhage
* sometimes hysterectomy (surgical removal of the uterus)

---

### Classic presentation

After delivery:

* placenta does not come out
* heavy bleeding may occur
* uterus may feel boggy if it cannot contract well
* later retained fragments can cause fever, pelvic pain, foul discharge, or persistent bleeding

---

## One-line intuition

Retained placenta is when the "afterbirth" does not fully leave, so the uterus cannot clamp down and stop bleeding normally.

---

## Pearl

Prior C-section (cesarean delivery, surgical delivery through the abdomen and uterus) increases risk of placenta accreta, especially when combined with placenta previa (placenta covering the cervical opening).

Rectal cancer is a malignant tumor in the rectum, which is the last part of the large intestine before the anus.

Most rectal cancers are adenocarcinomas, meaning cancers from gland-forming epithelial cells.

Break it down:

* adeno- = gland
* carcinoma = cancer from epithelial tissue

So rectal adenocarcinoma means:

> gland-forming epithelial cancer in the rectum.

---

### Core idea

The rectum stores stool right before defecation.

So if a tumor grows there, it can cause:

* blood in stool
* change in bowel habits
* narrower stools
* tenesmus
* constipation or obstruction if large

Tenesmus means the feeling that you still need to poop even after trying.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Bleeding:** tumor surface is fragile and ulcerates
* **Narrow stools:** tumor narrows the rectal lumen, meaning the inside channel
* **Tenesmus:** tumor irritates the rectal wall, making the body feel like stool is still there
* **Obstruction:** large tumor can block stool passage

---

### How it develops

Most colorectal cancers follow the adenoma-carcinoma sequence:

normal mucosa -> adenomatous polyp -> carcinoma

Adenoma means a benign gland-forming tumor.

The classic gene progression is:

* APC mutation first
* KRAS mutation
* p53 loss later

APC is a tumor suppressor gene, meaning it normally helps prevent uncontrolled cell growth.

---

### Important difference from colon cancer

Rectal cancer is anatomically close to:

* anal sphincters
* pelvic nerves
* bladder/prostate/uterus

So local invasion matters a lot.

This is why rectal cancer treatment often uses neoadjuvant chemoradiation, meaning chemotherapy and radiation given before surgery, to shrink the tumor and improve local control.

---

## One-line intuition

Rectal cancer is a gland-forming cancer in the stool-storage part of the gut, so it classically causes bleeding, stool-caliber changes, and tenesmus.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Rectal cancer can spread to the liver through the portal venous system, but very low rectal cancers can also drain to systemic veins, so they may spread to the lungs earlier than typical colon cancer.

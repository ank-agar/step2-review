An orbital fracture is a break in the bones around the eye socket.

The orbit = the bony socket that holds the eyeball, eye muscles, nerves, and fat.

Most high-yield type:

* orbital floor fracture
* also called a blowout fracture

---

### Core idea

Blunt trauma hits the eye area, pressure rises inside the orbit, and the thin orbital floor or medial wall breaks outward.

Think:

> eye gets punched → pressure has to escape → weakest wall breaks.

This can let orbital fat or extraocular muscles herniate into the sinus below.

Extraocular muscles = muscles that move the eye.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Diplopia (double vision):** inferior rectus muscle can get trapped, so the eye cannot move normally
* **Pain with eye movement:** trapped or swollen eye muscles hurt when they move
* **Enophthalmos:** the eye looks sunken because orbital contents shift backward/downward
* **Cheek/upper lip numbness:** infraorbital nerve injury
* **Periorbital bruising/swelling:** local trauma around the eye

Inferior rectus = the eye muscle that mainly pulls the eye downward.

Infraorbital nerve = sensory branch of CN V2 (cranial nerve 5, maxillary division) that gives sensation to the cheek, upper lip, and upper teeth.

---

### Important association

Orbital floor fractures can communicate with the maxillary sinus.

So patients are often told:

* do not blow the nose

because blowing the nose can push air from the sinus into the orbit and cause orbital emphysema.

Orbital emphysema = air trapped around the eye.

---

### One-line intuition

An orbital fracture is a broken eye socket wall, classically the thin floor, where trauma makes the orbit “blow out” into the sinus and can trap an eye muscle.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Child with eye trauma + nausea/vomiting + restricted eye movement but not much bruising = think trapdoor orbital fracture with muscle entrapment.

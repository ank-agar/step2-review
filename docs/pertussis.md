### Core idea

Pertussis = **whooping cough**, caused by **Bordetella pertussis**.

It is a **gram-negative coccobacillus**:

* gram-negative = has a thin peptidoglycan wall + outer membrane
* coccobacillus = short rod that looks partly round

It infects the **ciliated respiratory epithelium** (hair-like cells lining the airway that sweep mucus upward).

---

### Why the coughing happens

Bordetella pertussis attaches to airway cilia using adhesins, then releases toxins.

Main effect:

* cilia get damaged/paralyzed
* mucus cannot be cleared well
* airway gets irritated
* patient develops severe coughing fits

That is why pertussis causes **paroxysmal cough** (sudden intense coughing attacks).

The classic “whoop” happens because after a long coughing fit, the patient forcefully inhales through a narrowed/irritated airway.

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

* mild cold-like symptoms first
* then intense coughing spells
* post-tussive vomiting
* inspiratory whoop

Post-tussive vomiting = vomiting after coughing.

In infants, it can present more dangerously with:

* apnea
* cyanosis
* poor feeding

Apnea = pauses in breathing.  
Cyanosis = blue discoloration from low oxygen.

---

### Toxin mechanism

Pertussis toxin inactivates **Gi** (inhibitory G protein).

Normally:

* Gi inhibits adenylate cyclase
* adenylate cyclase makes cAMP (cyclic adenosine monophosphate)

So if pertussis toxin blocks Gi:

* adenylate cyclase is overactive
* cAMP increases

This contributes to immune cell dysfunction and the characteristic lymphocytosis.

Lymphocytosis = increased lymphocytes (a type of white blood cell).

---

## One-line intuition

Pertussis is a toxin-producing airway infection that disables the mucus-clearing cilia, so mucus/irritation builds up and causes violent coughing fits.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 pearl: **Bordetella pertussis causes lymphocytosis by increasing cAMP through Gi inhibition**.

Also remember prevention:

* DTaP = diphtheria, tetanus, acellular pertussis vaccine for children
* Tdap = tetanus, diphtheria, acellular pertussis booster for older children/adults

## Cirrhosis in pregnancy

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b6/Esophageal_varices_-_wale.jpg)

Cirrhosis in pregnancy means a pregnant patient already has cirrhosis (end-stage liver scarring with regenerative nodules).

Core issue:

* cirrhosis makes the liver stiff
* stiff liver blocks portal blood flow
* blocked portal blood flow causes portal hypertension (high pressure in the portal vein system)

Pregnancy adds extra stress because blood volume and cardiac output (blood pumped by the heart per minute) increase.

So pregnancy can worsen:

* varices (dilated veins, usually in the esophagus)
* ascites (fluid in the abdomen)
* hepatic encephalopathy (confusion from liver failure toxins, especially ammonia)
* coagulopathy (impaired clotting)
* liver decompensation (a previously stable liver becoming clinically worse)

---

### Why variceal bleeding is the scary one

The liver is scarred, so portal blood cannot flow through easily.

That blood gets redirected into collateral veins (backup venous pathways), especially around the esophagus.

During pregnancy:

* more blood volume
* more venous pressure
* more portal-system backup

= higher risk that esophageal varices rupture and bleed.

---

### Maternal and fetal risks

Maternal risks:

* GI (gastrointestinal) bleeding from varices
* worsening ascites
* hepatic encephalopathy
* postpartum hemorrhage (heavy bleeding after delivery) because clotting factors are made by the liver

Fetal risks:

* preterm birth
* fetal growth restriction
* stillbirth in severe maternal disease

---

### Management idea

These patients need high-risk OB (obstetrics) + hepatology (liver specialist) care.

Important screening:

* EGD (esophagogastroduodenoscopy; camera test of the esophagus, stomach, and duodenum) to look for varices
* treat high-risk varices with band ligation or beta blockers when appropriate

---

## One-line intuition

Pregnancy increases blood volume, and cirrhosis blocks portal blood flow, so the big danger is portal hypertension getting worse and causing variceal bleeding.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In cirrhosis, low platelets can be from splenomegaly (enlarged spleen) due to portal hypertension: blood backs up into the spleen, and the spleen sequesters platelets.

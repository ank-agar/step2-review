## Cervical spinal stenosis

Cervical spinal stenosis means narrowing of the spinal canal in the neck.

Break it down:

* cervical = neck region of the spine
* spinal canal = the bony tunnel that holds the spinal cord
* stenosis = narrowing

So the key idea is:

> the neck spinal canal gets too tight, so it can compress the spinal cord or nerve roots.

---

### Why symptoms happen

The cervical spinal cord carries signals to and from:

* arms
* legs
* hands
* bladder/bowel pathways

So if the cord is compressed, symptoms can be more widespread than just neck pain.

Common findings:

* **Neck pain:** degenerative changes irritate local joints/discs
* **Arm pain/numbness/weakness:** nerve roots are pinched as they exit the spine
* **Hand clumsiness:** spinal cord compression affects fine motor control
* **Gait problems/balance issues:** descending motor tracts to the legs are compressed
* **Hyperreflexia/spasticity:** upper motor neuron (UMN) pathways are affected

UMN (upper motor neuron) means the brain/spinal cord motor pathway before it reaches the peripheral nerve.

---

### Main causes

Usually from age-related degenerative changes:

* disc bulging
* osteophytes (bone spurs)
* thickened ligaments
* facet joint arthritis

These all take up extra space inside a canal that is supposed to leave room for the spinal cord.

Intuition:

The spinal canal is like a tunnel. If the walls thicken or a disc bulges inward, the tunnel gets smaller and the cord has less space.

---

### Important complication

If cervical stenosis compresses the spinal cord enough, it can cause cervical myelopathy.

Myelopathy = spinal cord dysfunction.

This is more serious than radiculopathy.

* radiculopathy = nerve root problem
* myelopathy = spinal cord problem

So:

* arm pain shooting down one dermatome = think radiculopathy
* hand clumsiness + gait imbalance + hyperreflexia = think myelopathy

Dermatome = skin area supplied by one spinal nerve root.

---

## One-line intuition

Cervical spinal stenosis is a tight neck spinal canal that can pinch the spinal cord, causing arm symptoms plus UMN signs in the legs.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 clue: cervical cord compression can cause **UMN signs below the lesion**, like hyperreflexia, spasticity, and Babinski sign, because the corticospinal tract is being compressed in the spinal cord.

### Core idea

Respiratory acidosis = the lungs are not blowing off enough CO2 (carbon dioxide), so CO2 builds up in the blood and makes the blood more acidic.

On ABG (arterial blood gas), think:

* pH low
* PaCO2 high

PaCO2 (partial pressure of arterial carbon dioxide) is basically how much CO2 is in arterial blood.

---

### Why CO2 makes acid

CO2 combines with water:

> CO2 + H2O -> H2CO3 -> H+ + HCO3-

Meaning:

* CO2 + water forms H2CO3 (carbonic acid)
* carbonic acid breaks into H+ (hydrogen ion) and HCO3- (bicarbonate)
* more H+ = lower pH = acidosis

So the intuition is:

> CO2 is an acid problem.

If you cannot breathe it out, you become acidotic.

---

### Common causes

Anything that causes hypoventilation (not breathing enough) can cause respiratory acidosis:

* COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease)
* opioid overdose
* severe asthma attack
* airway obstruction
* neuromuscular weakness, like Guillain-Barre syndrome or myasthenia gravis
* CNS (central nervous system) depression, like sedatives or brainstem injury

---

### Compensation

The kidneys try to help by keeping more HCO3- (bicarbonate), which is a base.

Acute respiratory acidosis:

* CO2 rises fast
* kidneys have not had time to compensate much
* pH is low

Chronic respiratory acidosis:

* CO2 has been high for days
* kidneys retain more HCO3-
* pH may be closer to normal

This is why a COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) patient can have high CO2 but a near-normal pH.

---

## One-line intuition

Respiratory acidosis is CO2 retention from poor ventilation, so CO2 turns into acid and drops the pH.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On ABG (arterial blood gas), respiratory acidosis is:

* pH < 7.35
* PaCO2 > 45 mmHg

HY trick: if pH and PaCO2 move in opposite directions, it is usually a primary respiratory problem.

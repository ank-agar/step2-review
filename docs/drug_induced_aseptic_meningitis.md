### Core idea

Drug-induced aseptic meningitis is meningitis caused by a medication, not by typical bacteria.

Break it down:

* **drug-induced** = triggered by a medication
* **aseptic** = no routine bacterial growth on CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) culture
* **meningitis** = inflammation of the meninges (protective layers around the brain and spinal cord)

So the idea is:

> A drug irritates or immune-activates the meninges, causing meningitis symptoms, but bacterial cultures are negative.

---

### Classic symptoms

Looks like meningitis:

* severe headache
* fever
* neck stiffness
* photophobia (light sensitivity)
* nausea/vomiting
* sometimes altered mental status

The key clue is timing:

* symptoms start after a new/recent medication
* symptoms improve after stopping that medication
* symptoms can recur if the drug is given again

---

### Common drug triggers

High-yield meds:

* NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs), especially ibuprofen
* TMP-SMX (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole)
* IVIG (intravenous immunoglobulin)
* monoclonal antibodies (lab-made antibodies used as targeted drugs)
* some intrathecal drugs (meds injected into the CSF space)

Important risk association:

* SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus) + NSAID exposure

---

### Why it happens

Two main mechanisms:

* **hypersensitivity reaction:** the immune system overreacts to the drug
* **direct meningeal irritation:** the drug irritates the meninges/CSF space

Reasoning:

The meninges sit in close contact with CSF (cerebrospinal fluid), so inflammation there gives meningitis symptoms:

* inflamed meninges hurt -> severe headache
* neck movement stretches inflamed meninges -> neck stiffness
* CNS (central nervous system) inflammation -> photophobia, nausea, confusion

---

### CSF findings

Lumbar puncture usually shows inflammatory CSF:

* increased WBCs (white blood cells)
* often increased protein
* usually normal glucose
* negative Gram stain/culture for typical bacteria

WBC (white blood cell) type can be neutrophilic or lymphocytic, so do not rely on that alone.

---

## One-line intuition

A medication makes the meninges angry, so the patient looks like they have meningitis, but bacterial cultures are negative and symptoms improve when the drug is stopped.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On Step 1, think **drug-induced aseptic meningitis** when meningitis symptoms appear after NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs), TMP-SMX (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole), or IVIG (intravenous immunoglobulin), especially with negative CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) bacterial cultures.

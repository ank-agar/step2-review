# Duodenal ulcer

### Core idea

A duodenal ulcer is an open sore in the duodenum, which is the first part of the small intestine right after the stomach.

So the location is:

* stomach makes acid
* acid passes through pylorus
* first part of duodenum gets injured
* a crater-like ulcer forms

---

### Main causes

Most duodenal ulcers are from:

* **H. pylori** (Helicobacter pylori, a spiral-shaped bacteria that colonizes the stomach)
* **NSAIDs** (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, like ibuprofen/naproxen)

Why H. pylori causes ulcers:

* H. pylori makes **urease** (enzyme that breaks urea into ammonia)
* ammonia helps it survive acid
* chronic inflammation weakens mucosal defense
* acid then damages the duodenal lining

Why NSAIDs cause ulcers:

* NSAIDs block prostaglandins
* prostaglandins normally increase mucus, bicarbonate, and blood flow
* less protection = acid injury

---

### Classic symptom pattern

Duodenal ulcer pain classically:

* is epigastric pain (upper middle abdominal pain)
* improves with meals
* returns a few hours after eating
* can wake the patient up at night

Why meals help:

Food temporarily buffers stomach acid, so less acid hits the ulcer for a little while.

---

### Complications

Important anatomy:

* **Anterior duodenal ulcer** can perforate
* **Posterior duodenal ulcer** can bleed from the gastroduodenal artery

Bleeding may cause:

* hematemesis (vomiting blood)
* melena (black, tarry stool from digested blood)
* anemia (low red blood cell count)

Perforation may cause:

* sudden severe abdominal pain
* free air under the diaphragm on upright chest x-ray

---

## One-line intuition

Duodenal ulcer = acid injury in the first part of the small intestine, usually because H. pylori or NSAIDs weakened the mucosal defenses.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Duodenal ulcer pain usually gets **better with food**, while gastric ulcer pain often gets **worse with food**.

# Loeffler syndrome

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/dpdx/ascariasis/images/6/Ascaris_larvae_tissue_400x_BAM1.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/080b/4587008/02674d6d5b05/LI-32-495-g001.jpg)

### Core idea

Loeffler syndrome is temporary eosinophilic pneumonia.

Meaning:

* eosinophils = allergy/parasite-fighting white blood cells
* pneumonia = inflammation/infection-like process in the lungs

So basically:

> something triggers eosinophils to enter the lungs, causing brief lung infiltrates.

---

### Classic cause

Most classically from helminths (parasitic worms), especially:

* Ascaris lumbricoides
* hookworm
* Strongyloides

The key Step 1 idea is larval migration.

Parasite eggs/larvae enter the body, then larvae migrate through the lungs before going back to the gastrointestinal tract (GI tract; stomach/intestines).

---

### Why the symptoms happen

When larvae pass through lung tissue, the immune system reacts with eosinophils.

That causes:

* dry cough
* wheezing
* mild fever sometimes
* transient pulmonary infiltrates on chest x-ray
* peripheral eosinophilia

Peripheral eosinophilia means increased eosinophils in the blood.

---

### Why it is transient

The larvae do not stay in the lungs permanently.

They pass through, irritate the lungs, then move on.

So the lung findings usually resolve on their own once the trigger is gone.

---

## One-line intuition

Loeffler syndrome = worms briefly travel through the lungs, and eosinophils chase them there.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, think:

* migrating helminth larvae
* cough/wheezing
* pulmonary infiltrates
* eosinophilia

This is a hypersensitivity-type lung reaction, not regular bacterial pneumonia.

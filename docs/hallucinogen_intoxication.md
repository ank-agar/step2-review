### Core idea

Hallucinogen intoxication = acute effects after taking a hallucinogen, causing distorted perception, mood, and thinking.

Classic drugs:

* LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide)
* psilocybin
* mescaline

Most classic hallucinogens stimulate 5-HT2A receptors (serotonin 2A receptors) in the brain.

---

### What you see

Common findings:

* hallucinations
* altered perception of time/space
* depersonalization (feeling detached from yourself)
* derealization (feeling like the world is unreal)
* anxiety or panic
* impaired judgment
* mydriasis (dilated pupils)
* tachycardia (fast heart rate)
* hypertension (high blood pressure)
* sweating
* tremor

---

### Why the findings happen

5-HT2A receptor stimulation increases cortical sensory signaling.

So the brain starts misprocessing sensory input:

* visual distortions
* intensified colors/sounds
* hallucinations
* altered sense of body/self

The sympathetic findings happen because hallucinogens can increase autonomic activation:

* dilated pupils
* fast heart rate
* sweating
* high blood pressure

---

### Treatment

Usually supportive:

* quiet room
* reassurance
* reduce stimulation

If severe agitation/panic:

* benzodiazepines

Benzodiazepines increase GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) activity, which calms CNS (central nervous system) excitation.

---

## One-line intuition

Hallucinogens overstimulate serotonin signaling in the cortex, so the brain misinterprets reality while the body looks mildly sympathetic.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Hallucinogen intoxication usually has **clear sensorium** compared with PCP (phencyclidine), which is more associated with violent behavior, nystagmus (involuntary eye movements), and severe dissociation.

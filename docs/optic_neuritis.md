# Optic neuritis

### Core idea

Optic neuritis = inflammation of the optic nerve, usually causing acute painful vision loss.

Break it down:

* optic = vision/optic nerve
* neuritis = nerve inflammation

So optic neuritis is basically:

> inflamed optic nerve → bad signal from eye to brain.

---

### Classic presentation

Usually:

* young adult
* unilateral vision loss
* pain with eye movement
* decreased color vision, especially red desaturation
* relative afferent pupillary defect, or RAPD (one pupil responds less when light is shined in the affected eye)

The eye itself may look normal on exam if the inflammation is behind the eyeball.

That is called retrobulbar neuritis:

* retro = behind
* bulbar = eyeball

So:

> the optic nerve is inflamed behind the eye, so the retina/fundus can look normal early.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The optic nerve is like the cable carrying visual information from the retina to the brain.

Inflammation damages myelin (insulation around nerve fibers), so the visual signal becomes slow/weak.

That causes:

* blurry or dim vision
* central scotoma (central blind spot)
* decreased color vision

Pain with eye movement happens because moving the eye can tug on the inflamed optic nerve/sheath.

---

### Important associations

Most Step 1 association:

* multiple sclerosis, or MS (autoimmune demyelinating disease of the central nervous system)

Also associated with:

* neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder, or NMOSD (autoimmune disease often affecting optic nerves and spinal cord)
* myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein antibody disease, or MOGAD (autoimmune demyelinating disease involving MOG antibodies)
* infections or systemic autoimmune disease, less commonly

---

## One-line intuition

Optic neuritis is demyelination/inflammation of the optic nerve, so the eye may be structurally okay but the visual cable to the brain is malfunctioning.

---

## Pearl

Optic neuritis is classically painful vision loss in a young adult and is strongly associated with multiple sclerosis, or MS.

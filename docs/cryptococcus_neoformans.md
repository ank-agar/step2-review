Cryptococcus neoformans is an encapsulated yeast fungus that classically causes meningitis in immunocompromised patients.

Big associations:

* AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome)
* low CD4 T cells (helper T cells)
* transplant/immunosuppression
* pigeon droppings

---

### Core idea

Think:

> inhaled yeast from bird droppings → lung infection → bloodstream spread → meningitis

It especially likes to cause CNS (central nervous system) disease, meaning brain/meninges involvement.

---

### Why it is dangerous

Cryptococcus has a thick polysaccharide capsule.

Polysaccharide = sugar-based coating.

Capsule = protective outer layer.

That capsule helps it:

* avoid phagocytosis (immune cells eating it)
* survive inside the host
* appear as a clear halo on India ink stain

So the classic lab image is:

> round budding yeast with a big clear halo.

---

### Classic presentation

Cryptococcal meningitis usually presents with:

* fever
* headache
* neck stiffness
* altered mental status
* increased opening pressure on lumbar puncture

Lumbar puncture = needle sampling of CSF (cerebrospinal fluid), the fluid around the brain and spinal cord.

---

### Diagnosis clues

High-yield tests:

* India ink stain: clear halo from capsule
* cryptococcal antigen test: detects capsule antigen
* mucicarmine stain: stains the capsule red
* urease positive: produces urease enzyme

Urease = enzyme that breaks urea into ammonia.

---

### Treatment idea

Severe cryptococcal meningitis is treated with:

* amphotericin B
* flucytosine
* then fluconazole maintenance

Do not overthink the drugs here:

* amphotericin B = strong broad antifungal
* flucytosine = added for synergy
* fluconazole = longer-term suppression

---

## One-line intuition

Cryptococcus neoformans is a pigeon-dropping-associated encapsulated yeast that uses its capsule to evade immune cells and classically causes meningitis in AIDS.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, if you see AIDS + meningitis + India ink halo, think Cryptococcus neoformans.

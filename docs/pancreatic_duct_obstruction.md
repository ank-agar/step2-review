### Core idea

Pancreatic duct obstruction means blockage of the pancreatic duct, which is the tube that drains pancreatic enzymes into the duodenum (first part of the small intestine).

So enzymes cannot flow out normally.

They back up inside the pancreas and can cause:

* pancreatic duct dilation
* pancreatic pain
* acute pancreatitis
* chronic pancreatitis over time

---

### Why obstruction causes pancreatitis

The pancreas makes digestive enzymes, but they are supposed to activate in the intestine.

If the duct is blocked:

* pancreatic juice cannot drain
* pressure builds up behind the blockage
* enzymes may activate too early inside the pancreas
* the pancreas starts digesting/injuring itself

That is the basic mechanism of pancreatitis.

---

### Common causes

Think: something blocks the outflow.

* gallstone stuck near the ampulla of Vater (where the common bile duct and pancreatic duct empty into the duodenum)
* pancreatic cancer, especially in the head of the pancreas
* chronic pancreatitis causing strictures (narrowed scarred segments)
* pancreatic duct stone
* sphincter of Oddi dysfunction (abnormal tightening of the valve controlling bile/pancreatic juice flow)

---

### Imaging intuition

If a duct is blocked, it dilates upstream.

Same idea as a traffic jam:

* blockage downstream
* fluid backs up upstream
* duct becomes wider

So on ultrasound, CT (computed tomography), MRCP (magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography), or ERCP (endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography), you may see a dilated pancreatic duct.

---

## One-line intuition

Pancreatic duct obstruction = pancreatic digestive juice is trapped, pressure rises, and the pancreas gets inflamed/injured.

---

## Pearl 🔥

A tumor in the head of the pancreas can obstruct both the common bile duct and pancreatic duct, causing the **double duct sign**: dilation of both ducts, which is concerning for pancreatic adenocarcinoma.

# Ankylosing spondylitis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/53/SI_joint.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b0/Bamboo_spine_ankylosing_spondylitis.jpg)

Ankylosing spondylitis is a chronic inflammatory arthritis that mainly affects the:

* sacroiliac joints (SI joints = joints between sacrum and ilium/pelvis)
* spine
* entheses (sites where tendons/ligaments attach to bone)

Break the name apart:

* ankylosing = stiffening/fusion
* spondylitis = inflammation of vertebrae/spine

So:

> inflammation of the spine that can eventually cause stiffness and fusion.

---

### Core idea

This is a seronegative spondyloarthropathy (arthritis group with negative rheumatoid factor, usually involving spine/entheses).

Classic patient:

* young man
* chronic low back pain
* morning stiffness
* improves with exercise
* worse with rest

That pattern matters because inflammatory back pain gets better when you move around.

---

### Why the findings happen

Inflammation starts around the SI joints and spine.

Over time, healing causes new bone formation between vertebrae:

* inflammation damages the joint/enthesis
* body repairs it with bone
* extra bone bridges vertebrae
* spine becomes stiff/fused

That gives the classic:

* bamboo spine on X-ray
* reduced spinal mobility
* reduced chest expansion if costovertebral joints (rib-spine joints) are involved

---

### Important associations

Strong association with:

* HLA-B27 (human leukocyte antigen B27, an immune-related genetic marker)
* anterior uveitis (inflammation of the front part of the eye)
* inflammatory bowel disease, meaning Crohn disease or ulcerative colitis
* psoriasis

---

## One-line intuition

Ankylosing spondylitis is inflammation at the pelvis-spine connection that heals with extra bone, slowly turning a flexible spine into a stiff fused spine.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Ankylosing spondylitis is **seronegative**, meaning rheumatoid factor (RF = rheumatoid factor) is usually negative, and it classically causes **back pain that improves with exercise**.

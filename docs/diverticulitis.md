## Diverticulitis

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17487/N00037-H_thumbnail.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/24390.jpg)

### Core idea

Diverticulitis = inflammation/infection of diverticula.

Break it down:

* diverticulum = one small outpouching of colon wall
* diverticula = multiple outpouchings
* diverticulosis = having diverticula, usually not inflamed
* diverticulitis = diverticula become inflamed/infected

So:

> diverticulosis is the pouch; diverticulitis is the angry infected pouch.

---

### Why it happens

Diverticula usually form where blood vessels penetrate the colon wall, because those are weak spots.

High pressure inside the colon pushes mucosa/submucosa outward through the muscular layer.

Then stool can get trapped inside a pouch:

* obstruction of the diverticulum
* bacterial overgrowth
* inflammation
* possible microperforation

Microperforation means a tiny leak/tear in the bowel wall, which is why severe cases can cause abscess or peritonitis (inflammation of the peritoneum, the lining of the abdominal cavity).

---

### Classic presentation

Most commonly:

* older patient
* LLQ pain (left lower quadrant pain)
* fever
* leukocytosis (high white blood cell count)
* change in bowel habits

Why LLQ?

Because diverticulitis most commonly affects the sigmoid colon, which sits in the left lower abdomen.

---

### Diagnosis intuition

CT (computed tomography) scan is commonly used because it can show:

* thickened colon wall
* inflamed surrounding fat
* abscess
* perforation

Colonoscopy is usually avoided during acute diverticulitis because inflamed bowel is more fragile, so scope trauma can increase perforation risk.

---

## One-line intuition

Diverticulitis is when a weak colon wall pouch gets blocked by stool, bacteria grow, and the pouch becomes inflamed/infected.

---

## HY pearl

Diverticulitis classically causes LLQ pain because it usually involves the sigmoid colon.

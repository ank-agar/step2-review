### Core idea

Acute laryngitis = short-term inflammation of the larynx (voice box), especially the vocal folds/vocal cords.

Most often it is from a viral URI (upper respiratory infection), like a cold.

---

### What you see clinically

Classic symptoms:

* hoarseness
* weak voice or temporary voice loss
* sore/raw throat
* dry cough
* sometimes fever if infectious

Acute usually means it lasts less than a few weeks.

---

### Why the voice gets hoarse

The vocal cords normally vibrate smoothly to make sound.

In laryngitis:

* inflammation makes them red/swollen
* mucus/secretions can coat them
* swollen cords cannot vibrate cleanly

So the sound becomes rough, weak, or absent.

Think: swollen guitar strings cannot vibrate normally.

---

### Common causes

* viral infection: most common
* voice overuse: yelling, singing, prolonged talking
* irritants: smoke, alcohol, fumes
* reflux: stomach acid irritating the larynx

Antibiotics usually do not help because most acute cases are viral, not bacterial.

---

### Treatment intuition

Main treatment is supportive:

* voice rest
* hydration
* humidified air
* avoid smoking/irritants
* treat reflux if that is the trigger

Whispering can still strain the vocal cords, so quiet voice/rest is better.

---

## One-line intuition

Acute laryngitis is a “cold/irritation of the voice box” where swollen vocal cords vibrate poorly, causing hoarseness.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Persistent hoarseness for more than 2-3 weeks, especially in a smoker, needs laryngoscopy (direct visualization of the larynx) to rule out vocal cord lesions or laryngeal cancer.

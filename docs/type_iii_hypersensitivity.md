## Type III hypersensitivity

![Image](https://open.oregonstate.education/app/uploads/sites/317/2026/01/Fig.-19.7.png)

### Core idea

Type III hypersensitivity = **immune complex deposition**.

Immune complexes means:

* antigen (the thing antibody binds)
* antibody, usually IgG (immunoglobulin G) or IgM (immunoglobulin M)

These antigen-antibody complexes float around in blood, then get stuck in tissues.

Common places:

* kidneys
* joints
* skin
* blood vessels

---

### Why tissue damage happens

The immune complex itself is not the main killer.

The damage happens because the complex:

* deposits in tissue
* activates complement (immune protein cascade)
* attracts neutrophils (acute inflammatory white blood cells)
* neutrophils release enzymes/free radicals
* nearby tissue gets injured

So think:

> immune complexes get stuck, complement calls neutrophils, neutrophils spill damage.

---

### Classic examples

Type III hypersensitivity shows up in:

* SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus)
* post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis
* serum sickness
* Arthus reaction
* rheumatoid arthritis, partly

Glomerulonephritis means inflammation of the glomeruli, which are the kidney filtering tufts.

---

### Arthus vs serum sickness

**Arthus reaction** = local type III reaction.

Example:

* injection site redness/swelling/pain after antigen exposure in someone with pre-existing IgG (immunoglobulin G)

**Serum sickness** = systemic type III reaction.

Classic symptoms:

* fever
* rash
* arthralgia (joint pain)
* sometimes proteinuria (protein in urine) from kidney involvement

---

## One-line intuition

Type III hypersensitivity is when antibody-antigen clumps get trapped in tissues and trigger complement + neutrophil inflammation.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Low C3 (complement component 3) can happen in immune-complex diseases because complement gets consumed while trying to clear the deposited complexes.

### Core idea

A short cervix means the cervix (the lower "neck" of the uterus that opens into the vagina) is shorter than expected during pregnancy.

Usually, this means:

* cervical length less than 25 mm (millimeters)
* measured by TVUS (transvaginal ultrasound, an ultrasound probe placed in the vagina)
* usually before 24 weeks of gestation

The cervix is supposed to stay long, firm, and closed during pregnancy.

If it becomes short too early, it is like the "plug/doorway" holding the pregnancy in is losing structural support.

---

### Why it matters

Short cervix increases risk of:

* cervical insufficiency (painless early cervical dilation)
* preterm birth (delivery before 37 weeks)
* second-trimester pregnancy loss in severe cases

Reasoning:

* Long cervix = more mechanical support
* Short cervix = less support
* Less support makes it easier for pressure from the pregnancy to open the cervix too early

---

### Ultrasound finding

You may see:

* shortened cervical canal
* funneling

Funneling means the internal os (opening from uterus into cervix) starts opening first, so the membranes bulge down into the cervical canal.

Think:

> the top of the cervix starts to open like a funnel, before the bottom fully opens.

---

### What helps prevent preterm birth

Common management ideas:

* vaginal progesterone for an incidentally found short cervix in a singleton pregnancy
* cerclage in selected high-risk patients

Progesterone helps keep the uterus quieter and supports cervical stability.

Cerclage means a stitch is placed around the cervix to help keep it closed.

---

## One-line intuition

A short cervix is an early weakening/shortening of the cervical "gate," so the pregnancy has a higher risk of coming out too soon.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association: short cervix is linked to cervical insufficiency, which classically causes painless cervical dilation in the second trimester.

## Persistent depressive disorder

![Image](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/-/scassets/images/org/health/articles/persistent-depressive-disorder-pdd)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/02/Neurotransmisores_afectados_durante_la_depresi%C3%B3n.png)

### Core idea

Persistent depressive disorder = chronic, long-lasting depressed mood.

Formerly called:

* dysthymia

Diagnostic intuition:

* depressed mood most of the day
* more days than not
* for at least 2 years in adults
* at least 1 year in children/adolescents

So it is less about a dramatic short crash and more about a long-term low baseline mood.

---

### Symptoms

Along with depressed mood, patients can have:

* poor appetite or overeating
* insomnia or hypersomnia (sleeping too little or too much)
* low energy/fatigue
* low self-esteem
* poor concentration
* hopelessness

Key distinction:

* MDD (major depressive disorder) = more intense depressive episode lasting at least 2 weeks
* PDD (persistent depressive disorder) = chronic depressive mood lasting years

---

### Why it feels different from MDD

In MDD (major depressive disorder), mood often drops sharply from a previous baseline.

In PDD (persistent depressive disorder), the person may feel like:

* “this is just how I am”
* low energy is normal
* hopelessness is part of their personality

That happens because the depressive state is chronic, so the brain adapts to a low mood baseline over time.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Depression involves abnormal mood regulation circuits, especially involving:

* serotonin (mood, sleep, appetite)
* norepinephrine (alertness, energy, concentration)
* dopamine (reward, motivation, pleasure)

So:

* low dopamine → anhedonia (loss of pleasure)
* low norepinephrine → low energy and poor concentration
* altered serotonin → mood, sleep, and appetite changes

---

## One-line intuition

Persistent depressive disorder is “low-grade depression that becomes the person’s long-term baseline.”

---

## Pearl 🔥

Double depression = PDD (persistent depressive disorder) with superimposed MDD (major depressive disorder).

Meaning:

* the patient is chronically depressed at baseline
* then has a more severe major depressive episode on top of it

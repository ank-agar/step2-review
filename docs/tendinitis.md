### Core idea

Tendinitis = inflammation of a tendon.

A tendon is the tough connective tissue that connects:

* muscle to bone

So tendinitis usually means:

* overuse
* irritation
* pain where the tendon is stressed

Common sites:

* Achilles tendon
* rotator cuff tendons
* patellar tendon
* elbow tendons
* wrist/thumb tendons

---

### Why it hurts

Repeated motion or strain causes tiny tendon injury.

That injury triggers inflammation:

* swelling
* tenderness
* pain with movement

The key idea:

> the tendon is the rope pulling on bone, and the rope gets irritated from too much repeated pulling.

---

### Symptoms

Usually:

* localized pain over the tendon
* worse with movement
* tenderness when pressing on it
* sometimes swelling or warmth

Example:

Achilles tendinitis hurts at the back of the heel because the Achilles tendon connects calf muscle to the calcaneus (heel bone).

---

### Tendinitis vs tendinosis

Important distinction:

* **tendinitis** = tendon inflammation
* **tendinosis** = chronic tendon degeneration without much inflammation

In real life, many chronic “tendinitis” cases are actually tendinosis.

So:

* acute overuse pain = more inflammatory
* chronic repetitive tendon pain = often degenerative

---

## One-line intuition

Tendinitis is an irritated, inflamed muscle-to-bone “rope” that hurts when you keep pulling on it.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Fluoroquinolones (antibiotics like ciprofloxacin) can cause tendinitis and even tendon rupture, classically involving the Achilles tendon.

### Core idea

A cutaneous abscess is a localized pocket of pus in the skin.

Cutaneous = skin.

Abscess = walled-off pus collection.

Pus is mostly:

* neutrophils
* dead tissue
* bacteria
* inflammatory debris

---

### Why it forms

Usually bacteria enter through:

* a hair follicle
* a small cut
* an insect bite
* skin trauma

The immune system sends neutrophils to kill the bacteria.

Neutrophils release enzymes and reactive oxygen species, which kill bacteria but also liquefy nearby tissue.

So you get a soft pocket of pus surrounded by inflamed skin.

That is why it is:

* red = increased blood flow from inflammation
* warm = increased blood flow
* painful = inflammatory mediators stimulate nerves
* swollen = fluid + pus buildup
* fluctuant = liquid pus moves under the skin

---

### Most common cause

The classic organism is **Staphylococcus aureus**.

Important subtype:

* MRSA (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus)

MRSA matters because it resists many beta-lactam antibiotics, like methicillin/nafcillin-type drugs.

---

### Treatment idea

The key treatment is usually:

* I&D (incision and drainage)

Why?

Because antibiotics often do not penetrate a walled-off pus pocket well.

So the main fix is to physically open it and drain the pus.

Antibiotics are added if there are systemic signs or higher-risk features, like fever, surrounding cellulitis, immunocompromise, or recurrent/severe infection.

---

## One-line intuition

A cutaneous abscess is the skin walling off bacteria into a painful pus pocket.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Abscess = neutrophils.

For Step 1, think **acute inflammation with liquefactive necrosis** because neutrophil enzymes digest tissue into pus.

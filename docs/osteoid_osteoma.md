### Core idea

Osteoid osteoma is a small benign bone-forming tumor.

Benign = non-cancerous.

It classically affects young males and usually occurs in long bones like:

* femur
* tibia

The key structure is the **nidus**.

Nidus = small central “nest” of tumor tissue.

In osteoid osteoma, the nidus makes osteoid.

Osteoid = unmineralized bone matrix, basically the soft collagen-rich material osteoblasts lay down before it becomes hard bone.

---

### Classic presentation

The classic clue is:

* bone pain worse at night
* pain relieved by NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs), especially aspirin or ibuprofen

This is very high-yield.

---

### Why NSAIDs help

The nidus makes lots of prostaglandins.

Prostaglandins = inflammatory pain-signaling molecules.

So the tumor is tiny, but it hurts a lot because it is chemically irritating the area.

NSAIDs block COX (cyclooxygenase), the enzyme needed to make prostaglandins.

So:

> less COX activity → less prostaglandin → less pain

That is why the pain can dramatically improve with NSAIDs.

---

### Imaging intuition

On imaging, think:

* small lucent nidus
* lots of surrounding sclerosis

Lucent = darker on X-ray/CT because it is less dense.

Sclerosis = dense reactive bone formation, which looks whiter.

So the bone is basically reacting to a tiny painful nidus by building a thick rim of dense bone around it.

CT (computed tomography) is especially good for seeing the nidus.

---

### Treatment idea

If symptoms are mild, NSAIDs can help.

If pain persists, the classic definitive treatment is radiofrequency ablation.

Radiofrequency ablation = using heat through a probe to destroy the nidus.

The goal is simple:

> kill the nidus → stop prostaglandin production → pain resolves

---

## One-line intuition

Osteoid osteoma is a tiny prostaglandin-producing bone nidus that causes severe night pain relieved by NSAIDs.

---

## Pearl 🔥

If the lesion is **>2 cm**, think **osteoblastoma**, not osteoid osteoma.

### Core idea

Allergic rhinitis = allergy-driven inflammation of the nasal mucosa (the lining inside the nose).

It is basically:

* allergen enters nose
* immune system overreacts
* mast cells release histamine
* nose becomes itchy, runny, sneezy, and congested

Common allergens:

* pollen
* dust mites
* mold
* animal dander

---

### Why the symptoms happen

This is a type I hypersensitivity reaction (IgE-mediated immediate allergic reaction).

Break it down:

* IgE (immunoglobulin E) antibodies sit on mast cells
* allergen binds/cross-links IgE
* mast cells degranulate, meaning they dump inflammatory chemicals
* histamine causes itching, mucus, sneezing, and swelling

So:

* **Sneezing/itching:** histamine stimulates sensory nerves
* **Runny nose:** glands make more mucus
* **Nasal congestion:** blood vessels dilate and nasal tissue swells
* **Watery/itchy eyes:** same allergic inflammation can affect conjunctiva (eye surface lining)

---

### Seasonal vs perennial

* **Seasonal:** happens during pollen seasons
* **Perennial:** happens year-round, often from dust mites, mold, or pets

---

## One-line intuition

Allergic rhinitis is your nose treating harmless airborne particles like dangerous invaders, so mast cells release histamine and make the nasal lining swollen, itchy, and mucus-filled.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Allergic rhinitis is part of the atopic triad:

* allergic rhinitis
* asthma
* atopic dermatitis (eczema)

HY link: all are associated with Th2 (T helper 2 cell) immune response, IgE, mast cells, and eosinophils.

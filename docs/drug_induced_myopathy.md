### Core idea

Drug-induced myopathy means muscle disease caused by a medication.

Myopathy = muscle dysfunction, usually causing:

* proximal muscle weakness
* muscle pain
* cramps
* elevated CK (creatine kinase; muscle enzyme released when muscle is damaged)

Most Step 1 association:

* statins

Statins = HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (cholesterol-lowering drugs).

---

### Why statins can hurt muscle

Statins block the mevalonate pathway.

That lowers cholesterol, but it can also reduce products that muscle cells need for normal membrane and mitochondrial function.

Mitochondria = ATP (adenosine triphosphate; cellular energy) production.

So the intuition is:

> less muscle-cell energy + unstable muscle membrane = muscle injury.

That is why patients can get:

* myalgias (muscle aches)
* weakness
* increased CK
* rarely rhabdomyolysis

Rhabdomyolysis = severe skeletal muscle breakdown that releases myoglobin (oxygen-binding muscle protein) into blood/urine and can cause AKI (acute kidney injury).

---

### Other drugs that can cause myopathy

Think of drugs that either directly injure muscle or mess with muscle metabolism:

* statins
* glucocorticoids
* alcohol
* antimalarials, like chloroquine
* colchicine
* antiretrovirals

Glucocorticoid myopathy is classically:

* painless proximal weakness
* normal or mildly elevated CK

because it is more muscle atrophy than muscle necrosis.

Necrosis = cell death from injury.

---

### Classic presentation

Usually:

* symmetric proximal weakness
* trouble climbing stairs
* trouble rising from a chair
* trouble lifting arms overhead

Proximal = closer to the trunk, like hips and shoulders.

Distal = farther from the trunk, like hands and feet.

So if the hip/shoulder muscles are weak, think myopathy.

---

### Statin autoimmune twist

Most statin muscle symptoms improve after stopping the statin.

But rare statin-associated autoimmune myopathy can persist even after stopping the drug.

Key association:

* anti-HMGCR antibodies

Anti-HMGCR antibodies = antibodies against HMG-CoA reductase, the statin target enzyme.

Intuition:

> regular statin myopathy = toxic/metabolic injury  
> anti-HMGCR myopathy = immune system keeps attacking muscle

---

## One-line intuition

Drug-induced myopathy is when a medication injures skeletal muscle, most famously statins causing proximal weakness, myalgias, and elevated CK.

---

## Pearl 🔥

If a statin patient has persistent proximal weakness and very high CK even after stopping the statin, think anti-HMGCR immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy.

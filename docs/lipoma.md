### Core idea

A lipoma is a benign tumor made of mature fat cells.

Benign means non-cancerous.

It usually sits in the subcutaneous tissue, meaning the fat layer just under the skin.

So the simple picture is:

> a soft, movable ball of fat under the skin.

---

### What it feels like

Classic lipoma:

* soft
* painless
* mobile
* slow-growing
* usually under the skin

Mobile means you can move it around a little when you press on it.

That happens because it is usually well-circumscribed, meaning it has a clear border from the surrounding tissue.

---

### Why it is soft and movable

It is made of adipocytes, meaning fat cells.

Fat is soft, so the mass feels soft/rubbery.

It is usually surrounded by a thin fibrous capsule, meaning a thin connective tissue covering.

That capsule helps separate it from nearby tissue, so it can feel like a distinct lump.

---

### Histology

On histology, meaning microscope appearance, lipoma shows:

* mature adipocytes
* minimal atypia
* sometimes thin fibrous septa

Atypia means abnormal-looking cells.

So lipoma looks like normal fat, but arranged as a discrete mass.

---

### Important distinction

Lipoma = benign fat tumor.

Liposarcoma = malignant fat tumor.

Malignant means cancerous.

Red flags that make doctors worry about something more serious:

* rapid growth
* pain
* firm/fixed mass
* deep location
* very large size

---

## One-line intuition

A lipoma is basically a harmless, encapsulated lump of mature fat under the skin.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Most lipomas are painless, but angiolipomas are often painful because they contain many small blood vessels.

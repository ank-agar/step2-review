### Core idea

A congenital pulmonary cyst is a cystic lung lesion present at birth.

The classic board-relevant version is **CPAM** (congenital pulmonary airway malformation), formerly called **CCAM** (congenital cystic adenomatoid malformation).

It means part of the fetal lung developed abnormally and formed cyst-like air spaces instead of normal alveoli (tiny air sacs for gas exchange).

---

### Why it happens

During fetal lung development, the branching airways do not mature normally.

So instead of forming normal bronchioles and alveoli, that region becomes:

* cystic
* disorganized
* poorly functional
* sometimes mass-like

Basically:

> bad lung branching during development = cystic chunk of lung.

---

### What it can cause

If large, the cystic lung tissue can compress nearby normal lung.

That can lead to:

* respiratory distress in a newborn
* recurrent pneumonia
* air trapping
* mediastinal shift, meaning the heart/trachea get pushed away from the lesion

If small, it can be found incidentally on prenatal ultrasound or later imaging.

---

### Important distinction

CPAM usually gets blood from the pulmonary circulation.

Pulmonary sequestration is different because it is abnormal lung tissue with systemic arterial blood supply, usually from the aorta.

That distinction is HY (high yield).

---

## One-line intuition

A congenital pulmonary cyst is a patch of fetal lung that branched wrong, so it becomes cystic useless lung tissue that can compress normal lung.

---

## Pearl 🔥

CPAM (congenital pulmonary airway malformation) is the classic congenital cystic lung lesion, while pulmonary sequestration is the one with abnormal systemic blood supply from the aorta.

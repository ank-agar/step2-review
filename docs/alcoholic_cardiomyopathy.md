## Alcoholic cardiomyopathy

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18070.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18136.jpg)

### Core idea

Alcoholic cardiomyopathy is a **dilated cardiomyopathy** (heart muscle disease where the ventricles become enlarged and weak) caused by long-term heavy alcohol use.

The heart becomes:

* dilated
* weak
* poor at squeezing blood forward

So it presents like **systolic heart failure** (heart failure due to weak contraction and reduced ejection fraction).

---

### Why alcohol causes this

Alcohol and its metabolite **acetaldehyde** (toxic breakdown product of alcohol) directly injure cardiac myocytes (heart muscle cells).

This causes:

* mitochondrial dysfunction (bad ATP/energy production)
* oxidative stress (free radical damage)
* impaired protein synthesis
* myocyte death

So the ventricle gets weak.

When the ventricle cannot squeeze well, blood stays inside it, volume builds up, and the chamber stretches out.

That is why alcoholic cardiomyopathy becomes a **dilated** cardiomyopathy.

---

### What it looks like clinically

Same as other causes of congestive heart failure:

* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* fatigue
* peripheral edema (leg swelling)
* pulmonary edema (fluid in lungs)
* S3 heart sound (extra heart sound from volume overload)
* arrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms)

---

## One-line intuition

Chronic alcohol poisons heart muscle cells, so the ventricle gets weak, stretches out, and cannot pump well.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Alcoholic cardiomyopathy is potentially partially reversible with alcohol abstinence, especially before severe permanent remodeling occurs.

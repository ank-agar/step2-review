### Core idea

Oral thrush is **oral candidiasis** (fungal infection of the mouth by Candida, usually Candida albicans).

It causes white, creamy plaques on:

* tongue
* cheeks
* palate
* gums

Classic clue:

* white plaques that can be scraped off
* leaving red, irritated, sometimes bleeding mucosa underneath

---

### Why it happens

Candida normally lives in the mouth in small amounts.

Thrush happens when Candida overgrows because local defenses are weakened.

Common causes:

* antibiotics: kill normal bacterial flora, so Candida can overgrow
* inhaled corticosteroids: local immunosuppression in the mouth
* HIV/AIDS (human immunodeficiency virus/acquired immunodeficiency syndrome): low T-cell immunity
* diabetes mellitus: high glucose helps fungal growth
* chemotherapy or immunosuppression
* dentures
* newborns

---

### Why the plaques are white

The white stuff is basically a superficial layer of:

* Candida organisms
* dead epithelial cells
* inflammatory debris

That is why it looks like “cottage cheese” and can often be wiped/scraped away.

---

### Treatment idea

Usually treated with antifungals, like:

* nystatin swish and swallow
* fluconazole if more severe

Also fix the trigger:

* rinse mouth after inhaled steroids
* control diabetes
* evaluate for immunosuppression if recurrent/severe

---

## One-line intuition

Oral thrush = Candida takes over the mouth surface when normal immune/flora control is weakened.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In a Step 1 vignette, **white oral plaques that scrape off** strongly suggests Candida; **oral hairy leukoplakia** (EBV = Epstein-Barr virus) is also white but classically **does not scrape off** and is on the lateral tongue.

### Core idea

Duodenitis = inflammation of the duodenum (first part of the small intestine, right after the stomach).

Think:

* stomach acid/irritants leave the stomach
* they hit the duodenal lining
* the lining gets inflamed, swollen, red, and sometimes eroded/ulcerated

---

### Common causes

Most high-yield causes:

* **H. pylori** (Helicobacter pylori, bacteria associated with gastritis and peptic ulcers)
* **NSAIDs** (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, like ibuprofen/naproxen)
* excess acid exposure
* celiac disease sometimes
* Crohn disease sometimes

---

### Why symptoms happen

Inflamed duodenal mucosa (inner lining) is irritated by acid and digestive enzymes, so patients can get:

* epigastric pain (upper middle abdominal pain)
* nausea
* bloating
* GI bleeding (gastrointestinal bleeding) if erosions/ulcers form

If it becomes ulcer-like, pain may improve briefly with food/antacids because food buffers acid.

---

### Pathology idea

On biopsy, duodenitis shows inflammatory cells in the duodenal mucosa.

Acute inflammation often means neutrophils (acute inflammatory white blood cells).

Chronic/peptic duodenitis can show:

* villous blunting
* Brunner gland hyperplasia
* gastric metaplasia

Brunner glands make alkaline mucus, so they enlarge as a protective response against acid.

---

## One-line intuition

Duodenitis is basically “gastritis, but in the first part of the small intestine,” usually from acid injury, H. pylori, or NSAIDs.

---

## Pearl 🔥

H. pylori most classically causes duodenal ulcers by increasing acid delivery to the duodenum, which injures the mucosa and can lead to peptic duodenitis.

## Subarachnoid hemorrhage

### Core idea

Subarachnoid hemorrhage is bleeding into the subarachnoid space.

Break it down:

* subarachnoid = below the arachnoid mater
* hemorrhage = bleeding
* arachnoid mater = middle meningeal layer covering the brain
* pia mater = thin layer stuck directly onto the brain

So the blood is between:

* arachnoid mater
* pia mater

This space normally contains CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) and major cerebral arteries.

---

### Classic cause

Most classic Step 1 cause:

* ruptured berry aneurysm

A berry aneurysm is a small outpouching of a cerebral artery, usually around the circle of Willis (arterial ring at the base of the brain).

When it ruptures, arterial blood spills into the CSF-filled subarachnoid space.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Thunderclap headache:** sudden arterial bleeding irritates the meninges hard and fast
* **Neck stiffness/photophobia:** blood in CSF irritates meninges, like meningitis does
* **Blood in basal cisterns/sulci on CT (computed tomography):** blood spreads through CSF spaces around the brain
* **Xanthochromia on LP (lumbar puncture):** RBCs (red blood cells) break down in CSF and make it yellow
* **Vasospasm:** blood products irritate arteries, making them constrict and potentially cause ischemia (low blood flow injury)

---

### One-line intuition

Subarachnoid hemorrhage is a ruptured brain artery leaking blood into the CSF space around the brain, causing a sudden “worst headache of life.”

---

## HY pearl 🔥

If CT (computed tomography) is negative but suspicion is high, do LP (lumbar puncture): xanthochromia supports subarachnoid hemorrhage.

### Core idea

Radiculopathy means a nerve root problem.

Break it down:

* radiculo- = nerve root
* -pathy = disease/problem

So radiculopathy = disease or irritation of a spinal nerve root.

A nerve root is the part of a nerve right as it leaves the spinal cord.

---

### What happens

Something compresses or irritates the nerve root, commonly:

* herniated disc
* bone spur
* foraminal stenosis (narrowing of the opening where the nerve exits)

That nerve root then sends abnormal signals down the area it supplies.

So symptoms can include:

* sharp/shooting pain
* numbness
* tingling
* weakness
* decreased reflexes

---

### Why pain travels down the arm or leg

Each nerve root supplies a predictable body region called a dermatome (skin sensory region supplied by one spinal nerve root).

So if the C6 nerve root is irritated, symptoms can shoot into a C6-type arm/thumb distribution.

If the L5 or S1 nerve root is irritated, symptoms can shoot down the leg, often called sciatica (radiating pain along sciatic nerve-type distribution).

---

### Cervical vs lumbar

* Cervical radiculopathy = nerve root problem in the neck
  * pain/tingling/weakness radiates into shoulder/arm/hand

* Lumbar radiculopathy = nerve root problem in the low back
  * pain/tingling/weakness radiates into buttock/leg/foot

---

## One-line intuition

Radiculopathy is a "pinched nerve root," so the symptoms show up downstream in that nerve root's territory.

---

## Pearl 🔥

A herniated disc usually compresses the traversing nerve root, not the exiting nerve root.

Example: L4-L5 disc herniation usually hits the L5 nerve root.

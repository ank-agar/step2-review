Malignant otitis externa = severe infection of the external auditory canal (EAC, ear canal) that spreads into the temporal bone/skull base.

Despite the name, **malignant does not mean cancer here**.

It means dangerous/aggressive.

It is also called necrotizing otitis externa.

### Core idea

Regular otitis externa is infection of ear canal skin.

Malignant otitis externa is when that infection keeps spreading deeper:

* external auditory canal (ear canal)
* cartilage/bone junction
* temporal bone
* skull base

So it becomes skull base osteomyelitis (bone infection at the base of the skull).

### Classic patient

Think:

* older patient
* diabetes mellitus
* immunocompromised patient
* severe ear pain, especially at night
* ear drainage
* granulation tissue in the external auditory canal

Granulation tissue = new inflamed healing tissue, often red/friable, that forms during chronic inflammation.

### Why diabetes is a risk

Diabetes causes:

* weaker immune response
* poorer blood supply from small vessel disease
* easier bacterial invasion

So an ear canal infection can spread into deeper tissues and bone instead of staying superficial.

### Most common bug

Most common cause:

* Pseudomonas aeruginosa

That is why treatment needs antipseudomonal antibiotics (antibiotics that cover Pseudomonas), often for a long time.

### Why cranial nerve problems can happen

The infection is near the skull base, where cranial nerves (nerves that come directly from the brain/brainstem) exit.

So spread can cause cranial nerve palsies (weakness or dysfunction of a cranial nerve), especially:

* CN VII (cranial nerve 7, facial nerve) -> facial weakness

That is a bad sign because it means deeper spread.

## One-line intuition

Malignant otitis externa is basically swimmer's ear that becomes a skull base bone infection, classically in an elderly diabetic patient with severe persistent ear pain.

## Pearl 🔥

Step 1 clue: **diabetic + severe nocturnal ear pain + Pseudomonas + granulation tissue in the ear canal = malignant otitis externa**, not simple otitis externa.

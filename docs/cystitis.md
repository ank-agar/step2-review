### Core idea

Cystitis = inflammation of the bladder.

Most commonly, it means a lower UTI (urinary tract infection) where bacteria infect the bladder.

Most common bug:

* E. coli

because E. coli normally lives in the GI tract (gastrointestinal tract), then can travel from the perineal area (area between genitals and anus) into the urethra (tube that carries urine out).

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Inflamed bladder wall becomes irritated, so you get:

* dysuria (pain or burning with urination)
* urinary frequency (peeing often)
* urgency (sudden need to pee)
* suprapubic pain (pain above the pubic bone)
* sometimes hematuria (blood in urine)

Why frequency/urgency?

The bladder is irritated, so it sends "I need to empty" signals even when it is not full.

Why burning?

Inflamed urinary lining gets exposed to acidic urine, which hurts.

---

### Why females get it more

Females have a shorter urethra, and the urethra is closer to the anus.

So bacteria have a shorter trip:

anus/perineum -> urethra -> bladder

---

### Important distinction

Cystitis = bladder infection/inflammation.

Pyelonephritis = kidney infection.

Think:

* cystitis: dysuria, frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain
* pyelonephritis: fever, chills, flank pain, nausea/vomiting

Flank pain or fever means the infection may have gone higher up to the kidney.

---

## One-line intuition

Cystitis is basically an irritated, inflamed bladder, usually from E. coli climbing up the urethra.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Nitrite-positive urine suggests gram-negative rods like E. coli, because many gram-negative bacteria convert nitrate to nitrite.

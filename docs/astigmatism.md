### Core idea

Astigmatism is a refractive error (problem bending light) where the cornea (clear front surface of the eye) or lens (focusing structure inside the eye) is curved unevenly.

Instead of being perfectly round like a basketball, it is more curved in one direction, like a football.

So light does **not** focus into one clean point on the retina (back light-sensing layer of the eye).

It focuses at multiple points/lines, causing:

* blurry vision
* distorted vision
* glare or streaking around lights
* eye strain/headache

---

### Why vision gets blurry

Normal eye:

* light rays bend evenly
* they meet at one sharp point on the retina

Astigmatism:

* one axis of light bends more than another axis
* vertical and horizontal light rays may focus at different depths
* the retina receives a smeared image instead of a sharp one

That is why someone may see letters, lights, or edges as stretched/ghosted instead of simply “out of focus.”

---

### Easy intuition

Think of the cornea like a camera lens.

If the lens is evenly curved, the image focuses sharply.

If the lens is warped in one direction, the image gets stretched or smeared.

That warped-lens effect = astigmatism.

---

### Treatment

Astigmatism is corrected with:

* glasses
* contact lenses
* toric lenses (contacts shaped to correct astigmatism)
* LASIK (laser-assisted in situ keratomileusis) or other refractive surgery in some patients

The correction works by adding the opposite curvature, so light gets refocused into one point again.

---

## One-line intuition

Astigmatism is when the eye’s focusing surface is unevenly curved, so light gets focused into a smear instead of one sharp point.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Astigmatism commonly occurs with myopia (nearsightedness) or hyperopia (farsightedness), so prescriptions often include a **cylinder** value and an **axis** value to describe how much astigmatism there is and which direction it points.

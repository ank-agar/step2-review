# Tardive dyskinesia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Basal_ganglia_circuits.svg/1280px-Basal_ganglia_circuits.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Dopamine_pathways.svg/1280px-Dopamine_pathways.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Tetrabenazine.svg/1920px-Tetrabenazine.svg.png)

Tardive dyskinesia is involuntary, repetitive movements that happen after long-term dopamine-blocking drugs.

Break it down:

* **tardive** = delayed/late
* **dyskinesia** = abnormal movement

So:

> late-onset abnormal movements after chronic dopamine blockade.

Classic cause:

* antipsychotics, especially typical antipsychotics like haloperidol

These block **D2 receptors** (dopamine type 2 receptors), especially in the nigrostriatal pathway (dopamine pathway from substantia nigra to striatum that controls movement).

---

### What it looks like

Usually repetitive movements of the mouth/face:

* lip smacking
* tongue protrusion
* chewing movements
* grimacing
* blinking

Can also involve:

* fingers
* arms/legs
* trunk

---

### Why it happens

Long-term D2 receptor blockade makes the brain compensate by increasing dopamine receptor sensitivity.

So when dopamine signal gets through, the motor system overreacts.

That causes extra unwanted movements.

Simple intuition:

* chronic dopamine blockade = brain becomes dopamine-sensitive
* dopamine-sensitive basal ganglia = abnormal involuntary movements

---

### High-yield distinction

Tardive dyskinesia is a **late** extrapyramidal symptom.

**EPS** (extrapyramidal symptoms) = antipsychotic movement side effects from dopamine blockade in the basal ganglia.

Timing matters:

* acute dystonia = hours to days
* akathisia = days to weeks
* parkinsonism = weeks
* tardive dyskinesia = months to years

---

### Treatment idea

Use a **VMAT2 inhibitor** (vesicular monoamine transporter 2 inhibitor), like:

* valbenazine
* deutetrabenazine
* tetrabenazine

VMAT2 normally loads dopamine into vesicles for release.

Blocking VMAT2 decreases dopamine release, which helps calm the excessive involuntary movements.

---

## One-line intuition

Tardive dyskinesia is the brain becoming too sensitive to dopamine after long-term dopamine blockade, causing delayed abnormal movements, especially lip smacking and tongue movements.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Tardive dyskinesia can be irreversible, so antipsychotics should be used at the lowest effective dose and patients should be monitored for abnormal mouth/tongue movements.

## Marfan syndrome

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/9611.jpg)

![Image](https://dermnetnz.org/assets/Uploads/Marfan-syndrome.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Ectopia_Lentis_in_Marfan_syndrome.jpg/960px-Ectopia_Lentis_in_Marfan_syndrome.jpg)

### Core idea

Marfan syndrome is an **autosomal dominant** (one mutated copy is enough) connective tissue disease caused by mutation in **FBN1** (fibrillin-1 gene).

Fibrillin helps make elastic connective tissue strong.

So defective fibrillin = weak stretchy tissues, especially in:

* aorta
* eye lens
* skeleton

---

### Classic findings

* Tall, thin body habitus
* Long limbs and fingers = **arachnodactyly** (spider-like fingers)
* Pectus excavatum/carinatum (sunken chest/protruding chest)
* Hypermobile joints
* Scoliosis (curved spine)
* **Ectopia lentis** (dislocated lens of the eye)
* Aortic root dilation (widening of the first part of the aorta)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Long limbs/fingers:** abnormal connective tissue affects skeletal growth
* **Ectopia lentis:** weak zonular fibers (tiny fibers holding the lens in place) let the lens shift
* **Aortic root dilation:** weak aortic wall stretches under pressure
* **Aortic dissection risk:** once the aorta dilates, blood can tear into the wall layers

The dangerous part is the heart/aorta, not the tall appearance.

---

### Key mechanism

FBN1 mutation causes abnormal fibrillin.

Fibrillin normally helps:

* support elastic tissue
* regulate **TGF-beta** (transforming growth factor beta), a growth/signaling protein

So Marfan causes weak connective tissue + excess abnormal growth signaling.

---

## One-line intuition

Marfan = bad fibrillin, so elastic tissues stretch too much: long body, loose lens, dilated aorta.

# Penetrating chest wound

A penetrating chest wound means something pierces through the chest wall.

Examples:

* stab wound
* gunshot wound
* shrapnel injury
* broken rib fragment puncturing inward

The danger is that the object can enter the thorax (chest cavity) and injure:

* lung
* pleura (lining around the lung)
* heart
* great vessels (large vessels like the aorta, vena cava, or pulmonary arteries)
* diaphragm (muscle separating chest from abdomen)

---

### Core idea

The chest normally works like a sealed negative-pressure box.

Negative pressure means pressure inside the chest is lower than outside air, which helps pull the lungs open during inspiration (breathing in).

If a penetrating wound creates a hole in that box, air or blood can enter the pleural space (space between the lung and chest wall).

That can cause:

* pneumothorax (air in the pleural space)
* hemothorax (blood in the pleural space)
* hemopneumothorax (blood and air in the pleural space)
* cardiac tamponade (blood/fluid compressing the heart in the pericardial sac)

---

### Sucking chest wound

If the wound is open to the outside, air can move through the chest wall wound.

This is called an open pneumothorax, also called a sucking chest wound.

Why "sucking"?

During inspiration:

* the chest expands
* pressure inside the thorax becomes more negative
* air gets pulled through the wound instead of through the trachea (windpipe)

So the injured lung does not inflate properly.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Dyspnea (shortness of breath):** the lung may collapse or be compressed
* **Decreased breath sounds:** less air is moving into that lung
* **Pleuritic chest pain:** pleura irritation causes sharp pain worse with breathing
* **Hypotension:** low blood pressure can happen from blood loss or cardiac tamponade
* **Tracheal deviation:** in tension pneumothorax, trapped air pushes the mediastinum (middle chest structures) away
* **JVD (jugular venous distension):** neck veins distend if blood cannot return to the heart, classically in tension pneumothorax or tamponade

---

### Big complication: tension pneumothorax

Tension pneumothorax = air enters the pleural space but cannot escape.

It becomes a one-way valve problem:

* more air enters with each breath
* pressure rises
* lung collapses
* mediastinum shifts
* venous return to the heart drops
* obstructive shock develops

Obstructive shock means shock from blocked blood flow back to or through the heart.

---

### Treatment idea

In real life, this is an emergency.

The basic idea is:

* seal the open chest wound
* give oxygen
* decompress trapped air if tension pneumothorax is suspected
* place a chest tube to drain air/blood
* surgery if the heart, great vessels, or ongoing bleeding are involved

Chest tube = tube placed into the pleural space to drain air or blood so the lung can re-expand.

---

## One-line intuition

A penetrating chest wound pokes a hole in the sealed chest box, so air/blood can enter the pleural space and collapse the lung or compress the heart/great vessels.

---

## Pearl

For trauma, think **penetrating chest wound = pneumothorax, hemothorax, and cardiac tamponade until proven otherwise**.

Classic tension pneumothorax findings: hypotension, JVD (jugular venous distension), absent breath sounds, and tracheal deviation away from the affected side.

### Core idea

V/Q mismatch means ventilation and perfusion are not matched.

* V = ventilation, meaning air reaching the alveoli (air sacs)
* Q = perfusion, meaning blood reaching the alveolar capillaries (tiny lung blood vessels)

So the lung has air and blood, but they are not meeting in the right proportions.

Normal V/Q is about 0.8.

---

### Intuition

Gas exchange needs two things in the same place:

* air bringing O2 (oxygen)
* blood picking up that O2

If air gets there but blood does not, the air is wasted.

If blood gets there but air does not, the blood is wasted.

Either way, arterial O2 drops because some blood leaves the lung poorly oxygenated.

---

### High V/Q

High V/Q means:

* ventilation is present
* perfusion is too low

Classic example:

* pulmonary embolism, meaning a clot blocking blood flow in the lung

Think:

> air is entering the alveolus, but blood cannot reach it.

This is dead space, meaning ventilated lung that is not perfused.

So:

* V is normal or high relative to Q
* Q is low
* V/Q goes up

---

### Low V/Q

Low V/Q means:

* perfusion is present
* ventilation is too low

Examples:

* asthma
* chronic bronchitis
* pneumonia
* pulmonary edema, meaning fluid in the lungs

Think:

> blood reaches the alveolus, but not enough air gets in.

This acts like a shunt, meaning blood passes through the lung without getting properly oxygenated.

So:

* V is low
* Q is relatively preserved
* V/Q goes down

---

### Why hypoxemia happens

Hypoxemia means low oxygen in arterial blood.

In V/Q mismatch, some lung units oxygenate blood well, but other units oxygenate blood poorly.

The poorly oxygenated blood mixes with the well oxygenated blood, so the final arterial PaO2 (arterial oxygen partial pressure) drops.

CO2 (carbon dioxide) usually does not rise early because the patient can breathe faster and blow off CO2.

---

### A-a gradient

V/Q mismatch causes an increased A-a gradient.

A-a gradient means alveolar-arterial gradient, which compares:

* oxygen in the alveoli
* oxygen in arterial blood

If alveolar oxygen is okay but arterial oxygen is low, the problem is gas exchange.

That is why V/Q mismatch increases the A-a gradient.

---

## One-line intuition

V/Q mismatch is air and blood failing to line up, so some lung units waste air and others waste blood.

---

## HY pearl

V/Q mismatch usually improves with supplemental O2 (oxygen), but a true right-to-left shunt responds poorly because blood is bypassing ventilated alveoli.

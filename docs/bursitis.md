## Bursitis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/Elbow_-_Inflammation_of_the_bursa.svg/1280px-Elbow_-_Inflammation_of_the_bursa.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f4/3D_still_showing_Bursitis.jpg)

Bursitis = inflammation of a bursa (a small fluid-filled sac that reduces friction between tendons, bones, muscles, and skin).

Think of a bursa like a tiny cushion or glide pad near a joint.

When it gets irritated:

* it fills with extra fluid
* it swells
* it becomes painful with movement or pressure

---

### Common locations

* Shoulder
* Elbow, especially olecranon bursa (bursa over the point of the elbow)
* Hip, especially trochanteric bursa (bursa near the greater trochanter of the femur)
* Knee

---

### Why it happens

Usually from:

* repetitive motion
* prolonged pressure, like leaning on elbows
* trauma
* infection
* crystal disease, like gout (urate crystal deposition)

So the basic idea is:

> too much irritation → inflamed bursa → swollen painful cushion

---

### Symptoms

Classically:

* localized joint-area pain
* swelling
* tenderness
* pain with movement or direct pressure

If it is septic bursitis (infected bursa), you may also see:

* warmth
* redness
* fever

---

## One-line intuition

Bursitis is when the joint’s friction-reducing cushion gets inflamed and swollen, so movement or pressure hurts.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Olecranon bursitis is classically associated with repeated elbow pressure, so it is sometimes called “student’s elbow.”

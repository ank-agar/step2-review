### Core idea

Factitious disorder imposed on another means:

* a caregiver intentionally fakes, exaggerates, or causes illness in someone else
* then presents that person as sick
* without an obvious external reward

The victim is usually a child, elderly person, or disabled person.

Old name:

* Munchausen syndrome by proxy

Factitious = artificially produced.

Imposed on another = the illness deception is done through another person.

---

### The key Step 1 distinction

The diagnosis is given to the caregiver, not the victim.

The victim may actually look sick because the caregiver may:

* lie about symptoms
* contaminate urine or blood samples
* give drugs/toxins
* withhold food or treatment
* repeatedly seek unnecessary procedures

So this is also a form of abuse.

---

### Why it happens

The caregiver wants to occupy the role of:

* devoted caregiver
* medically knowledgeable parent
* heroic helper

The psychological gain is attention, sympathy, or closeness to medical staff.

That is why there is no clear external reward like money, avoiding jail, or getting drugs.

---

### Classic clues

Think:

* many hospital visits
* lots of negative workups
* symptoms only happen when caregiver is present
* symptoms improve when the victim is separated from caregiver
* caregiver seems unusually calm or eager about invasive testing
* caregiver knows lots of medical terminology

---

### Compare with related disorders

* Factitious disorder imposed on self = person fakes illness in themselves for psychological attention
* Factitious disorder imposed on another = person fakes illness in someone else
* Malingering = faking illness for external reward, like money, opioids, avoiding work, or avoiding prison
* Somatic symptom disorder = symptoms are not intentionally faked; patient is genuinely distressed

---

## One-line intuition

The caregiver creates a “sick patient” out of someone else so the caregiver can receive attention through the medical system.

---

## Pearl 🔥

If the stem says the child’s symptoms disappear in the hospital or when separated from the parent, think factitious disorder imposed on another.

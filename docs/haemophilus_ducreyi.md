# Haemophilus ducreyi

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Ulcers%2C_fissures%2C_and_erosions.svg/960px-Ulcers%2C_fissures%2C_and_erosions.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Haemophilus_ducreyi_01.jpg/960px-Haemophilus_ducreyi_01.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c2/Chancroid_lesion_haemophilus_ducreyi_PHIL_3728_lores.jpg)

**Haemophilus ducreyi** is a gram-negative coccobacillus (small bacterium shaped between a sphere and a rod) that causes **chancroid**.

Chancroid = a sexually transmitted infection (STI, infection spread by sexual contact) that causes:

* painful genital ulcers
* soft, ragged ulcer edges
* purulent exudate (pus-like drainage)
* painful inguinal lymphadenopathy (swollen groin lymph nodes)

---

### Core idea

Think:

> H. **ducreyi** = “do cry” because the ulcer is painful.

This is the big Step 1 contrast:

* **Chancroid:** painful ulcer, caused by Haemophilus ducreyi
* **Syphilis:** painless chancre, caused by Treponema pallidum

---

### Why the ulcer is painful

The bacteria infect small breaks in genital skin/mucosa (moist lining tissue).

Then they cause local inflammation and tissue necrosis (cell death), so the lesion becomes:

* tender
* ulcerated
* friable (bleeds easily)
* pus-covered

So the pain is from active inflammation + tissue destruction.

---

### Lymph node clue

H. ducreyi can spread locally to groin lymph nodes.

That causes painful inguinal lymphadenitis (inflamed groin lymph nodes), sometimes forming a **bubo** (large, tender, pus-filled lymph node swelling).

---

### Lab clue

On Gram stain, Haemophilus ducreyi can appear in a:

* “school of fish” pattern
* “railroad track” pattern

It is also fastidious (hard to grow because it needs special culture conditions).

---

## One-line intuition

Haemophilus ducreyi is the “painful soft chancre” bacteria: painful genital ulcer + painful groin nodes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Haemophilus ducreyi requires **factor X** (hemin, an iron-containing growth factor), but not factor V (NAD, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide).

### Core idea

Impetigo is a superficial bacterial skin infection.

Classically:

* common in kids
* very contagious
* often around the mouth/nose
* causes honey-colored crusts

Main bugs:

* Staphylococcus aureus
* Streptococcus pyogenes, also called group A strep (GAS)

---

### Why the honey-colored crust happens

The bacteria infect the top layer of skin.

That causes:

* small vesicles (tiny fluid-filled blisters)
* pustules (pus-filled bumps)
* rupture of those lesions
* dried serum/pus on the surface

That dried fluid forms the classic golden-yellow, honey-colored crust.

---

### Two main forms

**Nonbullous impetigo**

* most common
* usually Staphylococcus aureus or Streptococcus pyogenes
* small vesicles/pustules rupture and crust

**Bullous impetigo**

* caused by Staphylococcus aureus
* makes larger bullae (big blisters)
* due to exfoliative toxin, which breaks keratinocyte adhesion in the epidermis (top skin layer)

Break the word apart:

* bullous = blister-forming
* exfoliative toxin = toxin that makes superficial skin cells separate

---

### One-line intuition

Impetigo is basically a contagious, superficial bacterial infection where tiny blisters break open and dry into honey-colored crusts.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Bullous impetigo is caused by Staphylococcus aureus exfoliative toxin targeting desmoglein 1 (a skin cell adhesion protein), similar mechanism to staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome, but localized.

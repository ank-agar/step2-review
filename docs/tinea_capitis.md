### Core idea

Tinea capitis = fungal infection of the scalp hair follicles and hair shafts.

* tinea = dermatophyte fungal infection
* capitis = head/scalp
* dermatophytes = fungi that eat keratin, the protein in skin, hair, and nails

So this is basically:

> ringworm of the scalp.

Not an actual worm.

---

### What causes it

Usually caused by dermatophytes such as:

* Trichophyton tonsurans
* Microsporum canis

Trichophyton tonsurans is a common cause in the United States.

Break it down:

* tricho- = hair
* phyton = plant/fungus-like organism

So Trichophyton is a fungus that loves hair/keratin.

---

### Why the findings happen

The fungus invades the hair follicle and hair shaft.

That causes:

* **scaly scalp:** fungus/inflammation disrupts the superficial skin
* **patchy hair loss:** infected hairs become weak and break
* **black dots:** broken hairs leave tiny dark stubs at the scalp surface
* **itching:** local inflammation irritates the skin
* **lymphadenopathy:** nearby lymph nodes enlarge because the immune system is reacting

Lymphadenopathy = swollen lymph nodes.

---

### Endothrix vs ectothrix

Two classic hair invasion patterns:

* **Endothrix:** spores are inside the hair shaft
* **Ectothrix:** spores are outside/on the surface of the hair shaft

Intuition:

* endo = inside
* ecto = outside

Endothrix infection can make hairs break at the scalp surface, giving the classic black-dot appearance.

---

### Kerion

Kerion = very inflamed, boggy, pus-filled scalp plaque from tinea capitis.

This is not just a simple scaly patch. It is the immune system strongly reacting to the fungus.

Why it matters:

* painful swelling
* pustules/pus
* can cause scarring alopecia if untreated

Alopecia = hair loss.

---

### Treatment intuition

Tinea capitis needs oral antifungals.

Why?

Because the fungus is down in the hair follicle and hair shaft, so topical creams usually cannot penetrate deeply enough.

Common oral options:

* griseofulvin
* terbinafine

Antifungal shampoos, such as selenium sulfide or ketoconazole, can help decrease spread but usually do not cure it alone.

---

## One-line intuition

Tinea capitis is a keratin-loving fungal infection that gets into scalp hair shafts, making hairs fragile, scaly, and patchily fall out.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Tinea capitis in a child with patchy alopecia plus scale should make you think fungal infection, and the key Step 1 treatment idea is **oral antifungal**, not topical-only therapy.

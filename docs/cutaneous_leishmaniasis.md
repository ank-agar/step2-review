## Cutaneous leishmaniasis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Leishmaniasis_life_cycle_diagram_en.svg/960px-Leishmaniasis_life_cycle_diagram_en.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/leishmaniasis/media/images/cutaneous-leishmaniasis-ulcer.jpg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/PHIL_Images/28708/28708_lores.jpg)

### Core idea

Cutaneous leishmaniasis is a skin infection caused by **Leishmania**, an intracellular protozoan (single-celled parasite) spread by a **sandfly bite**.

Cutaneous = skin.

So the simple idea is:

> sandfly injects parasite into skin → parasite lives inside macrophages → chronic skin ulcer forms.

---

### Classic presentation

Usually after travel or living in an endemic area:

* painless skin papule (small bump)
* slowly enlarges
* becomes an ulcer with raised edges
* often on exposed skin, like arms, legs, or face

The “volcano-like” ulcer is the classic mental image.

---

### Why the ulcer happens

The sandfly injects **promastigotes** (flagellated Leishmania form in the sandfly).

Macrophages (immune cells that eat microbes) phagocytose them, but the parasite survives inside and becomes **amastigotes** (non-flagellated intracellular form in humans).

Then:

* infected macrophages accumulate in the skin
* chronic inflammation damages local tissue
* the center breaks down → ulcer
* the edges stay inflamed and thick → raised border

So the lesion is basically a local battle between macrophages and a parasite that learned how to live inside macrophages.

---

### Diagnosis clue

Biopsy or smear can show:

* amastigotes inside macrophages
* “dot-dash” look from nucleus + kinetoplast

Kinetoplast = mitochondrial DNA structure seen in some protozoa.

---

## One-line intuition

Cutaneous leishmaniasis is a sandfly-transmitted parasite that hides in macrophages and creates a slow, painless, raised-edge skin ulcer.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association: **Leishmania survives inside macrophages**, so protection depends on **Th1 cells** (T helper 1 cells) activating macrophages with **IFN-gamma** (interferon-gamma).

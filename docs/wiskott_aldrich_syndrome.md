## Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome

![Image](https://dermnetnz.org/assets/Uploads/Wiskott-Aldrich-Syndrome.png)

![Image](https://upload.medbullets.com/topic/105004/images/wiskott-aldrich-syndrome-picture.jpg)

Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome is an X-linked recessive immunodeficiency caused by mutation in the WAS gene.

X-linked recessive means the mutated gene is on the X chromosome, so it classically affects boys.

The classic triad is:

* eczema
* thrombocytopenia
* recurrent infections

Thrombocytopenia means low platelet count.

---

### Core idea

The WAS gene makes WASP (Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein), which helps immune cells rearrange their actin cytoskeleton.

Actin cytoskeleton = the internal scaffold cells use to move, change shape, and form connections.

So if WASP is defective:

* T cells cannot signal/interact well
* B cell antibody responses are abnormal
* platelets are small and decreased

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Recurrent infections:** defective T cell and B cell function
* **Eczema:** immune dysregulation causes allergic/inflammatory skin disease
* **Petechiae/purpura/bruising:** low platelets cause small bleeds into skin
* **Bloody diarrhea/epistaxis:** thrombocytopenia causes mucosal bleeding

Petechiae = tiny pinpoint skin bleeds.

Purpura = larger purple skin bleeding spots.

Epistaxis = nosebleed.

---

### Labs to remember

Classic immunoglobulin pattern:

* low IgM (immunoglobulin M)
* high IgA (immunoglobulin A)
* high IgE (immunoglobulin E)

Also:

* low platelet count
* small platelets

That "small platelets" part is very testable.

---

## One-line intuition

Wiskott-Aldrich = immune cells cannot use their cytoskeleton properly, so you get bad immunity + eczema + small low platelets that bleed.

### Core idea

Cholecystitis = inflammation of the gallbladder.

Break the word apart:

* chole = bile
* cyst = bladder/sac
* -itis = inflammation

So cholecystitis literally means:

> inflammation of the bile sac, aka the gallbladder.

---

### Most common cause

Usually a gallstone blocks the cystic duct.

Cystic duct = the tube that drains bile from the gallbladder into the biliary tree.

That blockage causes:

* bile trapped inside the gallbladder
* gallbladder distension
* wall inflammation
* sometimes bacterial infection later

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **RUQ pain:** RUQ (right upper quadrant) is where the gallbladder sits
* **Pain after fatty meals:** fat makes the small intestine release CCK (cholecystokinin), which tells the gallbladder to squeeze
* **Fever/leukocytosis:** leukocytosis means increased white blood cells, from inflammation/infection
* **Nausea/vomiting:** inflamed biliary tract irritates nearby GI (gastrointestinal) reflexes

The key intuition:

The gallbladder tries to squeeze bile out, but the exit is blocked, so pressure and inflammation build up.

---

### Classic exam findings

* RUQ tenderness
* fever
* leukocytosis
* positive Murphy sign

Murphy sign = pain/inspiratory arrest when pressing over the gallbladder during inspiration.

Why?

When the patient breathes in, the diaphragm pushes the liver and gallbladder downward into your hand. If the gallbladder is inflamed, that hurts.

---

### Imaging clue

US (ultrasound) often shows:

* gallstones
* gallbladder wall thickening
* pericholecystic fluid
* sonographic Murphy sign

Pericholecystic fluid = fluid around the gallbladder from inflammation.

---

## One-line intuition

A stone blocks the gallbladder's exit, bile gets trapped, and the gallbladder becomes swollen, painful, and inflamed.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Acute cholecystitis is usually from cystic duct obstruction, while choledocholithiasis means a stone in the CBD (common bile duct), which is more likely to cause jaundice because bile cannot drain from the liver into the intestine.

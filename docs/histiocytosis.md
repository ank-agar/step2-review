### Core idea

Histiocytosis means abnormal buildup/proliferation of histiocytes.

Histiocytes are tissue immune cells, mainly:

* macrophages
* dendritic cells

The Step 1 classic is **Langerhans cell histiocytosis**.

Langerhans cells are dendritic antigen-presenting cells (APCs; cells that show antigen to T cells), normally found in the skin.

---

### What happens

In Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH; Langerhans cell histiocytosis), these cells proliferate abnormally and infiltrate tissues.

Common sites:

* bone, especially skull
* skin
* lung
* pituitary gland

So symptoms can include:

* lytic bone lesions, especially skull lesions
* skin rash
* lung disease
* diabetes insipidus from pituitary involvement

Diabetes insipidus means decreased antidiuretic hormone (ADH; hormone that helps retain water), causing excessive urination and thirst.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Bone pain/lytic lesions:** proliferating Langerhans cells destroy bone
* **Rash:** abnormal immune cells infiltrate skin
* **Diabetes insipidus:** pituitary involvement decreases ADH
* **Lung disease:** Langerhans cells infiltrate lung tissue, classically in smokers

Think:

> abnormal dendritic cells pile up and damage whatever tissue they enter.

---

### Classic biopsy clues

LCH cells are positive for:

* CD1a
* S100
* CD207, also called langerin

Electron microscopy can show:

* Birbeck granules

These look like:

* tennis rackets

That is the famous buzzword.

---

## One-line intuition

Histiocytosis is too many tissue immune cells; on Step 1, think Langerhans cells causing lytic skull lesions, rash, diabetes insipidus, and tennis racket Birbeck granules.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Langerhans cell histiocytosis is a **dendritic cell** problem, not a B cell or T cell problem.

HY marker combo:

* CD1a+
* S100+
* Birbeck granules

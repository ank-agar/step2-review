### Core idea

Hypersensitivity pneumonitis, or **HP** (hypersensitivity pneumonitis), is inflammation of the **alveoli** (tiny air sacs) and small airways after repeated inhalation of an antigen (immune-triggering substance).

Classic triggers:

* moldy hay → farmer's lung
* bird proteins/droppings → bird fancier's lung
* contaminated humidifiers/hot tubs

So the basic idea is:

> you breathe in an antigen repeatedly, and the immune system inflames the lung where gas exchange happens.

---

### Why it happens

HP is mainly a mix of:

* **type III hypersensitivity** (immune complex deposition)
* **type IV hypersensitivity** (delayed T-cell-mediated inflammation)

Break that down:

* inhaled antigen reaches alveoli
* immune system reacts too strongly
* lymphocytes (immune cells) and macrophages (phagocytic immune cells) enter the interstitium (space between alveoli)
* chronic inflammation can eventually cause fibrosis (scarring)

That is why HP is an **interstitial lung disease** (disease of the lung support tissue around alveoli).

---

### Symptoms

Common findings:

* cough
* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* fever/chills after exposure
* fatigue
* inspiratory crackles

Reasoning:

The alveolar walls get inflamed and thickened, so oxygen has a harder time crossing into blood. That causes dyspnea and decreased **DLCO** (diffusing capacity of lung for carbon monoxide; a test of gas transfer).

---

### Imaging / pathology

On **HRCT** (high-resolution computed tomography), you can see:

* ground-glass opacities
* centrilobular nodules
* air trapping
* mosaic attenuation

Pathology can show:

* lymphocytic interstitial inflammation
* poorly formed noncaseating granulomas
* giant cells

Noncaseating granuloma = granuloma without cheesy necrosis.

---

## One-line intuition

HP is basically an allergic immune attack on the alveoli from repeated inhaled exposure, causing inflammation first and fibrosis later if the trigger continues.

---

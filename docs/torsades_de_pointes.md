## Torsades de pointes

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Torsades_de_Pointes_%28polymorphic_VT%29.svg/1280px-Torsades_de_Pointes_%28polymorphic_VT%29.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6b/Rhythm_torsade_%28CardioNetworks_ECGpedia%29.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4f/Tosadesdepointes.jpg)

Torsades de pointes is a type of **polymorphic VT (ventricular tachycardia)** where the QRS complexes seem to twist around the baseline on an **ECG (electrocardiogram)**.

Break the phrase apart:

* torsades = twisting
* pointes = points/peaks

So the idea is:

> the ventricular rhythm is fast and chaotic-looking, with the peaks changing direction like they are twisting.

---

### Core idea

Torsades usually happens when the **QT interval** is prolonged.

The QT interval is the time from ventricular depolarization to ventricular repolarization:

* depolarization = the ventricle fires
* repolarization = the ventricle resets

So a long QT means:

> the ventricles are taking too long to reset.

---

### Why long QT causes torsades

If repolarization is delayed, some ventricular cells are still vulnerable when an extra beat comes in.

That extra beat can trigger an **early afterdepolarization** (abnormal extra firing before the cell fully resets).

Then the heart can enter a rapid ventricular rhythm where different ventricular areas fire unevenly, causing the classic twisting ECG pattern.

Intuition:

> Long QT = slow reset. Slow reset = easier for an early beat to hit at the wrong time. Wrong-time beat = torsades risk.

---

### Common causes

Think things that prolong QT:

* hypokalemia (low potassium)
* hypomagnesemia (low magnesium)
* congenital long QT syndrome (inherited delayed ventricular repolarization)
* antiarrhythmics (drugs for abnormal heart rhythms), especially class IA and class III
* macrolides like erythromycin
* fluoroquinolones
* antipsychotics

---

### Treatment intuition

Give **IV magnesium sulfate** because magnesium helps suppress the early afterdepolarizations that trigger torsades.

If unstable:

* defibrillate/cardioversion depending on pulse and clinical scenario

If recurrent with bradycardia (slow heart rate):

* increase heart rate with pacing or isoproterenol

Why?

Faster heart rate shortens the QT interval, so there is less time for the dangerous early afterdepolarization to happen.

---

## One-line intuition

Torsades de pointes is **polymorphic ventricular tachycardia caused by delayed ventricular reset**, classically from long QT.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, torsades = **long QT + polymorphic VT + treat with IV magnesium sulfate**, even if serum magnesium is normal.

# Bronchopneumonia

### Core idea

Bronchopneumonia = pneumonia (infection/inflammation of lung airspaces) that starts around bronchioles (small airway branches) and spreads into nearby alveoli (tiny air sacs).

So instead of one whole lobe being filled, you get patchy infected spots scattered through the lung.

---

### Break the word apart

* broncho- = bronchi/bronchioles, meaning airways
* pneumonia = inflamed/infected lung airspaces

So:

> airway-centered pneumonia.

---

### What is happening pathologically

The infection begins in small airways, then spreads outward into nearby alveoli.

Those alveoli fill with:

* neutrophils (acute bacterial infection cells)
* pus
* fluid
* fibrin (sticky clot-like inflammatory protein)

This causes consolidation (airspaces become filled with inflammatory material instead of air).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Fever:** cytokines (immune signaling proteins) reset the hypothalamus (temperature control center)
* **Productive cough:** inflamed airways make mucus/pus
* **Shortness of breath:** alveoli are filled with fluid/pus, so gas exchange is worse
* **Patchy CXR findings:** CXR (chest x-ray) shows scattered areas of consolidation because the infection spreads around multiple bronchioles

---

### Bronchopneumonia vs lobar pneumonia

* **Bronchopneumonia:** patchy, multifocal, airway-centered
* **Lobar pneumonia:** one big continuous lobe is involved

Intuition:

Bronchopneumonia is like multiple little fires starting around small airways.

Lobar pneumonia is like one whole room catching fire.

---

### Common bugs

Often bacterial, especially:

* Staph aureus (Staphylococcus aureus)
* H. influenzae (Haemophilus influenzae)
* Klebsiella
* E. coli (Escherichia coli)
* Pseudomonas

These are especially relevant in hospital-acquired pneumonia.

---

## One-line intuition

Bronchopneumonia is patchy bacterial pneumonia centered around bronchioles, with nearby alveoli filling with neutrophils and pus.

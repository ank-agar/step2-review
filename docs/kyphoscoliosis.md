## Kyphoscoliosis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/06/Meyers_b13_s0296_b1.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562183/bin/scoliosis.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562183/bin/Wiki_pre-op.jpg)

Kyphoscoliosis = abnormal spine curvature in **2 directions**:

* **kyphosis** = excessive forward/backward rounding of the spine, usually thoracic spine (upper/mid back)
* **scoliosis** = abnormal side-to-side curvature of the spine

So kyphoscoliosis means:

> the spine is both hunched forward and curved sideways.

---

### Core idea

Think:

* kyphosis = side view problem
* scoliosis = front/back view problem
* kyphoscoliosis = both together

This is a 3D spine deformity because the vertebrae (spine bones) can also rotate.

---

### Why symptoms happen

The spine and rib cage help make the chest wall.

So if the spine is severely curved:

* the chest wall becomes stiff/distorted
* lungs cannot expand well
* breathing becomes harder
* severe cases can cause restrictive lung disease

**Restrictive lung disease** means the lungs cannot fully expand, so total lung capacity is decreased.

---

### Causes/associations

Kyphoscoliosis can be:

* idiopathic (unknown cause)
* congenital (present from birth due to abnormal vertebral development)
* neuromuscular, like cerebral palsy or muscular dystrophy
* connective tissue related, like Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (abnormal collagen/connective tissue disorder)

---

## One-line intuition

Kyphoscoliosis is a spine that is bent forward and sideways, which can distort the rib cage and limit lung expansion.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Severe kyphoscoliosis can cause **restrictive lung disease** because the problem is not the alveoli (air sacs) themselves; it is the chest wall mechanics limiting lung expansion.

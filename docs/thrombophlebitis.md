## Thrombophlebitis

![Image](https://dermnetnz.org/assets/Uploads/vascular/thrombophlebitis2.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Great_saphenous_vein_thrombosis_05091312009.jpg/250px-Great_saphenous_vein_thrombosis_05091312009.jpg)

Thrombophlebitis = inflammation of a vein because there is a thrombus (blood clot) in it.

Break the word apart:

* thrombo- = clot
* phleb- = vein
* -itis = inflammation

So it literally means:

> inflamed vein due to a clot.

---

### Core idea

A clot forms inside a vein, and that clot irritates the vein wall.

That irritation causes:

* pain
* redness
* warmth
* swelling
* a tender, hard, cord-like vein

The "cord" feeling happens because the superficial vein (vein close to the skin) is clotted and inflamed, so it becomes firm instead of soft/collapsible.

---

### Superficial vs deep

**Superficial thrombophlebitis** = clot + inflammation in a superficial vein.

This is usually more local and less dangerous.

**DVT (deep vein thrombosis)** = clot in a deep vein, usually in the leg.

DVT is more dangerous because the clot can embolize (break off and travel) to the lungs, causing a PE (pulmonary embolism).

---

### Why it happens

Think Virchow triad:

* stasis = slow blood flow
* endothelial injury = damage to the inner vein lining
* hypercoagulability = blood is too prone to clot

Examples:

* long immobility
* varicose veins
* IV (intravenous) catheter irritation
* cancer
* pregnancy
* estrogen therapy/OCPs (oral contraceptive pills)
* inherited thrombophilia (increased tendency to clot), like factor V Leiden

---

## One-line intuition

Thrombophlebitis is a vein that becomes painful, red, and firm because a clot inside it is inflaming the vein wall.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Migratory thrombophlebitis = recurrent clots in different superficial veins.

HY Step 1 association: think Trousseau syndrome, classically from pancreatic adenocarcinoma causing a hypercoagulable state.

### Core idea

Pill esophagitis is inflammation/ulceration of the esophagus (food pipe) caused by a pill getting stuck and dissolving on the mucosa (inner lining).

Basically:

* pill sits in esophagus
* drug dissolves locally
* chemical irritation burns the lining
* ulcer forms

---

### Classic presentation

Sudden symptoms after taking a pill, especially before bed:

* odynophagia (painful swallowing)
* dysphagia (difficulty swallowing)
* retrosternal chest pain (pain behind the sternum/breastbone)
* feeling like a pill is stuck

The pain can feel scary because the esophagus runs through the chest, so esophageal pain can mimic cardiac chest pain.

---

### Why it happens

The esophagus is not just an open pipe. It has narrow spots and needs gravity + peristalsis (wave-like muscle contraction) to move pills down.

If someone takes a pill with little water or lies down right after:

* less water pushes the pill forward
* gravity no longer helps
* pill transit slows
* pill dissolves directly against the wall

That local drug concentration causes a chemical burn.

---

### Common culprits

High-yield causes:

* doxycycline
* tetracycline
* bisphosphonates (osteoporosis drugs like alendronate)
* NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, like ibuprofen)
* potassium chloride
* iron pills
* vitamin C

Doxycycline is the classic testable one.

---

### Endoscopy finding

EGD (esophagogastroduodenoscopy, camera exam of esophagus/stomach/duodenum) may show:

* discrete ulcers
* erosions
* "kissing ulcers" on opposite sides of the esophagus

"Kissing" ulcers happen because the pill can press between opposite mucosal walls and injure both sides.

---

## One-line intuition

A pill gets stuck, dissolves in one spot, and chemically burns the esophageal lining.

---

## HY pearl

If a vignette says a patient took doxycycline at night with little water, then woke up with painful swallowing and chest pain, think pill esophagitis.

Prevention: take pills upright with plenty of water and stay upright afterward.

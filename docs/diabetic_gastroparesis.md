## Diabetic gastroparesis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a5/Stomach_emptying_into_duodenum.svg/960px-Stomach_emptying_into_duodenum.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Openchowski_stomach_innervation_b%26w.jpg/960px-Openchowski_stomach_innervation_b%26w.jpg)

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/23063/FB_JAN23_DDPB_DYK_Gastroparesis_and_Diabetes_thumbnail.png)

Diabetic gastroparesis means delayed stomach emptying caused by diabetes.

Break the word apart:

* gastro- = stomach
* -paresis = weakness/partial paralysis

So it basically means:

> the stomach is weak and empties too slowly.

---

### Core idea

Long-standing diabetes can damage autonomic nerves (nerves that control automatic body functions), especially the vagus nerve (cranial nerve X, the main parasympathetic nerve to the gut).

The vagus nerve normally tells the stomach to contract and push food into the duodenum (first part of the small intestine).

If that nerve is damaged:

* stomach contractions become weak
* food stays in the stomach too long
* there is delayed gastric emptying (slow movement of stomach contents into the small intestine)

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Food sitting in the stomach causes:

* early satiety (feeling full quickly)
* nausea
* vomiting old/undigested food
* bloating
* abdominal discomfort

It can also make diabetes harder to control because glucose (blood sugar) absorption becomes unpredictable:

* insulin may start working before food is absorbed → hypoglycemia (low blood glucose)
* food may absorb later → hyperglycemia (high blood glucose)

---

### Diagnosis

Classic test:

* gastric emptying scintigraphy (a nuclear medicine test that tracks how fast a labeled meal leaves the stomach)

Key concept:

* symptoms + delayed emptying + no mechanical obstruction

Mechanical obstruction means a physical blockage, like a tumor or stricture (narrowed area).

---

## One-line intuition

Diabetes damages the stomach's autopilot nerves, so the stomach becomes a slow, weak pump.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Metoclopramide treats gastroparesis by blocking dopamine D2 receptors (dopamine type 2 receptors), which increases GI (gastrointestinal) motility, but it can cause EPS (extrapyramidal symptoms, movement side effects like dystonia or Parkinson-like symptoms).

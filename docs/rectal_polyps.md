### Core idea

Rectal polyps are small growths that stick out from the mucosa (inner lining) of the rectum (last part of the large intestine before the anus).

Most are benign (not cancer), but some can become malignant (cancerous), especially adenomatous polyps (gland-forming premalignant polyps).

---

### Types to know

* **Pedunculated polyp:** has a stalk, like a mushroom
* **Sessile polyp:** flat/broad-based, sitting directly on the mucosa
* **Hyperplastic polyp:** usually low malignant potential
* **Adenomatous polyp:** premalignant, can progress to colorectal cancer

---

### Why symptoms happen

Many rectal polyps are asymptomatic (cause no symptoms).

If symptoms occur:

* **rectal bleeding:** fragile surface vessels can break
* **mucus in stool:** rectal mucosa makes mucus
* **tenesmus:** feeling like you still need to poop because the polyp irritates the rectum
* **change in bowel habits:** larger polyps can partially obstruct or irritate stool passage

---

### Why doctors remove them

Polyps are removed during colonoscopy (camera test of the colon/rectum) or sigmoidoscopy (camera test of the rectum and sigmoid colon) because pathology (microscope exam) tells whether there is dysplasia (abnormal precancerous cells).

Intuition:

> A polyp is like extra mucosal tissue growing into the bowel lumen. The bigger and more dysplastic it gets, the more cancer-risk it carries.

---

### One-line intuition

Rectal polyps are mucosal bumps in the rectum; most are benign, but adenomas matter because they are a possible step on the adenoma-carcinoma pathway.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1: Adenomatous polyps with **villous histology**, **large size**, or **high-grade dysplasia** have higher risk of progressing to colorectal adenocarcinoma (gland-forming cancer of the colon/rectum).

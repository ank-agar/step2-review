# Cor pulmonale

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18131.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/6b30/5399693/38b01f10d0f6/ATM-12-61-g001.jpg)

Cor pulmonale = right-sided heart failure caused by lung disease.

Break it down:

* **cor** = heart
* **pulmonale** = lung-related

So the idea is:

> The lungs become the problem first, then the right side of the heart fails from pumping against high pressure.

---

### Core mechanism

Normally, the right ventricle (RV; right lower chamber of the heart) pumps blood into the lungs.

If chronic lung disease causes pulmonary hypertension (high blood pressure in the lung arteries), the RV has to push harder.

Over time, the RV becomes:

* hypertrophied (thickened from working harder)
* dilated (stretched out)
* eventually weak

That causes right-sided heart failure.

---

### Why lung disease causes it

Common causes:

* COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease)
* interstitial lung disease (scarring/stiffening of lung tissue)
* chronic pulmonary emboli (repeated clots in lung arteries)
* obstructive sleep apnea (airway collapse during sleep causing low oxygen)

The key trigger is often chronic hypoxia (low oxygen).

Low oxygen makes pulmonary vessels constrict.

That raises pulmonary vascular resistance (resistance in lung blood vessels), so the RV is pumping into a tighter, higher-pressure circuit.

---

### Symptoms/signs

Because it is right-sided heart failure, blood backs up into the body:

* JVD (jugular venous distension; bulging neck veins)
* peripheral edema (leg swelling)
* hepatomegaly (enlarged liver)
* ascites (fluid in the abdomen)

And because the original problem is lung disease, patients may also have:

* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* cyanosis (bluish discoloration from low oxygen)
* chronic cough or wheezing, depending on the cause

---

## One-line intuition

Bad lungs make high pressure in the lung arteries, so the right heart gets overworked and eventually fails.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Cor pulmonale is specifically right heart failure from lung disease/pulmonary hypertension, not from left-sided heart failure backing up into the lungs.

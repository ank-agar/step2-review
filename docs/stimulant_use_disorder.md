### Core idea

Stimulant use disorder is compulsive, harmful use of stimulants despite negative consequences.

Stimulants include:

* cocaine
* amphetamines
* methamphetamine
* prescription stimulants when misused

They increase CNS (central nervous system) activity, especially dopamine and norepinephrine signaling.

---

### Why stimulants are addictive

Stimulants strongly activate the mesolimbic dopamine pathway:

* VTA (ventral tegmental area)
* nucleus accumbens
* prefrontal cortex

This pathway is basically the brain's reward-learning circuit.

So the brain learns:

> this drug = very high reward, repeat this behavior.

Over time, normal rewards feel weaker, and drug cues become very powerful.

---

### Mechanism intuition

Cocaine mainly blocks DAT (dopamine transporter), so dopamine stays in the synapse longer.

Amphetamines enter the presynaptic neuron and increase dopamine release, partly through DAT (dopamine transporter) and VMAT (vesicular monoamine transporter) effects.

Either way:

* more dopamine in synapse
* more reward pathway firing
* more reinforcement
* more craving

---

### Clinical features

Think:

* craving
* inability to cut down
* tolerance
* withdrawal
* using despite school/work/relationship problems
* risky use

Acute stimulant intoxication can cause:

* tachycardia
* hypertension
* mydriasis
* agitation
* anxiety/paranoia
* decreased appetite
* insomnia

Withdrawal often causes the opposite:

* fatigue
* depression
* hypersomnia
* increased appetite
* intense craving

---

## One-line intuition

Stimulants hijack the dopamine reward pathway, so the brain starts prioritizing the drug over normal rewards.

---

## HY pearl

Cocaine intoxication classically causes sympathetic activation plus mydriasis, and severe cases can cause coronary vasospasm, myocardial infarction, seizures, and hyperthermia.

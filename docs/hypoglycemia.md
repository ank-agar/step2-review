Hypoglycemia = low blood glucose (low sugar in the blood).

Usually this means blood glucose is:

* **< 70 mg/dL**  

mg/dL = milligrams per deciliter, a concentration measurement.

---

### Core idea

Glucose is the brain’s main fuel.

So when glucose gets too low, the body reacts in 2 big ways:

1. **Adrenergic symptoms** from epinephrine (adrenaline)
2. **Neuroglycopenic symptoms** from low glucose reaching the brain

Adrenergic = sympathetic “fight-or-flight” response.  
Neuroglycopenic = brain glucose deprivation.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Low glucose triggers epinephrine release to warn you and raise blood sugar.

That causes:

* sweating
* tremor
* anxiety
* palpitations
* hunger

If glucose keeps falling, the brain itself runs low on fuel.

That causes:

* confusion
* blurry vision
* weakness
* seizure
* coma

---

### Why insulin can cause it

Insulin lowers blood glucose by pushing glucose into tissues and promoting storage.

So too much insulin, or normal insulin without enough food, can drop blood glucose too low.

Classic setup:

* took insulin
* skipped meal
* exercised more than usual

Exercise matters because muscle uses more glucose.

---

### Body’s normal rescue response

When blood glucose falls, the body should:

* decrease insulin
* increase glucagon (pancreatic hormone that raises blood glucose)
* increase epinephrine
* increase cortisol (stress hormone)
* increase growth hormone

Glucagon tells the liver:

* break down glycogen into glucose = glycogenolysis
* make new glucose = gluconeogenesis

Glycogen = stored glucose.  
Gluconeogenesis = making new glucose, usually in liver.

---

## One-line intuition

Hypoglycemia is when blood sugar is too low, so the body first gives you “adrenaline alarm” symptoms, then the brain starts malfunctioning because it lacks fuel.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Whipple triad is the key diagnostic idea:

* symptoms of hypoglycemia
* low measured plasma glucose
* symptoms improve after glucose is given

This helps prove the symptoms are actually from low glucose.

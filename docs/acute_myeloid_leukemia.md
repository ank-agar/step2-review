### Core idea

Acute myeloid leukemia is AML (acute myeloid leukemia): a cancer where immature myeloid cells get stuck as blasts (very immature blood-forming cells) and rapidly build up in the bone marrow.

Myeloid cells normally become:

* neutrophils
* monocytes
* eosinophils
* basophils
* red blood cells
* platelets

In AML, the marrow gets packed with abnormal myeloblasts (immature myeloid precursor cells).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Anemia:** blasts crowd out red blood cell production, so less oxygen-carrying blood is made
* **Infections:** blasts crowd out normal neutrophils, so bacterial/fungal defense drops
* **Bleeding/bruising:** blasts crowd out platelet production
* **Bone pain:** marrow expands because it is packed with leukemic cells
* **High WBC (white blood cell count):** many abnormal blasts spill into blood

---

### Key smear finding

AML can show Auer rods (needle-like reddish granules inside myeloblasts).

Think:

> Auer rods = myeloid lineage clue.

They are made of fused azurophilic granules (primary granules containing enzymes like MPO, meaning myeloperoxidase).

---

### Big Step 1 association

Acute promyelocytic leukemia is APL (acute promyelocytic leukemia), a subtype of AML.

Classic:

* t(15;17) translocation
* PML-RARA fusion gene
* many Auer rods
* DIC (disseminated intravascular coagulation), meaning widespread clotting factor activation causing both clotting and bleeding

Why DIC happens:

abnormal promyelocytes release procoagulant granules, so the clotting system gets massively activated and consumed.

---

## One-line intuition

AML is when immature myeloid blasts take over the bone marrow, so normal blood production fails.

---

## Pearl 🔥

AML is usually MPO-positive (myeloperoxidase-positive), while ALL (acute lymphoblastic leukemia) is usually TdT-positive (terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase-positive).

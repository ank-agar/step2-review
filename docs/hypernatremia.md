## Hypernatremia

### Core idea

Hypernatremia = high serum sodium, usually **Na+ > 145 mEq/L**.

But the real problem is usually not "too much salt."

It is usually:

* too little free water
* compared with sodium

So the blood becomes **hypertonic** (more concentrated than cells).

---

### What happens to cells?

High Na+ outside the cell pulls water **out of cells** by **osmosis** (water moving toward the more concentrated side).

So cells shrink.

This matters most in the brain:

* brain cells shrink
* cerebral blood vessels can stretch/tear
* neurologic symptoms happen

Symptoms can include:

* thirst
* confusion
* weakness
* seizures
* coma

---

### Why it happens

Common causes are things that make you lose water more than salt:

* dehydration
* diabetes insipidus (low ADH effect, so kidneys cannot reabsorb water well)
* osmotic diuresis (peeing out water because glucose/solute drags water into urine)
* poor water access, especially in elderly or altered mental status patients

ADH = antidiuretic hormone, which tells the kidney to reabsorb water.

---

## One-line intuition

Hypernatremia means the blood is too concentrated, so water leaves cells and brain cells shrink.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Correct chronic hypernatremia slowly because rapid correction can push water back into brain cells too fast, causing **cerebral edema** (brain swelling).

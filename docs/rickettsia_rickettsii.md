**Rickettsia rickettsii** is the bacteria that causes **Rocky Mountain spotted fever**.

It is:

* small
* gram-negative
* obligate intracellular
* tick-borne

Obligate intracellular means it has to live inside host cells.

---

### Core idea

Think:

> Tick bite → Rickettsia rickettsii enters blood vessels → endothelial cell infection → vasculitis → fever + rash.

Endothelial cells are the cells lining blood vessels.

Vasculitis means blood vessel inflammation.

---

### Why the rash happens

Rickettsia rickettsii infects endothelial cells.

That damages small blood vessels, causing:

* leakage
* inflammation
* tiny hemorrhages

So the rash can become **petechial**, meaning small red-purple spots from bleeding under the skin.

Classic rash pattern:

* starts on wrists/ankles
* spreads centrally
* can involve palms and soles

---

### Classic symptoms

Rocky Mountain spotted fever (RMSF) classically causes:

* fever
* headache
* myalgias (muscle aches)
* rash

The rash often appears after the fever starts, so do not wait for rash before treating.

---

### Treatment

Treat with **doxycycline**.

This is true even in children because untreated Rocky Mountain spotted fever can be rapidly fatal.

---

## One-line intuition

Rickettsia rickettsii is a tick-borne intracellular bug that attacks blood vessel lining, so the patient gets fever plus a vascular rash.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Rocky Mountain spotted fever is treated with doxycycline immediately if suspected, even before confirmatory testing, because delayed treatment greatly increases mortality.

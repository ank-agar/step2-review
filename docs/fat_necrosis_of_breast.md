# Fat necrosis of breast

![Image](https://media.springernature.com/lw1200/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1186%2Fs12944-019-1078-4/MediaObjects/12944_2019_1078_Fig1_HTML.png)

![Image](https://nccommons.org/m/4/41/Fat_necrosis_breast_%28Radiopaedia_18715%29.png)

Fat necrosis of the breast = benign death of breast fat tissue, usually after injury.

Necrosis = cell death.

Fat necrosis means the fat cells die, leak oily contents, and trigger inflammation/scarring.

---

### Core idea

A piece of breast fat gets damaged, often from:

* trauma
* surgery
* biopsy
* radiation
* breast reduction/reconstruction

Then that area can become a firm lump.

Important: it is benign, but it can mimic breast cancer.

---

### Why it feels like a lump

Injury damages fat cells.

The fat cells spill lipid/oil.

The body reacts with:

* macrophages = immune cells that eat debris
* giant cells = fused macrophages
* fibrosis = scar tissue
* calcification = calcium deposition

So the area becomes firm, irregular, or gritty.

That is why fat necrosis can feel scary on exam.

---

### Imaging idea

Fat necrosis can look like:

* oil cyst = round pocket of liquefied fat/oil
* rim calcification = calcium around the dead fat
* irregular/spiculated mass sometimes

Spiculated means having sharp radiating edges.

That matters because spiculated breast lesions can also suggest malignancy.

So if imaging is classic, it may be called benign. If it looks suspicious, biopsy may be needed.

---

## One-line intuition

Fat necrosis of breast is basically a bruise/injury to breast fat that heals with oily cysts, inflammation, scar tissue, and sometimes calcification.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1-style clue: breast fat necrosis is benign but can mimic breast cancer with a hard irregular mass, skin retraction, or calcifications.

# Tracheal foreign body

![Image](https://open.oregonstate.education/app/uploads/sites/309/2025/08/2308_The_Trachea.jpg)

![Image](https://media.springernature.com/lw685/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1186%2Fs12880-021-00561-z/MediaObjects/12880_2021_561_Fig1_HTML.jpg)

A tracheal foreign body is an object stuck in the trachea (windpipe).

So instead of food/object going down the esophagus (food pipe), it accidentally enters the airway and gets lodged before the airway splits into the right and left main bronchi (large air tubes going to each lung).

Common examples:

* peanut
* toy piece
* coin
* food bolus

---

### Core idea

The trachea is the shared air pipe for both lungs.

So if something blocks the trachea, it can reduce airflow to both lungs at once.

That is why a tracheal foreign body can be more immediately dangerous than a foreign body stuck farther down in one bronchus.

---

### What symptoms happen?

Classically:

* sudden choking episode
* coughing
* stridor (high-pitched sound from upper airway narrowing)
* dyspnea (trouble breathing)
* wheezing if the blockage is partial
* cyanosis (bluish skin from low oxygen) if severe

Why stridor?

Because the object narrows the upper/main airway, so air moves through a tight tube and makes a high-pitched sound.

---

### Why it can be missed

Some foreign bodies are radiolucent (do not show up well on X-ray), like peanuts or plastic.

So a normal chest X-ray does not fully rule it out if the story sounds right.

Bronchoscopy (camera scope into the airways) is both diagnostic and therapeutic:

* diagnostic = finds the object
* therapeutic = can remove the object

---

## One-line intuition

A tracheal foreign body is basically a physical plug in the windpipe, so both lungs can suddenly lose airflow.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Most aspirated foreign bodies go into the right main bronchus because it is wider, shorter, and more vertical, but a true tracheal foreign body is especially dangerous because it sits above the split to both lungs.

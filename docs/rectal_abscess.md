# Rectal abscess

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Abscess_diag_02.svg/960px-Abscess_diag_02.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459167/bin/perirectal__abscess.jpg)

A rectal abscess is a pocket of pus near the rectum/anus.

More precise wording:

* anorectal abscess = abscess around the anus or rectum
* perianal abscess = closer to the skin around the anus
* perirectal abscess = deeper, around the rectum

Abscess means a walled-off collection of pus from infection.

---

### Core idea

Anal glands can get blocked.

Then bacteria/stool material get trapped inside, causing:

* infection
* inflammation
* pus buildup
* severe pain/swelling near the anus or rectum

So the abscess is basically:

> blocked anal gland + bacteria = pus pocket

---

### Why it hurts so much

The rectal/anal area has a lot of sensory nerves.

As pus builds up, pressure rises in a tight space, so patients can get:

* throbbing anal/rectal pain
* pain with sitting
* pain with bowel movements
* swelling/redness
* fever/chills if infection is more systemic

Systemic means affecting the whole body, not just one local area.

---

### Treatment idea

Abscesses usually need incision and drainage.

Incision = cutting it open.

Drainage = letting pus come out.

Antibiotics alone often do not fix it because antibiotics have trouble penetrating a walled-off pus collection.

---

## One-line intuition

A rectal abscess is an infected pus pocket near the anus/rectum, usually from a clogged anal gland.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Anorectal abscess can progress to an anal fistula, which is an abnormal tunnel connecting the anal canal to the skin.

# Western equine encephalitis virus

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/db/14996_lores.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/68/EMD-5210.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fd/Vies_de_transmissi%C3%B3_del_virus_de_l%27encefalitis_equina_occidental_.jpg)

Western equine encephalitis virus is a mosquito-borne alphavirus (RNA virus in the Togaviridae family) that can cause encephalitis (brain inflammation).

Break the name apart:

* western = classically western North America
* equine = horses
* encephalitis = inflammation of the brain
* virus = infectious particle that replicates inside host cells

So the big idea is:

> a mosquito-transmitted virus that can inflame the brain, especially in horses and sometimes humans.

---

### Transmission

Main cycle:

* birds = reservoir hosts (animals that maintain the virus in nature)
* Culex mosquitoes = vectors (organisms that transmit infection)
* humans and horses = dead-end hosts

Dead-end host means humans/horses usually do not build enough virus in the blood to pass it efficiently back to mosquitoes.

---

### Why symptoms happen

Most infections are asymptomatic (no symptoms).

If symptomatic:

* fever
* headache
* myalgias (muscle aches)
* nausea/vomiting

Severe cases can cause CNS (central nervous system) disease:

* meningitis (inflammation of the meninges, the coverings around the brain/spinal cord)
* encephalitis (brain inflammation)
* seizures
* altered mental status
* coma in severe cases

Why?

The virus can cross into the CNS (central nervous system), infect neurons/glial cells (brain support cells), and trigger inflammation. Brain swelling + neuronal injury = seizures, confusion, and neurologic deficits.

---

### Diagnosis/treatment

Diagnosis is usually by serology (blood/CSF antibody testing), especially IgM (immunoglobulin M, early antibody).

CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) may show viral meningitis-type findings:

* lymphocytic pleocytosis (increased lymphocytes in fluid)
* mildly elevated protein
* normal glucose

Treatment is supportive:

* fluids
* fever/pain control
* seizure management if needed

No routine human vaccine and no specific antiviral therapy.

---

## One-line intuition

Western equine encephalitis virus is basically a bird-mosquito virus that accidentally spills into horses/humans and can cause brain inflammation.

---

## Pearl

For Step 1, remember the arbovirus (arthropod-borne virus) pattern:

* mosquito bite
* summer/fall exposure
* fever + headache
* possible encephalitis

And WEEV (Western equine encephalitis virus) is an alphavirus, like EEEV (Eastern equine encephalitis virus) and VEEV (Venezuelan equine encephalitis virus).

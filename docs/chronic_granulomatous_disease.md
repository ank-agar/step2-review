### Core idea

Chronic granulomatous disease is an inherited phagocyte killing defect.

The problem is defective NADPH oxidase (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate oxidase), so neutrophils/macrophages cannot make a normal respiratory burst.

Respiratory burst = rapid production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), like superoxide, used to kill microbes inside phagolysosomes.

---

### Why infections happen

Normally:

* NADPH oxidase makes superoxide
* superoxide helps form hydrogen peroxide and other ROS (reactive oxygen species)
* ROS help kill engulfed bacteria/fungi

In CGD (chronic granulomatous disease):

* phagocytes can engulf microbes
* but they cannot kill them well
* so patients get recurrent bacterial/fungal infections

Classic organisms are catalase-positive organisms.

Catalase-positive organisms break down their own hydrogen peroxide, so the CGD patient cannot “borrow” microbial hydrogen peroxide for killing.

High-yield examples:

* Staphylococcus aureus
* Serratia marcescens
* Burkholderia cepacia
* Nocardia
* Aspergillus

---

### Why granulomas form

Granuloma = organized collection of macrophages trying to wall something off.

Because the immune system cannot fully clear certain microbes, it keeps trying to contain them instead.

So you get chronic inflammation and granulomas, especially in:

* lungs
* lymph nodes
* liver
* GI tract (gastrointestinal tract)
* GU tract (genitourinary tract)

This can cause obstruction, like bowel or urinary tract obstruction.

---

### Diagnosis

Best test: DHR test (dihydrorhodamine test).

DHR becomes fluorescent when neutrophils make ROS (reactive oxygen species).

In CGD:

* little/no ROS
* little/no fluorescence

Older test: NBT test (nitroblue tetrazolium test).

Normal neutrophils turn NBT blue.

CGD neutrophils do not turn blue well.

---

## One-line intuition

CGD = neutrophils can eat the bug, but cannot bleach-bomb it with oxidative burst, so infections persist and granulomas form.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Most common form is X-linked CGD due to CYBB mutation affecting gp91phox, a subunit of NADPH oxidase.

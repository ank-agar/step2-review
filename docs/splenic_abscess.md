# Splenic abscess

### Core idea

A splenic abscess is a pus-filled infection inside the spleen.

Abscess = localized collection of pus from infection.

So the spleen basically gets seeded with bacteria/fungi, then the immune system walls it off into a pocket of pus.

---

### Why it happens

Most commonly, infection reaches the spleen through blood.

Big risk settings:

* infective endocarditis (infection of the heart valves)
* bacteremia (bacteria in the blood)
* immunosuppression (weakened immune system)
* sickle cell disease causing splenic infarcts (dead spleen tissue from blocked blood flow)
* trauma

Reasoning:

* the spleen filters blood
* if infected blood passes through, organisms can lodge there
* if there is already damaged/dead splenic tissue, bacteria love to grow there

Dead tissue + bacteria = abscess.

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

* fever
* LUQ pain (left upper quadrant pain)
* leukocytosis (high white blood cell count)
* sometimes left shoulder pain

Left shoulder pain happens because the spleen sits near the diaphragm; diaphragm irritation can refer pain to the shoulder through the phrenic nerve.

---

### Imaging idea

On imaging, a splenic abscess often looks like a cystic/low-density lesion in the spleen.

If you see gas inside a splenic lesion, that is very suspicious for abscess because gas suggests infection by gas-forming organisms.

---

## One-line intuition

The spleen filters infected blood, bacteria get trapped in damaged splenic tissue, and a walled-off pus pocket forms.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Splenic abscess is classically associated with infective endocarditis because septic emboli (infected clots/debris traveling in blood) can lodge in the spleen and seed infection.

## Vulvovaginal candidiasis

![Image](https://dermnetnz.org/assets/Uploads/1266-small.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Candida_Gram_stain.jpg/960px-Candida_Gram_stain.jpg)

Vulvovaginal candidiasis means a yeast infection of the:

* vulva (external female genital area)
* vagina (internal canal)

Most commonly caused by Candida albicans, which is a fungus/yeast.

---

### Core idea

Candida can normally live in the vagina without causing symptoms.

Disease happens when it overgrows and triggers inflammation.

Classic findings:

* vulvar/vaginal itching
* burning
* soreness
* pain with urination or sex
* thick white “cottage cheese” discharge
* normal vaginal pH

---

### Why it happens

Normally, vaginal bacteria help keep Candida controlled.

If that balance gets disrupted, Candida can overgrow.

Common triggers:

* antibiotics (kill normal protective bacteria)
* pregnancy or estrogen exposure
* diabetes mellitus (high glucose helps yeast grow)
* immunosuppression (weaker immune control)

---

### Diagnosis clue

On potassium hydroxide prep, aka KOH prep (a microscopy test using potassium hydroxide to dissolve cells/debris), you can see:

* budding yeast
* pseudohyphae (elongated Candida forms that look like fungal filaments)

This is why the microscopy image matters: Candida is not just “discharge” clinically; you can actually see fungal forms.

---

## One-line intuition

Vulvovaginal candidiasis is Candida overgrowth in the vagina/vulva causing itchy inflammation and thick white discharge, usually with normal vaginal pH.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Normal vaginal pH helps distinguish Candida from bacterial vaginosis and Trichomonas vaginalis (a sexually transmitted protozoan parasite), which usually raise vaginal pH.

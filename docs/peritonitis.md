# Peritonitis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c5/Peritoneum.svg)

Peritonitis = inflammation of the peritoneum (thin membrane lining the abdominal wall and covering many abdominal organs).

Think:

> irritated abdominal lining = severe abdominal pain + guarding/rigidity.

---

### Core idea

The peritoneum is normally a smooth, slippery lining.

When it gets inflamed by bacteria, pus, blood, bile, stool, or gastric contents, it becomes extremely painful.

That is why peritonitis is classically an **acute abdomen** (sudden severe abdominal condition often needing urgent surgery).

---

### Why the abdomen becomes rigid

The parietal peritoneum (peritoneum lining the abdominal wall) has somatic nerves (body-wall pain nerves).

So when it is irritated:

* pain is sharp and well localized
* the abdominal muscles reflexively tighten
* you get guarding and rebound tenderness

This is the body trying to keep the inflamed peritoneum from moving.

---

### Common causes

Peritonitis often comes from a hole or leak in the gastrointestinal tract (GI tract = digestive tube from mouth to anus), such as:

* ruptured appendix
* perforated peptic ulcer
* diverticulitis with perforation
* bowel ischemia (low blood flow causing bowel injury)
* trauma or surgery

Also important:

* spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, or SBP (infection of ascitic fluid without an obvious bowel perforation)
* peritoneal dialysis, or PD (dialysis using the peritoneal cavity), which can introduce infection

---

### Symptoms

Classic findings:

* severe abdominal pain
* fever
* nausea/vomiting
* abdominal tenderness
* guarding
* rebound tenderness
* rigid “board-like” abdomen

If severe, bacteria/inflammation can spread into the blood and cause sepsis (life-threatening whole-body inflammatory response to infection).

---

## One-line intuition

Peritonitis is when the abdominal lining gets contaminated and inflamed, so even small movements of the abdomen become very painful.

---

## Pearl 🔥

SBP (spontaneous bacterial peritonitis) is classically tested in patients with cirrhosis (chronic liver scarring) and ascites (fluid in the abdomen).

Diagnosis: ascitic fluid neutrophils ≥ 250 cells/mm³.

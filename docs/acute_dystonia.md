## Acute dystonia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Basal_ganglia_circuits.svg/1280px-Basal_ganglia_circuits.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Dopamine_pathways.svg/1280px-Dopamine_pathways.svg.png)

![Image](https://psychonautwiki.org/w/images/2/2f/Medication-induced_dystonia.JPG)

Acute dystonia is a sudden, involuntary muscle spasm after a dopamine-blocking drug.

Break it down:

* **acute** = happens quickly
* **dystonia** = abnormal sustained muscle contraction/posture

So:

> acute dystonia = sudden muscle twisting/spasm, usually soon after starting or increasing a dopamine-blocking medication.

Classic causes:

* antipsychotics, especially high-potency typical antipsychotics like haloperidol
* metoclopramide
* prochlorperazine

Metoclopramide and prochlorperazine are antiemetics (anti-nausea/vomiting drugs) that also block dopamine.

---

### What it looks like

Acute dystonia often affects the face and neck:

* **torticollis** = painful twisting of the neck
* **trismus** = jaw spasm/lockjaw
* **oculogyric crisis** = eyes forced upward
* tongue protrusion
* facial grimacing
* back arching

Rare but dangerous:

* **laryngeal dystonia** = spasm of the larynx (voice box), which can threaten the airway

---

### Why it happens

The basal ganglia help smooth out movement.

Basal ganglia = deep brain movement-control circuits.

Normally, movement is balanced by:

* dopamine
* acetylcholine

Acetylcholine is a neurotransmitter that can push muscle-control circuits in the opposite direction from dopamine.

When a drug blocks **D2 receptors** (dopamine type 2 receptors), dopamine signaling suddenly drops.

That creates a relative excess of acetylcholine activity.

So the movement system becomes unbalanced:

* less dopamine
* relatively more acetylcholine
* basal ganglia output gets abnormal
* muscles contract in painful fixed positions

That is why anticholinergic drugs help.

Anticholinergic means blocking acetylcholine.

---

### Treatment

Treat with:

* benztropine
* diphenhydramine

Diphenhydramine is an antihistamine, but it also has anticholinergic effects.

Then stop or reduce the offending drug if possible.

---

### High-yield timing

Acute dystonia is an **early EPS**.

**EPS** (extrapyramidal symptoms) = movement side effects from dopamine blockade in the basal ganglia.

Timing pattern:

* acute dystonia = hours to days
* akathisia = days to weeks
* parkinsonism = weeks
* tardive dyskinesia = months to years

---

## One-line intuition

Dopamine blockade suddenly removes the movement-smoothing signal, so acetylcholine becomes relatively too strong and muscles lock into spasms.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Acute dystonia is treated with **benztropine or diphenhydramine**, while tardive dyskinesia is treated with **VMAT2 inhibitors** (vesicular monoamine transporter 2 inhibitors) like valbenazine.

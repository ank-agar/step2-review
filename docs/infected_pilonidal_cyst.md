### Core idea

An infected pilonidal cyst is basically:

* a hair/debris-filled pocket under the skin
* located near the tailbone, at the top of the buttock cleft
* that becomes infected and turns into an abscess

Abscess = a walled-off collection of pus.

Pilonidal literally means:

* pilo- = hair
* nidal = nest

So think:

> "hair nest" under the skin near the tailbone that gets infected.

---

### Why it happens

Loose hairs get pushed into the skin in the gluteal cleft (butt crack).

The body sees the hair as foreign material, so it causes:

* inflammation
* bacterial infection
* pus formation
* pain/swelling

This is why it often hurts more when sitting: pressure is being put right on the inflamed abscess.

---

### Classic findings

Look for:

* painful swelling near the top of the buttock cleft
* redness/warmth
* drainage of pus or blood
* foul smell sometimes
* fever if more severe
* small midline pits or openings in the cleft

If it drains, pain may temporarily improve because pressure inside the abscess goes down.

---

### Treatment idea

If it is acutely infected and full of pus, antibiotics alone usually are not enough.

Why?

Because antibiotics have poor penetration into a closed pus pocket.

So the key treatment is usually:

* incision and drainage

Incision and drainage = cutting it open enough to let pus out.

Antibiotics may be added if there is surrounding cellulitis (skin/soft tissue infection), fever, immunosuppression (weakened immune system), or more extensive infection.

---

## One-line intuition

An infected pilonidal cyst is a painful pus-filled "hair nest" near the tailbone.

---

## Pearl

Pilonidal disease is most common in young men and is associated with hair, friction, and a deep gluteal cleft.

### Core idea

Constrictive pericarditis = the pericardium (fibrous sac around the heart) becomes thick, scarred, stiff, and sometimes calcified.

So the heart is trapped in a rigid shell.

The myocardium (heart muscle) may contract fine, but the ventricles cannot fill well during diastole (relaxation/filling phase).

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The stiff pericardium prevents the heart from expanding, so blood backs up behind the heart.

That causes right-sided heart failure findings:

* JVP (jugular venous pressure) increases
* peripheral edema (leg swelling)
* ascites (fluid in the abdomen)
* hepatomegaly (enlarged liver)

Think:

> heart cannot fill → venous blood has nowhere to go → veins get congested.

---

### Classic exam findings

* **Pericardial knock:** early diastolic sound caused by sudden stopping of ventricular filling
* **Kussmaul sign:** JVP (jugular venous pressure) rises with inspiration

Why Kussmaul sign happens:

Normally, inspiration pulls more venous blood into the right heart.

But in constrictive pericarditis, the right ventricle cannot expand to accept that extra blood, so blood backs up into the neck veins.

---

### Causes

Common Step 1 causes:

* prior cardiac surgery
* chest radiation
* recurrent pericarditis (inflammation of the pericardium)
* TB (tuberculosis), especially worldwide

---

## One-line intuition

Constrictive pericarditis is a stiff external shell around the heart causing a diastolic filling problem.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Constrictive pericarditis can mimic restrictive cardiomyopathy (stiff heart muscle disease), but constrictive pericarditis is an outside-the-heart problem, while restrictive cardiomyopathy is a myocardium (heart muscle) problem.

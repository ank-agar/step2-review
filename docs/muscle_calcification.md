### Core idea

Muscle calcification means calcium deposits form inside or around muscle.

The Step 1 version is usually:

* **myositis ossificans** = bone-like tissue forming inside muscle after trauma
* **heterotopic ossification** = bone forming in the wrong place, outside the skeleton

Break it down:

* **myo-** = muscle
* **-itis** = inflammation
* **ossificans** = turning into bone

So myositis ossificans sounds like “inflamed muscle turning into bone.”

---

### Why it happens

Usually there is muscle injury first:

* trauma
* surgery
* burns
* neurologic injury, like spinal cord injury or traumatic brain injury

The injury causes inflammation and tissue repair signals.

Those repair signals can accidentally push local stem-like connective tissue cells toward an **osteoblast** fate.

* **osteoblast** = bone-forming cell

So instead of just repairing muscle, the area starts making mineralized bone-like tissue.

---

### What it looks like

Early:

* painful swollen muscle
* may look like a soft tissue mass

Later:

* firm lump
* decreased range of motion if near a joint
* X-ray shows calcification/ossification in soft tissue

Key imaging idea:

* mature myositis ossificans often calcifies more at the outside/rim
* some malignant tumors can calcify more centrally

That “rim first” pattern is a useful clue.

---

## One-line intuition

Muscle calcification is basically injured muscle healing incorrectly, so calcium/bone gets laid down where soft muscle should be.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Myositis ossificans is a classic example of **metaplasia**.

**Metaplasia** = one mature cell/tissue type is replaced by another mature cell/tissue type.

HY nuance: it is not skeletal muscle fibers literally becoming bone; it is usually local connective tissue/progenitor cells in the injured muscle area differentiating into bone-forming cells.

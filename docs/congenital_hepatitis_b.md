### Core idea

Congenital hepatitis B means a baby gets HBV (hepatitis B virus) from the mother around birth.

Most Step 1 questions are really testing:

* mother is HBsAg-positive (hepatitis B surface antigen-positive)
* baby is exposed during delivery
* newborn needs vaccine + HBIG (hepatitis B immune globulin) right away

Important: this is not inherited genetically.

It is vertical transmission, meaning infection passes from mother to baby.

---

### Why babies are high risk

Adults usually clear acute HBV infection because their immune system attacks infected liver cells.

Newborns have an immature immune response, so they often do not clear the virus well.

That means congenital/perinatal HBV has a high risk of becoming chronic hepatitis B.

Chronic HBV can later cause:

* cirrhosis
* hepatocellular carcinoma, meaning liver cancer

---

### What to do for the newborn

If the mother is HBsAg-positive:

* give hepatitis B vaccine
* give HBIG (hepatitis B immune globulin)
* give both within 12 hours of birth

Why both?

* vaccine = active immunity, meaning the baby makes their own antibodies
* HBIG = passive immunity, meaning pre-made antibodies give immediate protection

So HBIG protects now, vaccine protects later.

---

### Serology intuition

HBsAg (hepatitis B surface antigen) means:

* current infection

HBeAg (hepatitis B e antigen) means:

* high viral replication
* more infectious
* higher risk of mother-to-baby transmission

So a pregnant patient who is HBsAg-positive and HBeAg-positive is especially high risk for transmitting HBV to the baby.

---

## One-line intuition

Congenital hepatitis B is baby-acquired HBV from an infected mother, and because newborns cannot clear it well, the big danger is chronic infection.

---

## Pearl

HBV is a DNA virus, but it uses reverse transcriptase, which is why it is treated/prevented conceptually like a retrovirus-adjacent virus on Step 1.

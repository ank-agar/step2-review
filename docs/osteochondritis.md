### Core idea

Osteochondritis means inflammation/injury involving:

* osteo- = bone
* chondro- = cartilage
* -itis = inflammation

So it is a problem at the bone-cartilage interface of a joint.

The most tested form is **osteochondritis dissecans** (OCD) (a condition where a piece of bone plus overlying cartilage can partially or fully separate from the joint surface).

---

### What happens

A small area of subchondral bone (bone just under cartilage) loses blood supply or gets repetitive microtrauma (tiny repeated injuries).

That area becomes weak, so the overlying cartilage can crack or loosen.

If it progresses, the bone-cartilage piece can break off and become a loose body inside the joint.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Pain with activity:** the damaged joint surface is stressed during movement
* **Swelling:** joint irritation causes inflammation/effusion (extra fluid in the joint)
* **Catching/locking:** a loose fragment can physically block smooth joint motion
* **Instability/giving way:** the joint surface is no longer smooth and mechanically reliable

---

### Common sites

Most common:

* knee, especially the medial femoral condyle (inner part of the thigh bone at the knee)

Also can affect:

* elbow
* ankle

---

## One-line intuition

Osteochondritis dissecans is like a pothole forming in the joint surface: the bone under the cartilage weakens, and the cartilage-bone cap can loosen or break off.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In a young athlete with knee pain + swelling + catching/locking, think **osteochondritis dissecans**; MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) helps determine whether the fragment is stable or unstable.

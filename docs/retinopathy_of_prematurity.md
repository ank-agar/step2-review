Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) is abnormal retinal blood vessel growth in premature infants.

Retina = the light-sensing layer in the back of the eye.

Premature babies are at risk because their retinal vessels are not done growing yet.

---

### Core idea

In the womb, retinal blood vessels grow gradually from the optic disc (where the optic nerve exits the eye) outward.

If a baby is born too early:

* the retina is still partly avascular (without blood vessels)
* oxygen exposure after birth can disrupt normal vessel growth
* later, the oxygen-starved retina releases VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor)
* VEGF makes fragile abnormal vessels grow

Those abnormal vessels can scar and pull on the retina.

Worst case:

* retinal detachment
* blindness

---

### Why oxygen matters

ROP has a classic 2-phase idea:

* **Phase 1:** too much relative oxygen after birth suppresses VEGF, so normal retinal vessel growth stops
* **Phase 2:** the growing retina becomes hypoxic (low oxygen), so VEGF rises and abnormal neovascularization (new blood vessel growth) happens

So the problem is not just "oxygen is bad."

It is:

> premature retina + disrupted oxygen environment = abnormal vessel growth.

---

### How it is graded

ROP is described by:

* **Zone:** where it is in the retina
* **Stage:** how severe the abnormal vessel growth is
* **Plus disease:** dilated/tortuous posterior retinal vessels, meaning more severe disease

Stages:

* Stage 1: thin demarcation line
* Stage 2: raised ridge
* Stage 3: extraretinal fibrovascular proliferation
* Stage 4: partial retinal detachment
* Stage 5: total retinal detachment

---

## One-line intuition

ROP is when a premature baby's unfinished retinal blood vessels stop growing normally, then overgrow abnormally and can pull the retina off.

---

## Pearl 🔥

High-yield risk factors:

* prematurity
* low birth weight
* oxygen therapy

Classic treatment for severe ROP is laser ablation of the peripheral avascular retina or anti-VEGF therapy.

## Mirizzi syndrome

![Image](https://mdpi-res.com/diagnostics/diagnostics-11-01660/article_deploy/html/images/diagnostics-11-01660-g001.png)

![Image](https://www.e-ce.org/upload/thumbnails/ce-2021-015f1.jpg)

![Image](https://www.e-ce.org/upload/thumbnails/ce-2021-015f2.jpg)

Mirizzi syndrome is when a gallstone gets stuck in the gallbladder neck/Hartmann pouch or cystic duct and compresses the nearby bile duct from the outside.

Key anatomy:

* gallbladder = stores bile
* cystic duct = tube from gallbladder
* common hepatic duct = bile duct coming from liver
* common bile duct = shared bile duct going toward the intestine

So the core problem is:

> a stuck gallstone near the cystic duct squeezes the common hepatic duct/common bile duct, causing obstructive jaundice.

Obstructive jaundice means bile cannot drain normally, so bilirubin backs up into the blood.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Jaundice:** bile is blocked, so bilirubin rises
* **RUQ pain:** right upper quadrant pain from gallbladder inflammation/stone disease
* **Fever/cholangitis:** stagnant bile can get infected; cholangitis means infection of the bile ducts
* **Dilated ducts above the blockage:** bile backs up upstream
* **Normal duct below the blockage:** the obstruction is higher up near the gallbladder neck/cystic duct

---

### Intuition

Most gallstones block from inside a duct.

Mirizzi syndrome is different:

* the stone is usually **not inside the common bile duct**
* it is stuck next door in the cystic duct/gallbladder neck
* it compresses the bile duct from the outside

Like a rock stuck beside a hose, pinching the hose closed.

---

### Why surgeons care

Chronic inflammation can make the gallbladder stick to the bile duct and even erode into it, forming a cholecystobiliary fistula.

Cholecystobiliary fistula = abnormal connection between the gallbladder and bile duct.

This distorts anatomy and increases risk of bile duct injury during cholecystectomy, which means surgical removal of the gallbladder.

---

## One-line intuition

Mirizzi syndrome = gallstone stuck in the gallbladder neck/cystic duct compresses the bile duct from the outside, causing obstructive jaundice.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY clue: obstructive jaundice + gallstones + bile duct dilation above the gallbladder neck with a normal distal common bile duct should make you think **Mirizzi syndrome**, not just ordinary choledocholithiasis.

Choledocholithiasis = gallstone inside the common bile duct.

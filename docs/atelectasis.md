### Core idea

Atelectasis = collapse of alveoli (tiny air sacs in the lung).

So that part of the lung becomes:

* poorly inflated
* low in oxygen exchange
* smaller in volume

Think:

> alveoli lose air -> lung tissue shrinks down.

---

### Why it happens

Common mechanisms:

* **Airway obstruction:** mucus plug, tumor, foreign body blocks airflow
* **Compression:** pleural effusion (fluid around lung), pneumothorax (air in pleural space), mass pushes on lung
* **Post-op shallow breathing:** pain/anesthesia makes patient take small breaths, so alveoli do not fully open
* **Surfactant loss:** surfactant (substance that lowers alveolar surface tension) is low, so alveoli collapse more easily

The obstruction idea is the easiest:

air gets trapped past the blockage -> blood absorbs that air -> no new air enters -> alveoli collapse.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Low oxygen:** collapsed alveoli cannot participate in gas exchange
* **White opacity on chest X-ray:** collapsed lung becomes denser than air-filled lung
* **Volume loss:** the affected lung region physically shrinks
* **Trachea/mediastinum may shift toward the affected side:** collapsed lung pulls structures toward it
* **Decreased breath sounds:** less air is moving through that collapsed region

Key contrast:

* **Atelectasis:** volume loss, structures pulled toward collapse
* **Pleural effusion/pneumothorax:** extra fluid/air pushes structures away if large

---

### Common Step 1 setting

Postoperative patient with fever and mild hypoxemia soon after surgery.

Why?

Pain + anesthesia -> shallow breaths -> alveoli collapse.

Treatment/prevention:

* incentive spirometry
* early ambulation
* good pain control

---

## One-line intuition

Atelectasis is lung air sacs collapsing, so that area becomes airless, small, and bad at oxygen exchange.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Atelectasis causes an intrapulmonary shunt (blood passes by alveoli that are not ventilated), giving low V/Q (ventilation/perfusion) because perfusion remains but ventilation drops.

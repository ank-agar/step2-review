### Core idea

Fat embolism = fat globules enter the bloodstream and block small vessels.

Classic setting:

* long bone fracture
* pelvic fracture
* orthopedic surgery

Why long bones?

Because bone marrow contains fat. When the bone breaks, marrow fat can leak into torn veins and travel through blood.

---

### What happens

Fat globules usually go first to the lungs.

This causes:

* mechanical blockage of small pulmonary vessels
* inflammation from free fatty acids
* endothelial injury, meaning damage to the blood vessel lining
* hypoxemia, meaning low blood oxygen

So the patient gets respiratory distress.

---

### Fat embolism syndrome

Fat embolism syndrome (FES) is when fat emboli cause symptoms.

Classic triad:

* respiratory distress
* neurologic symptoms, like confusion or coma
* petechial rash, meaning tiny red-purple pinpoint bleeds in the skin

Timing is HY:

* usually 24-72 hours after fracture or orthopedic trauma

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Respiratory distress:** fat blocks lung microvasculature and causes inflammation
* **Confusion/coma:** fat can reach brain vessels or cause severe hypoxemia
* **Petechiae:** fat damages small dermal capillaries, especially in upper body areas
* **Thrombocytopenia:** platelets get consumed around fat globules
* **Anemia:** inflammation and microvascular injury can damage red blood cells

---

## One-line intuition

A broken long bone spills marrow fat into blood, and the fat clogs tiny vessels, especially in the lungs and brain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On Step 1, think fat embolism when a patient has a long bone fracture and then 1-3 days later develops shortness of breath, confusion, and petechiae.

# Mixed acid-base disturbance

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/20/Grogono_Acid-Base-Diagram.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/dd/Acid-base_homeostasis.png)

A **mixed acid-base disturbance** means the patient has **more than one primary acid-base problem happening at the same time**.

Key idea:

* **simple disorder** = one main problem + normal compensation
* **mixed disorder** = two or more main problems at once

So it is **not just compensation**.

---

### Core intuition

Your body controls blood pH (how acidic/basic the blood is) mainly with:

* lungs: control CO2 (carbon dioxide), which acts like acid
* kidneys: control HCO3- (bicarbonate), which acts like base

So:

* high CO2 = respiratory acidosis
* low CO2 = respiratory alkalosis
* low HCO3- = metabolic acidosis
* high HCO3- = metabolic alkalosis

A mixed disturbance means **both systems are pushing pH in abnormal ways**, sometimes in the same direction, sometimes in opposite directions.

---

### Why it matters

In a simple disorder, compensation should make sense.

Example:

* metabolic acidosis = low HCO3-
* body compensates by hyperventilating
* hyperventilation lowers CO2

So low HCO3- + appropriately low CO2 = simple metabolic acidosis with respiratory compensation.

But if the CO2 is **not low enough**, or is even high, that means there is another problem too.

Example:

* low HCO3- = metabolic acidosis
* high CO2 = respiratory acidosis

That is mixed metabolic acidosis + respiratory acidosis.

---

### Common examples

**DKA + vomiting**

DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis) causes metabolic acidosis because ketones are acids.

Vomiting causes metabolic alkalosis because you lose stomach acid.

So the pH may look less abnormal than expected because acid and base problems are fighting each other.

---

**Salicylate poisoning**

Salicylates, like aspirin, classically cause:

* respiratory alkalosis early because they stimulate the respiratory center, causing hyperventilation
* metabolic acidosis later because they build up organic acids

So this is a classic mixed disorder:

* low CO2
* low HCO3-

---

**COPD + diarrhea**

COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) can cause respiratory acidosis because patients retain CO2.

Diarrhea causes metabolic acidosis because HCO3- is lost in stool.

Together:

* high CO2
* low HCO3-
* very low pH

Both problems are pushing toward acidosis.

---

### How to spot it on ABG questions

ABG (arterial blood gas) gives pH, PaCO2 (arterial carbon dioxide pressure), and HCO3- (bicarbonate).

Ask:

1. Is the pH acidotic or alkalotic?
2. Is CO2 explaining it?
3. Is HCO3- explaining it?
4. Is compensation appropriate?

Big clue:

* if compensation is **too much, too little, or going the wrong direction**, think mixed disorder.

For metabolic acidosis, use Winter formula:

Expected PaCO2 = 1.5 x HCO3- + 8 +/- 2

If measured PaCO2 is:

* higher than expected = extra respiratory acidosis
* lower than expected = extra respiratory alkalosis

---

## One-line intuition

A mixed acid-base disturbance is when the ABG does not fit one clean story because **more than one primary acid-base process is happening at once**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, **salicylate poisoning = mixed respiratory alkalosis + metabolic acidosis** is the classic testable mixed acid-base disorder.

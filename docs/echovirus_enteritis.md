### Core idea

Echovirus enteritis = intestinal inflammation/infection caused by an echovirus.

Break it down:

* echo = enteric cytopathic human orphan virus
* entero- = intestine
* -itis = inflammation

So literally:

> an echovirus infecting the gut and causing intestinal irritation.

---

### What kind of virus is echovirus?

Echovirus is an enterovirus (virus that commonly enters through the gastrointestinal tract).

It is:

* non-enveloped
* positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus
* in the Picornaviridae family
* transmitted mainly fecal-oral

Positive-sense RNA (ribonucleic acid) means the viral RNA can act like mRNA (messenger RNA) right away, so host ribosomes can translate it into viral proteins.

---

### Why it causes enteritis

The virus is swallowed, reaches the intestine, and infects intestinal epithelial cells (cells lining the gut).

That leads to:

* local inflammation
* watery diarrhea
* abdominal discomfort
* fever sometimes
* nausea/vomiting sometimes

The key intuition:

Echovirus enters through the gut, replicates in gut lining cells, and the immune response irritates the intestine.

---

### Bigger clinical picture

Many echovirus infections are mild or asymptomatic.

But echovirus can also cause:

* aseptic meningitis (meningitis without typical bacterial growth)
* rash
* neonatal sepsis-like illness
* myocarditis (heart muscle inflammation), rarely

So if you see echovirus on Step 1, do not think only diarrhea.

Think:

> enterovirus family + fecal-oral spread + aseptic meningitis/rash possible.

---

### One-line intuition

Echovirus enteritis is basically a fecal-oral, non-enveloped RNA virus irritating the intestinal lining after replicating in the gut.

---

## Pearl

Non-enveloped viruses are generally tougher in the environment because they do not have a fragile lipid envelope. That is why many fecal-oral viruses, like enteroviruses, can survive stomach acid and spread well through contaminated hands, water, or surfaces.

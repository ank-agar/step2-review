### Core idea

Trigeminal neuralgia is severe, sudden facial pain from irritation of the trigeminal nerve (cranial nerve V, the main sensory nerve of the face).

Classically it feels like:

* electric shock pain
* stabbing pain
* brief episodes lasting seconds to minutes
* usually one-sided

---

### Where the pain is

The trigeminal nerve has 3 branches:

* V1 = ophthalmic branch: forehead/eye area
* V2 = maxillary branch: cheek/upper jaw
* V3 = mandibular branch: lower jaw

Most cases affect V2 or V3.

---

### Why it happens

Most classic cases are due to a blood vessel compressing the trigeminal nerve near the pons (part of the brainstem).

That compression damages myelin (the insulating layer around nerves).

Less myelin means the nerve becomes electrically unstable, so tiny normal stimuli can trigger huge pain signals.

So things like:

* chewing
* talking
* brushing teeth
* shaving
* touching the face
* cold wind

can trigger sharp facial pain.

---

### Important causes

Think:

* vascular compression, usually by the superior cerebellar artery
* multiple sclerosis (MS, autoimmune demyelination in the central nervous system)
* tumor compressing the nerve

MS is especially important if the patient is younger or has bilateral symptoms.

---

## One-line intuition

The trigeminal nerve is like an exposed facial pain wire: small touch signals can short-circuit into severe electric shock pain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

First-line treatment is carbamazepine or oxcarbazepine, because they block voltage-gated sodium channels and calm down overactive firing nerves.

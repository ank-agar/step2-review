## Primary insomnia

![Image](https://sleep.hms.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/assets/Insomnia%20Pictures/NeurocogTheory-9.22.21.PNG)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279126/bin/fnct-antmy-hyptl-pit_fig-41.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/9474.jpg)

Primary insomnia means insomnia that is **not caused by another medical, psychiatric, substance-related, or environmental problem**.

So the patient has trouble:

* falling asleep
* staying asleep
* waking too early
* feeling nonrestorative sleep

but you cannot explain it by something else like:

* major depressive disorder
* generalized anxiety disorder
* obstructive sleep apnea
* hyperthyroidism
* caffeine/stimulants
* pain
* poor sleep environment

---

### Core idea

The brain is tired, but the arousal system is still too active.

Think:

> “Sleep drive is present, but the brain cannot fully switch into sleep mode.”

This is why insomnia is often linked to **hyperarousal** (excess wake-promoting brain/body activation).

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Normally sleep needs:

* enough sleep pressure
* proper circadian rhythm (internal 24-hour sleep-wake clock)
* low arousal

In primary insomnia, the arousal piece stays too high.

So the patient may be lying in bed exhausted, but the body is still in a wake-like state:

* increased sympathetic tone (fight-or-flight activity)
* racing thoughts
* increased cortisol (stress hormone)
* increased wake-promoting brain activity

That is why trying harder to sleep can make it worse: effort increases arousal.

---

### Diagnosis intuition

Primary insomnia is basically a diagnosis of exclusion.

You first ask:

> “Is something else causing the insomnia?”

If yes, treat that cause.

If no obvious cause is found, then primary insomnia becomes more likely.

---

### Treatment

First-line treatment is **CBT-I** (cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia).

CBT-I works by breaking the learned association:

> bed = frustration/wakefulness

and rebuilding:

> bed = sleep

Common pieces include:

* stimulus control
* sleep restriction
* relaxation training
* changing maladaptive sleep thoughts

---

## One-line intuition

Primary insomnia = trouble sleeping because the sleep-wake switch is biased toward “awake,” not because another disease is keeping the person up.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For USMLE Step 1, remember: **primary insomnia is not due to another disorder**, while insomnia from depression often has **early-morning awakening** and other depressive symptoms like anhedonia (loss of interest/pleasure).

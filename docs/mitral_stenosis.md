### Core idea

Mitral stenosis means narrowing of the mitral valve.

The mitral valve sits between:

* LA (left atrium)
* LV (left ventricle)

So if the valve is narrowed, blood has trouble going:

> LA -> LV

especially during diastole (ventricular filling).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Increased LA pressure:** blood backs up behind the tight mitral valve
* **LA dilation:** the left atrium stretches from chronically high pressure
* **Atrial fibrillation:** stretched atrial tissue can become electrically unstable
* **Pulmonary congestion:** pressure backs up from LA into pulmonary veins/lungs
* **Pulmonary hypertension:** long-term lung vessel pressure can rise

So the sequence is:

> tight mitral valve -> blood stuck in LA -> LA enlargement -> lung backup

---

### Classic auscultation

Mitral stenosis causes:

* loud S1 (first heart sound)
* opening snap
* low-pitched diastolic rumble

Why diastolic?

Because the mitral valve normally opens during diastole, when blood flows from LA to LV.

Why an opening snap?

The stenotic valve is stiff, so when it opens under high LA pressure, it makes a sharp snapping sound.

---

### Most common cause

Classically:

* rheumatic heart disease

Rheumatic heart disease means valve damage after rheumatic fever, which is an immune reaction after group A Streptococcus infection.

---

## One-line intuition

Mitral stenosis is a narrow door between the left atrium and left ventricle, so blood backs up into the left atrium and lungs.

---

## Pearl

Mitral stenosis is associated with atrial fibrillation and left atrial thrombus because LA (left atrial) enlargement causes stasis (slow blood flow) and electrical irritation.

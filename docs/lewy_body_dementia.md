# Lewy body dementia

![Image](https://neurotorium.org/wp-content/uploads/xx-synuclein.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Lewy_bodies_%28alpha_synuclein_inclusions%29.svg/960px-Lewy_bodies_%28alpha_synuclein_inclusions%29.svg.png)

Lewy body dementia is a neurodegenerative disease (progressive neuron loss) caused by abnormal buildup of alpha-synuclein (a neuronal protein) inside neurons.

Those alpha-synuclein clumps are called Lewy bodies.

Core idea:

* Lewy bodies in cortex = dementia/cognitive problems
* Lewy bodies in basal ganglia/substantia nigra = Parkinsonism (slow movement, rigidity, tremor)
* Lewy body effect on visual/attention circuits = visual hallucinations and fluctuating cognition

So it feels like:

> Alzheimer-like dementia + Parkinson-like movement symptoms + early visual hallucinations.

Important symptoms:

* fluctuating cognition = attention/alertness changes a lot during the day
* visual hallucinations = seeing things that are not there
* Parkinsonism = bradykinesia (slow movement), rigidity, resting tremor
* REM sleep behavior disorder = acting out dreams because normal REM (rapid eye movement) sleep paralysis is lost
* autonomic dysfunction = blood pressure, sweating, constipation, urinary issues

Why hallucinations happen:

Lewy bodies disrupt cortical and cholinergic pathways.

Cholinergic means acetylcholine-based signaling, which is important for attention, memory, and perception. When this signaling is impaired, the brain misprocesses visual information.

## DLB vs Parkinson disease dementia

Use the timing rule:

* Dementia with Lewy bodies = dementia happens before or within 1 year of Parkinsonism
* Parkinson disease dementia = Parkinson disease symptoms are present for more than 1 year before dementia

## One-line intuition

Lewy body dementia is alpha-synuclein clogging neurons in both thinking circuits and movement circuits, so patients get dementia plus Parkinson-like findings.

## Pearl 🔥

Patients with Lewy body dementia are very sensitive to antipsychotics (dopamine-blocking drugs), which can severely worsen Parkinsonism and confusion.

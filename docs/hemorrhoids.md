# Hemorrhoids

Hemorrhoids are swollen/enlarged venous cushions in the anal canal.

Basically:

* normal anal cushions help with continence
* increased pressure makes them dilate, stretch, and sometimes prolapse
* then they can bleed, itch, hurt, or feel like a lump

Common causes of increased pressure:

* constipation/straining
* pregnancy
* prolonged sitting
* chronic diarrhea
* low-fiber diet

---

### Internal vs external

The key landmark is the **pectinate line** (line in the anal canal separating visceral-type upper anal canal from somatic-type lower anal canal).

**Internal hemorrhoids:**

* above the pectinate line
* usually painless
* can cause bright red blood with bowel movements

Why painless?

Above the pectinate line has visceral innervation (autonomic nerve supply), which is bad at sensing sharp pain.

**External hemorrhoids:**

* below the pectinate line
* painful, especially if thrombosed
* may form a tender blue-purple lump

Why painful?

Below the pectinate line has somatic innervation (body-wall type sensory nerves), mainly via the inferior rectal nerve from the pudendal nerve.

---

### Why they bleed

Straining increases venous pressure and mechanically irritates the thin mucosa over the hemorrhoid.

So the classic bleeding is:

* bright red
* on toilet paper or coating stool
* not usually mixed deeply into the stool

---

## One-line intuition

Hemorrhoids are pressure-swollen anal venous cushions; internal ones bleed more and hurt less, external ones hurt more because they are below the pectinate line.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 point: internal hemorrhoids drain to the portal system via the superior rectal vein, while external hemorrhoids drain to the systemic circulation via the inferior rectal vein.

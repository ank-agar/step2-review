### Core idea

Nephrolithiasis means kidney stones.

Break it down:

* nephro- = kidney
* lith = stone
* -iasis = condition

So nephrolithiasis = condition of stones forming in the kidney.

These stones are hard mineral deposits that form when urine gets too concentrated.

---

### Why stones form

Normally, urine keeps minerals dissolved.

But if there is:

* low urine volume, usually dehydration
* too much calcium, oxalate, uric acid, phosphate, or cystine
* low citrate, which normally inhibits stone formation

then minerals can precipitate, meaning come out of solution and crystallize.

Think of it like salt water drying out: as water decreases, crystals are more likely to appear.

---

### Why the pain happens

The stone itself hurts most when it moves into or blocks the ureter (tube carrying urine from kidney to bladder).

Blockage causes:

* urine backup
* stretching of the renal pelvis/ureter
* ureteral spasm

That gives classic renal colic, meaning severe wave-like flank pain.

Pain often radiates:

* flank
* lower abdomen
* groin

because the ureter and groin pain pathways overlap.

---

### Classic findings

* severe colicky flank pain
* hematuria (blood in urine)
* nausea/vomiting
* urinary urgency if stone is near the bladder

Fever with a stone is dangerous because it suggests infected obstruction, which is a urologic emergency.

---

### Common stone types

* calcium oxalate: most common
* calcium phosphate
* uric acid
* struvite, usually from urease-positive bacteria
* cystine, from cystinuria (inherited cystine transport defect)

---

## One-line intuition

Nephrolithiasis is when concentrated urine lets minerals crystallize into stones, and symptoms explode when a stone blocks the ureter.

---

## Pearl

Calcium oxalate stones are associated with envelope-shaped crystals on urinalysis and can be seen with ethylene glycol poisoning, which also causes anion gap metabolic acidosis.

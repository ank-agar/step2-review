### Core idea

Kawasaki disease is an acute vasculitis (inflammation of blood vessels) in kids, especially medium-sized arteries.

Most important vessel involved:

* coronary arteries (arteries that supply the heart muscle)

So the big fear is:

* coronary artery aneurysm (ballooning/dilation of a weakened artery wall)

---

### Classic findings

Think: prolonged fever + mucocutaneous (mucus membranes + skin) findings.

Classic features:

* fever for at least 5 days
* bilateral conjunctivitis (red eyes on both sides, usually without pus)
* strawberry tongue/cracked red lips
* polymorphous rash (rash with variable appearance)
* cervical lymphadenopathy (swollen neck lymph nodes)
* red/swollen hands and feet, then peeling skin later

---

### Why the findings happen

The immune system inflames blood vessels throughout the body.

That causes:

* **red eyes:** inflamed conjunctival vessels
* **strawberry tongue/cracked lips:** inflamed oral mucosa (lining of the mouth)
* **rash:** inflamed small skin vessels
* **swollen hands/feet:** leaky inflamed vessels cause edema (fluid swelling)
* **coronary aneurysms:** inflammation weakens the coronary artery wall, so it balloons outward

---

### Treatment

Treat with:

* IVIG (intravenous immunoglobulin, pooled antibodies given through a vein)
* aspirin

Why IVIG helps:

* it calms the abnormal immune activation
* it lowers the risk of coronary artery aneurysms

---

## One-line intuition

Kawasaki disease is a kid with persistent fever + red mucosa/skin findings, where the Step 1 danger is coronary artery aneurysm.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Kawasaki disease is one of the few times children get aspirin, because the benefit for preventing/treating coronary complications outweighs the usual concern for Reye syndrome (acute encephalopathy and liver injury after aspirin use in children with viral illness).

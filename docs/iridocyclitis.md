### Core idea

Iridocyclitis = inflammation of the:

* iris (colored part of the eye that controls pupil size)
* ciliary body (muscle/secretory tissue behind the iris that helps focus the lens and makes aqueous humor, the fluid in the front of the eye)

So it is basically a form of anterior uveitis (inflammation of the front part of the uvea, the vascular middle layer of the eye).

---

### Symptoms

Classically causes:

* painful red eye
* photophobia (light sensitivity)
* blurry vision
* small or irregular pupil
* tearing

Photophobia happens because bright light makes the iris constrict, and moving an inflamed iris/ciliary body hurts.

---

### What you see on eye exam

Inflammatory cells leak into the anterior chamber (fluid-filled space between cornea and iris), causing:

* cells and flare (white blood cells and protein floating in the anterior chamber)
* keratic precipitates (inflammatory deposits on the inner cornea)
* posterior synechiae (iris sticking to the lens)
* sometimes hypopyon (pus-like white blood cell layering in the anterior chamber)

---

### Why complications happen

Inflammation can make the iris stick to nearby structures.

If the iris sticks to the lens, aqueous humor cannot flow normally through the pupil, so pressure can rise and cause glaucoma (optic nerve damage from high eye pressure).

Chronic inflammation can also increase risk of cataracts (clouding of the lens).

---

### Associations

Think autoimmune/inflammatory causes, especially:

* HLA-B27 disease (human leukocyte antigen B27-associated disease)
* ankylosing spondylitis (inflammatory arthritis of the spine/sacroiliac joints)
* reactive arthritis (arthritis after gastrointestinal or genitourinary infection)
* psoriatic arthritis (arthritis associated with psoriasis)
* inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn disease or ulcerative colitis)

It can also be triggered by infection or trauma.

---

## One-line intuition

Iridocyclitis is an inflamed, angry iris + ciliary body, so light and pupil movement become painful.

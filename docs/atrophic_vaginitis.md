## Atrophic vaginitis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/85/Vaginal_Mucosa_Normal_vs_Menopause.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/24345.jpg)

![Image](https://www.mayoclinic.org/-/media/kcms/gbs/patient-consumer/images/2021/09/11/01/28/vaginal-atrophy-dryness-8col-3815106-002-0.jpg)

Atrophic vaginitis is thinning, drying, and inflammation of the vaginal mucosa (vaginal lining) due to low estrogen.

Most commonly happens after menopause.

Break it down:

* atrophic = tissue gets thin/shrunken
* vaginitis = inflammation of the vagina

So:

> low estrogen makes the vaginal lining thin, dry, fragile, and irritated.

---

### Why low estrogen causes this

Estrogen normally keeps the vaginal epithelium (surface lining) thick, moist, elastic, and glycogen-rich.

Glycogen feeds lactobacilli (normal protective vaginal bacteria), which make lactic acid and keep vaginal pH acidic.

When estrogen falls:

* epithelium becomes thin
* lubrication decreases
* rugae (vaginal folds) decrease
* pH rises because lactobacilli decrease
* tissue becomes fragile and inflamed

That is why patients can get:

* vaginal dryness
* itching/burning
* dyspareunia (pain with sex)
* postcoital bleeding (bleeding after sex)
* recurrent UTIs (urinary tract infections)

---

### Exam clue

The vaginal mucosa may look:

* pale
* smooth
* dry
* thin/friable

Friable means it bleeds easily when touched.

---

## One-line intuition

Estrogen is like the “maintenance signal” for vaginal lining; when estrogen drops, the lining becomes thin, dry, less acidic, and easy to irritate.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Atrophic vaginitis is now often called GSM (genitourinary syndrome of menopause), because low estrogen affects both genital symptoms and urinary symptoms like dysuria (painful urination), urgency, and recurrent UTIs.

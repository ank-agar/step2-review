Cryptococcus gattii is an encapsulated yeast fungus that causes cryptococcosis (fungal infection, classically of the lungs or central nervous system).

Big picture:

* It lives in the environment, especially trees/soil/decaying wood
* People inhale it into the lungs
* It can stay in the lungs or spread through blood to the CNS (central nervous system), causing meningitis (infection of the meninges, the coverings of the brain/spinal cord)

The key Step 1 idea:

* Cryptococcus = encapsulated yeast
* Capsule = antiphagocytic shield
* Antiphagocytic = prevents macrophages/neutrophils from eating it

So the capsule helps it survive in the host.

---

### Why C. gattii is special

C. gattii is closely related to Cryptococcus neoformans, but the classic distinction is:

* C. neoformans: classically in immunocompromised patients, especially AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome)
* C. gattii: can infect immunocompetent patients too

Immunocompetent means the immune system is relatively normal.

---

### What disease does it cause?

Most important presentations:

* pneumonia (lung infection)
* meningitis (infection of brain/spinal cord coverings)
* cryptococcoma (mass-like fungal lesion, especially in lung or brain)

Reasoning:

You inhale the organism first, so the lung is the first stop. If it escapes into the blood, it can reach the CNS (central nervous system).

---

### Diagnosis clue

Cryptococcus is famous for:

* thick polysaccharide capsule
* positive cryptococcal antigen test
* India ink showing a clear halo around yeast

Polysaccharide means sugar-based capsule.

The “halo” happens because the capsule does not take up the ink, so it looks like a clear ring around the yeast.

---

## One-line intuition

C. gattii is an inhaled encapsulated yeast that can cause lung disease and meningitis, even in healthy people.

---

## Pearl 🔥

C. gattii is more associated with immunocompetent hosts than C. neoformans, while C. neoformans is the classic AIDS-associated cryptococcal meningitis organism.

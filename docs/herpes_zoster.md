### Core idea

Herpes zoster = shingles.

It is reactivation of varicella-zoster virus (VZV), the same virus that causes chickenpox.

After chickenpox, VZV hides in a sensory ganglion (cluster of nerve cell bodies). Years later, it can reactivate and travel down that nerve to the skin.

---

### Classic presentation

Painful vesicular rash.

* vesicular = blister-like
* dermatomal = following one skin region supplied by one sensory nerve
* unilateral = one-sided

So the classic Step 1 picture is:

* older or immunocompromised patient
* burning/tingling pain first
* then grouped vesicles
* in one dermatome
* does not cross the midline

---

### Why it follows a stripe

The virus is sleeping in one dorsal root ganglion (sensory nerve ganglion near the spinal cord).

When it wakes up, it travels down that same sensory nerve.

That nerve supplies one strip of skin, called a dermatome.

So the rash looks like a band or stripe.

---

### Why it hurts

VZV reactivates inside sensory nerves.

So symptoms are not just skin findings. The nerve itself is inflamed, causing:

* burning pain
* tingling
* hypersensitivity
* postherpetic neuralgia (persistent nerve pain after the rash resolves)

---

### Complications to know

* Postherpetic neuralgia: chronic pain after shingles
* Herpes zoster ophthalmicus: shingles involving the eye area
* Ramsay Hunt syndrome: VZV reactivation in the facial nerve causing facial paralysis plus ear vesicles

---

## One-line intuition

Chickenpox virus goes dormant in a sensory nerve ganglion, then later reactivates and runs down one nerve, causing a painful one-sided blistering rash in that nerve's skin territory.

---

## HY pearl

Tzanck smear shows multinucleated giant cells, but it cannot distinguish herpes simplex virus (HSV) from varicella-zoster virus (VZV).

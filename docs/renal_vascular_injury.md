### Core idea

Renal vascular injury means damage to the kidney blood vessels:

* renal artery = brings blood into the kidney
* renal vein = drains blood out of the kidney
* smaller intrarenal vessels = branches inside the kidney

So the problem is either:

* **bleeding** because a vessel is torn
* **ischemia/infarction** because blood flow is blocked

Infarction means tissue death from loss of blood supply.

---

### Common causes

Usually from:

* blunt trauma
* penetrating trauma
* renal biopsy
* surgery/procedures

The big trauma mechanism is rapid deceleration.

Why?

The kidney moves, but the renal artery/vein are relatively fixed at the hilum (entry/exit area of the kidney), so the vessels can stretch, tear, dissect, or clot.

---

### What can happen

Renal vascular injury can cause:

* hematoma = collection of blood
* active hemorrhage = ongoing bleeding
* pseudoaneurysm = blood-filled outpouching from injured vessel wall
* arteriovenous fistula = abnormal connection between artery and vein
* thrombosis = clot inside vessel
* renal infarct = dead kidney tissue from blocked blood flow

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Hematuria:** blood leaks into the urinary tract
* **Flank pain:** kidney capsule/retroperitoneum gets irritated or stretched by bleeding
* **Acute kidney injury:** less perfusion means less filtration
* **Hypertension:** injured/ischemic kidney releases renin (enzyme that activates RAAS, the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system), which raises blood pressure
* **Absent or weak enhancement on CT:** contrast cannot enter tissue if the artery is blocked

CT (computed tomography) with IV (intravenous) contrast is the key imaging test in trauma because it can show bleeding, infarction, and urinary leakage.

---

### One-line intuition

Renal vascular injury = the kidney's blood pipes are damaged, so the kidney either bleeds or loses blood supply.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Main renal artery thrombosis after blunt trauma can cause a nonenhancing kidney on contrast CT because blood cannot reach the renal parenchyma.

### Core idea

Respiratory syncytial virus, or **RSV (respiratory syncytial virus)**, is a common virus that infects the respiratory tract, especially in:

* infants
* young children
* older adults
* immunocompromised patients (patients with weakened immune systems)

It is famous for causing:

* bronchiolitis (inflammation of the small airways)
* pneumonia (infection/inflammation of lung tissue)

---

### Why it is called “syncytial”

Syncytial comes from **syncytium**, meaning:

> multiple cells fused together into one big multinucleated cell.

RSV has an **F protein (fusion protein)** that makes infected respiratory epithelial cells fuse with nearby cells.

So the name is basically saying:

> this respiratory virus makes cells fuse together.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

RSV infects the small airways, especially the bronchioles (tiny air tubes in the lungs).

That causes:

* airway swelling
* mucus plugging
* sloughed epithelial cells (dead lining cells falling into the airway)
* narrowed bronchioles

That is why patients, especially infants, can get:

* cough
* rhinorrhea (runny nose)
* wheezing
* tachypnea (fast breathing)
* increased work of breathing

Intuition:

The tiny airways are already narrow in infants, so even a little swelling + mucus can block airflow and cause wheezing.

---

### Virology facts

RSV is an:

* enveloped virus
* negative-sense single-stranded RNA (ribonucleic acid) virus
* Pneumoviridae virus, classically taught with paramyxovirus-like respiratory viruses

Important proteins:

* **G protein (attachment glycoprotein):** helps the virus attach to airway cells
* **F protein (fusion protein):** helps the virus enter cells and makes syncytia

---

## One-line intuition

RSV is a tiny-airway virus in babies: it plugs bronchioles with swelling, mucus, and dead cells, causing bronchiolitis and wheezing.

---

## Pearl 🔥

The **F protein (fusion protein)** is high yield because it causes syncytia and is targeted by **palivizumab/nirsevimab (monoclonal antibodies used for RSV prevention in high-risk infants/young children)**.

Pemphigus is an autoimmune blistering disease where antibodies attack **desmosomes** (cell-cell anchors that hold skin cells together).

Main target:

* **desmoglein** = the "glue" protein inside desmosomes

So the intuition is:

> antibodies break the glue between keratinocytes (skin cells), so the epidermis falls apart and forms fragile blisters.

This cell separation is called **acantholysis** (loss of connection between keratinocytes).

---

### Pemphigus vulgaris

Most important Step 1 form.

Autoantibodies are usually against:

* **desmoglein 3**
* sometimes **desmoglein 1** too

Classically causes:

* painful oral mucosal erosions
* flaccid blisters
* positive Nikolsky sign

**Nikolsky sign** = gentle rubbing of skin causes the epidermis to slough off.

Why?

The blister is **intraepidermal** (inside the epidermis), so the blister roof is thin and weak.

That makes the blisters flaccid and easy to rupture.

---

### Histology

Pemphigus vulgaris has:

* suprabasal split
* acantholysis
* "row of tombstones"

**Suprabasal** = just above the basal layer of the epidermis.

The basal cells stay attached to the basement membrane, but the cells above them detach.

So it looks like a row of basal cells still standing there, like tombstones.

---

### Direct immunofluorescence

Shows **IgG** (immunoglobulin G antibody) in a net-like pattern between epidermal cells.

Think:

* pemphigus = antibodies between skin cells
* pemphigoid = antibodies at the basement membrane

---

## One-line intuition

Pemphigus = autoantibodies destroy desmosomal glue, so keratinocytes separate and form fragile intraepidermal blisters.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Pemphigus vulgaris is worse than bullous pemphigoid because it often involves the mouth and has fragile flaccid blisters; bullous pemphigoid has tense blisters because the split is deeper, below the epidermis.

## Bronchial carcinoid tumor

![Image](https://media.springernature.com/full/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1186%2Fs12890-018-0767-5/MediaObjects/12890_2018_767_Fig4_HTML.png)

A bronchial carcinoid tumor is a low-grade neuroendocrine tumor (tumor from hormone-like nerve/endocrine cells) that grows in the bronchus (large airway of the lung).

Think:

* tumor in the airway
* usually central
* slow-growing compared with small cell carcinoma
* can obstruct the bronchus

---

### Why symptoms happen

Because the tumor grows inside/near the bronchus, it can block airflow.

So you can get:

* cough
* wheezing
* hemoptysis (coughing up blood)
* recurrent pneumonia
* atelectasis (lung collapse from airway blockage)

The key intuition is:

> A mass plugs the airway, so the lung beyond the plug cannot ventilate well.

---

### Histology idea

Carcinoid tumors are neuroendocrine tumors, so they often have:

* uniform cells
* organoid/nested growth pattern
* "salt and pepper" chromatin (finely speckled nuclear material)
* positive neuroendocrine markers like chromogranin and synaptophysin

---

### Typical vs atypical

Typical carcinoid:

* lower mitotic activity (fewer dividing cells)
* no necrosis (dead tumor tissue)
* better prognosis

Atypical carcinoid:

* more mitoses
* may have necrosis
* more aggressive

---

## One-line intuition

Bronchial carcinoid tumor is a slow-growing neuroendocrine tumor that commonly sits in a central airway and causes problems by obstructing the bronchus.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Bronchial carcinoid can cause carcinoid syndrome (flushing, diarrhea, bronchospasm), but this is much less common than with GI carcinoid tumors (gastrointestinal carcinoid tumors).

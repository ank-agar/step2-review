# Portal hypertension

![Image](https://www.mayoclinic.org/-/media/kcms/gbs/patient-consumer/images/2026/04/15/19/28/portal-hypertension-9124450-001-0-632px.jpg)

![Image](https://nccommons.org/m/8/84/Caput_medusae_sign_-_portal_hypertension_%28Radiopaedia_64007-72759_B_4%29.jpg)

### Core idea

Portal hypertension = increased pressure in the portal venous system (veins that drain blood from the GI tract, meaning gastrointestinal tract, spleen, and pancreas into the liver).

Most common cause:

* cirrhosis (scarred liver)

Think:

> blood from the gut wants to go through the liver, but the liver is scarred and stiff, so blood backs up.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Esophageal varices:** backed-up portal blood escapes into esophageal veins, making fragile dilated veins that can rupture
* **Ascites:** high portal pressure pushes fluid out into the abdomen
* **Splenomegaly:** blood backs up into the spleen, making it enlarge
* **Thrombocytopenia:** low platelets because the enlarged spleen traps platelets
* **Caput medusae:** paraumbilical veins (veins around the belly button) reopen and dilate as blood tries to bypass the liver

---

### Important portosystemic connections

Portosystemic anastomoses (connections between portal veins and systemic veins) become clinically important.

High yield sites:

* esophagus → esophageal varices
* rectum → hemorrhoids/rectal varices
* umbilicus → caput medusae

Basically:

> blocked liver route = blood finds side roads.

---

## One-line intuition

Portal hypertension is traffic backing up before a scarred liver, so blood detours through fragile collateral veins and fluid leaks into the belly.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Ascites from portal hypertension usually has **SAAG (serum-ascites albumin gradient) > 1.1 g/dL**, because high hydrostatic pressure pushes fluid out while albumin mostly stays in the blood.

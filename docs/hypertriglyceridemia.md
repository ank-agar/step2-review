# Hypertriglyceridemia

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK395574/bin/sec-hypertryglycerid-Image002.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d1/Chylomicron.svg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK326743/bin/lipid_triglyc-mbol-Image002.jpg)

Hypertriglyceridemia means high triglycerides (fat molecules used for energy storage) in the blood.

Main particles carrying triglycerides:

* **Chylomicrons:** carry dietary fat from the intestine
* **VLDL (very-low-density lipoprotein):** carries triglycerides made by the liver

So the basic idea is:

> too many triglyceride-rich particles are floating around in plasma.

---

### Why triglycerides get high

Usually from increased production or decreased breakdown.

Common causes:

* diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar from insulin problems)
* obesity/metabolic syndrome (insulin resistance + abdominal obesity + dyslipidemia)
* alcohol use
* hypothyroidism (low thyroid hormone)
* chronic kidney disease
* pregnancy
* drugs like estrogen, steroids, protease inhibitors, and thiazides

Genetic causes can also happen, especially problems with:

* LPL (lipoprotein lipase), the enzyme that breaks triglycerides out of chylomicrons/VLDL
* ApoC-II (apolipoprotein C-II), which activates LPL

---

### Why pancreatitis happens

Very high triglycerides can cause acute pancreatitis (inflammation of the pancreas).

Reasoning:

* triglyceride-rich particles build up in pancreatic capillaries
* pancreatic lipase breaks triglycerides into free fatty acids
* free fatty acids are toxic locally
* pancreatic tissue gets inflamed

Classically, pancreatitis risk becomes important when triglycerides are very high, especially around **>1000 mg/dL**.

---

### Skin finding

Severe hypertriglyceridemia can cause eruptive xanthomas (small yellow-red lipid-filled papules).

Why?

Triglyceride-rich particles leak into tissues, macrophages (immune cells that eat debris) take up lipid, and you get foamy lipid-filled cells in the skin.

---

## One-line intuition

Hypertriglyceridemia is basically too much fat traffic in the blood, usually from excess VLDL/chylomicrons or poor LPL-mediated clearance.

---

## Pearl 🔥

LPL (lipoprotein lipase) needs ApoC-II (apolipoprotein C-II) to work; deficiency of either can cause chylomicron buildup, severe hypertriglyceridemia, pancreatitis, and eruptive xanthomas.

### Core idea

Alcoholic fatty liver disease is fat buildup inside hepatocytes (liver cells) caused by chronic heavy alcohol use.

Another name is hepatic steatosis:

* hepatic = liver
* steatosis = fat accumulation

So literally:

> alcohol causes fat to accumulate inside liver cells.

---

### Why alcohol causes fatty liver

Alcohol metabolism increases NADH (reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide), which pushes the liver into a “store fat” state.

High NADH causes:

* decreased fatty acid oxidation = the liver burns less fat
* increased triglyceride synthesis = the liver makes/stores more fat
* impaired lipid export = fat gets trapped in hepatocytes

Triglycerides are stored fat molecules.

So the liver becomes full of fat droplets.

---

### What you see on histology

Classic finding:

* macrovesicular steatosis

Macrovesicular steatosis means one big fat vacuole (empty-looking bubble) pushes the hepatocyte nucleus to the side.

Intuition:

The liver cell gets stuffed with fat, like a balloon filling up and shoving everything else to the edge.

---

### Why it matters

Alcoholic fatty liver is the earliest and most reversible form of alcohol-associated liver disease.

Progression can be:

* fatty liver
* alcoholic hepatitis
* fibrosis
* cirrhosis

Fibrosis means scar tissue formation.

Cirrhosis means diffuse liver scarring with regenerative nodules that distort liver architecture.

---

### Labs

Alcohol-related liver injury often has:

* AST > ALT
* usually AST:ALT ratio > 2

AST = aspartate aminotransferase.

ALT = alanine aminotransferase.

Why AST is higher:

Alcohol damages mitochondria, and AST is found in mitochondria more than ALT is.

---

## One-line intuition

Alcohol metabolism makes the liver stop burning fat and start trapping fat, so hepatocytes become packed with triglyceride droplets.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Alcoholic fatty liver is reversible with alcohol cessation, but continued alcohol use can progress to alcoholic hepatitis and cirrhosis.

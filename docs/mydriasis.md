### Core idea

Mydriasis = dilation of the pupil.

So the black center of the eye gets bigger.

Opposite:

* mydriasis = big pupil
* miosis = small pupil

---

### Why it happens

Pupil size is controlled by 2 iris muscles:

* dilator pupillae muscle = opens the pupil
* sphincter pupillae muscle = closes the pupil

Sympathetic nervous system stimulation (fight-or-flight system) activates the dilator pupillae muscle.

That muscle pulls the iris outward, so the pupil gets larger.

Parasympathetic nervous system stimulation (rest-and-digest system) activates the sphincter pupillae muscle.

That squeezes the pupil smaller.

So:

> sympathetic up = mydriasis  
> parasympathetic up = miosis

---

### Common causes

Mydriasis can happen with:

* darkness, because the eye wants to let in more light
* fear/arousal, because sympathetic tone increases
* anticholinergic drugs (drugs that block acetylcholine, the main parasympathetic neurotransmitter), like atropine
* sympathomimetic drugs (drugs that mimic sympathetic activity), like cocaine or amphetamines
* CN III palsy (oculomotor nerve palsy), especially if parasympathetic fibers are compressed

---

## One-line intuition

Mydriasis is the eye opening the pupil wider, usually because sympathetic dilation is winning or parasympathetic constriction is blocked.

---

## Pearl

In uncal herniation, CN III (oculomotor nerve) can get compressed, knocking out parasympathetic pupil constriction and causing an ipsilateral blown pupil (a large, poorly reactive pupil on the same side).

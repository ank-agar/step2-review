### Core idea

Candida albicans is an opportunistic fungus.

Opportunistic means it usually lives harmlessly on the body, but causes disease when host defenses are weakened or the local environment changes.

It commonly lives in:

* mouth
* GI tract (gastrointestinal tract)
* vagina
* skin folds

---

### What it causes

Classic Candida albicans infections:

* oral thrush: white plaques in the mouth
* vulvovaginal candidiasis: itching + thick white discharge
* diaper rash/intertrigo: red rash in moist skin folds
* esophagitis: painful swallowing, especially in immunocompromised patients
* candidemia: Candida in the blood

---

### Why it becomes pathogenic

Candida can switch forms:

* yeast form = round budding cells
* pseudohyphae = elongated connected cells
* hyphae = invasive filament-like form

This shape-shifting is called dimorphism.

Dimorphism means one organism can grow in different forms depending on the environment.

The hyphal form helps it invade tissue, so it becomes more pathogenic.

---

### Risk factors

Think: normal flora gets disturbed or immunity drops.

Common risk factors:

* antibiotics: kill competing bacteria, so Candida overgrows
* diabetes mellitus: high glucose helps fungal growth
* pregnancy or estrogen exposure
* neutropenia (low neutrophils)
* HIV (human immunodeficiency virus)
* steroids or chemotherapy
* central venous catheter: risk for bloodstream infection

---

### Diagnosis clues

On microscopy, Candida shows:

* budding yeast
* pseudohyphae

Candida albicans also forms germ tubes.

Germ tubes are early hyphal outgrowths from yeast cells.

So:

> germ tube positive = think Candida albicans.

---

## One-line intuition

Candida albicans is normal yeast flora that turns invasive when defenses drop, especially by switching into hyphae/pseudohyphae.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Candida albicans is a germ tube-positive fungus that causes thrush and vaginal candidiasis, especially after antibiotics, diabetes mellitus, or immunosuppression.

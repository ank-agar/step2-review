## Gestational diabetes

Gestational diabetes is high blood glucose (blood sugar) first recognized during pregnancy.

Core idea:

* the placenta (organ that connects mom and fetus) makes hormones
* those hormones cause insulin resistance (body does not respond well to insulin)
* insulin (hormone that moves glucose from blood into cells) cannot keep up
* glucose stays high in mom's blood

So the issue is not usually "no insulin."

It is:

> pregnancy hormones make mom resistant to insulin.

---

### Why it happens

During pregnancy, the placenta wants to make sure the fetus gets enough nutrients.

So placental hormones partially block maternal insulin action.

That means mom's cells take up less glucose, leaving more glucose in the blood for the baby.

Usually the pancreas (organ that makes insulin) compensates by making more insulin.

Gestational diabetes happens when the pancreas cannot increase insulin enough.

---

### Why the baby gets big

Glucose crosses the placenta.

Insulin does **not** cross the placenta.

So if mom has high glucose:

* baby gets high glucose
* baby's pancreas makes more insulin
* fetal insulin acts like a growth signal
* baby can become macrosomic (abnormally large baby)

One-line intuition:

> Mom's glucose feeds the baby, and baby's insulin makes the baby grow.

---

### Important complications

For baby:

* macrosomia (large baby)
* shoulder dystocia (shoulders get stuck during delivery)
* neonatal hypoglycemia (low blood glucose after birth)

Why neonatal hypoglycemia happens:

* before birth, baby is exposed to lots of maternal glucose
* baby makes lots of insulin
* after delivery, maternal glucose supply stops
* baby's insulin is still high
* blood glucose drops

For mom:

* increased risk of preeclampsia (hypertension with end-organ signs in pregnancy)
* increased future risk of type 2 diabetes mellitus (insulin resistance diabetes)

---

### Screening

Usually screened around 24-28 weeks gestation (pregnancy age).

Why then?

Placental hormones and insulin resistance rise as pregnancy progresses, so gestational diabetes often shows up in the late 2nd trimester.

---

## One-line intuition

Gestational diabetes is placenta-driven insulin resistance causing maternal hyperglycemia (high blood glucose), which sends too much glucose to the baby.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Gestational diabetes classically causes **neonatal hypoglycemia** because fetal hyperinsulinemia (high insulin in the fetus) persists after the maternal glucose supply is suddenly removed at delivery.

## Developmental dysplasia of the hip

### Core idea

Developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) means the infant hip joint does not form or sit normally.

The problem is usually:

* shallow acetabulum (hip socket)
* unstable femoral head (ball of the thigh bone)
* possible subluxation (partial dislocation) or dislocation (fully out of the socket)

So the simple picture is:

> the ball is not held deeply and securely inside the socket.

---

### Why it happens

The femoral head and acetabulum shape each other as the baby grows.

If the femoral head is not sitting correctly in the socket:

* the acetabulum stays shallow
* the hip becomes unstable
* the femoral head can slide partly or completely out

That is why early treatment matters: keeping the ball in the socket helps the socket develop normally.

---

### Classic findings

In a newborn, think:

* asymmetric thigh/gluteal folds
* limited hip abduction (limited movement of the thigh away from midline)
* leg-length difference
* positive Barlow test
* positive Ortolani test

Barlow test:

* tries to push an unstable hip out of the socket

Ortolani test:

* tries to reduce (put back in place) a dislocated hip
* classically gives a palpable "clunk"

---

### Risk factors

High-yield risk factors:

* breech position (baby positioned butt-first in uterus)
* female sex
* firstborn
* family history
* oligohydramnios (low amniotic fluid)

Why breech/oligohydramnios matter:

* less room in the uterus can keep the hips in abnormal positions
* abnormal positioning can prevent normal socket formation

---

## One-line intuition

DDH is a loose/shallow infant hip socket problem where the femoral head does not stay centered, so the socket cannot mold normally around it.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

DDH is associated with breech birth and presents with limited hip abduction plus positive Barlow/Ortolani maneuvers; treatment in young infants is usually a Pavlik harness (brace that keeps the hips flexed and abducted).

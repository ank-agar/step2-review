# Perinephric abscess

![Image](https://d16qt3wv6xm098.cloudfront.net/rZ1WcJO5TfmgWut8N_zv-R0KSNye31cJ/_.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK543798/bin/455516_1_En_20_Fig1_HTML.jpg)

### Core idea

A perinephric abscess is a pocket of pus around the kidney.

Break it down:

* peri- = around
* nephric = kidney
* abscess = walled-off pus collection

So:

> pus trapped in the space around the kidney.

More specifically, it forms in the perinephric space, which is the fatty space between the renal capsule and Gerota fascia.

* renal capsule = thin covering directly on the kidney
* Gerota fascia = tougher outer fascial layer around the kidney/fat

---

### Why it happens

Most commonly, infection starts as a urinary tract infection and ascends upward:

* bladder infection
* pyelonephritis (kidney infection)
* renal abscess
* spread into perinephric fat
* perinephric abscess

The key idea is:

> bacteria get into/around the kidney, the immune system walls it off, and that walled-off pocket fills with pus.

Common organisms:

* E. coli
* Klebsiella
* Staphylococcus aureus

---

### Risk factors

Think: anything that helps bacteria stay in the urinary tract or weakens host defense.

* diabetes mellitus
* kidney stones
* urinary obstruction
* recurrent urinary tract infection
* immunosuppression
* abnormal urinary tract anatomy

Kidney stones are especially important because obstruction causes urine stasis, and stagnant urine is easier for bacteria to infect.

---

### How it presents

Classic symptoms:

* fever
* chills
* flank pain
* costovertebral angle tenderness

Costovertebral angle tenderness means pain when tapping the back over the kidney area.

Important: urinary symptoms may be absent.

So unlike a simple urinary tract infection, they may not have obvious dysuria (painful urination), frequency, or urgency.

---

### Diagnosis

Best test:

* CT scan with contrast

CT (computed tomography) is useful because it shows:

* where the pus collection is
* how big it is
* whether it has spread
* whether there is an obstructing stone

---

### Treatment

Treatment is usually:

* IV antibiotics
* drainage if the abscess is large or not improving

IV means intravenous, meaning through a vein.

Drainage is often percutaneous drainage, meaning a needle/catheter is placed through the skin into the abscess using CT or ultrasound guidance.

---

## One-line intuition

A perinephric abscess is basically a kidney infection that escaped into the fat around the kidney and became a walled-off pus pocket.

---

## Pearl 🔥

If pyelonephritis does not improve after antibiotics, think complication like renal abscess, perinephric abscess, obstruction, or infected stone.

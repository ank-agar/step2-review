# Papilledema

![Image](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/-/scassets/images/org/health/articles/24445-papilledema.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459120/bin/Papilledema.jpg)

Papilledema = swelling of the optic disc (the spot in the back of the eye where the optic nerve exits) due to increased ICP (intracranial pressure = pressure inside the skull).

Key idea:

* high pressure in the skull
* pressure travels through CSF (cerebrospinal fluid = fluid around the brain and spinal cord)
* that pressure reaches the optic nerve sheath
* the optic disc bulges forward into the eye

So papilledema is basically:

> high brain pressure showing up as a swollen optic nerve head.

---

### Why it happens

The optic nerve is covered by meninges (protective layers around the brain and spinal cord), and its surrounding space is continuous with the brain's CSF space.

So if ICP rises, that pressure gets transmitted down the optic nerve sheath.

This compresses axons (nerve fibers) and blocks axoplasmic flow (movement of materials inside nerve fibers), causing the optic disc to swell.

---

### What you see on fundoscopic exam

Fundoscopic exam = looking at the retina with an ophthalmoscope.

Classic findings:

* blurred optic disc margins
* elevated/swollen optic disc
* engorged retinal veins
* retinal hemorrhages sometimes
* usually bilateral, meaning both eyes

Important: papilledema specifically means optic disc swelling from increased ICP. Not every swollen optic disc is papilledema.

---

### Symptoms

Can cause:

* headache
* nausea/vomiting
* transient visual obscurations (brief episodes where vision dims or blacks out)
* diplopia (double vision), often from CN VI (cranial nerve 6 = abducens nerve) palsy

CN VI is vulnerable to increased ICP because it has a long intracranial course.

---

### Causes to think of

Anything that raises ICP:

* brain tumor
* intracranial hemorrhage (bleeding inside the skull)
* meningitis (infection/inflammation of the meninges)
* hydrocephalus (too much CSF in the ventricles)
* cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (clot in the brain's venous drainage)
* idiopathic intracranial hypertension, or IIH (high ICP without a mass lesion or infection)

Idiopathic = unknown cause.

---

## One-line intuition

Papilledema is when pressure inside the skull backs up into the optic nerve and makes the optic disc look swollen.

---

## HY pearl

Papilledema is a sign of increased ICP until proven otherwise, so the next step is usually urgent neuroimaging before lumbar puncture (spinal tap), because removing CSF when a mass lesion is present can risk brain herniation.

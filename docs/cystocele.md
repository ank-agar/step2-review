# Cystocele

### Core idea

A cystocele is bladder prolapse into the anterior vaginal wall.

Anterior = front.

So the simple image is:

> bladder falls backward/downward and bulges into the front wall of the vagina.

It is a type of pelvic organ prolapse, meaning pelvic organs drop because their support structures weaken.

---

### Why it happens

The bladder is normally supported by:

* pelvic floor muscles
* connective tissue/fascia
* vaginal wall support

If those weaken, the bladder is no longer held up well, so it pushes into the vagina.

Common risk factors:

* vaginal childbirth
* multiparity, meaning multiple pregnancies/births
* aging/menopause
* obesity
* chronic coughing
* chronic constipation/straining
* heavy lifting

The shared theme is either:

* damaged pelvic support, or
* chronically increased intra-abdominal pressure, meaning repeated pressure from inside the abdomen pushing downward.

---

### Symptoms

Can cause:

* vaginal bulge or pressure
* urinary frequency/urgency
* incomplete bladder emptying
* urinary retention
* stress urinary incontinence, meaning urine leakage with coughing/laughing/sneezing
* recurrent UTIs, meaning urinary tract infections

Why urinary symptoms happen:

The bladder and urethra get kinked or displaced, so urine flow and storage are affected.

---

### Differentiate from nearby prolapses

* cystocele = bladder into anterior vaginal wall
* rectocele = rectum into posterior vaginal wall
* uterine prolapse = uterus descends downward

Anterior wall problem = think bladder.

Posterior wall problem = think rectum.

---

## One-line intuition

A cystocele is a weakened pelvic floor letting the bladder sag into the front wall of the vagina.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Cystocele is classically associated with stress urinary incontinence because loss of anterior vaginal wall/pelvic support also reduces support around the urethra, so increased abdominal pressure can force urine out.

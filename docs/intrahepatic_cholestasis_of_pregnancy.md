# Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/images/PX0001UT_PRESENTATION.jpeg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/6c6d/4893793/f22db0372d33/nihms787728f2.jpg)

### Core idea

Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) = pregnancy hormones cause poor bile flow inside the liver.

Break it down:

* intrahepatic = inside the liver
* cholestasis = bile flow is slowed or blocked
* pregnancy = usually late 2nd or 3rd trimester

Bile (fluid made by the liver to help digest fats) cannot move out normally, so bile acids build up in the blood.

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

* intense itching
* no primary rash
* palms and soles
* worse at night
* late pregnancy

The key lab is elevated serum bile acids (bile acids measured in the blood).

Liver enzymes can also rise:

* AST (aspartate aminotransferase)
* ALT (alanine aminotransferase)

---

### Why the itching happens

Bile acids accumulate in the liver, then spill into the bloodstream.

Those bile acids deposit in skin/activate itch pathways, causing pruritus (itching).

Important: there is usually no primary rash because the skin itself is not the original problem. Any skin marks are usually from scratching.

---

### Why pregnancy causes it

High estrogen and progesterone metabolites (pregnancy hormone breakdown products) can impair bile transporters.

Bile transporters = liver proteins that normally push bile acids out of hepatocytes (liver cells) into bile ducts.

So the simple mechanism is:

> pregnancy hormones slow bile export → bile acids rise → itching + fetal risk

---

### Why we care

Mom is usually okay after delivery, but the fetus can be at risk.

Fetal complications include:

* preterm birth
* fetal distress
* meconium passage
* stillbirth risk, especially with high bile acids

Reasoning: bile acids can cross the placenta and affect the fetal heart/placenta, so the baby is the bigger concern.

---

### Treatment idea

Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA, ursodiol) is commonly used to improve bile flow and reduce bile acids/pruritus (itching).

Delivery timing is based on bile acid level, gestational age (how far along the pregnancy is), and fetal/maternal risk.

---

## One-line intuition

ICP is a late-pregnancy bile backup inside the liver: bile acids spill into blood, causing itchy palms/soles and possible fetal danger.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

In a pregnant patient with severe itching of palms/soles and no rash, think intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy and check serum bile acids.

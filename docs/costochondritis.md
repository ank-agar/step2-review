### Core idea

Costochondritis = inflammation of the costal cartilage, which is the cartilage connecting the ribs to the sternum (breastbone).

Break the word:

* costo- = rib
* chondro- = cartilage
* -itis = inflammation

So costochondritis literally means:

> inflamed rib cartilage.

---

### Where the pain is

Pain is usually at the front of the chest where the ribs meet the sternum.

This area is called the costosternal junction:

* costo = rib
* sternal = sternum
* junction = connection point

Most commonly, it affects the upper ribs, especially ribs 2-5.

---

### Why it hurts with breathing or movement

The rib cage moves every time you:

* take a deep breath
* cough
* twist your torso
* lift/push/pull with your arms

If the cartilage joint is inflamed, that movement tugs on the irritated area, causing sharp or achy chest wall pain.

---

### Big test clue

The pain is usually reproducible with palpation (pressing on the painful spot).

That is the key intuition:

* heart pain usually comes from inside the chest
* costochondritis pain comes from the chest wall itself

So pressing directly on the inflamed cartilage often recreates the pain.

Important: chest pain still needs serious causes ruled out first, especially myocardial infarction (heart attack), pulmonary embolism (blood clot in the lung), pneumothorax (collapsed lung), and pericarditis (inflammation around the heart).

---

### Costochondritis vs Tietze syndrome

They are similar, but:

* costochondritis = chest wall pain without obvious swelling
* Tietze syndrome = similar pain but with visible/local swelling, usually at one rib joint

Easy memory:

> Tietze has Tumefaction.

Tumefaction means swelling.

---

## One-line intuition

Costochondritis is basically "sore inflamed rib-sternum cartilage," so chest wall movement and pressing on the spot hurt.

---

## Pearl

Costochondritis can mimic acute coronary syndrome (reduced blood flow to the heart), but the Step 1-style clue is localized chest pain that is reproducible with palpation over the costochondral or costosternal junction.

# Gestational trophoblastic disease

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459155/bin/hydatiform__mole2.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Invasive_hydatidiform_mole.jpg/960px-Invasive_hydatidiform_mole.jpg)

Gestational trophoblastic disease (GTD) is abnormal growth of trophoblasts (placental cells that normally help form the placenta).

Core idea:

* trophoblasts are supposed to make placenta
* in GTD, they proliferate abnormally
* because trophoblasts make beta-hCG (beta-human chorionic gonadotropin), GTD often causes very high beta-hCG

---

### Main types

The Step 1 way to organize it:

* hydatidiform mole = molar pregnancy
* invasive mole = mole that invades into myometrium (uterine muscle)
* choriocarcinoma = malignant trophoblastic tumor
* placental site trophoblastic tumor = rare tumor from implantation-site trophoblasts

Most Step 1 questions focus on complete vs partial hydatidiform mole.

---

### Complete mole

Complete mole = empty egg gets fertilized.

So there is:

* no maternal nuclear DNA (DNA from the mother in the nucleus)
* only paternal nuclear DNA
* usually 46,XX
* no fetus
* diffuse swollen chorionic villi (placental villi)
* very high beta-hCG

Why no fetus?

Maternal genes are needed for embryo development. Paternal genes push placental/trophoblast growth. So if it is all paternal DNA, you get placenta-like tissue overgrowth without a real embryo.

Classic findings:

* vaginal bleeding
* uterus larger than expected for dates
* hyperemesis gravidarum (severe nausea/vomiting in pregnancy)
* early preeclampsia (hypertension and proteinuria/end-organ damage before 20 weeks)
* hyperthyroidism symptoms because beta-hCG can stimulate TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone) receptors
* "snowstorm" ultrasound pattern

---

### Partial mole

Partial mole = normal egg gets fertilized by 2 sperm.

So there is:

* maternal DNA present
* extra paternal DNA
* usually triploid, like 69,XXY
* some fetal tissue may be present
* focal swollen chorionic villi
* beta-hCG elevated, but usually less than complete mole

One-line memory:

* complete mole = completely paternal
* partial mole = partially fetal tissue can be present

---

### Why beta-hCG is high

Trophoblasts normally make beta-hCG.

So if trophoblasts are overgrowing, beta-hCG rises a lot.

That explains:

* positive pregnancy test
* severe nausea/vomiting
* theca-lutein cysts (ovarian cysts from high beta-hCG stimulation)
* hyperthyroidism-like symptoms

---

### Choriocarcinoma

Choriocarcinoma is malignant GTD (gestational trophoblastic disease) made of trophoblasts.

Important features:

* very high beta-hCG
* hemorrhagic tumor
* early hematogenous spread (spread through blood)
* often metastasizes to lungs

Key pathology idea:

* malignant cytotrophoblasts and syncytiotrophoblasts
* no chorionic villi

That "no villi" point helps separate choriocarcinoma from a mole.

---

## One-line intuition

GTD is when placental trophoblast tissue grows too much; the more trophoblast growth, the more beta-hCG-related symptoms you get.

---

## Pearl

Complete mole is p57 negative because p57 is maternally expressed, and complete moles have no maternal nuclear DNA.

Partial mole is p57 positive because maternal DNA is present.

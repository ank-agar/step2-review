### Core idea

Aphthous ulcers are canker sores: painful, shallow ulcers on the oral mucosa (lining inside the mouth).

They usually look like:

* round or oval ulcer
* white/yellow-gray center
* red rim around it
* very painful, especially with acidic/spicy foods

---

### Why they happen

The key idea is immune-mediated mucosal injury.

That means the immune system causes local damage to the mouth lining, creating a small ulcer.

Common triggers:

* minor trauma, like biting the cheek
* stress or poor sleep
* vitamin B12, folate, or iron deficiency
* inflammatory bowel disease, meaning Crohn disease or ulcerative colitis
* Behcet disease

---

### Not herpes

Aphthous ulcers are not caused by HSV (herpes simplex virus).

Classic difference:

* aphthous ulcers: inside the mouth on non-keratinized mucosa, like inner lips, cheeks, floor of mouth, or underside of tongue
* HSV ulcers: often on keratinized mucosa, like hard palate or gingiva (gums), and may start as vesicles (small fluid-filled blisters)

So aphthous ulcer = painful punched-out sore, not contagious.

---

### Why they hurt so much

The ulcer removes the protective epithelial layer (surface lining).

So nerve endings underneath are exposed to:

* saliva
* food
* acid
* friction from chewing/talking

That is why a tiny lesion can feel so painful.

---

## One-line intuition

An aphthous ulcer is a small immune-triggered crater in the mouth lining that exposes sensitive tissue underneath.

---

## HY pearl

Recurrent oral aphthous ulcers plus genital ulcers plus uveitis (eye inflammation) = think Behcet disease.

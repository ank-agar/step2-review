### Core idea

Adenovirus is a **non-enveloped, double-stranded DNA virus**.

Break that down:

* **non-enveloped** = no lipid outer coat
* **double-stranded DNA** = it carries DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) as its genetic material
* **icosahedral capsid** = geometric protein shell with many flat faces

So think:

> tough DNA virus with no envelope.

---

### What it causes

Adenovirus commonly causes:

* upper respiratory infection
* pharyngitis (sore throat)
* conjunctivitis (pink eye)
* pneumonia
* gastroenteritis (stomach/intestine inflammation causing diarrhea/vomiting)
* hemorrhagic cystitis (bloody bladder inflammation)

Classic Step 1 association:

* **pharyngoconjunctival fever** = fever + sore throat + conjunctivitis

---

### Why it is tough

Because adenovirus is **non-enveloped**, it does not rely on a fragile lipid membrane.

That means it can survive better on surfaces and resist drying, acid, detergents, and some disinfectants.

This is why non-enveloped viruses are often spread by:

* fecal-oral transmission
* respiratory droplets
* contaminated surfaces

---

### Why it causes conjunctivitis

The virus infects epithelial cells (surface-lining cells), including cells in:

* respiratory tract
* conjunctiva (thin membrane over the eye)
* GI tract (gastrointestinal tract)
* bladder

So the same virus can give you cold symptoms, pink eye, diarrhea, or bladder irritation depending on which epithelium it infects.

---

## One-line intuition

Adenovirus is a tough non-enveloped DNA virus that loves epithelial surfaces, so it causes respiratory illness, pink eye, diarrhea, and sometimes bloody cystitis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Adenovirus is **non-enveloped + double-stranded DNA + icosahedral**, and it classically causes **pharyngoconjunctival fever**.

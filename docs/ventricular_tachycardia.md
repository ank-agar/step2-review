## Ventricular tachycardia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Torsades_de_Pointes_%28polymorphic_VT%29.svg/3840px-Torsades_de_Pointes_%28polymorphic_VT%29.svg.png)

Ventricular tachycardia, or **VT** (ventricular tachycardia), is a fast heart rhythm that starts in the **ventricles** (the lower pumping chambers of the heart).

Core idea:

* the ventricles are firing too fast
* the signal is not coming from the normal SA node (sinoatrial node, the heart's normal pacemaker)
* because the signal spreads abnormally through ventricular muscle, the QRS complex is usually wide

---

### What it looks like on ECG

VT usually shows:

* fast rate, often > 120 beats/min
* wide QRS complexes
* regular rhythm if monomorphic
* possible AV dissociation (atria and ventricles beating independently)

**Monomorphic VT** (one-shape ventricular tachycardia) means every QRS looks similar.

**Polymorphic VT** (many-shape ventricular tachycardia) means the QRS shape keeps changing.

**Torsades de pointes** (twisting of the points) is a special polymorphic VT associated with prolonged QT interval (delayed ventricular repolarization).

---

### Why it is dangerous

The ventricles are supposed to fill with blood before squeezing.

In VT, they beat so fast that:

* filling time decreases
* stroke volume (blood pumped per beat) drops
* blood pressure can crash
* it can degenerate into ventricular fibrillation, or **VF** (chaotic ventricular rhythm)

So VT can cause:

* palpitations
* syncope (fainting)
* hypotension (low blood pressure)
* sudden cardiac death

---

### Common causes

Think: irritated or scarred ventricular myocardium (heart muscle).

Common causes:

* prior myocardial infarction, or **MI** (heart attack)
* ischemia (low blood flow)
* cardiomyopathy (disease of heart muscle)
* electrolyte problems, especially low K+ (potassium) or low Mg2+ (magnesium)
* QT-prolonging drugs

---

## One-line intuition

VT is when the ventricles stop following the normal top-down wiring and start rapidly firing from below, so the heart beats fast but pumps poorly.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On Step 1, a **wide-complex tachycardia** (fast rhythm with wide QRS complexes) should be treated as VT until proven otherwise, especially in an older patient or someone with prior MI (myocardial infarction).

Blast injuries are injuries caused by an explosion.

Think of an explosion as hurting you in multiple ways at once:

* pressure wave hits the body
* debris flies into the body
* body gets thrown into something
* heat/smoke/chemicals cause extra injury

---

### Main types

**Primary blast injury**

Direct injury from the blast pressure wave.

This especially damages air-filled organs:

* ears
* lungs
* GI tract (gastrointestinal tract = stomach/intestines)

Why?

Air compresses much more than tissue/fluid, so pressure changes tear structures at air-fluid interfaces.

Classic findings:

* tympanic membrane rupture (eardrum rupture)
* blast lung
* bowel perforation

---

**Secondary blast injury**

Injury from flying fragments.

Basically:

> stuff flies into the person.

Examples:

* shrapnel wounds
* penetrating eye injury
* penetrating trauma

---

**Tertiary blast injury**

Injury from the person being thrown into something.

Basically:

> person flies into stuff.

Examples:

* fractures
* traumatic amputation
* blunt head trauma
* coup-contrecoup brain injury

Coup-contrecoup means the brain hits one side of the skull, then rebounds and hits the opposite side.

---

**Quaternary blast injury**

Everything else from the explosion.

Examples:

* burns
* smoke inhalation
* toxic gas exposure
* crush injury from building collapse

---

**Quinary blast injury**

Injury from contaminants released by the explosion.

Examples:

* chemical exposure
* biological exposure
* radiation exposure

This is lower yield than the first 4, but good to recognize.

---

## One-line intuition

Primary = blast wave hits you.

Secondary = debris hits you.

Tertiary = you hit something.

Quaternary = burns/smoke/crush.

Quinary = chemical/biologic/radiation contamination.

---

## Why blast lung happens

Blast lung is pulmonary barotrauma (lung injury from pressure change).

The blast wave rapidly compresses air in the alveoli (tiny lung air sacs), causing:

* alveolar rupture
* pulmonary hemorrhage
* pulmonary edema
* hypoxemia

Hypoxemia means low oxygen in the blood.

So the patient can have:

* shortness of breath
* chest pain
* cough
* hemoptysis (coughing blood)

---

## Pearl

The most Step 1-testable primary blast injury is tympanic membrane rupture, but the most life-threatening primary blast injury is blast lung.

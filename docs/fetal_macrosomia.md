Fetal macrosomia means a fetus/newborn is **too large**, usually:

* birth weight > 4000 g
* or sometimes > 4500 g

Related term:

* **LGA (large for gestational age)** = weight > 90th percentile for that gestational age

So:

* **macrosomia** = absolute big baby
* **LGA (large for gestational age)** = big compared with babies of the same gestational age

---

### Main cause to know

Classically associated with maternal diabetes, including **GDM (gestational diabetes mellitus)**.

Why?

* maternal glucose crosses placenta
* fetal pancreas responds by making more insulin
* insulin acts like a growth factor
* fetus grows larger, especially with more fat and broader shoulders

Important: maternal insulin does **not** cross the placenta, but maternal glucose does.

---

### Why it matters

A large fetus is harder to deliver vaginally.

Major complication:

* **shoulder dystocia** = fetal shoulder gets stuck behind the maternal pubic bone during delivery

This can cause:

* brachial plexus injury (nerve injury affecting the arm)
* clavicle fracture
* hypoxia (low oxygen)
* increased C-section risk
* postpartum hemorrhage (bleeding after delivery), often from uterine atony (uterus fails to contract well)

---

### After birth

Baby can get **hypoglycemia (low blood glucose)**.

Why?

The baby was exposed to high maternal glucose in utero, so fetal insulin was high. After birth, maternal glucose supply stops, but insulin stays high for a while, dropping the baby's glucose.

---

## One-line intuition

Maternal glucose overfeeds the fetus, fetal insulin makes the fetus grow, and the big shoulders create delivery risk.

---

## HY pearl

In an infant of a diabetic mother, think:

* macrosomia
* neonatal hypoglycemia
* shoulder dystocia
* hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (thickened heart muscle)

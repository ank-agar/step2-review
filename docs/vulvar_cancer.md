### Core idea

Vulvar cancer is cancer of the vulva, meaning the external female genital area.

The vulva includes:

* labia majora = outer lips
* labia minora = inner lips
* clitoris
* urethral opening
* vaginal opening
* perineum

Most vulvar cancers are squamous cell carcinoma, meaning cancer from squamous cells, which are flat surface epithelial cells.

---

### Why it happens

Two big pathways:

* HPV-related pathway: HPV (human papillomavirus) causes DNA damage in squamous epithelial cells
* Lichen sclerosus pathway: lichen sclerosus is a chronic inflammatory vulvar skin disease that can predispose to cancer over time

Intuition:

Chronic epithelial injury or viral oncogene activity makes vulvar surface cells accumulate mutations, then they can progress from precancer to invasive cancer.

---

### What it looks like clinically

Can present as:

* chronic vulvar itching
* pain or burning
* nonhealing ulcer
* lump or wart-like growth
* bleeding
* skin color change or thickened vulvar skin

Important: persistent vulvar itching in an older patient is not automatically “just irritation.”

---

### Precancer step

VIN (vulvar intraepithelial neoplasia) means abnormal precancerous squamous cells are still within the epithelium.

Break it down:

* vulvar = vulva
* intraepithelial = inside the epithelial surface layer
* neoplasia = abnormal new growth

So VIN is the “abnormal surface-cell stage” before invasion.

---

### Diagnosis and spread

Diagnosis is by biopsy, meaning taking tissue and looking under the microscope.

Vulvar cancer can spread to inguinal lymph nodes, meaning lymph nodes in the groin.

Why groin nodes?

Because lymph from the vulva drains mainly to superficial inguinal lymph nodes.

---

## One-line intuition

Vulvar cancer is usually squamous cancer of the external genital skin, often driven by HPV or chronic inflammatory vulvar disease.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 pearl: vulvar cancer classically spreads first to superficial inguinal lymph nodes.

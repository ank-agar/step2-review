## Goiter

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/-/media/Images/Health-Information/Endocrine-Diseases/ThyroidAnatomy_758x864.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/17071.jpg)

### Core idea

A goiter is an enlarged thyroid gland.

The thyroid is the butterfly-shaped endocrine gland in the front of the neck that makes thyroid hormone.

So:

> goiter = big thyroid

It is a physical finding, not one specific disease.

---

### Why it happens

The thyroid grows when it is being overstimulated or structurally abnormal.

Common causes:

* iodine deficiency
* Graves disease
* Hashimoto thyroiditis
* multinodular goiter
* thyroid nodules

Iodine deficiency is the classic Step 1 cause.

Why?

* iodine is needed to make thyroid hormone
* low thyroid hormone makes the pituitary release more TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone)
* TSH stimulates thyroid growth
* chronic stimulation makes the thyroid enlarge

---

### Hormone levels

A goiter can be:

* hyperthyroid = too much thyroid hormone
* hypothyroid = too little thyroid hormone
* euthyroid = normal thyroid hormone level

So do not assume goiter automatically means hyperthyroidism.

---

### Symptoms from size

Large goiters can compress nearby structures:

* trachea = breathing issues
* esophagus = swallowing issues
* recurrent laryngeal nerve = hoarseness

---

## One-line intuition

The thyroid is either trying too hard, being stimulated too much, or growing nodules, so the gland becomes visibly enlarged in the neck.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Iodine deficiency causes low thyroid hormone, which causes high TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone), and TSH acts like a growth signal for the thyroid → goiter.

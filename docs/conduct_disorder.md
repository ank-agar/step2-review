# Conduct disorder

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/41/Conduct_disorder_framework.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/88/A_framework_for_understanding_conduct_disorder.png)

Conduct disorder is a childhood/adolescent psychiatric disorder where a kid has a **repetitive pattern of violating rules or the rights of others**.

Think:

* aggression toward people/animals
* destruction of property
* lying/stealing
* serious rule-breaking

The key is **persistent pattern**, not one bad decision.

---

### Core idea

Conduct disorder is basically:

> “I repeatedly break major social rules and harm others’ rights.”

This is more severe than oppositional defiant disorder (ODD = oppositional defiant disorder), where the kid is mainly angry, defiant, and argumentative but usually **not** seriously violent, stealing, or destroying property.

---

### Why the findings happen

Conduct disorder often involves impaired control of:

* impulses
* aggression
* empathy
* response to punishment

So the child may keep doing harmful behaviors even when there are consequences.

Some patients also have **callous-unemotional traits** (low guilt, low empathy, shallow emotional response), which makes the disorder more severe.

---

### Diagnosis pattern

DSM-5 (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition) pattern:

* at least 3 symptoms in the past 12 months
* at least 1 symptom in the past 6 months
* causes impairment in school, home, or social life

Examples:

* bullying or fighting
* cruelty to animals
* fire-setting or vandalism
* breaking into places
* lying for gain
* truancy (skipping school) before age 13

---

### Big association

Conduct disorder in childhood can progress to antisocial personality disorder (ASPD = antisocial personality disorder) in adulthood.

But ASPD cannot be diagnosed until age 18, and there must be evidence of conduct disorder before age 15.

---

## One-line intuition

Conduct disorder is when a child repeatedly crosses the line from “defiant” into **harmful rule-breaking that violates other people’s rights**.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 distinction:

* ODD (oppositional defiant disorder) = argues/defies authority
* Conduct disorder = violates rights/laws
* ASPD (antisocial personality disorder) = adult version, age at least 18, with conduct disorder before age 15

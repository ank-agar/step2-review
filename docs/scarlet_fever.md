Scarlet fever is basically:

**strep throat + toxin-mediated rash**

It is caused by **Group A Streptococcus** (GAS; *Streptococcus pyogenes*, a gram-positive cocci bacterium that commonly causes strep throat).

---

### Core idea

Some strains of *Streptococcus pyogenes* make **erythrogenic exotoxin** (a toxin that causes red skin findings).

That toxin triggers an immune reaction, giving:

* fever
* sore throat
* red “sandpaper” rash
* strawberry tongue

So the rash is not just bacteria sitting in the skin.

It is mainly the **toxin/immune response** showing up on the skin.

---

### Classic findings

* **Fever**
* **Pharyngitis** (throat inflammation)
* **Tonsillar exudates** (pus-like white patches on the tonsils)
* **Strawberry tongue** (red bumpy tongue from inflamed papillae, which are small tongue bumps)
* **Sandpaper rash** (fine rough red rash)
* **Pastia lines** (brighter red rash in skin folds like the armpit/groin/elbow crease)
* **Circumoral pallor** (pale area around the mouth)
* Later: **desquamation** (skin peeling), especially fingertips/toes

---

### Why the tongue changes

Early on, the tongue can look white because it gets a coating.

Then that coating sheds off, leaving enlarged red papillae.

That gives the classic:

> red, bumpy “strawberry tongue”

---

### Why the rash feels like sandpaper

The toxin causes diffuse skin inflammation.

That inflammation makes the skin:

* red
* finely bumpy
* rough to touch

So the “sandpaper” feel is from many tiny raised inflamed bumps.

---

### Treatment

Treat with **penicillin** or **amoxicillin** (beta-lactam antibiotics that kill susceptible bacteria by blocking cell wall synthesis).

Reason treatment matters:

* shortens illness
* decreases spread
* prevents **acute rheumatic fever** (autoimmune heart/joint/brain disease after untreated strep throat)

---

## One-line intuition

Scarlet fever is **Group A strep pharyngitis where a toxin makes the skin and tongue turn red and rough**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Scarlet fever is caused by **lysogenic phage-encoded erythrogenic toxin**.

Meaning: a bacteriophage (virus that infects bacteria) gives *Streptococcus pyogenes* the gene for the toxin.

That is why only certain Group A strep strains cause scarlet fever.

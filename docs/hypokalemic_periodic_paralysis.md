## Hypokalemic periodic paralysis

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1338/bin/hpp-Image001.gif)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1338/bin/hpp-Image002.gif)

![Image](https://dm5migu4zj3pb.cloudfront.net/manuscripts/66000/66535/medium/JCI66535.sb1.jpg)

Hypokalemic periodic paralysis is a channelopathy (ion channel disorder) where patients get episodic attacks of flaccid muscle weakness/paralysis with low serum potassium.

Break it down:

* hypo- = low
* kalemic = potassium in blood
* periodic = comes in episodes
* paralysis = weakness/inability to move

So:

> low-potassium episodes causing temporary muscle paralysis.

---

### Core idea

The problem is usually a mutation in skeletal muscle ion channels:

* **CACNA1S** = gene for a skeletal muscle calcium channel
* **SCN4A** = gene for a skeletal muscle sodium channel

These channels help muscle fibers fire action potentials (electrical signals that make muscle contract).

When potassium gets too low outside the muscle cell, the abnormal channel makes the muscle membrane electrically unstable/inexcitable.

Result:

* sodium channels stay inactivated
* muscle action potentials fail
* the patient becomes weak or paralyzed

---

### Why attacks happen after carbs/exercise

Big trigger:

* high-carbohydrate meal

Why?

Carbs increase insulin. Insulin pushes potassium into cells.

So blood potassium falls:

* serum K+ (potassium ion) decreases
* muscle membrane becomes inexcitable
* weakness/paralysis attack happens

Other triggers:

* rest after strenuous exercise
* stress
* cold
* high-salt meals

---

### What it looks like

Classically:

* episodic proximal muscle weakness
* legs often affected more than arms
* normal strength between attacks
* low potassium during the attack
* respiratory muscles usually spared

Proximal means closer to the trunk, like thighs/hips/shoulders.

---

### Important association

Can also happen with hyperthyroidism (too much thyroid hormone).

This is called thyrotoxic periodic paralysis.

Think:

* young Asian man
* hyperthyroid symptoms
* weakness after heavy carbs/exercise
* low potassium

---

## One-line intuition

Potassium shifts into cells, blood potassium drops, and mutated skeletal muscle channels make the muscle electrically unable to contract.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 trigger: high-carb meal → insulin release → potassium shifts into cells → hypokalemia → periodic paralysis attack.

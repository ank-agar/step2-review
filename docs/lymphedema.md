### Core idea

Lymphedema = swelling caused by poor lymph drainage.

Lymph is protein-rich fluid that normally drains from tissues back into the blood through lymphatic vessels.

So if lymph vessels/nodes are:

* blocked
* removed
* damaged
* congenitally abnormal

then fluid gets stuck in the tissue.

---

### Why the swelling happens

Normally:

* capillaries leak fluid into tissues
* lymphatics pick up that extra fluid/protein
* lymph returns it to venous blood

In lymphedema:

* lymph cannot drain
* protein-rich fluid accumulates in the interstitium (space between cells)
* protein pulls in more water
* chronic inflammation causes fibrosis (scarring)

That is why early lymphedema can be soft/pitting, but chronic lymphedema becomes firm and nonpitting.

---

### Common causes

Secondary lymphedema is more common and happens after lymphatic damage from:

* lymph node dissection, especially after breast cancer surgery
* radiation therapy
* tumor obstruction
* infection
* filariasis from Wuchereria bancrofti, a parasite that blocks lymphatics

Primary lymphedema means the lymphatic vessels developed abnormally from birth.

---

### Key findings

Classically:

* chronic limb swelling
* heaviness/tightness
* thickened skin
* recurrent cellulitis (bacterial skin infection)
* nonpitting edema later

Stemmer sign = inability to pinch the skin at the base of the toe/finger because the skin is thickened from lymphedema.

---

## One-line intuition

Lymphedema is like a clogged tissue drain: fluid and protein enter the tissue, but the lymphatic exit route is blocked.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Most common worldwide cause of lymphedema is filariasis due to Wuchereria bancrofti, but in the United States it is commonly due to malignancy treatment, especially lymph node removal or radiation.

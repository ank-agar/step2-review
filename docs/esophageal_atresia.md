## Esophageal atresia

Esophageal atresia means the esophagus does not form as one continuous tube.

So the baby has:

* upper esophagus ending in a blind pouch
* lower esophagus disconnected from the upper pouch
* often a connection to the trachea

Trachea = airway.

Esophagus = food tube.

Atresia = closed off / absent opening.

---

### Core idea

Food cannot pass from mouth to stomach because the esophagus is interrupted.

So the newborn has:

* drooling / excess secretions
* choking or coughing with feeds
* cyanosis (bluish skin from low oxygen)
* inability to pass a nasogastric tube into the stomach

Nasogastric tube = tube from nose to stomach.

---

### Important association

Most cases are associated with TEF (tracheoesophageal fistula).

TEF = abnormal connection between trachea and esophagus.

Most common type:

* upper esophagus ends blindly
* lower esophagus connects to trachea

This is why air can enter the stomach:

* trachea → fistula → distal esophagus → stomach

So abdominal gas on x-ray suggests a distal TEF.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Drooling:** saliva cannot go down into the stomach, so it pools in the upper pouch
* **Choking with feeds:** milk hits the blind pouch and can spill into the airway
* **Cyanosis:** aspiration (breathing fluid into lungs) impairs oxygenation
* **Coiled tube on x-ray:** the tube cannot pass the blind pouch, so it curls up proximally
* **Gas in stomach:** air travels from trachea through the fistula into the GI (gastrointestinal) tract

---

### Step 1 association

Think VACTERL association.

VACTERL =

* Vertebral defects
* Anal atresia
* Cardiac defects
* Tracheoesophageal fistula
* Esophageal atresia
* Renal defects
* Limb defects

So if you see esophageal atresia, look for other congenital anomalies.

---

## One-line intuition

The food tube is blocked, and it often has a wrong connection to the airway, so feeds cannot reach the stomach and can enter the lungs.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Esophageal atresia with distal TEF classically shows:

* newborn choking/drooling with feeds
* nasogastric tube coiled in the upper pouch
* gas in the stomach

Gas in the stomach = distal fistula is likely present.

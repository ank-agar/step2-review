### Core idea

Brain metastasis means cancer started somewhere else in the body, then spread to the brain.

So it is a **secondary brain tumor**, not a primary brain tumor.

Common sources:

* lung cancer
* breast cancer
* melanoma
* kidney cancer
* colon cancer

---

### Why it goes to the brain

Cancer cells can break off from the original tumor and enter the bloodstream.

Then they can lodge in small brain blood vessels and grow into new tumor deposits.

That is why brain metastases often appear:

* multiple
* well-circumscribed
* near the gray-white junction

Gray-white junction = where gray matter (neuronal cell body-rich brain tissue) meets white matter (axon-rich brain tissue).

Reason: blood vessels narrow and branch there, so traveling tumor cells can get trapped.

---

### What it looks like

On MRI (magnetic resonance imaging), brain metastases often show:

* ring-enhancing lesions
* surrounding vasogenic edema
* sometimes multiple lesions

Vasogenic edema = fluid leakage from disrupted blood vessels into brain tissue.

Why edema happens:

* tumor damages the blood-brain barrier
* vessels become leaky
* fluid collects around the tumor

Blood-brain barrier = tight vessel wall system that normally protects the brain from many blood substances.

---

### Symptoms

Symptoms depend on where the metastasis lands:

* headache
* seizure
* weakness
* confusion/personality change
* vision or speech problems

One-line intuition:

> A cancer cell takes a blood ride to the brain, gets stuck, grows there, and irritates/compresses surrounding brain tissue.

---

## Pearl

The most common brain tumors in adults are **metastases**, not primary brain tumors.

Also HY: multiple ring-enhancing brain lesions in an adult with known cancer = think brain metastases.

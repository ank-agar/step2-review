### Core idea

Hyperthyroidism in pregnancy = too much thyroid hormone during pregnancy.

Most common cause:

* Graves disease = autoimmune hyperthyroidism where antibodies stimulate the TSH receptor (thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor)

So the thyroid gets told:

> "Make more T3 and T4."

T3 (triiodothyronine) and T4 (thyroxine) increase metabolism.

---

### Why pregnancy makes this tricky

Early pregnancy has high hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin).

hCG can weakly stimulate the TSH receptor because it looks a little like TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone).

So in early pregnancy:

* TSH can be low normally
* mild hyperthyroid labs can happen transiently

But Graves is more pathologic because TRAb (thyroid receptor antibody) is positive and can cross the placenta.

---

### Labs

Typical overt hyperthyroidism:

* low TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone)
* high free T4 (thyroxine) and/or high T3 (triiodothyronine)

Subclinical hyperthyroidism:

* low TSH
* normal T3/T4

Subclinical disease usually does not need treatment in pregnancy.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Too much thyroid hormone makes the body run "too fast":

* weight loss despite eating
* heat intolerance
* tremor
* palpitations
* tachycardia (fast heart rate)
* anxiety
* diarrhea

Graves-specific clues:

* diffuse goiter (enlarged thyroid gland)
* ophthalmopathy (eye involvement, classically bulging eyes)
* pretibial myxedema (nonpitting skin thickening over the shins)

---

### Why the fetus matters

Maternal TRAb (thyroid receptor antibody) can cross the placenta.

That means maternal Graves antibodies can stimulate the fetal thyroid too, causing:

* fetal tachycardia (fast fetal heart rate)
* fetal goiter
* growth restriction
* preterm birth
* neonatal hyperthyroidism

Antithyroid drugs can also cross the placenta, so too much medication can cause fetal hypothyroidism (low thyroid hormone) and fetal goiter.

So treatment is a balance:

> control mom's hyperthyroidism, but do not over-block the baby's thyroid.

---

### Treatment idea

Use antithyroid drugs:

* PTU (propylthiouracil) in the 1st trimester
* methimazole after the 1st trimester if needed

Why?

* methimazole is avoided early because it can cause congenital anomalies
* PTU is preferred early but has liver toxicity risk, so many patients switch later

Radioactive iodine is contraindicated in pregnancy because it can destroy the fetal thyroid.

---

## One-line intuition

In pregnancy, hyperthyroidism is usually Graves disease, and the key danger is that both antibodies and medications can cross the placenta and affect the fetal thyroid.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Graves disease often improves later in pregnancy because pregnancy is relatively immunosuppressive, but it can flare postpartum when the immune system rebounds.

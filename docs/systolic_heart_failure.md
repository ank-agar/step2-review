### Core idea

Systolic heart failure = the heart cannot squeeze well during systole (ventricular contraction).

So the left ventricle pumps out too little blood.

This is also called:

* HFrEF (heart failure with reduced ejection fraction)
* reduced EF (ejection fraction)

EF (ejection fraction) = percent of blood in the left ventricle that gets pumped out with each beat.

In systolic heart failure:

* contractility is decreased
* stroke volume is decreased
* EF is decreased, usually less than or equal to 40%

---

### Why the findings happen

If the ventricle is weak, it cannot empty well.

So blood stays behind in the left ventricle after systole.

That causes:

* increased LV volume
* ventricular dilation
* backup into the left atrium and lungs
* pulmonary edema, meaning fluid in the lungs
* dyspnea, meaning shortness of breath
* orthopnea, meaning shortness of breath when lying flat

Think:

> weak pump -> blood backs up -> lungs get wet.

---

### Common causes

Major causes include:

* myocardial infarction (heart attack), because dead myocardium (heart muscle) cannot contract
* dilated cardiomyopathy (enlarged weak heart muscle)
* chronic hypertension (high blood pressure), because the heart eventually burns out from pumping against high pressure
* myocarditis (inflammation of heart muscle)

---

### Systolic vs diastolic

Systolic heart failure:

* problem is squeezing
* EF is low
* ventricle is often dilated and weak

Diastolic heart failure:

* problem is filling
* EF is preserved
* ventricle is often stiff/thick

One-line contrast:

> Systolic = can’t squeeze. Diastolic = can’t relax/fill.

---

## One-line intuition

Systolic heart failure is a weak, dilated left ventricle that cannot squeeze enough blood forward, so blood backs up into the lungs.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HFrEF (heart failure with reduced ejection fraction) has mortality benefit from GDMT (guideline-directed medical therapy), especially ACE inhibitors (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors), ARBs (angiotensin receptor blockers), ARNIs (angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors), beta blockers, MRAs (mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists), and SGLT2 inhibitors (sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors).

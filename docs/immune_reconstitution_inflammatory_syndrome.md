### Core idea

Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS) = when the immune system starts recovering, then overreacts to an infection that was already there.

Classic setting:

* patient has HIV (human immunodeficiency virus) with very low CD4 count (helper T-cell count)
* starts ART (antiretroviral therapy)
* viral load drops and immune function improves
* then the patient suddenly worsens from inflammation against an opportunistic infection (infection that takes advantage of weak immunity)

So the weird part is:

> treatment is working, but the patient looks worse.

---

### Why it happens

Before ART (antiretroviral therapy), the immune system is too weak to make much inflammation.

So infections like:

* tuberculosis
* MAC (Mycobacterium avium complex)
* cryptococcus
* CMV (cytomegalovirus)

can be present but not cause a huge immune reaction.

After ART:

* CD4 T cells recover
* immune cells start recognizing old pathogen antigen (pieces of microbe)
* cytokines (immune signaling proteins) rise
* inflammation suddenly increases

That inflammation causes symptoms.

It is not always because the pathogen is growing more. Sometimes it is the immune system finally noticing the pathogen and reacting too hard.

---

### Two types

**Paradoxical IRIS**

The infection was already diagnosed and being treated, but then it gets clinically worse after immune recovery.

Example:

* known tuberculosis
* starts tuberculosis treatment + ART
* then fever, lymph nodes, lung findings worsen

**Unmasking IRIS**

The infection was hidden/subclinical, then immune recovery reveals it.

Example:

* patient starts ART
* then suddenly develops symptoms of previously unnoticed tuberculosis or cryptococcal meningitis

---

### One-line intuition

IRIS is like turning the immune system back on and it immediately attacks leftover infection so aggressively that the inflammation becomes the problem.

---

### Classic clues

Think IRIS when you see:

* advanced HIV/AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome)
* very low CD4 count, often less than 100 cells/mm3
* recent start of ART (antiretroviral therapy)
* clinical worsening despite falling HIV viral load
* inflammation around a known or hidden opportunistic infection

Common timing:

* usually weeks to a few months after starting ART

---

### Important distinction

IRIS is a diagnosis of exclusion, meaning you must rule out other causes first:

* drug toxicity
* poor medication adherence
* resistant infection
* new opportunistic infection
* treatment failure

This matters because IRIS can look like the infection is getting worse, but the mechanism is excessive immune inflammation.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 pearl: cryptococcal meningitis is a big exception where ART (antiretroviral therapy) is usually delayed after starting antifungal therapy, because early immune recovery can trigger dangerous CNS (central nervous system) IRIS with increased intracranial pressure.

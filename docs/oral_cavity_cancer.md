## Oral cavity cancer

![Image](https://www.barrowneuro.org/wp-content/uploads/8000_oralcavity_flat-copy.jpg)

![Image](https://www.barrowneuro.org/wp-content/uploads/im-0026817-01_WEB-1024x720.png)

![Image](https://dermnetnz.org/assets/Uploads/reactions/w/oral-scc01.jpg)

Oral cavity cancer means cancer arising in the mouth, especially from the mucosal lining (wet epithelial surface inside the mouth).

Most cases are:

* SCC (squamous cell carcinoma)

Squamous cells are flat epithelial cells that line the oral cavity. So the intuition is:

> chronic irritation damages the mouth lining, and the squamous cells can become dysplastic, then malignant.

Dysplasia means abnormal cell growth that is not fully cancer yet.

---

### Where it can happen

Oral cavity includes:

* lips
* anterior 2/3 of tongue
* floor of mouth
* buccal mucosa (inner cheek lining)
* gingiva (gums)
* hard palate

Common high-risk sites:

* lateral tongue
* floor of mouth
* lower lip

---

### Risk factors

Big ones:

* tobacco
* alcohol
* betel nut chewing
* poor oral hygiene
* chronic irritation

Tobacco + alcohol together are especially bad because both injure the mucosa, and alcohol helps carcinogens penetrate the tissue more easily.

HPV (human papillomavirus) is more classically linked to oropharyngeal cancer, especially tonsil/base of tongue cancer, not classic oral cavity cancer.

---

### What it looks like

Can present as:

* nonhealing mouth ulcer
* red patch called erythroplakia
* white patch called leukoplakia
* bleeding lesion
* loose teeth
* tongue pain or difficulty moving the tongue
* neck mass from lymph node spread

Why ulcers happen:

Cancer cells invade and destroy the normal surface epithelium, so the mucosa breaks down and does not heal normally.

---

## One-line intuition

Oral cavity cancer is usually squamous cells in the mouth lining turning malignant after chronic carcinogen exposure.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Erythroplakia (red oral patch) is more concerning for dysplasia/cancer than leukoplakia (white oral patch).

# Dubin-Johnson syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Metabolismo_de_la_bilirrubina.svg/500px-Metabolismo_de_la_bilirrubina.svg.png)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/231f/3679424/d738a2383a5e/gr1.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/231f/3679424/a61df5d50450/gr2.jpg)

Dubin-Johnson syndrome is a benign inherited liver disorder where the liver cannot properly secrete conjugated bilirubin into bile.

Key defect:

* mutation in **ABCC2**
* defective **MRP2** (multidrug resistance-associated protein 2)
* autosomal recessive inheritance

MRP2 is the transporter on the canalicular side of the hepatocyte.

Canalicular side = the side of the liver cell facing the bile canaliculus, which is the tiny channel that drains bile.

So normally:

* bilirubin gets conjugated in the liver
* conjugated bilirubin is pumped into bile by MRP2
* bile carries it into the intestine

In Dubin-Johnson:

* conjugation works
* the pump into bile is defective
* conjugated bilirubin backs up into blood

That causes:

* mild jaundice
* dark urine, because conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble
* normal or near-normal liver enzymes
* no major liver damage

Classic pathology:

* black liver
* dark coarse pigment in hepatocytes

Hepatocytes = liver cells.

The liver turns dark because pigmented metabolites accumulate inside the liver cells.

---

### Intuition

Think:

> bilirubin gets processed, but it cannot exit the hepatocyte into bile.

So this is a **conjugated hyperbilirubinemia** problem, not an unconjugated bilirubin problem.

Conjugated hyperbilirubinemia = increased direct bilirubin.

---

### Dubin-Johnson vs Rotor

Both cause benign conjugated hyperbilirubinemia.

Big Step 1 distinction:

* Dubin-Johnson = black liver
* Rotor syndrome = normal-colored liver

---

## Pearl 🔥

Dubin-Johnson syndrome has increased urinary coproporphyrin I.

HY memory:

* **D**ubin-Johnson = **D**ark liver
* defect is excretion of **D**irect bilirubin into bile

## Renal abscess

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/69/Illu_kidney.jpg)

![Image](https://media.springernature.com/lw685/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1186%2Fs13244-019-0826-3/MediaObjects/13244_2019_826_Fig10_HTML.png)

### Core idea

A renal abscess is a pocket of pus inside or around the kidney.

Abscess = localized collection of pus from infection.

Renal = kidney.

So:

> kidney infection gets walled off into a pus-filled cavity.

---

### How it happens

Usually it comes from:

* ascending UTI (urinary tract infection) spreading up to the kidney
* acute pyelonephritis (kidney infection) that does not fully resolve
* hematogenous spread (bacteria spreading through the blood), classically from Staphylococcus aureus

The immune system sends neutrophils (acute infection white blood cells), tissue gets destroyed, and pus collects in one spot.

---

### Why the findings happen

* Fever/chills: systemic infection
* Flank pain: kidney capsule and surrounding tissue get inflamed
* CVA tenderness (costovertebral angle tenderness): tapping over the kidney hurts
* Pyuria (white blood cells in urine): infection involves the urinary tract
* Persistent fever despite antibiotics: pus is walled off, so antibiotics may not penetrate well

---

### Imaging clue

On CT (computed tomography), think:

* rim-enhancing fluid collection
* thick irregular wall
* sometimes gas inside

Why rim enhancement?

The outside wall is inflamed and vascular, while the center is dead tissue/pus.

---

### Treatment idea

Small abscess:

* antibiotics

Large abscess or very sick patient:

* antibiotics + drainage

Drainage matters because pus is like a closed-off infected pocket.

---

## One-line intuition

A renal abscess is basically pyelonephritis that got trapped into a pus pocket instead of staying diffuse.

---

## Pearl 🔥

If a patient has pyelonephritis symptoms but keeps having fever after appropriate antibiotics, think renal abscess or obstruction.

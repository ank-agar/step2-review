# Glioblastoma

![Image](https://cdn.sciencefigures.org/svg/color/brain_glioblastoma_t2_perilesional_edema.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c1/GBM_pseudopalisading_necrosis.jpg)

Glioblastoma = the most aggressive adult primary brain tumor.

It is a malignant astrocytic tumor, meaning it comes from astrocytes (star-shaped glial cells that support neurons).

GBM (glioblastoma multiforme) is WHO (World Health Organization) grade 4.

---

### Core idea

Glioblastoma is basically:

* fast-growing
* infiltrative
* necrotic
* very vascular

It usually occurs in older adults and often arises in the cerebral hemispheres.

Classic imaging idea:

* ring-enhancing brain mass
* central necrosis
* can cross the corpus callosum and form a “butterfly glioma”

Corpus callosum = the white matter bridge connecting the left and right cerebral hemispheres.

---

### Why the findings happen

Glioblastoma grows so fast that parts of the tumor outgrow their blood supply.

That causes:

* **hypoxia:** low oxygen
* **necrosis:** tissue death
* **ring enhancement:** contrast highlights the active tumor rim around dead center

On histology (microscope), the classic finding is:

* pseudopalisading necrosis

Break it down:

* pseudo = false
* palisading = cells lining up like a fence
* necrosis = dead tissue

So pseudopalisading necrosis means tumor cells line up around areas of dead tissue.

---

### Important pathology clues

Glioblastoma often shows:

* pseudopalisading necrosis
* microvascular proliferation
* GFAP positivity

GFAP (glial fibrillary acidic protein) is a marker of glial origin, so it helps tell you this tumor is coming from glial cells.

---

## One-line intuition

Glioblastoma is an aggressive astrocyte-derived brain tumor that grows fast, becomes necrotic in the middle, and invades across brain tissue.

---

## Pearl 🔥

USMLE loves:

> glioblastoma = pseudopalisading necrosis + vascular proliferation.

And if it crosses the corpus callosum, think “butterfly glioma.”

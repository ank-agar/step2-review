### Core idea

Desquamative interstitial pneumonia, or **DIP (desquamative interstitial pneumonia)**, is a smoking-related **ILD (interstitial lung disease)** where the alveoli get filled with lots of macrophages.

Alveoli = tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens.

Macrophages = immune “cleanup” cells.

So the simple picture is:

> smoking injury → macrophages pile up inside alveoli → gas exchange gets harder → cough and shortness of breath.

---

### Why the name is weird

“Desquamative” means cells are sloughing off.

But DIP is kind of misnamed.

The main thing is **not** epithelial cells peeling off.

The main thing is:

* lots of pigmented macrophages inside alveolar spaces
* mild thickening/inflammation of the interstitium

Interstitium = the thin tissue/wall between alveoli.

---

### Classic associations

Strongly associated with:

* cigarette smoking
* sometimes marijuana smoking
* rarely occupational exposures or autoimmune disease

Typical patient:

* middle-aged smoker
* progressive dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* dry cough
* possible clubbing

---

### What you see

Histology:

* alveoli full of brown “smoker’s macrophages”
* relatively preserved lung architecture
* mild interstitial inflammation/fibrosis

Imaging:

* **HRCT (high-resolution computed tomography)** often shows diffuse ground-glass opacities, usually lower-lobe predominant

Ground-glass opacity = hazy lung opacity where you can still see vessels through it.

---

## One-line intuition

DIP is basically a smoker’s lung reaction where macrophages stuff the alveoli, making the lung look hazy and making gas exchange worse.

---

## Pearl 🔥

DIP is on the same smoking-related spectrum as **RB-ILD (respiratory bronchiolitis-associated interstitial lung disease)**, but DIP is more diffuse and severe:

* RB-ILD = macrophages mostly around respiratory bronchioles
* DIP = macrophages diffusely filling alveoli

Also, DIP generally has a better prognosis than **UIP (usual interstitial pneumonia)**, especially if the patient stops smoking.

### Core idea

A pharyngeal foreign body means an object is stuck in the pharynx (throat space behind the nose/mouth and above the esophagus/trachea).

Common examples:

* fish bone
* chicken bone
* dental appliance
* pill
* coin or toy in kids

---

### Why symptoms happen

The pharynx is a shared pathway for:

* air going to the larynx/trachea (voice box/windpipe)
* food going to the esophagus (food tube)

So something stuck there can cause:

* throat pain
* foreign body sensation
* dysphagia (difficulty swallowing)
* odynophagia (painful swallowing)
* drooling
* coughing/choking
* airway distress if it blocks breathing

Sharp objects are more dangerous because they can pierce mucosa (inner lining), causing bleeding, infection, abscess (pus collection), or perforation (hole through the wall).

---

### High-yield locations

Fish bones often lodge in areas where the pharynx narrows or has folds:

* tonsils
* base of tongue
* vallecula (space between tongue base and epiglottis)
* piriform sinus (pocket beside the laryngeal opening)
* hypopharynx (lower pharynx)

Intuition:

> The throat is not a smooth pipe; it has pockets and curves, so thin sharp objects can snag.

---

### Diagnosis/treatment idea

First concern is always airway.

Then clinicians may use:

* laryngoscopy (looking at larynx/pharynx with a scope)
* X-ray if the object is radiopaque (visible on X-ray)
* CT (computed tomography) if X-ray is negative but suspicion is high, especially for sharp bones or complications
* endoscopy (camera procedure) to remove it

Important: fish bones can be hard to see on plain X-ray, so a negative X-ray does not always rule it out.

---

## One-line intuition

A pharyngeal foreign body is something stuck in the throat passage, dangerous mainly because it can obstruct the airway or pierce the thin pharyngeal wall.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Pharyngeal foreign body + drooling, stridor (high-pitched breathing sound), respiratory distress, or inability to handle secretions = airway emergency.

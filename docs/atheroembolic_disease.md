### Core idea

Atheroembolic disease means pieces of atherosclerotic plaque break off and shower downstream into small arteries.

Break it down:

* athero- = atherosclerotic plaque, meaning cholesterol-rich plaque in an artery wall
* embolic = material traveling through blood and lodging somewhere else

So this is basically:

> cholesterol plaque debris breaks off, travels through arteries, and blocks tiny vessels.

---

### Why it happens

Usually there is a big unstable plaque in a large artery, often the aorta.

Then something disrupts it, like:

* cardiac catheterization
* angiography
* vascular surgery
* anticoagulation or thrombolysis sometimes

The plaque releases cholesterol crystals.

Those crystals travel downstream and get stuck in small arteries.

---

### Why the findings happen

The cholesterol crystals cause:

* mechanical blockage of small arteries
* local inflammation
* ischemia, meaning poor blood flow causing tissue injury

That is why you can see:

* acute kidney injury, because kidneys get lots of blood flow
* livedo reticularis, meaning a lace-like purple skin pattern
* blue toe syndrome, meaning painful blue/purple toes
* abdominal pain if gut vessels are hit
* eosinophilia, meaning increased eosinophils, because the crystals trigger inflammation

---

### Classic Step-style setup

Older patient with atherosclerosis gets a recent vascular procedure, then develops:

* rising creatinine
* livedo reticularis or blue toes
* eosinophilia

Think:

> cholesterol emboli from ruptured atherosclerotic plaque.

---

### Histology

On biopsy, cholesterol crystals dissolve during tissue processing, so they leave empty needle-shaped spaces.

These are called:

* cholesterol clefts

Intuition:

The crystal was there, but it got washed out, leaving a long empty slit.

---

## One-line intuition

Atheroembolic disease is a "shower" of cholesterol crystals from a ruptured plaque that clogs tiny arteries and causes kidney + skin findings.

---

## HY pearl

After cardiac catheterization, acute kidney injury plus livedo reticularis points to atheroembolic disease, not just contrast nephropathy. Contrast nephropathy usually causes kidney injury without blue toes, livedo reticularis, or eosinophilia.

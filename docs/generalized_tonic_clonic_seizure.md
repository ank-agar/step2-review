### Core idea

A generalized tonic-clonic seizure is the classic "big seizure" where abnormal electrical activity involves both sides of the brain from the start.

Break the name apart:

* generalized = both cerebral hemispheres are involved
* tonic = stiffening
* clonic = rhythmic jerking
* seizure = abnormal excessive neuronal firing

So:

> both sides of the brain fire abnormally, causing loss of consciousness, body stiffening, then rhythmic jerking.

---

### What happens

Usually the sequence is:

* sudden loss of consciousness
* tonic phase: muscles become rigid/stiff
* clonic phase: rhythmic jerking of arms/legs
* postictal phase: confusion, sleepiness, headache, muscle soreness

Postictal means after the seizure.

---

### Why the findings happen

* Loss of consciousness: both hemispheres are involved, so awareness networks are disrupted
* Stiff body first: sustained motor neuron activation causes continuous muscle contraction
* Jerking after: firing becomes more intermittent, so muscles contract-relax-contract-relax
* Tongue biting/incontinence: strong uncontrolled muscle activity plus impaired awareness
* Sleepy/confused afterward: neurons are exhausted and brain activity is recovering

---

### Key distinction

Generalized tonic-clonic seizure:

* starts bilaterally
* no focal onset

Focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizure:

* starts in one brain region
* then spreads to both hemispheres

Clinically they can look very similar, so EEG (electroencephalogram, brain electrical recording) and history matter.

---

## One-line intuition

The whole brain gets pulled into an electrical storm, so the whole body first stiffens, then rhythmically jerks, then the patient is wiped out afterward.

---

## Pearl

Status epilepticus = seizure lasting more than 5 minutes, or repeated seizures without return to baseline consciousness; treat urgently with benzodiazepines like lorazepam.

## Hepatitis D

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d4/Hepatitis-d-virion-Pathogens-04-00046-g001-1024.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470436/bin/Hepatitis__D.jpg)

### Core idea

Hepatitis D is liver inflammation caused by HDV (hepatitis D virus).

The key thing:

* HDV is defective
* it needs HBV (hepatitis B virus) to infect people
* specifically, it needs HBsAg (hepatitis B surface antigen) as its outer coat

So:

> no HBV = no hepatitis D infection.

---

### Why it needs hepatitis B

HDV has its own RNA (ribonucleic acid) genome, but it cannot make a complete viral envelope by itself.

It “borrows” HBsAg (hepatitis B surface antigen) from HBV.

Think:

* HDV = dangerous passenger
* HBV surface antigen = the car it needs to enter liver cells

That is why hepatitis B vaccination also protects against hepatitis D.

---

### Two ways infection happens

### Coinfection

Coinfection means HBV (hepatitis B virus) and HDV (hepatitis D virus) infect the patient at the same time.

Usually:

* acute hepatitis
* often clears
* lower risk of chronic infection

### Superinfection

Superinfection means someone already has chronic HBV, then gets HDV later.

This is worse because the patient already has HBsAg available, so HDV can spread efficiently.

Usually:

* more severe hepatitis
* higher risk of chronic HDV
* faster progression to cirrhosis (scarred, chronically damaged liver)

---

### What it causes

HDV can cause:

* jaundice (yellow skin/eyes from bilirubin buildup)
* high AST/ALT (aspartate aminotransferase/alanine aminotransferase; liver injury enzymes)
* fulminant hepatitis (sudden severe liver failure)
* chronic hepatitis
* cirrhosis

The reason liver enzymes rise is that infected hepatocytes (liver cells) are being injured, so AST/ALT leak into the blood.

---

## One-line intuition

Hepatitis D is a defective RNA virus that piggybacks on hepatitis B surface antigen, making hepatitis B infection much more severe.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HDV superinfection of a chronic HBV carrier is classically more dangerous than HBV/HDV coinfection because it has a much higher risk of chronic hepatitis and cirrhosis.

## Shigella

![Image](https://www.frontiersin.org/files/Articles/141342/xml-images/fimmu-06-00219-g001.webp)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil///PHIL_Images/22177/22177_lores.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/29/Shigella_stool.jpg)

Shigella is a gram-negative rod that causes bacterial dysentery.

Bacterial dysentery = inflammatory diarrhea with:

* fever
* abdominal cramps
* blood/pus in stool
* tenesmus (painful feeling that you still need to poop even after going)

---

### Core idea

Shigella invades the colonic mucosa.

So unlike a purely toxin-mediated watery diarrhea, it physically damages/inflames the colon.

That is why you get:

* blood = mucosal destruction
* pus/WBCs (white blood cells) = inflammatory invasion
* fever = inflammation
* cramps/tenesmus = irritated colon/rectum

---

### How it invades

Shigella enters through M cells (microfold cells; immune-sampling cells over Peyer patches).

Then it:

* escapes macrophages
* enters epithelial cells
* escapes the phagosome (intracellular vesicle)
* uses actin rockets to spread cell-to-cell

Actin rockets = it hijacks the host cell cytoskeleton to push itself into neighboring cells.

---

### Important toxin

Shigella dysenteriae makes Shiga toxin.

Shiga toxin inactivates the 60S ribosomal subunit.

Translation:

* ribosome = protein-making machine
* 60S = large subunit of the eukaryotic ribosome
* inhibiting it blocks protein synthesis

This can cause HUS (hemolytic uremic syndrome), especially after bloody diarrhea.

HUS = hemolytic anemia + thrombocytopenia (low platelets) + acute kidney injury.

---

## One-line intuition

Shigella is basically an invasive colon infection: it gets into cells, spreads sideways using actin, and causes bloody inflammatory diarrhea.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Shigella has a very low infectious dose, so it spreads easily person-to-person, especially in daycare or crowded settings.

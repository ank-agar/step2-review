## Gout

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Uric_acid_in_the_knee.svg/960px-Uric_acid_in_the_knee.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Gout_Signs_and_Symptoms.jpg/1280px-Gout_Signs_and_Symptoms.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/1222.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK546606/bin/gouta.jpg)

Gout is inflammatory arthritis caused by deposition of monosodium urate crystals (uric acid salt crystals) in joints.

Classic presentation:

* sudden severe joint pain
* red, hot, swollen joint
* usually the first metatarsophalangeal joint (MTP joint = big toe joint)

This classic big toe gout is called podagra (gout affecting the big toe).

---

### Core idea

Purines (DNA/RNA breakdown products) get broken down into uric acid.

If uric acid gets too high in blood, it can crystallize in joints.

Those crystals trigger a huge neutrophil (acute inflammatory white blood cell) response.

So:

> high uric acid -> needle-shaped urate crystals -> neutrophils attack crystals -> painful hot swollen joint

---

### Why it hurts so much

The crystals are not just sitting there quietly.

They activate inflammation:

* neutrophils rush in
* cytokines (inflammatory signaling proteins) are released
* the joint gets hot, red, swollen, and extremely tender

That is why gout feels sudden and dramatic.

---

### Diagnosis

Joint aspiration shows:

* needle-shaped crystals
* negatively birefringent crystals under polarized light

Birefringent means the crystals change color depending on how light passes through them.

Memory:

* gout = negative, needle-shaped
* pseudogout = positive, rhomboid-shaped

---

### Risk factors

Things that increase uric acid:

* high cell turnover, like leukemia/lymphoma or tumor lysis syndrome
* alcohol
* red meat/seafood
* chronic kidney disease (kidneys cannot excrete uric acid well)
* thiazide diuretics (water pills that reduce uric acid excretion)
* loop diuretics (water pills like furosemide that reduce uric acid excretion)

---

### Treatment intuition

Acute flare:

* NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs), like indomethacin
* colchicine
* glucocorticoids (steroids)

Chronic prevention:

* allopurinol or febuxostat inhibit xanthine oxidase (enzyme that makes uric acid)
* probenecid increases renal uric acid excretion

Important: do not start allopurinol during an acute flare if the patient is not already taking it, because sudden uric acid shifts can worsen the flare.

---

## One-line intuition

Gout is when excess uric acid crystallizes in joints, and the immune system attacks those crystals so hard that the joint becomes suddenly red, hot, swollen, and very painful.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Gout crystals are negatively birefringent, needle-shaped monosodium urate crystals; pseudogout crystals are positively birefringent, rhomboid-shaped calcium pyrophosphate crystals.

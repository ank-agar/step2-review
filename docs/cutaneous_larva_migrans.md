# Cutaneous larva migrans

Cutaneous larva migrans means:

* **cutaneous** = skin
* **larva** = immature worm
* **migrans** = migrating/moving

So it is literally:

> worm larvae migrating through the skin.

Usually this is from **dog/cat hookworm larvae**, especially **Ancylostoma braziliense**.

---

### Core idea

Humans are the wrong host.

The larvae can enter human skin, but they cannot complete their normal life cycle in humans.

So instead of growing into adult worms, they wander around in the superficial skin.

That creates the classic:

* itchy
* red
* raised
* winding/serpiginous track

**Serpiginous** means snake-like or wavy.

---

### Why it happens

Animal hookworms normally live in dogs/cats.

Their eggs are passed in animal feces into warm, moist sand or soil.

Then larvae develop in the environment.

When bare skin touches contaminated sand/soil, the larvae penetrate the skin.

Common exposure:

* walking barefoot on beach sand
* sitting/lying on contaminated sand
* tropical or subtropical travel

---

### Why the rash looks like a squiggly line

The larva is physically moving through the epidermis (outer skin layer).

As it moves, the immune system reacts behind it, making an inflamed track.

That is why the lesion can look like it is “creeping” forward over days.

Another name is:

* **creeping eruption**

---

### Key symptoms

The biggest symptom is:

* intense itching

Usually no major systemic symptoms because the larvae mostly stay in the skin.

---

### Treatment

Treat with anti-helminthic drugs.

**Anti-helminthic** means anti-worm medication.

Common options:

* albendazole
* ivermectin

---

## One-line intuition

Dog/cat hookworm larvae accidentally enter human skin, get stuck because humans are the wrong host, and crawl around causing itchy snake-like tracks.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Cutaneous larva migrans = **serpiginous pruritic rash after beach/soil exposure**.

**Pruritic** means itchy.

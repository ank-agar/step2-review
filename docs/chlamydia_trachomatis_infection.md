### Core idea

Chlamydia trachomatis is an obligate intracellular bacterium (must live inside host cells) that commonly causes a sexually transmitted infection.

It infects columnar epithelial cells (mucus-secreting lining cells), especially in:

* cervix
* urethra
* rectum
* conjunctiva (eye surface)

---

### What it causes

In females:

* cervicitis (inflammation of the cervix)
* pelvic inflammatory disease, or PID (infection/inflammation of upper female reproductive tract)
* infertility/ectopic pregnancy from fallopian tube scarring

In males:

* urethritis (inflammation of urethra)
* epididymitis (inflammation of epididymis)

In newborns:

* conjunctivitis (eye infection)
* pneumonia (lung infection)

---

### Why it is often silent

Chlamydia can cause mild inflammation at first, so many patients are asymptomatic (no symptoms).

But even if symptoms are mild, the infection can ascend:

* cervix -> uterus -> fallopian tubes

That inflammation can scar the tubes, which is why untreated infection can cause infertility or ectopic pregnancy.

---

### Life cycle intuition

Chlamydia has 2 main forms:

* elementary body, or EB (infectious form)
* reticulate body, or RB (replicating form)

Think:

* EB = enters cells
* RB = replicates inside cells

So the bacteria hides inside host cells, multiplies, then releases more infectious EBs.

---

### Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually with NAAT (nucleic acid amplification test), which detects bacterial DNA or RNA.

Treatment is usually:

* doxycycline
* azithromycin in pregnancy

Also treat sexual partners to prevent reinfection.

---

## One-line intuition

Chlamydia is a quiet intracellular STI (sexually transmitted infection) that loves epithelial cells and becomes dangerous because chronic inflammation scars reproductive tubes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Chlamydia trachomatis has no classic peptidoglycan cell wall, so beta-lactams (cell wall synthesis inhibitors like penicillin) are not the go-to treatment; use doxycycline or azithromycin instead.

# Herpangina

![Image](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/mmanual-ssq1ci05/media/professional/images/5/9/2/592-herpangina-s186-springer-high.jpg?mw=2048&sc_lang=en-ca)

Herpangina is a viral infection that causes:

* fever
* sore throat
* painful small vesicles/ulcers in the back of the mouth

Vesicles are little fluid-filled blisters.

Ulcers are shallow breaks in the mucosa (the moist lining of the mouth).

---

### Classic location

Think:

> herpangina = posterior mouth lesions

Usually on the:

* soft palate
* uvula
* tonsillar pillars
* posterior pharynx (back of the throat)

That posterior location is the big Step 1 clue.

---

### Cause

Most commonly caused by enteroviruses, especially:

* coxsackievirus A
* sometimes coxsackievirus B
* echovirus
* enterovirus 71

Enteroviruses are viruses that like the GI (gastrointestinal) tract and often spread by fecal-oral transmission.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The virus infects the mucosa in the posterior oropharynx (back part of the mouth/throat).

That causes local inflammation:

* inflamed mucosa becomes red and painful
* small vesicles form
* vesicles rupture into shallow ulcers
* swallowing hurts, so kids may avoid drinking

Big complication to watch for:

* dehydration

because painful mouth sores make the child not want to drink.

---

### Herpangina vs HSV vs hand-foot-mouth

HSV (herpes simplex virus) gingivostomatitis:

* usually more anterior mouth
* gums, lips, tongue, hard palate
* more diffuse gingival inflammation

Herpangina:

* posterior mouth
* soft palate/uvula/tonsillar pillars
* usually no hand/foot rash

Hand-foot-mouth disease:

* also often coxsackievirus
* oral lesions plus rash on hands and feet

---

## One-line intuition

Herpangina is coxsackie/enterovirus making painful little ulcers in the back of a kid's throat.

---

## HY pearl

If a question stem says painful oral ulcers on the soft palate/tonsillar pillars in a child with fever, think herpangina; if it adds hand and foot lesions, think hand-foot-mouth disease.

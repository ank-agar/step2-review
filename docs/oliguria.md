## Oliguria

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/30/Urinary_system.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/69/Renal_corpuscle-en.svg)

Oliguria means low urine output.

Classic definition:

* adults: less than 400-500 mL urine/day
* or less than 0.5 mL/kg/hour

Break the word apart:

* oligo- = little/few
* -uria = urine

So oliguria literally means:

> not enough urine.

---

### Core idea

Urine output depends on:

* blood getting to the kidney
* the kidney filtering that blood
* urine leaving through the urinary tract

So oliguria can happen from problems **before, inside, or after** the kidney.

---

### Main categories

**Prerenal** = before the kidney

The kidney is structurally okay, but not enough blood reaches it.

Examples:

* dehydration
* hemorrhage
* heart failure
* shock

Why urine drops:

Low renal perfusion means low GFR (glomerular filtration rate), so less fluid gets filtered into urine.

---

**Intrinsic renal** = inside the kidney

The kidney tissue itself is injured.

Examples:

* acute tubular necrosis
* glomerulonephritis
* acute interstitial nephritis

Why urine drops:

Damaged nephrons cannot filter or move fluid normally.

---

**Postrenal** = after the kidney

The kidney may make urine, but urine cannot drain out.

Examples:

* kidney stone
* enlarged prostate
* tumor compressing urinary tract

Why urine drops:

Back-pressure builds up and reduces filtration.

---

## One-line intuition

Oliguria is the kidney saying: **I am not getting enough flow, I am injured, or my outflow is blocked.**

---

## Pearl

In a hospitalized patient, oliguria is an early warning sign for AKI (acute kidney injury), and the first Step 1 move is to sort it into **prerenal vs intrinsic renal vs postrenal**.

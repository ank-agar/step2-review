## Hepatorenal syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/HRS_and_ascites_pathophysiology.svg/960px-HRS_and_ascites_pathophysiology.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK430856/bin/HRS__flowchart.jpg)

### Core idea

Hepatorenal syndrome, or HRS (hepatorenal syndrome), is kidney failure caused by severe liver disease.

The key point:

* the kidneys are structurally normal
* but they get too little effective blood flow
* so GFR (glomerular filtration rate, kidney filtering rate) drops

So this is functional kidney failure, not primary kidney destruction.

---

### Why liver disease hurts the kidneys

In cirrhosis (chronic liver scarring), portal hypertension (high pressure in the portal venous system) causes splanchnic vasodilation (widening of blood vessels in the gut circulation).

That means blood pools in the abdominal/gut circulation.

The body senses:

> “I do not have enough effective arterial blood volume.”

Even though the patient may look fluid-overloaded with ascites (fluid in the belly), the arteries feel underfilled.

So the body activates:

* RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system)
* SNS (sympathetic nervous system)
* ADH (antidiuretic hormone)

These systems try to save blood pressure by constricting vessels and holding onto salt/water.

But the kidney gets hit with renal vasoconstriction (narrowing of kidney blood vessels), so renal blood flow falls.

Result:

* decreased GFR (glomerular filtration rate)
* increased creatinine
* low urine sodium
* oliguria (low urine output)

---

### Intuition

The liver disease makes the body’s blood distribution weird:

* too much blood sitting in the splanchnic/gut vessels
* not enough effective blood reaching the kidneys

So the kidneys “shut down” from low perfusion, even though the kidneys themselves are not the original problem.

---

### Classic setting

Think HRS (hepatorenal syndrome) in a patient with:

* advanced cirrhosis
* ascites (fluid in the abdomen)
* rising creatinine
* very low urine sodium
* bland urine sediment
* no improvement after volume expansion with albumin

Bland urine sediment means the urine does not show lots of casts/cells suggesting intrinsic kidney damage.

---

### Why albumin is used

Albumin (major blood plasma protein) helps pull fluid into the bloodstream and expand effective circulating volume.

If kidney function improves after albumin, it may have been simple prerenal azotemia (kidney underperfusion from low effective blood volume).

If it does not improve, HRS (hepatorenal syndrome) becomes more likely.

---

### Treatment idea

Treat by trying to reverse the circulatory problem:

* albumin to expand plasma volume
* vasoconstrictors to tighten the dilated splanchnic vessels
* liver transplant is definitive treatment

Common vasoconstrictor options include terlipressin, norepinephrine, or midodrine plus octreotide depending on setting.

---

## One-line intuition

Hepatorenal syndrome is kidney failure from severe liver disease causing splanchnic vasodilation, low effective arterial volume, and compensatory renal vasoconstriction.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HRS (hepatorenal syndrome) has low urine sodium because RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system) is strongly activated, so the kidneys avidly reabsorb sodium even while GFR (glomerular filtration rate) is falling.

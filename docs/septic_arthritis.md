# Septic arthritis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/09/Knee_diagram.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/28/SepticArth2011_%28cropped%29.jpg)

### Core idea

Septic arthritis = infection inside a joint space.

Usually bacteria enter the synovial fluid (lubricating fluid inside a joint), causing:

* acute joint pain
* swelling
* warmth
* fever
* decreased range of motion

Most common joint:

* knee

Most common organism overall:

* Staphylococcus aureus

---

### Why it is dangerous

The joint is basically a closed space.

So if bacteria get in, neutrophils (acute infection white blood cells) rush in and release inflammatory enzymes.

That causes:

* pus in the joint
* cartilage destruction
* permanent joint damage if not treated quickly

So think:

> infected joint fluid = cartilage gets eaten up fast.

---

### How it presents

Classic presentation:

* acute monoarthritis (one inflamed joint)
* severe pain with both active and passive movement
* swollen, hot joint
* fever sometimes

Important clue:

* pain with passive movement means the joint itself is inflamed, not just muscle/tendon around it.

---

### Diagnosis

Do arthrocentesis (needle aspiration of joint fluid).

Synovial fluid findings:

* high WBC count (white blood cell count)
* mostly PMNs (polymorphonuclear neutrophils)
* low glucose
* positive Gram stain/culture sometimes

Do not rely only on ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate) or CRP (C-reactive protein), because those just say "inflammation" and do not prove infection.

---

### Treatment

Treat with:

* urgent joint drainage
* IV antibiotics (intravenous antibiotics)

Reason:

* antibiotics kill bacteria
* drainage removes the pus/inflammatory load from the closed joint space

---

## One-line intuition

Septic arthritis is an abscess inside a joint, so you have to tap/drain it before it destroys the cartilage.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In a young sexually active patient with migratory polyarthralgia (joint pains that move around), tenosynovitis (inflamed tendon sheath), and dermatitis (skin lesions), think disseminated Neisseria gonorrhoeae infection causing septic arthritis.

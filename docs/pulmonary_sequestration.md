### Core idea

Pulmonary sequestration is a congenital (present from birth) chunk of abnormal lung tissue that:

* does not connect normally to the bronchial tree (airway tubes)
* does not participate in gas exchange
* gets blood from systemic circulation, usually the aorta

So basically:

> a useless piece of lung that is separated from the airway and fed by the wrong artery.

---

### Why the findings happen

Normal lung gets:

* air from bronchi (airway branches)
* blood from pulmonary arteries (arteries going from heart to lungs)

Sequestered lung gets:

* little/no normal airway connection
* arterial blood directly from the aorta

That abnormal aortic blood supply is the key.

Because it is not draining/ventilating normally, it can cause:

* recurrent pneumonia/infections
* cough
* hemoptysis (coughing blood)
* heart strain if the abnormal blood flow is large

---

### Types

**Intralobar sequestration**

* inside a normal lung lobe
* no separate pleura (lung covering)
* more common
* venous drainage usually to pulmonary veins
* often presents later with recurrent infections

**Extralobar sequestration**

* separate from the normal lung
* has its own pleura
* often found in infants
* venous drainage usually to systemic veins
* more associated with other congenital anomalies

---

## One-line intuition

Pulmonary sequestration = isolated, nonfunctional lung tissue with an abnormal feeding artery from the aorta.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 clue: if a congenital lung mass has a systemic feeding artery from the aorta, think pulmonary sequestration.

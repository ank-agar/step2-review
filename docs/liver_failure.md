### Core idea

Liver failure means the liver is no longer doing its main jobs well enough.

The liver normally:

* detoxifies blood
* makes bile (fluid that helps digest fat and excrete bilirubin)
* makes clotting factors (proteins that help blood clot)
* makes albumin (main blood protein that keeps fluid inside vessels)
* processes ammonia into urea (less toxic waste product excreted by kidneys)

So if the liver fails, all those jobs fail together.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Jaundice (yellow skin/eyes):** bilirubin (yellow heme breakdown product) builds up because the liver cannot process/excrete it
* **Bleeding/easy bruising:** decreased clotting factor production
* **Ascites (fluid in the abdomen):** low albumin plus portal hypertension (high pressure in the portal venous system) pushes fluid out
* **Hepatic encephalopathy (brain dysfunction from liver disease):** ammonia builds up and affects the brain
* **Asterixis (flapping tremor):** neurotoxicity from ammonia causes impaired posture control
* **Hypoglycemia (low blood glucose):** liver cannot do normal glycogenolysis/gluconeogenesis (making/releasing glucose)

---

### Acute vs chronic

**Acute liver failure** = rapid severe liver dysfunction, often with:

* jaundice
* coagulopathy (impaired clotting)
* encephalopathy

Classic cause to remember:

* acetaminophen toxicity

**Chronic liver failure** usually comes from cirrhosis (diffuse liver scarring with regenerative nodules), caused by things like:

* alcohol use
* chronic hepatitis B/C
* nonalcoholic fatty liver disease

---

## One-line intuition

Liver failure is when the body's chemical processing plant shuts down, so toxins, bilirubin, fluid problems, and bleeding problems all show up.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 pearl: the most important sign that liver failure is becoming severe is **hepatic encephalopathy**, because it means toxins like ammonia are reaching and affecting the brain.

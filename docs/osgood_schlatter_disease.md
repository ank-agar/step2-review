### Core idea

Osgood-Schlatter disease is traction apophysitis (inflammation of a growth plate attachment site) of the tibial tubercle (the bump on the upper shinbone just below the kneecap).

Basically:

* quadriceps muscle (front thigh muscle) pulls on the patella (kneecap)
* patella pulls on the patellar tendon (tendon from kneecap to shinbone)
* patellar tendon pulls on the tibial tubercle
* repeated pulling irritates the growth plate there

So the pain is right below the knee, at the tibial tubercle.

---

### Who gets it?

Classically:

* active adolescents
* during growth spurts
* sports with running/jumping

Examples:

* basketball
* soccer
* volleyball
* sprinting
* gymnastics

---

### Why kids get it

In adults, the tendon-bone connection is stronger because growth plates are closed.

But in adolescents, the tibial tubercle still has an apophysis (secondary growth center where a tendon attaches).

That growth area is relatively weak, so repeated quadriceps pulling causes:

* microtrauma (tiny repeated injury)
* inflammation
* pain
* swelling
* sometimes a prominent bony bump

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* anterior knee pain (front knee pain)
* tenderness over tibial tubercle
* worse with running, jumping, squatting, kneeling, or stairs
* improves with rest
* possible visible/palpable bump below kneecap

X-ray can show fragmentation or elevation of the tibial tubercle, but diagnosis is usually clinical.

---

## One-line intuition

Osgood-Schlatter disease is a growing athlete's patellar tendon repeatedly yanking on an immature tibial tubercle growth plate.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 association: Osgood-Schlatter disease = tibial tubercle pain; Sinding-Larsen-Johansson syndrome = inferior patellar pole pain. Both are traction apophysitis (tendon-pull inflammation at an immature bony attachment), but the pain location tells them apart.

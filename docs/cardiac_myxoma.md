## Cardiac myxoma

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18078.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/0699/3709226/11ef90d1f7bf/36FF3.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/0699/3709226/704d1e32b239/36FF4.jpg)

Cardiac myxoma is a benign primary heart tumor.

Primary = starts in the heart itself.

Benign = not cancerous/invasive, but still dangerous because it can block blood flow or embolize (break off and travel through blood).

Most common location:

* left atrium
* attached near the interatrial septum (wall between the atria), especially near the fossa ovalis (thin remnant of the fetal foramen ovale)

---

### Core idea

Think:

> a floppy tumor ball hanging inside the left atrium.

It can act like a "ball-valve" at the mitral valve.

Mitral valve = valve between the left atrium and left ventricle.

So when blood tries to go from left atrium to left ventricle, the tumor can partially block the opening.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Dyspnea (shortness of breath):** tumor blocks left atrium to left ventricle flow, so blood backs up into lungs
* **Syncope (fainting):** sudden obstruction can drop cardiac output (blood pumped by the heart)
* **Positional symptoms:** tumor moves with gravity/body position
* **Stroke or emboli:** pieces of tumor or clot can break off and travel to brain/systemic circulation
* **Fever, weight loss, malaise:** myxomas can release IL-6 (interleukin-6), an inflammatory cytokine (immune signaling molecule)

---

### Classic Step 1 association

Cardiac myxoma can be part of **Carney complex**.

Carney complex = syndrome with:

* cardiac myxomas
* skin pigmentation/lentigines (small brown-black spots)
* endocrine tumors (hormone-producing tumors)

---

## One-line intuition

Cardiac myxoma is a benign but dangerous left atrial tumor that causes problems by blocking the mitral valve or throwing emboli.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Most common primary cardiac tumor in adults = **cardiac myxoma**.

Most common cardiac tumor overall = **metastasis** (spread from another cancer).

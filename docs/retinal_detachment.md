## Retinal detachment

### Core idea

Retinal detachment = the retina (light-sensing layer in the back of the eye) separates from the underlying retinal pigment epithelium, aka RPE (supportive pigmented layer that feeds/maintains photoreceptors).

Think:

* retina = wallpaper
* RPE/choroid = wall + blood supply
* detachment = wallpaper peeling off the wall

---

### Why it causes vision loss

Photoreceptors (light-detecting retinal cells) depend on the RPE/choroid for nutrients and oxygen.

So when the retina lifts off:

* photoreceptors lose support
* vision in that area drops
* if the macula (central vision area) detaches, central vision can be permanently damaged

---

### Classic symptoms

Usually painless:

* flashes of light
* new floaters
* blurry vision
* “curtain” or shadow coming over vision

The “curtain” happens because part of the retina is no longer working, so that part of the visual field goes dark.

---

### Common mechanism

Most are rhegmatogenous retinal detachments.

Rhegmatogenous = caused by a retinal tear/hole.

Mechanism:

* vitreous humor (gel inside the eye) pulls on retina
* retina tears
* fluid slips through the tear
* fluid separates retina from RPE

---

## One-line intuition

The retina peels away from its blood/nutrient support layer, so the photoreceptors stop working.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Retinal detachment is an ophthalmologic emergency because detached photoreceptors can die, especially if the macula (central retina for sharp vision) becomes involved.

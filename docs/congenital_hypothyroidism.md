# Congenital hypothyroidism

Congenital hypothyroidism = low thyroid hormone present at birth.

Most commonly, the baby has a problem with thyroid gland development, like:

* thyroid agenesis (thyroid gland never forms)
* thyroid dysgenesis (thyroid gland forms abnormally)
* ectopic thyroid (thyroid gland is in the wrong place)

So the baby cannot make enough T4 (thyroxine) and T3 (triiodothyronine).

---

### Core idea

Thyroid hormone is needed for:

* brain development
* growth
* metabolism
* heat production

So if a newborn has low thyroid hormone, the biggest danger is impaired brain development.

That is why newborn screening matters so much.

---

### Classic findings

Congenital hypothyroidism can cause:

* prolonged jaundice
* poor feeding
* lethargy
* constipation
* hypotonia (low muscle tone)
* macroglossia (large tongue)
* umbilical hernia
* cool, mottled skin
* developmental delay if untreated

The reason these happen:

* low thyroid hormone slows metabolism
* slow gut movement causes constipation
* low metabolic activity makes the baby sleepy/cold
* impaired tissue development causes growth and neurodevelopment problems

---

### Labs

In primary congenital hypothyroidism:

* low T4 (thyroxine)
* high TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone)

Why high TSH?

The pituitary gland is basically yelling at the thyroid:

> “Make more thyroid hormone.”

But the thyroid gland cannot respond properly, so TSH stays high.

---

## One-line intuition

The baby is born without enough thyroid hormone, so metabolism slows and brain development is at risk.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Congenital hypothyroidism is screened for at birth because early levothyroxine (synthetic T4/thyroxine) prevents intellectual disability.

# Acanthosis nigricans

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/49/Glucoregulatory_hyperbola.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/29/Acanthosis_nigricans.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/61/AN_histo_faible_gr.jpg)

Acanthosis nigricans is dark, thick, velvety skin usually in body folds like the:

* neck
* axillae (armpits)
* groin

The key association is insulin resistance, meaning the body's tissues do not respond well to insulin.

---

### Why insulin resistance causes it

If cells resist insulin, the pancreas compensates by making more insulin.

So:

* insulin resistance
* high insulin levels
* insulin stimulates IGF-1 (insulin-like growth factor 1) receptors on skin cells
* keratinocytes (epidermal skin cells) and fibroblasts (connective tissue cells) proliferate
* skin becomes thick, dark, and velvety

That is why acanthosis nigricans is basically a skin clue for high insulin.

---

### What it points toward

Common associations:

* obesity
* type 2 diabetes mellitus
* PCOS (polycystic ovarian syndrome)
* metabolic syndrome

Rare but important:

* internal malignancy, especially gastric adenocarcinoma

Malignancy-associated acanthosis nigricans is more concerning when it is sudden onset, widespread, or involves mucosa (moist lining surfaces like the mouth).

---

## One-line intuition

Acanthosis nigricans is the skin getting overstimulated by high insulin, so it grows thicker and darker in folds.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1: acanthosis nigricans + obesity usually means insulin resistance, but sudden diffuse acanthosis nigricans in an older adult can be a paraneoplastic sign, classically from gastric adenocarcinoma.

### Core idea

Metabolic acidosis = blood is too acidic because the **metabolic component** is abnormal.

On labs:

* pH is low
* HCO3- (bicarbonate) is low

Think:

> the body has either gained acid or lost bicarbonate.

---

### Why bicarbonate is low

HCO3- (bicarbonate) is a base, so it buffers acid.

If extra acid appears in the body:

* H+ (hydrogen ion/acid) rises
* HCO3- gets used up trying to neutralize it
* blood pH falls

So low HCO3- is the key metabolic acidosis finding.

---

### Compensation

The lungs try to fix it by hyperventilating.

CO2 (carbon dioxide) acts like acid in the blood:

CO2 + H2O ⇌ H2CO3 ⇌ H+ + HCO3-

So if you blow off CO2:

* CO2 decreases
* acid decreases
* pH rises toward normal

That is why metabolic acidosis causes deep/rapid breathing, especially **Kussmaul respirations** (deep, labored breathing from severe metabolic acidosis).

---

### Big Step 1 split

Metabolic acidosis is divided by the anion gap.

Anion gap = unmeasured anions (negatively charged ions) in the blood.

Formula:

> Na+ - (Cl- + HCO3-)

Na+ = sodium  
Cl- = chloride  
HCO3- = bicarbonate

High anion gap metabolic acidosis means there are extra acids in the blood.

Classic causes:

* lactic acidosis
* ketoacidosis, like DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis)
* renal failure/uremia
* toxins, like methanol, ethylene glycol, salicylates

Normal anion gap metabolic acidosis usually means bicarbonate was lost and chloride increased to replace it.

Classic causes:

* diarrhea
* renal tubular acidosis

---

## One-line intuition

Metabolic acidosis = too much acid or too little bicarbonate, so the lungs compensate by breathing off CO2.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Diarrhea causes **normal anion gap metabolic acidosis** because you lose HCO3- (bicarbonate) in stool, and Cl- (chloride) rises to keep charge balanced.

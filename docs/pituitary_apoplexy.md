## Pituitary apoplexy

### Core idea

Pituitary apoplexy is sudden bleeding or infarction (tissue death from loss of blood flow) inside the pituitary gland, usually inside a pituitary adenoma (benign pituitary tumor).

Think:

> pituitary tumor suddenly bleeds/swells in a tight skull space

That sudden swelling compresses nearby structures.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Sudden severe headache:** acute bleeding/swelling stretches the sellar region (pituitary area in the skull)
* **Bitemporal hemianopia:** loss of outer visual fields in both eyes because the enlarged pituitary compresses the optic chiasm (where optic nerves cross)
* **Ophthalmoplegia:** weak eye movements because cranial nerves III, IV, and VI (eye movement nerves) run nearby in the cavernous sinus
* **Adrenal crisis:** low ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone) causes low cortisol, which can cause hypotension (low blood pressure), shock, nausea, and confusion

---

### Why bitemporal hemianopia?

The pituitary sits right under the optic chiasm.

The optic chiasm carries crossing nasal retinal fibers.

Those fibers see the temporal visual fields.

So compression from below causes:

* right outer vision loss
* left outer vision loss

= **bitemporal hemianopia**.

---

### Classic presentation

Sudden:

* worst headache
* visual loss
* double vision
* nausea/vomiting
* altered mental status
* hypotension from acute adrenal insufficiency (not enough cortisol)

---

## One-line intuition

Pituitary apoplexy = a pituitary adenoma suddenly bleeds or infarcts, rapidly swelling and crushing the optic chiasm + pituitary hormone output.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 move: if a patient has sudden headache + bitemporal hemianopia + signs of adrenal insufficiency, think **pituitary apoplexy** and give urgent glucocorticoids (cortisol-like steroids).

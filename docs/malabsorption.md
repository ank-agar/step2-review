### Core idea

Malabsorption = the intestine cannot absorb nutrients properly.

So food gets digested, but nutrients do not make it across the intestinal wall into:

* blood
* lymph (fluid pathway that carries fats)

---

### Why symptoms happen

Most absorption happens in the small intestine because it has:

* villi (finger-like folds)
* microvilli (tiny surface projections on intestinal cells)

These increase surface area.

So if villi are damaged or digestion is incomplete:

* less surface area = less absorption
* nutrients stay in the gut lumen (inside space of the intestine)
* water follows osmotically = diarrhea
* calories are lost = weight loss
* fat is lost in stool = steatorrhea (fatty, bulky, foul-smelling stool)

---

### Big causes to know

Malabsorption can happen from:

* celiac disease: autoimmune gluten reaction that causes villous atrophy (flattening of villi)
* pancreatic insufficiency: lack of digestive enzymes, especially lipase (fat-digesting enzyme)
* bile acid problems: poor micelle formation, so fats cannot be absorbed well
* Crohn disease: inflammation damages absorptive bowel
* small intestinal bacterial overgrowth: bacteria consume nutrients and deconjugate bile acids

---

### Fat-soluble vitamin problems

Fat malabsorption causes low:

* vitamin A
* vitamin D
* vitamin E
* vitamin K

Think:

* low vitamin D = bone pain/osteomalacia (soft bones)
* low vitamin K = bleeding tendency

---

## One-line intuition

Malabsorption is when the gut surface or digestive machinery is broken, so nutrients stay in the stool instead of entering the body.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Step 1 loves celiac disease: gluten triggers immune injury in the small intestine, causing villous atrophy, which decreases absorptive surface area and leads to diarrhea, weight loss, iron deficiency anemia, and steatorrhea.

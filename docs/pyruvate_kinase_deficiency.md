### Core idea

Pyruvate kinase deficiency is an autosomal recessive (needs 2 bad copies) red blood cell enzyme defect that causes chronic hemolytic anemia (red blood cells break down too early).

Pyruvate kinase normally does the last ATP-making step of glycolysis:

* PEP (phosphoenolpyruvate) -> pyruvate
* ADP (adenosine diphosphate) -> ATP (adenosine triphosphate)

So if pyruvate kinase is low:

* less ATP is made
* RBCs (red blood cells) cannot maintain their membrane well
* RBCs become rigid/deformed
* spleen destroys them

---

### Why RBCs are hit so hard

RBCs do not have mitochondria (ATP-producing organelles).

So they rely almost entirely on glycolysis (glucose breakdown pathway) for ATP.

That means:

* pyruvate kinase broken -> low ATP
* low ATP -> fragile RBC membrane
* fragile RBC membrane -> extravascular hemolysis (RBC destruction by macrophages, mainly in spleen)

---

### Findings

Common findings:

* anemia
* jaundice from indirect bilirubin (unconjugated bilirubin from RBC breakdown)
* splenomegaly (big spleen)
* pigment gallstones from chronic hemolysis
* increased reticulocytes (young RBCs)

Blood smear may show:

* echinocytes/burr cells (spiky RBCs)
* pyknocytes (small dense damaged RBCs)

---

### Key lab pattern

Hemolysis labs:

* increased LDH (lactate dehydrogenase, released from damaged cells)
* increased indirect bilirubin
* decreased haptoglobin (protein that binds free hemoglobin)
* increased reticulocyte count

---

## One-line intuition

RBCs are glycolysis-only cells, so if pyruvate kinase cannot make enough ATP, the RBC membrane fails and the spleen removes the damaged cells.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Pyruvate kinase deficiency is the most common glycolytic enzyme defect causing chronic nonspherocytic hemolytic anemia (hemolytic anemia without classic spherocytes).

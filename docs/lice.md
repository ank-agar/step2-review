Lice are tiny ectoparasites (parasites that live on the outside of the body) that feed on human blood.

The main human lice are:

* head lice: live on scalp hair
* body lice: live in clothing seams and feed on skin
* pubic lice: live in pubic hair/coarse body hair

---

### Core idea

Lice are not a hygiene problem.

They spread mostly by close contact, then cling to hair or clothing using claw-like legs.

They cause itching because their saliva triggers a hypersensitivity reaction (immune reaction to something the body sees as foreign).

---

### Life cycle

Lice go:

* egg/nit
* nymph
* adult

Nits are eggs cemented to hair shafts.

That is why nits do not brush off easily like dandruff.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* itching: allergic reaction to louse saliva
* excoriations: scratch marks from itching
* secondary infection: bacteria can enter through scratched skin
* visible nits: eggs are glued tightly to hair near the scalp

---

### Step 1 distinction

Head lice usually cause itching but do not classically transmit major systemic disease.

Body lice are the dangerous one for disease transmission.

Body lice can transmit:

* Rickettsia prowazekii: epidemic typhus
* Bartonella quintana: trench fever
* Borrelia recurrentis: relapsing fever

---

## One-line intuition

Lice are blood-sucking insects that grab onto hair/clothing, glue their eggs in place, and make you itch because your immune system reacts to their saliva.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Body lice are associated with poor living conditions/crowding and are the lice that transmit epidemic typhus.

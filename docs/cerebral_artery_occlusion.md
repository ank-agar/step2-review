## Cerebral artery occlusion

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4d/Right_dense_MCA_sign_as_seen_on_CT_brain.jpg)

Cerebral artery occlusion = blockage of an artery supplying the brain.

Break it down:

* cerebral = brain
* artery = vessel carrying oxygenated blood
* occlusion = blockage

So the core idea is:

> a blocked brain artery causes ischemic stroke because brain tissue downstream loses blood flow.

---

### What usually blocks it?

Most commonly:

* thrombus = clot formed locally
* embolus = clot/debris that travels from elsewhere

Common sources:

* atrial fibrillation = irregular atrial rhythm that causes blood stasis and clot formation
* carotid atherosclerosis = plaque in the carotid artery that can send emboli upward
* hypercoagulable state = tendency to form clots

---

### Why symptoms happen

The blocked artery supplies a specific brain territory.

When blood stops:

* neurons lose oxygen/glucose
* ATP (adenosine triphosphate) falls
* ion pumps fail
* cells swell and die

That is why symptoms are sudden and focal, like:

* one-sided weakness
* one-sided numbness
* facial droop
* aphasia (language problem)
* vision loss

---

### Big Step 1 pattern

MCA (middle cerebral artery) occlusion is the classic/high-yield one.

MCA supplies lateral brain cortex, including motor/sensory areas for face and arm.

So MCA stroke often causes:

* contralateral face/arm weakness
* contralateral face/arm sensory loss
* aphasia if dominant hemisphere is affected
* neglect if nondominant hemisphere is affected

Contralateral = opposite side of the body.

---

## One-line intuition

A cerebral artery occlusion is like a traffic jam in a brain artery: everything downstream gets starved, so the body functions controlled by that brain area suddenly stop working.

---

## Pearl

The MCA (middle cerebral artery) is the most common site of ischemic stroke, and a dense MCA sign on CT (computed tomography) suggests an acute clot sitting inside the MCA.

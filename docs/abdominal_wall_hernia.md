### Core idea

An abdominal wall hernia is when abdominal contents push through a weak spot or defect in the abdominal wall.

Usually the thing pushing out is:

* fat
* peritoneum (thin lining of the abdominal cavity)
* bowel

Think:

> inside pressure pushes stuff through a weak wall.

---

### Why the bulge happens

The abdominal wall normally holds organs in.

If there is a weak point, increased intra-abdominal pressure (pressure inside the belly) can force tissue outward.

Common pressure triggers:

* coughing
* heavy lifting
* pregnancy
* obesity
* constipation/straining
* ascites (fluid buildup in the abdomen)

So the bulge may get worse when standing, coughing, or straining.

---

### Common types

* Inguinal hernia: in the groin
* Femoral hernia: below the inguinal ligament (band running from hip to pubic bone)
* Umbilical hernia: at the belly button
* Epigastric hernia: upper midline abdomen
* Incisional hernia: through a prior surgical incision

---

### Key complications

* Reducible: can be pushed back in
* Incarcerated: trapped and cannot be pushed back in
* Strangulated: blood supply is cut off

Strangulation is the scary one because ischemia (low blood flow causing tissue injury) can cause bowel necrosis (tissue death).

Red flags:

* severe pain
* tender firm bulge
* skin color change
* vomiting
* fever
* bowel obstruction symptoms

---

## One-line intuition

An abdominal wall hernia is basically a weak spot in the belly wall letting fat or bowel poke outward.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Indirect inguinal hernia goes lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels; direct inguinal hernia goes medial to them.

Mnemonic:

* MDs do not LIe
* Medial = Direct
* Lateral = Indirect

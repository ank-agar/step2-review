Lightning injury = tissue injury from a huge, very brief electrical discharge.

Key idea:

* lightning has extremely high voltage
* exposure time is very short
* current often travels over the body surface, called flashover
* but it can still disrupt the heart, brain, skin, ears, and muscles

---

### Main ways it injures you

Lightning can hit by:

* **direct strike:** lightning directly hits the person
* **side flash:** lightning hits a nearby object, then jumps to the person
* **contact injury:** person is touching something lightning travels through
* **ground current:** lightning spreads through the ground and enters one foot, exits the other
* **blunt trauma:** shock wave throws the person or causes falling objects

Ground current and side flash are more common than a direct strike.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Cardiac arrest:** electricity can depolarize the heart all at once, causing arrhythmia (abnormal heart rhythm) or asystole (no heartbeat)
* **Respiratory arrest:** brainstem respiratory centers can be stunned, so breathing stops even if the heart can restart
* **Burns:** current and heat damage skin, especially at entry/exit points or near metal objects
* **Lichtenberg figures:** fern-like red skin markings from superficial electrical discharge; these are not true deep burns and usually fade
* **Rhabdomyolysis:** muscle breakdown can release myoglobin (muscle oxygen-binding protein), which can injure kidneys
* **Cataracts/hearing loss:** eye lens and inner ear structures are sensitive to electrical/pressure injury

---

### One-line intuition

Lightning is like a massive electrical reset button: it can stun the heart and brain instantly, while leaving surprisingly little external damage.

---

### Testable management idea

In a lightning strike with multiple victims, use **reverse triage**:

* prioritize patients who look dead or apneic (not breathing)
* because respiratory arrest may be reversible if ventilated quickly

This is different from usual trauma triage, where pulseless/apneic patients are often lower priority.

---

## HY pearl

Lichtenberg figures are pathognomonic (highly characteristic) for lightning injury, but they are usually transient and fade within about 24 hours.

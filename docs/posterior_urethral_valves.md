### Core idea

Posterior urethral valves are abnormal congenital flaps of tissue in the posterior urethra (the part of the urethra near the bladder/prostate).

They act like a partial blockage, so urine has trouble leaving the bladder.

This happens in males.

---

### Why the findings happen

Think:

> valve blocks urine outflow → pressure backs up → bladder/ureters/kidneys dilate

So you get:

* weak urinary stream or inability to urinate: urine cannot exit normally
* distended bladder: urine piles up behind the obstruction
* hydronephrosis (dilation of the kidney collecting system): pressure backs up to the kidneys
* hydroureter (dilated ureter): pressure backs up through the ureters
* renal dysplasia (abnormal kidney development): chronic fetal obstruction damages kidney formation
* oligohydramnios (low amniotic fluid): fetus makes less urine into the amniotic fluid

---

### Classic imaging clue

On VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram; x-ray study of the bladder/urethra while urinating), you can see:

* dilated posterior urethra
* thick/trabeculated bladder wall
* possible vesicoureteral reflux (backflow of urine from bladder to ureters/kidneys)

Prenatal ultrasound can show the **keyhole sign**:

* enlarged bladder + dilated posterior urethra

It looks like a keyhole because the bladder is the big round part and the dilated urethra is the narrow part.

---

## One-line intuition

Posterior urethral valves are like a congenital one-way traffic jam at the bladder outlet, causing urine pressure to back up into the bladder, ureters, and kidneys.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Posterior urethral valves are the most common cause of lower urinary tract obstruction in male infants and can cause bilateral hydronephrosis plus oligohydramnios.

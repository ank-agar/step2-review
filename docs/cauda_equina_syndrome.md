### Core idea

Cauda equina syndrome = compression of the cauda equina (bundle of lumbosacral nerve roots below the end of the spinal cord).

Cauda equina means "horse tail" because those nerve roots look like a tail hanging down.

It is a neurosurgical emergency because those nerves control:

* legs
* bladder
* bowel
* sexual function
* sensation around the perineum (area between the genitals and anus)

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* severe low back pain
* bilateral sciatica (shooting pain down the legs)
* saddle anesthesia (numbness in the groin, inner thighs, buttocks, and perianal area)
* urinary retention or incontinence
* fecal incontinence
* decreased anal sphincter tone
* lower extremity weakness or sensory loss

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Saddle anesthesia:** sacral nerve roots S2-S5 carry sensation from the perineum
* **Bladder dysfunction:** sacral parasympathetic nerves S2-S4 help contract the detrusor muscle (bladder wall muscle), so damage causes urinary retention
* **Bowel dysfunction:** sacral roots help control anal sphincters
* **Leg pain/weakness:** lumbar and sacral motor/sensory roots are compressed
* **Lower motor neuron signs:** this is nerve root damage, so reflexes can be decreased rather than increased

---

### Common causes

Most common Step 1 cause:

* massive lumbar disc herniation

Other causes:

* spinal tumor
* epidural abscess (infection in the epidural space, outside the dura mater)
* epidural hematoma (blood collection in the epidural space)
* trauma
* severe lumbar spinal stenosis (narrowing of the spinal canal)

---

### One-line intuition

The spinal cord has already ended, but the dangling nerve roots below it get squeezed, causing leg symptoms plus bladder/bowel/saddle-area problems.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Cauda equina syndrome causes lower motor neuron findings because it injures nerve roots, not the spinal cord itself.

Best test is MRI (magnetic resonance imaging), and treatment is urgent surgical decompression if compression is present.

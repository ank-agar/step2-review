Appendicitis in pregnancy = inflammation of the appendix during pregnancy.

The big idea:

* it is the most common non-obstetric surgical emergency in pregnancy
* non-obstetric means not directly caused by the uterus, placenta, or fetus
* diagnosis can be harder because pregnancy already causes nausea, vomiting, abdominal discomfort, and increased WBC count

WBC count = white blood cell count.

---

### Why it presents differently

Normally appendicitis causes:

* periumbilical pain first
* then pain moves to the RLQ

RLQ = right lower quadrant.

In pregnancy, the enlarging uterus can push abdominal organs upward and sideways.

So pain may be:

* RLQ early in pregnancy
* right flank or RUQ later in pregnancy

RUQ = right upper quadrant.

That is why appendicitis in pregnancy can look less “classic.”

---

### Why it is dangerous

The danger is delay.

Delay can lead to:

* perforation
* peritonitis
* preterm labor
* fetal loss

Perforation = the appendix ruptures.

Peritonitis = inflammation/infection of the peritoneum, which is the lining of the abdominal cavity.

So the reasoning is:

> pregnancy makes symptoms harder to interpret → diagnosis may be delayed → appendix has more time to rupture → maternal and fetal risk goes up.

---

### Diagnosis

Usually start with ultrasound.

Ultrasound = imaging with sound waves, no radiation.

If ultrasound is unclear, MRI is commonly used.

MRI = magnetic resonance imaging.

CT is usually avoided if possible because CT uses ionizing radiation, but it can still be used if needed.

CT = computed tomography.

---

### Treatment

Treatment is appendectomy.

Appendectomy = surgical removal of the appendix.

Key point:

* do not delay surgery just because the patient is pregnant
* laparoscopic appendectomy is often used

Laparoscopic = minimally invasive surgery using small incisions and a camera.

---

## One-line intuition

Appendicitis in pregnancy is the same inflamed appendix, but pregnancy makes the pain location and labs less reliable, so you have to diagnose and treat early before rupture.

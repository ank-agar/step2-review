### Core idea

Urinary bladder cancer = malignant growth of cells in the bladder, usually from the **urothelium** (transitional epithelium lining the urinary tract).

Most common type:

* **urothelial carcinoma** = cancer of the urothelial/transitional cells

Old name you may see:

* **transitional cell carcinoma** = older name for urothelial carcinoma

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

* painless hematuria

**Hematuria** = blood in urine.

So the classic patient is:

* older adult
* smoker
* painless blood in urine

---

### Why smoking causes it

Carcinogens (cancer-causing chemicals) from tobacco enter the blood, get filtered by the kidneys, and sit in urine inside the bladder.

So the bladder lining gets repeated chemical exposure.

That is why smoking strongly increases bladder cancer risk.

---

### Other risk factors

High-yield risks:

* smoking
* aromatic amines, like aniline dyes
* cyclophosphamide
* chronic bladder irritation
* Schistosoma haematobium infection

**Aromatic amines** = chemical compounds used in some dyes/rubber/industrial exposures.

**Cyclophosphamide** = chemotherapy/immunosuppressive drug that can irritate the bladder.

**Schistosoma haematobium** = parasite associated with squamous cell carcinoma of the bladder.

---

### Important pathology split

Most bladder cancers are:

* urothelial carcinoma

But chronic irritation can push the bladder lining toward **squamous metaplasia**.

**Squamous metaplasia** = one mature cell type changes into squamous-type cells because of chronic irritation.

That increases risk of:

* squamous cell carcinoma

---

### Why invasion matters

The key staging idea is whether cancer has invaded the **muscularis propria**.

**Muscularis propria** = the thick detrusor muscle layer of the bladder wall.

If it stays superficial:

* easier to remove locally
* often treated with transurethral resection

If it invades muscle:

* more aggressive
* higher risk of spread

**Transurethral resection** = removing tumor through the urethra using a scope.

---

## One-line intuition

Bladder cancer is usually urothelial cancer from carcinogens sitting in stored urine, classically causing painless hematuria.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Schistosoma haematobium is classically linked to **squamous cell carcinoma** of the bladder, while smoking is classically linked to **urothelial carcinoma**.

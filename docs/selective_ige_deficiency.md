## Selective IgE deficiency

![Image](https://mastcellaware.com/images/mastcell-img-labels-2x-2.jpg)

![Image](https://mastcellaware.com/images/mastcells-lg-1.jpg)

Selective IgE deficiency means very low or absent IgE (immunoglobulin E) with the other major antibody classes usually normal:

* IgG (immunoglobulin G)
* IgA (immunoglobulin A)
* IgM (immunoglobulin M)

So "selective" means:

> only IgE is low, not all antibodies.

---

### Core idea

IgE is the antibody class most associated with:

* type I hypersensitivity (immediate allergy)
* mast cell activation
* basophil activation
* defense against helminths (parasitic worms)

Normally:

allergen binds IgE on mast cells -> mast cell degranulation -> histamine release -> allergy symptoms.

If IgE is very low, that IgE-mast-cell pathway is weaker.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Fewer classic IgE-mediated allergies:** less IgE is available to coat mast cells
* **Possible recurrent respiratory infections:** IgE is part of mucosal/immune defense, though this is much less classic than IgA (immunoglobulin A) deficiency
* **Possible autoimmune association:** very low IgE can be a marker of broader immune dysregulation
* **Normal IgG/IgA/IgM:** explains why this is not a global B-cell failure

Important: this is way less USMLE-classic than selective IgA deficiency.

---

## One-line intuition

Selective IgE deficiency = the "allergy/parasite antibody" is missing, while the main everyday antibody classes are still present.

---

## Pearl

HY comparison: selective IgA deficiency is the big Step 1 antibody deficiency to know: low IgA with normal IgG and IgM, causing recurrent mucosal infections, Giardia, autoimmune disease, and possible anaphylaxis to blood products.

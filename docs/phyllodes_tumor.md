# Phyllodes tumor

Phyllodes tumor is a breast tumor made of:

* epithelial tissue (gland/duct lining tissue)
* stromal tissue (connective tissue support around glands)

So it is a **fibroepithelial tumor** (tumor with both fibrous stromal + epithelial parts).

---

### Core idea

Think:

> fibroadenoma-like breast mass, but the stroma grows too much and pushes into spaces like leaves.

"Phyllodes" means leaf-like.

The classic histology is:

* leaf-like projections
* cleft-like spaces
* increased stromal cellularity

---

### Why it looks leaf-like

The stromal cells proliferate and bulge into duct-like spaces.

That creates folded projections that look like leaves under the microscope.

So the key abnormal part is mainly the **stroma** (connective tissue), not the duct epithelium.

---

### Benign vs malignant

Phyllodes tumors can be:

* benign (not invading/spreading)
* borderline (intermediate behavior)
* malignant (cancerous, can metastasize)

Malignant ones behave more like a **sarcoma** (cancer of mesenchymal/connective tissue), so they tend to spread hematogenously (through blood) rather than lymphatically (through lymph nodes).

---

### Treatment idea

Treatment is wide local excision, meaning remove the tumor with a rim of normal tissue.

Why?

Because if you just shell it out, leftover stromal tumor cells can cause local recurrence.

---

## One-line intuition

A phyllodes tumor is a fibroadenoma-like breast mass where overgrown stroma makes leaf-like fronds and can sometimes act like a sarcoma.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 distinction: fibroadenoma is common, benign, and hormonally responsive; phyllodes tumor is rarer, often larger/rapid-growing, has leaf-like architecture, and malignant cases spread hematogenously.

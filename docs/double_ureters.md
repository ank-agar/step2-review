# Double ureters

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Duplicated_ureter-hi.svg/500px-Duplicated_ureter-hi.svg.png)

![Image](https://images2.urology-textbook.com/ureter_duplex_skizze.jpg)

![Image](https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/images/4/49/Duplicated_ureter_.jpg)

![Image](https://images2.urology-textbook.com/Ureter_duplex_Urogramm1.jpg)

Double ureters means one kidney has **2 ureters** instead of 1.

A ureter is the tube that carries urine from the kidney to the bladder.

This is also called:

* duplicated ureter
* duplex collecting system
* duplex kidney

---

### Core idea

Normally:

* 1 kidney drains through 1 ureter into the bladder

In double ureters:

* 1 kidney has 2 drainage tubes
* each tube may drain a different part of the kidney

Think:

> one kidney, two pipes.

---

### Types

**Incomplete duplication:**

* 2 ureters start from the kidney
* then join together before entering the bladder
* forms a Y-shape

**Complete duplication:**

* 2 ureters stay separate
* both enter the bladder separately

---

### Why it happens

During development, the ureter comes from the **ureteric bud**.

If the ureteric bud splits too early, or if 2 ureteric buds form, you can get duplicated ureters.

So this is a congenital anomaly, meaning present from birth.

---

### Why it matters clinically

Many people have no symptoms.

But abnormal drainage can cause:

* recurrent UTIs (urinary tract infections)
* hydronephrosis (swelling of the kidney from backed-up urine)
* vesicoureteral reflux (urine flowing backward from bladder toward kidney)
* ureterocele (ballooning of the lower end of the ureter)
* ectopic ureter (ureter drains into the wrong place)

---

### One-line intuition

Double ureters are a plumbing duplication problem: usually benign, but if the extra tube drains badly, urine backs up or refluxes and causes infections.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In complete ureteral duplication, remember the **Weigert-Meyer rule**:

* upper pole ureter tends to insert lower/medial and can obstruct
* lower pole ureter tends to insert higher/lateral and can reflux

So upper pole = obstruction/ureterocele, lower pole = reflux.

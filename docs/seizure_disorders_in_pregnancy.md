### Core idea

Seizures in pregnancy are scary because you have to think about **both mom and fetus**.

Big causes:

* **Epilepsy** = recurrent unprovoked seizures from abnormal brain electrical activity
* **Eclampsia** = seizure in pregnancy due to preeclampsia, usually after 20 weeks gestation
* metabolic/toxic causes, like hypoglycemia (low blood glucose), hyponatremia (low sodium), drugs, infection, or stroke

---

### Most important test-taking distinction

If a pregnant patient has a new seizure **after 20 weeks**, think:

> eclampsia until proven otherwise.

Why?

Because preeclampsia causes endothelial dysfunction (blood vessel lining damage), vasospasm (blood vessel narrowing), and brain edema (swelling), which irritates the cortex and triggers seizures.

Classic clues:

* hypertension (high blood pressure)
* proteinuria (protein in urine)
* headache
* visual symptoms
* RUQ (right upper quadrant) pain from liver involvement
* seizure = eclampsia

---

### Treatment intuition

For **eclampsia**, give:

* magnesium sulfate

Magnesium sulfate prevents/treats seizures by calming neuromuscular excitability.

Important: magnesium sulfate is for eclampsia seizures; it is not the usual chronic epilepsy drug.

---

### Epilepsy during pregnancy

If the patient already has epilepsy, the goal is:

> prevent maternal seizures while minimizing fetal medication risk.

Why not just stop ASMs (antiseizure medications)?

Because uncontrolled tonic-clonic seizures can cause:

* maternal trauma
* fetal hypoxia (low oxygen)
* fetal bradycardia (slow heart rate)
* miscarriage or preterm labor

So the risk is a balance:

* too much medication risk = teratogenicity (birth defect risk)
* too little seizure control = seizures can harm mom and fetus

---

### Medication pearl

Avoid **valproate** if possible in pregnancy because it has high teratogenic risk, especially:

* neural tube defects
* cognitive/developmental problems

Safer commonly used options include lamotrigine and levetiracetam, but levels may fall during pregnancy because plasma volume and drug clearance increase.

So pregnancy can make seizure meds “weaker” by diluting/clearing them faster.

---

## One-line intuition

Pregnancy seizures are either **a known epilepsy problem needing safe seizure control** or **a new dangerous OB (obstetric) emergency like eclampsia**, especially after 20 weeks.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1: **seizure + pregnancy after 20 weeks = magnesium sulfate**, not phenytoin, because the key diagnosis is eclampsia.

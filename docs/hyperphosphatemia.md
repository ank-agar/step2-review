### Core idea

Hyperphosphatemia = high phosphate in the blood.

Phosphate is normally controlled mainly by the kidney.

So the biggest cause is:

* chronic kidney disease

because damaged kidneys cannot excrete phosphate well.

---

### Why it matters

High phosphate binds calcium.

So:

* phosphate goes up
* free calcium goes down
* low calcium stimulates PTH (parathyroid hormone)

PTH (parathyroid hormone) then tries to raise calcium by pulling calcium from bone.

That causes:

* secondary hyperparathyroidism
* renal osteodystrophy (bone disease from chronic kidney disease)
* metastatic calcification (calcium-phosphate deposition in normal tissues)

---

### Main causes

Think of phosphate as either:

* not being excreted
* being released from cells
* being taken in too much

Common causes:

* chronic kidney disease
* tumor lysis syndrome (rapid cancer cell breakdown after treatment)
* rhabdomyolysis (skeletal muscle breakdown)
* hypoparathyroidism (low PTH)
* pseudohypoparathyroidism (body is resistant to PTH)
* phosphate-containing laxatives/enemas

---

### Why hypoparathyroidism causes high phosphate

PTH normally tells the kidney:

* keep calcium
* dump phosphate

So low PTH means:

* less phosphate excretion in urine
* phosphate stays in blood

That gives:

* low calcium
* high phosphate

---

### CKD pattern

In CKD (chronic kidney disease):

* kidney cannot dump phosphate
* kidney cannot activate vitamin D well
* less active vitamin D means less calcium absorption from gut
* calcium falls
* PTH rises secondarily

So classic CKD mineral pattern:

* high phosphate
* low calcium
* high PTH
* low active vitamin D

---

## One-line intuition

Hyperphosphatemia is usually a kidney problem: phosphate cannot leave the body, so it binds calcium and drives secondary PTH release.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, high phosphate + low calcium points toward low PTH effect, like hypoparathyroidism or pseudohypoparathyroidism; high phosphate + CKD points toward secondary hyperparathyroidism.

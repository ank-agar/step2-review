# Renal artery stenosis

### Core idea

Renal artery stenosis means narrowing of the renal artery.

* renal = kidney
* artery = blood vessel carrying blood away from the heart
* stenosis = narrowing

So the kidney gets less blood flow.

The kidney interprets this as:

> “Blood pressure is too low.”

even if the rest of the body’s blood pressure is actually high.

---

### Why hypertension happens

Low blood flow to the kidney activates the RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system).

RAAS causes:

* renin release from juxtaglomerular cells (blood pressure-sensing kidney cells)
* angiotensin II formation
* aldosterone release

Angiotensin II:

* constricts blood vessels
* preferentially constricts the efferent arteriole (the vessel leaving the glomerulus)

Aldosterone:

* increases sodium and water retention
* increases potassium loss

So renal artery stenosis causes:

* hypertension
* low potassium sometimes
* kidney ischemia (low oxygen/blood flow to kidney tissue)

---

### Causes

Two big causes:

* atherosclerosis = plaque buildup, usually older patients
* fibromuscular dysplasia = abnormal artery wall growth, classically younger women

Fibromuscular dysplasia can give a “string of beads” appearance on angiography (blood vessel imaging).

---

### ACE inhibitor clue

ACE inhibitors (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors) block angiotensin II.

That dilates the efferent arteriole.

Usually this is helpful.

But in bilateral renal artery stenosis (narrowing of both renal arteries), the kidneys depend on angiotensin II to keep glomerular pressure up.

So if you give an ACE inhibitor:

* efferent arteriole dilates
* glomerular filtration pressure drops
* creatinine rises

That is why a big creatinine increase after starting an ACE inhibitor suggests bilateral renal artery stenosis.

---

## One-line intuition

The kidney is under-perfused, so it panics and turns on RAAS, raising blood pressure for the whole body.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Renal artery stenosis can cause secondary hypertension with an abdominal bruit (whooshing vascular sound), and ACE inhibitors can trigger acute kidney injury in bilateral disease.

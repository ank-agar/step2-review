## Premature ventricular contractions

### Core idea

Premature ventricular contractions, or **PVCs** (**premature ventricular contractions**), are early extra beats that start in the ventricles (**the lower chambers of the heart**) instead of starting normally from the SA node (**sinoatrial node, the heart's normal pacemaker**).

So the ventricle fires too early.

---

### What it looks like on ECG

ECG (**electrocardiogram**) clues:

* early beat
* wide QRS complex (**ventricular depolarization wave**)
* no normal P wave (**atrial depolarization wave**) before it
* usually followed by a compensatory pause (**a longer pause after the early beat**)

---

### Why the QRS is wide

Normally, electricity travels fast through the His-Purkinje system (**specialized ventricular conduction fibers**).

In a PVC, the impulse starts in ventricular muscle instead.

That means it spreads cell-to-cell through myocardium (**heart muscle**), which is slower.

Slower ventricular activation = wider QRS.

---

### Why there is a pause after it

The PVC comes early, so the next normal SA node impulse may arrive while the ventricles are still refractory (**temporarily unable to respond**).

So that normal beat gets "skipped," creating a pause.

---

### Common causes/triggers

PVCs can happen in normal people, but triggers include:

* caffeine
* stress/anxiety
* low potassium or magnesium
* myocardial ischemia (**low blood flow to heart muscle**)
* structural heart disease

---

## One-line intuition

A PVC is a ventricle jumping the line and firing early, so the beat is wide, weird-looking, and followed by a pause.

---

## Pearl 🔥

PVCs after MI (**myocardial infarction, heart attack**) matter because irritable ventricular tissue can trigger dangerous ventricular arrhythmias (**abnormal rhythms from the ventricles**), especially ventricular tachycardia (**fast ventricular rhythm**).

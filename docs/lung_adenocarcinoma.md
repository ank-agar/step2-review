### Core idea

Lung adenocarcinoma is a type of lung cancer that comes from gland-forming epithelial cells (surface-lining cells that can make secretions like mucus).

Break the word apart:

* adeno- = gland
* carcinoma = cancer from epithelial cells

So:

> gland-forming cancer of the lung epithelium.

---

### Where it usually occurs

Usually in the peripheral lung, meaning farther out near the smaller airways and alveoli (tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens).

That is different from squamous cell carcinoma, which is more classically central near the bronchi (large airways).

---

### Who gets it

Classic associations:

* most common lung cancer overall
* most common lung cancer in nonsmokers
* more common in women compared with other lung cancers
* still can happen in smokers

Important:

Smoking increases risk for basically all lung cancers, but adenocarcinoma is the one Step 1 loves for nonsmoker/peripheral lung cancer.

---

### Why the findings happen

Because the tumor forms glands and/or produces mucin (mucus-like material):

* pathology may show gland formation
* mucin may be present
* tumor often grows near alveoli, so it can look like a peripheral lung nodule

Some tumors spread along pre-existing alveolar walls. That pattern is called lepidic growth (tumor cells crawling along alveolar walls instead of immediately destroying them).

---

### Important mutations

Common driver mutations include:

* EGFR (epidermal growth factor receptor)
* ALK (anaplastic lymphoma kinase)
* KRAS (Kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene homolog)

Intuition:

These mutations act like stuck-on growth signals, so the lung epithelial cells keep dividing when they should not.

---

## One-line intuition

Lung adenocarcinoma is the peripheral, gland-forming lung cancer, classically seen in nonsmokers and often driven by growth-signal mutations.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 clue:

* peripheral lung mass + nonsmoker = think lung adenocarcinoma
* central hilar mass + keratin pearls = think squamous cell carcinoma
* central mass + neuroendocrine markers/chromogranin/synaptophysin = think small cell carcinoma

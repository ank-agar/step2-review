### Core idea

Perimenopause = the transition period before menopause when ovarian function becomes irregular.

Menopause = 12 straight months with no menstrual period.

So perimenopause is basically:

* still having ovarian activity
* but ovulation becomes less predictable
* estrogen and progesterone levels fluctuate
* periods become irregular

---

### What is happening hormonally

The ovaries have fewer responsive follicles (egg-containing ovarian structures).

Because fewer follicles respond well:

* less consistent estrogen production
* less consistent progesterone production
* the brain increases FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) to push the ovary harder

Important: estrogen does not just smoothly decline at first.

It can swing up and down a lot, which is why symptoms can feel random.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Irregular periods:** ovulation becomes inconsistent, so the endometrium (uterine lining) gets built and shed unpredictably
* **Hot flashes/night sweats:** fluctuating estrogen affects hypothalamic temperature regulation
* **Mood changes/sleep issues:** estrogen affects serotonin, norepinephrine, and sleep regulation
* **Vaginal dryness/dyspareunia (pain with sex):** lower estrogen thins and dries vaginal epithelium
* **Heavier bleeding sometimes:** anovulatory cycles (cycles without ovulation) can cause unopposed estrogen stimulation of the endometrium

---

### Key distinction

Perimenopause:

* periods are changing but not gone for 12 months yet
* pregnancy is still possible

Menopause:

* no period for 12 months
* ovarian estrogen production is persistently low

---

## One-line intuition

The ovaries are becoming less reliable, so hormones fluctuate and the menstrual cycle becomes irregular before finally stopping.

---

## Pearl 🔥

FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) tends to rise in menopause/perimenopause because the ovary makes less inhibin B (a hormone that normally suppresses FSH), so the pituitary “presses the gas” harder.

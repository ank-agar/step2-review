### Core idea

Autoimmune vasculitis means:

> the immune system attacks blood vessel walls, causing vessel inflammation.

Vasculitis = vessel inflammation:

* vascul- = blood vessel
* -itis = inflammation

Autoimmune = the immune system mistakenly attacks self tissue.

---

### What happens to the vessel

The inflamed vessel wall can become:

* swollen
* narrowed
* leaky
* damaged/necrotic

Necrotic means tissue is dying.

So blood flow goes down, and blood can leak out of the vessel.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Palpable purpura:** small skin vessels leak red blood cells into the skin, making raised purple spots  
  Palpable means you can feel it; purpura means purple bleeding into skin.

* **Kidney problems:** inflamed glomeruli leak blood/protein into urine  
  Glomeruli are the kidney's tiny filtering capillaries.

* **Lung bleeding:** small lung vessels get inflamed and leak blood.

* **Neuropathy:** inflamed vessels supplying nerves cause nerve ischemia  
  Ischemia means low blood flow/oxygen.

---

### ANCA-associated vasculitis

A classic autoimmune vasculitis group is **ANCA-associated vasculitis**.

ANCA = anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody.

Break it down:

* anti = against
* neutrophil = white blood cell that fights bacteria
* cytoplasmic = inside-cell material
* antibody = immune protein that binds a target

So ANCA is an antibody that targets proteins inside neutrophils.

The big intuition:

> ANCA activates neutrophils, and angry neutrophils attack small vessel walls.

---

### Important types

* **GPA (granulomatosis with polyangiitis):** sinus/lung/kidney disease, often c-ANCA/PR3  
  PR3 = proteinase 3.

* **MPA (microscopic polyangiitis):** lung/kidney disease, no granulomas, often p-ANCA/MPO  
  MPO = myeloperoxidase.

* **EGPA (eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis):** asthma + eosinophils + vasculitis  
  Eosinophils are allergy/parasite-type white blood cells.

---

## One-line intuition

Autoimmune vasculitis is the immune system inflaming blood vessels, so organs get damaged from leaky vessels and poor blood flow.

---

## Pearl

Palpable purpura is a big Step 1 clue for **small-vessel vasculitis** because inflamed tiny vessels leak blood into the skin, making non-blanching raised purple lesions.

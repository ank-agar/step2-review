Angiodysplasia = abnormal, dilated, fragile blood vessels in the GI tract (gastrointestinal tract).

Most common location:

* cecum/right colon
* especially in older adults

Break the word apart:

* angio- = blood vessel
* dysplasia = abnormal formation/development

So:

> abnormal blood vessels in the bowel wall that can easily bleed.

---

### Core idea

The bowel wall has small vessels in the mucosa (inner lining) and submucosa (layer under the mucosa).

In angiodysplasia, those vessels become:

* dilated
* tortuous (twisted)
* thin-walled
* fragile

That makes them prone to painless bleeding.

---

### Why does it happen?

Think chronic intermittent pressure on bowel wall veins.

Colon contractions can partly block venous drainage over time:

* venous outflow gets blocked
* small vessels dilate
* vessels become fragile
* small artery-to-vein connections may form

That is why this is usually an acquired degenerative lesion in elderly patients, not a cancer.

---

### Classic presentation

Usually:

* older patient
* painless lower GI bleeding
* iron deficiency anemia from chronic occult bleeding

Occult bleeding = bleeding that is not obvious to the eye but is detected by testing or by anemia.

If bleeding is visible:

* hematochezia = bright red blood per rectum
* melena = black tarry stool from digested blood, more likely if bleeding is higher up or slower

---

### Diagnosis/treatment idea

Colonoscopy often shows a flat red vascular lesion.

Treatment if bleeding:

* endoscopic coagulation/cautery
* APC (argon plasma coagulation), which uses ionized argon gas to burn/coagulate the bleeding vessel

If it is found incidentally and not bleeding, it often does not need treatment.

---

## One-line intuition

Angiodysplasia is an old, fragile, tangled blood vessel patch in the bowel wall that causes painless GI bleeding.

---

## HY pearl

Angiodysplasia is associated with aortic stenosis in Heyde syndrome: turbulent flow across the stenotic aortic valve can degrade vWF (von Willebrand factor), causing acquired vWF deficiency and more GI bleeding.

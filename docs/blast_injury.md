### Core idea

A blast injury is trauma caused by an explosion.

The key is that explosions hurt you in multiple ways:

* pressure wave
* flying fragments
* body being thrown
* burns/smoke/toxins

So do not think "blast injury = just shrapnel."

---

### The 4 main mechanisms

**Primary blast injury** = pressure wave injury.

This mainly damages air-filled organs because gas compresses/expands with pressure changes:

* lungs
* middle ear
* gastrointestinal tract

Examples:

* tympanic membrane rupture
* blast lung
* bowel perforation

Tympanic membrane = eardrum.

---

**Secondary blast injury** = flying debris/shrapnel injury.

This is from objects launched by the explosion:

* metal fragments
* glass
* rocks
* building debris

These cause penetrating trauma or blunt trauma.

---

**Tertiary blast injury** = the person gets thrown.

The blast wind accelerates the body, then the body hits something.

So injuries look like high-energy blunt trauma:

* fractures
* traumatic brain injury
* solid organ injury
* crush injury

Traumatic brain injury = brain damage from an external force.

---

**Quaternary blast injury** = everything else.

Examples:

* burns
* smoke inhalation
* toxic gas exposure
* radiation/chemical exposure
* worsening of asthma or heart disease

---

### Why lungs are high yield

Blast lung happens because the pressure wave injures alveoli.

Alveoli = tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens.

That can cause:

* pulmonary contusion
* pneumothorax
* hemoptysis
* hypoxemia
* air embolism

Pulmonary contusion = bruising/bleeding into lung tissue.

Pneumothorax = air in the pleural space causing lung collapse.

Hemoptysis = coughing up blood.

Hypoxemia = low oxygen in the blood.

Air embolism = air bubble in blood vessels.

---

## One-line intuition

Blast injury = explosion trauma from pressure + projectiles + being thrown + heat/toxins.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Primary blast injury classically damages **air-filled organs**, especially:

* ear
* lung
* bowel

So after an explosion, eardrum rupture should make you think: "Was the pressure wave strong enough to also injure lungs or bowel?"

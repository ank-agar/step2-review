# Vocal cord polyp

A vocal cord polyp is a benign growth on one vocal cord.

Benign = non-cancerous.

It is usually caused by vocal trauma, meaning repeated irritation from things like:

* yelling
* singing strain
* chronic coughing
* smoking
* reflux irritation

---

### Core idea

The vocal cords normally vibrate smoothly against each other to make sound.

A polyp is like a soft bump on the edge of one cord, so it messes up:

* vibration
* closure
* smooth airflow

That causes:

* hoarseness
* breathy voice
* rough/raspy voice
* vocal fatigue

---

### Why the voice gets hoarse

When you talk, the vocal cords need to close and vibrate evenly.

If one cord has a polyp:

* the cords cannot meet cleanly
* air leaks through the gap
* vibration becomes irregular

So the voice sounds breathy/hoarse because sound production becomes inefficient.

---

### Polyp vs nodule

High-yield difference:

* **Polyp:** usually unilateral (one-sided), larger, softer
* **Nodules:** usually bilateral (both sides), smaller, callus-like

Unilateral = on one side.

Bilateral = on both sides.

Think:

* polyp = one bigger bump
* nodules = paired “kissing” bumps from repeated contact

---

## One-line intuition

A vocal cord polyp is a soft bump on one vocal cord that prevents smooth closure/vibration, causing a hoarse or breathy voice.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Vocal cord polyps are benign and usually present with hoarseness, but persistent hoarseness still needs laryngoscopy (scope exam of the voice box) to rule out more serious lesions like laryngeal cancer.

### Core idea

A xanthoma is a yellowish cholesterol-rich deposit in skin, tendons, or soft tissue.

Think:

* high blood lipids
* lipids leak/deposit into tissue
* macrophages eat the lipid
* macrophages become foamy lipid-filled cells
* visible yellow bumps/plaques form

Macrophages are immune cells that "eat" debris. Foam cells are lipid-filled macrophages that look bubbly under the microscope.

---

### Why it happens

If LDL (low-density lipoprotein, "bad cholesterol") or triglycerides (blood fats) are very high, the body has too much circulating lipid.

That lipid can deposit in tissues:

* eyelids → xanthelasma
* tendons → tendon xanthomas
* elbows/knees/buttocks → tuberous xanthomas
* trunk/extensor surfaces → eruptive xanthomas

The yellow color is basically stored lipid/cholesterol sitting in tissue.

---

### Step 1 association

Xanthomas should make you think of hyperlipidemia, especially:

* familial hypercholesterolemia
* diabetes mellitus with severe hypertriglyceridemia
* primary biliary cholangitis

Familial hypercholesterolemia often involves defective LDL receptors, so LDL cannot be cleared well from blood.

So:

> defective LDL clearance → very high LDL → cholesterol deposits → xanthomas + premature atherosclerosis

Atherosclerosis means cholesterol plaque buildup inside arteries.

---

### One-line intuition

Xanthomas are the skin/tendon version of "too much lipid in the blood, so lipid starts getting stored where it should not."

---

## Pearl 🔥

Tendon xanthomas, especially in the Achilles tendon, are highly suggestive of familial hypercholesterolemia and should make you worry about early myocardial infarction (heart attack).

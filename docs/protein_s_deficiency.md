### Core idea

Protein S is a natural anticoagulant (anti-clotting protein).

It helps activated protein C, also called APC (activated protein C), turn off:

* factor Va
* factor VIIIa

Factor Va and factor VIIIa are pro-clotting helpers, so protein S normally helps put the brakes on clot formation.

---

### What happens in protein S deficiency

If protein S is low or not working:

* APC (activated protein C) cannot work well
* factor Va and factor VIIIa stay active longer
* thrombin formation increases
* fibrin clot formation increases

So the patient becomes hypercoagulable (more likely to form clots).

---

### What clots do you think about?

Mainly venous clots:

* DVT (deep vein thrombosis), usually in the leg
* PE (pulmonary embolism), a clot that travels to the lung

Less commonly, severe newborn cases can cause purpura fulminans (widespread small-vessel clotting causing purple skin necrosis).

---

### Why it is not a bleeding disorder

Even though it is a "deficiency," the missing thing is an anti-clotting protein.

So:

* missing clotting factor = bleeding
* missing anti-clotting factor = clotting

Protein S deficiency is the second one.

---

## One-line intuition

Protein S is the helper that lets protein C shut off clotting factors Va and VIIIa, so losing protein S removes the brake and causes excess clotting.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Protein C and protein S are vitamin K-dependent proteins, so warfarin can initially lower them and cause a transient hypercoagulable state, which is why patients are often bridged with heparin early on.

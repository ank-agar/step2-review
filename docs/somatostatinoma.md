### Core idea

Somatostatinoma is a rare neuroendocrine tumor (NET = tumor from hormone-producing nerve/endocrine-type cells) that secretes too much somatostatin.

Somatostatin normally inhibits other hormones.

So too much somatostatin = the body gets "turned down" hormonally.

---

### Where it comes from

Usually from:

* pancreas, especially pancreatic delta cells
* duodenum (first part of the small intestine)

Delta cells normally make somatostatin.

---

### Classic symptoms

High somatostatin causes:

* diabetes mellitus (high blood glucose)
* gallstones
* steatorrhea (fatty stool)
* diarrhea
* weight loss

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Diabetes mellitus:** somatostatin inhibits insulin, so glucose stays high
* **Gallstones:** somatostatin decreases gallbladder contraction, so bile sits still and forms stones
* **Steatorrhea:** somatostatin decreases pancreatic enzyme secretion, so fat is not digested well
* **Diarrhea/weight loss:** poor digestion and absorption causes nutrient loss

Break the word apart:

* somatostatin = hormone that inhibits hormone release
* -oma = tumor

So:

> somatostatinoma = tumor that makes too much inhibitory hormone.

---

## One-line intuition

Somatostatinoma is like a tumor pressing the "shut off" button on GI (gastrointestinal) and pancreatic secretions.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Somatostatin inhibits insulin, glucagon, gastrin, growth hormone (GH), and pancreatic secretions, so somatostatinoma classically gives the triad:

* diabetes mellitus
* gallstones
* steatorrhea

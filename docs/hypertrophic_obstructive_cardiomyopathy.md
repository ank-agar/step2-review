## Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/af/Hypertrophic_obstructive_cardiomyopathy.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18141.jpg)

### Core idea

Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, or HOCM (hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy), is when the heart muscle gets abnormally thick, especially the interventricular septum (wall between the left and right ventricles).

The thick septum blocks blood from leaving the left ventricle (main pumping chamber) into the aorta (big artery leaving the heart).

So think:

> big septum = small outflow tract = blood has trouble exiting.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Systolic murmur:** blood is forced through a narrowed left ventricular outflow tract, or LVOT (exit path from left ventricle to aorta)
* **Worse with standing/Valsalva:** less preload (blood returning to heart) makes the ventricle smaller, so the thick septum blocks the outflow tract more
* **Better with squatting:** more preload makes the ventricle fuller/bigger, so the outflow tract opens up more
* **Syncope with exercise:** the heart cannot increase output well when the body demands more blood
* **Sudden death in young athletes:** thick/disorganized myocardium (heart muscle) can trigger dangerous arrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms)

---

### What causes it

Usually autosomal dominant (one bad copy can cause disease).

Often due to sarcomere (muscle contraction unit) protein mutations, especially:

* beta-myosin heavy chain
* myosin-binding protein C

Histology (microscope finding):

* myofiber disarray = heart muscle cells arranged chaotically instead of parallel

---

## One-line intuition

HOCM is a genetically thickened septum that acts like a doorstop in the left ventricle's exit path.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HOCM murmur gets louder with decreased preload, like standing or Valsalva, because a smaller left ventricle makes the obstruction worse.

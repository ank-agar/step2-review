# Raynaud syndrome

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/17127.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1f/Raynaud_syndrome_on_female_airman%27s_hand.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8b/A_thermal_image_demonstrating_the_loss_of_heat_in_a_Reynaud%27s_sufferer.jpg)

Raynaud syndrome is episodic vasospasm (sudden narrowing of blood vessels) of small arteries, usually in the fingers or toes.

Classic trigger:

* cold
* emotional stress

Classic color changes:

* white = ischemia (low blood flow)
* blue = cyanosis (low oxygen in the blood/tissue)
* red = reperfusion (blood rushes back in)

---

### Core idea

The digital arteries (small arteries supplying the fingers/toes) overreact to cold or stress and clamp down too hard.

So blood cannot reach the tips well.

That causes:

* cold/numb fingers
* pain or tingling
* sharply demarcated color change

Then when the vessels relax, blood rushes back and the skin turns red/throbs.

---

### Primary vs secondary

**Primary Raynaud** = occurs by itself.

Usually:

* younger patient
* symmetric
* mild
* no tissue damage

**Secondary Raynaud** = due to another disease or exposure.

Think:

* systemic sclerosis/scleroderma (autoimmune fibrosis of skin and organs)
* SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus)
* rheumatoid arthritis (autoimmune inflammatory arthritis)
* smoking/nicotine
* vibrating tools
* beta blockers

Secondary is more concerning because prolonged ischemia can cause ulcers or gangrene (tissue death).

---

## One-line intuition

Raynaud = fingers temporarily "turn off blood flow" because small arteries spasm too much in response to cold/stress.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Step 1 loves the association between **Raynaud phenomenon + systemic sclerosis**. If you see Raynaud with tight/thickened skin, dysphagia (trouble swallowing), or anti-centromere antibody, think **CREST syndrome**: Calcinosis, Raynaud, Esophageal dysmotility, Sclerodactyly, Telangiectasias.

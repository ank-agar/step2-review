# Neurogenic shock

Neurogenic shock is a type of distributive shock where damage to the nervous system causes loss of sympathetic tone.

Sympathetic tone = the baseline "squeeze" your sympathetic nervous system gives blood vessels and the heart.

So the core problem is:

* spinal cord injury, usually cervical or high thoracic
* loss of sympathetic output
* blood vessels dilate
* blood pools in the body
* blood pressure drops

---

### Classic findings

Neurogenic shock causes:

* hypotension = low blood pressure
* bradycardia = slow heart rate
* warm, flushed skin early

This is the weird part:

Most shock states cause tachycardia (fast heart rate), because the body tries to compensate.

But in neurogenic shock, the sympathetic signal is knocked out, so the heart cannot appropriately speed up.

Also, the vagus nerve is still active.

Vagus nerve = main parasympathetic nerve that slows the heart.

So you get:

> low blood pressure + slow heart rate

That combo after spinal cord trauma is very suspicious for neurogenic shock.

---

### Why the skin is warm

In hypovolemic shock, like blood loss, the body clamps down blood vessels to preserve blood flow to the brain and heart.

That causes cool, clammy skin.

But in neurogenic shock, the vessels cannot clamp down because sympathetic tone is lost.

So the blood vessels stay dilated, especially in the skin.

That gives:

* warm skin
* flushed skin
* low systemic vascular resistance

Systemic vascular resistance = how tightly the body's blood vessels are squeezed.

---

### High-yield cause

Usually from spinal cord injury above T6.

T6 = sixth thoracic spinal cord level.

Why above T6?

Because important sympathetic output to the heart and blood vessels comes from the upper thoracic spinal cord. If the injury is high enough, the brain's sympathetic commands cannot get down to the body.

---

### Neurogenic shock vs spinal shock

Do not mix these up:

* Neurogenic shock = cardiovascular problem: low blood pressure + bradycardia from loss of sympathetic tone
* Spinal shock = neurologic problem: temporary loss of reflexes and flaccid paralysis below a spinal cord injury

Flaccid paralysis = weak, limp muscles.

So:

> neurogenic shock is about circulation; spinal shock is about reflexes/motor function.

---

## One-line intuition

Neurogenic shock is what happens when a high spinal cord injury cuts off the body's "fight-or-flight squeeze," so vessels dilate and the heart slows down.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 pearl: neurogenic shock is the shock type with hypotension plus bradycardia, while hypovolemic shock usually has hypotension plus tachycardia.

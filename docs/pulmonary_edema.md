### Core idea

Pulmonary edema = fluid buildup in the lungs, especially in the:

* interstitium (space around alveoli and capillaries)
* alveoli (air sacs where gas exchange happens)

So instead of alveoli being filled with air, they start filling with fluid.

---

### Why this causes shortness of breath

Normally:

* air enters alveoli
* oxygen crosses into capillaries
* carbon dioxide leaves the blood

In pulmonary edema:

* fluid creates a thicker barrier between air and blood
* less oxygen crosses into blood
* alveoli become heavier and harder to ventilate

So the patient gets dyspnea (shortness of breath), hypoxemia (low blood oxygen), and crackles on lung exam.

---

### Main mechanism

The big Step 1 mechanism is:

* left heart failure increases pulmonary venous pressure
* pressure backs up into lung capillaries
* hydrostatic pressure (fluid-pushing pressure) rises
* fluid gets pushed out of capillaries into lung tissue/alveoli

One-line version:

> Left heart backs up blood into the lungs, so fluid gets squeezed into the alveoli.

---

### Chest X-ray findings

Classic findings include:

* bilateral perihilar opacities
* bat-wing pattern
* Kerley B lines
* cardiomegaly (enlarged heart), if cardiogenic
* pleural effusions sometimes

Perihilar means around the hilum (central lung area where bronchi, blood vessels, and nerves enter).

Bat-wing pattern means the fluid is most prominent centrally and spreads outward like wings.

---

### Cardiogenic vs noncardiogenic

Cardiogenic pulmonary edema = caused by heart problems.

Common causes:

* left-sided heart failure
* mitral valve disease
* fluid overload

Noncardiogenic pulmonary edema = not primarily from heart failure.

Common causes:

* ARDS (acute respiratory distress syndrome)
* sepsis
* inhaled toxins
* high altitude
* opioid overdose
* TRALI (transfusion-related acute lung injury)

Intuition:

* cardiogenic = pressure problem
* noncardiogenic = leaky capillary problem

---

## One-line intuition

Pulmonary edema is when the lungs become a wet sponge, so oxygen has trouble crossing from air into blood.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Pink frothy sputum is classic for acute pulmonary edema because fluid leaks into alveoli and mixes with air/blood-tinged secretions.

### Core idea

Smoke inhalation means breathing in hot smoke/toxic chemicals, causing injury to:

* upper airway
* lower airway/lungs
* blood/tissues from toxic gases

The big danger is not just “dirty air.”

It is:

* airway swelling
* bronchospasm (narrowing of airways)
* mucus/cast plugging
* carbon monoxide poisoning
* cyanide poisoning

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Hoarseness/stridor:** heat injures the upper airway, causing edema (swelling)
* **Wheezing/dyspnea:** smoke chemicals irritate bronchi, causing bronchospasm and inflammation
* **Soot in mouth/sputum:** carbon particles deposit in airways
* **Hypoxemia:** airway plugging + edema impair ventilation
* **Normal pulse ox can be misleading:** carbon monoxide (CO) can make pulse oximetry look falsely normal
* **Lactic acidosis:** cyanide (CN) blocks mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, so cells cannot use oxygen well

---

### Carbon monoxide part

CO (carbon monoxide) binds hemoglobin much more strongly than oxygen.

So it does 2 bad things:

* blocks oxygen from binding hemoglobin
* makes remaining bound oxygen harder to release to tissues

That is why CO poisoning causes a **left shift** of the oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve.

Translation:

> oxygen is stuck on hemoglobin and does not unload well into tissues.

---

### Cyanide part

CN (cyanide) blocks cytochrome c oxidase in the electron transport chain.

So even if oxygen reaches the tissue, the mitochondria cannot use it.

That causes:

* tissue hypoxia
* lactic acidosis
* altered mental status/seizures in severe cases

---

## One-line intuition

Smoke inhalation burns/swells the airway while CO blocks oxygen delivery and CN blocks oxygen use.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In a burn patient with facial burns, hoarseness, soot, or enclosed-space fire exposure, think early airway protection because airway edema can worsen over hours.

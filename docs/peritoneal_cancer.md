### Core idea

Peritoneal cancer = cancer involving the **peritoneum** (thin lining of the abdominal cavity and abdominal organs).

Think of the peritoneum like a slippery inner “wallpaper” lining the belly.

Cancer can involve it in 2 main ways:

* **Primary peritoneal cancer:** starts in the peritoneum itself
* **Peritoneal carcinomatosis:** cancer spreads to the peritoneum from somewhere else

Most commonly, spread comes from:

* ovarian cancer
* colon cancer
* stomach cancer
* pancreatic cancer
* appendix cancer

---

### Why it causes belly swelling

Cancer cells can seed across the peritoneal surface like tiny implants.

This irritates the lining and blocks normal fluid drainage, causing:

* **ascites** (fluid buildup in the abdomen)
* abdominal distension/bloating
* vague abdominal pain
* early satiety, meaning feeling full quickly
* bowel obstruction if tumor implants trap or narrow bowel loops

---

### Imaging clue

On CT (computed tomography), peritoneal spread may show:

* ascites
* peritoneal nodules/thickening
* **omental caking**

The **omentum** is a fatty apron-like fold of peritoneum that hangs over the intestines.

So **omental caking** means tumor has infiltrated that fatty apron, making it look thick and mass-like.

---

### Step 1-style connection

Primary peritoneal carcinoma is histologically similar to **serous ovarian carcinoma**.

Why?

The ovarian surface epithelium and peritoneal lining are closely related embryologically, so they can form similar tumor types.

---

## One-line intuition

Peritoneal cancer is cancer growing on the abdominal lining, often as widespread surface “seeds” that cause ascites and omental caking.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Peritoneal spread is classically associated with **ovarian cancer**, and **CA-125** (cancer antigen 125) can be elevated because the peritoneum gets irritated by tumor.

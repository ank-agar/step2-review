# Secondary amenorrhea

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/Hormones_estradiol%2C_progesterone%2C_LH_and_FSH_during_menstrual_cycle.svg/330px-Hormones_estradiol%2C_progesterone%2C_LH_and_FSH_during_menstrual_cycle.svg.png)

### Core idea

Secondary amenorrhea = periods stop after they already started before.

Usually defined as:

* no menses for 3 months if cycles were previously regular
* no menses for 6 months if cycles were previously irregular

Amenorrhea means absence of menstruation.

---

### First thought

Always rule out pregnancy first.

Pregnancy is the most common cause of secondary amenorrhea.

Why?

In pregnancy, human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) maintains progesterone, so the endometrium stays in place instead of shedding as a period.

---

### Think of the cycle as a pathway

Normal menstruation needs:

* hypothalamus releases gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)
* pituitary releases follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH)
* ovary makes estrogen/progesterone
* uterus builds and sheds endometrium
* outflow tract is open

So secondary amenorrhea happens when any part of that pathway gets interrupted.

---

### Big causes

* **Pregnancy:** most common overall
* **Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea:** stress, weight loss, eating disorder, excessive exercise decrease gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)
* **Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS):** chronic anovulation, meaning no regular ovulation
* **Hyperprolactinemia:** high prolactin suppresses gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)
* **Thyroid disease:** abnormal thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) can disrupt prolactin and the reproductive axis
* **Primary ovarian insufficiency:** ovaries fail early, so follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) rises
* **Asherman syndrome:** intrauterine adhesions/scarring, classically after dilation and curettage (D&C)

---

### Why the findings happen

If the hypothalamus is suppressed:

* low gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)
* low follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)
* low luteinizing hormone (LH)
* low estrogen
* thin endometrium
* no period

If prolactin is high:

* prolactin blocks gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)
* less follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)/luteinizing hormone (LH)
* less ovulation
* amenorrhea ± galactorrhea, meaning milk discharge not related to breastfeeding

If the ovary fails:

* ovary cannot make enough estrogen
* pituitary tries harder
* follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) becomes high

If the uterus is scarred:

* hormones may be normal
* but the endometrium cannot grow/shed normally
* so bleeding does not happen

---

## One-line intuition

Secondary amenorrhea means the period system used to work, but now pregnancy, brain/pituitary signaling, ovaries, thyroid/prolactin, or the uterus is blocking the monthly bleed.

---

## Pearl 🔥

High prolactin causes amenorrhea because prolactin inhibits gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), which lowers follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH).

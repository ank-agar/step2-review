# Gonorrhea

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil///PHIL_Images/2334/2334_lores.jpg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil///PHIL_Images/16874/16874_lores.jpg)

Gonorrhea is an **STI (sexually transmitted infection)** caused by **Neisseria gonorrhoeae**.

Break the organism down:

* **Neisseria** = genus of bacteria
* **gonorrhoeae** = the species that causes gonorrhea
* **diplococci** = round bacteria that come in pairs

Classic Step 1 description:

* **gram-negative diplococci inside neutrophils**

Neutrophils are acute inflammation cells, so if you see bacteria sitting inside neutrophils, think:

> acute pus-forming infection.

---

### Where it infects

Gonorrhea likes **mucosal surfaces**.

So it can infect:

* urethra
* cervix
* rectum
* pharynx
* conjunctiva in newborns

That is why symptoms depend on the site.

---

### Common symptoms

In males:

* urethritis
* dysuria
* purulent discharge

**Urethritis** means inflammation of the urethra.

In females:

* cervicitis
* increased discharge
* dysuria
* pelvic pain if it ascends

**Cervicitis** means inflammation of the cervix.

Important: many females can be asymptomatic, so the infection can silently ascend.

---

### Why it can cause PID

If gonorrhea climbs upward from the cervix into the upper reproductive tract, it can cause **PID (pelvic inflammatory disease)**.

PID can involve:

* uterus
* fallopian tubes
* ovaries

Why this matters:

* inflamed fallopian tubes can scar
* scarring can cause infertility
* scarring also increases ectopic pregnancy risk

**Ectopic pregnancy** means pregnancy implanted outside the uterus, classically in the fallopian tube.

---

### Disseminated gonorrhea

Sometimes it spreads through the blood and causes **DGI (disseminated gonococcal infection)**.

Classic pattern:

* migratory polyarthralgia
* tenosynovitis
* dermatitis

**Polyarthralgia** means pain in multiple joints.

**Tenosynovitis** means inflammation around a tendon sheath.

Dermatitis is skin inflammation, often with pustular or necrotic lesions.

---

### Diagnosis

Most common test:

* **NAAT (nucleic acid amplification test)**

NAAT detects bacterial genetic material.

Gram stain can be very useful in symptomatic males because it may show:

* gram-negative diplococci inside neutrophils

---

### Treatment idea

Treat with **ceftriaxone**, a third-generation cephalosporin antibiotic.

Also cover chlamydia if it has not been excluded, because gonorrhea and chlamydia commonly travel together.

---

## One-line intuition

Gonorrhea is a mucosa-loving gram-negative diplococcus that causes purulent urethritis/cervicitis and can ascend to PID or spread to joints/skin.

---

## Pearl

Neisseria gonorrhoeae is **oxidase-positive** and grows on **Thayer-Martin agar**, and it ferments glucose only.

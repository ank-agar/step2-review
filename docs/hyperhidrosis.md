Hyperhidrosis = excessive sweating beyond what is needed for temperature control.

Break the word apart:

* hyper- = too much
* hidrosis = sweating

So:

> too much sweating.

---

### Core idea

The sweat glands are not usually structurally abnormal.

The problem is usually overactive nerve signaling to eccrine sweat glands (main sweat glands used for watery cooling sweat).

Common sites:

* palms
* soles
* axillae (armpits)
* face/scalp

---

### Why the sweating happens

Sweat glands are activated by the sympathetic nervous system (fight-or-flight autonomic nerves).

But the weird Step 1 detail is:

* most sympathetic nerves release norepinephrine (NE; main sympathetic neurotransmitter)
* eccrine sweat glands are sympathetic but release acetylcholine (ACh; parasympathetic-type neurotransmitter)

So hyperhidrosis is basically:

> too much sympathetic cholinergic stimulation of eccrine sweat glands.

Cholinergic = using acetylcholine (ACh).

---

### Primary vs secondary

Primary focal hyperhidrosis:

* starts younger
* often symmetric
* commonly palms/soles/axillae
* often family history
* usually stops during sleep

Secondary hyperhidrosis:

* due to another condition or drug
* more likely generalized
* can happen during sleep

Think secondary causes like:

* hyperthyroidism (too much thyroid hormone)
* infection/fever
* malignancy (cancer)
* menopause
* hypoglycemia (low blood glucose)
* medications

---

### Treatment logic

* Aluminum chloride: plugs sweat ducts locally
* Anticholinergics: block acetylcholine (ACh) signaling
* Botulinum toxin: blocks acetylcholine (ACh) release from nerves
* Iontophoresis: uses electrical current, especially for palms/soles
* Sympathectomy: cuts sympathetic nerve input in severe cases

---

## One-line intuition

Hyperhidrosis is not “bigger sweat glands” — it is mostly normal eccrine sweat glands being told to sweat too much by overactive sympathetic cholinergic nerves.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Eccrine sweat glands are a classic exception: they are sympathetically innervated but use acetylcholine (ACh), not norepinephrine (NE).

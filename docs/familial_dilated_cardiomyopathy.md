### Core idea

Familial dilated cardiomyopathy is inherited dilated cardiomyopathy.

Break it down:

* familial = runs in families
* dilated = enlarged/stretched out
* cardiomyopathy = disease of the heart muscle

So the heart muscle becomes weak and stretched, especially the ventricles (main pumping chambers), causing poor systolic function (poor squeezing/pumping).

---

### Why the heart dilates

Many cases involve mutations in proteins that help heart muscle cells contract or stay structurally connected, like:

* titin = giant sarcomere (muscle-contracting unit) protein
* cytoskeletal proteins = internal support/scaffolding proteins
* nuclear envelope proteins = proteins supporting the nucleus (DNA-containing center of the cell)

If the heart muscle cells cannot generate force normally, the ventricle has to handle more leftover blood after each beat.

More leftover blood = more volume = chamber stretches over time.

That gives:

* dilated ventricle
* thin/weakened wall
* reduced ejection fraction (percent of blood pumped out each beat)
* heart failure symptoms

---

### Key inheritance idea

Most familial dilated cardiomyopathy is autosomal dominant (one mutated copy of a gene is enough to increase disease risk).

That means it can show up in multiple generations:

* parent affected
* child affected
* males and females affected

But penetrance (how often a mutation actually causes disease) can be incomplete, so someone may carry the mutation and look normal for years.

---

### What patients can get

Because the heart cannot pump forward well:

* fatigue from low cardiac output (low blood flow to body)
* dyspnea (shortness of breath) from pulmonary congestion (fluid backing up into lungs)
* leg edema (swelling) from venous backup
* S3 heart sound from volume overload
* arrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms)
* sudden cardiac death in severe cases

---

## One-line intuition

Familial dilated cardiomyopathy is a genetic weak-pump heart: the ventricle stretches out because the muscle cannot squeeze well.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Dilated cardiomyopathy causes systolic dysfunction, meaning the main problem is weak contraction and decreased ejection fraction.

Classic Step 1 association: dilated cardiomyopathy can be caused by genetic mutations, alcohol, doxorubicin, viral myocarditis, peripartum cardiomyopathy, and hemochromatosis.

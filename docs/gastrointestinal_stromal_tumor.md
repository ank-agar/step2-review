### Core idea

Gastrointestinal stromal tumor, or **GIST** (gastrointestinal stromal tumor), is a tumor from the **stromal/support cells** of the GI (gastrointestinal) tract.

Most commonly:

* stomach
* small intestine

It comes from **interstitial cells of Cajal** (pacemaker cells that help coordinate gut motility).

---

### Why it happens

Most GISTs have activating mutations in:

* **KIT** (receptor tyrosine kinase; also called CD117)
* **PDGFRA** (platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha)

These are growth-signal receptors.

So the intuition is:

> The tumor cell gets a stuck-on growth signal, so it keeps proliferating.

---

### How it presents

Can cause:

* GI bleeding, because the mass can ulcerate into the lumen
* abdominal pain/fullness, because it grows as a mass
* obstruction, if large
* incidental finding, because some are asymptomatic

It often grows outward from the bowel wall, so lymph node spread is less classic than with many GI carcinomas (epithelial cancers).

---

### Diagnosis clue

On pathology, GIST is often:

* spindle-shaped cells
* **CD117-positive** (KIT-positive)
* often **DOG1-positive** (discovered on GIST-1; a diagnostic marker)

---

### Treatment

Classic targeted therapy:

* **imatinib** - a tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI; drug that blocks overactive growth-signal kinases)

Why imatinib works:

* GIST is often driven by overactive KIT/PDGFRA signaling
* imatinib blocks that signaling pathway

---

## One-line intuition

GIST is a GI wall support-cell tumor driven by overactive KIT/PDGFRA growth signaling, classically CD117-positive and treated with imatinib.

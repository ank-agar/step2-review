## Mitral valve prolapse

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470288/bin/2000px-Heart_mitral_prolapse.svg.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18148.jpg)

Mitral valve prolapse (MVP) is when the mitral valve leaflets are floppy and bulge backward into the left atrium during systole (ventricular contraction).

The mitral valve normally sits between:

* left atrium (LA)
* left ventricle (LV)

Normally, during systole, the LV squeezes blood forward into the aorta, and the mitral valve closes tightly so blood cannot go backward into the LA.

In MVP:

* the valve leaflets are too loose/redundant
* they balloon backward into the LA
* this can cause mitral regurgitation (MR), meaning blood leaks backward from LV to LA

---

### Classic sound

MVP causes:

* mid-systolic click
* late systolic murmur

Why?

The click happens when the floppy valve suddenly snaps/tenses during systole.

The murmur happens after the valve starts leaking backward into the LA.

---

### Maneuvers

MVP gets earlier with decreased LV volume, especially:

* standing
* Valsalva maneuver (forced exhalation against a closed airway)

Why?

Less blood in the LV means the floppy valve has less chamber space/tension holding it down, so it prolapses sooner.

Squatting increases LV volume, so the click happens later.

---

## One-line intuition

The mitral valve is too floppy, so when the LV squeezes, the valve parachutes backward into the LA instead of sealing flat.

---

## Pearl 🔥

MVP is the most common valvular heart lesion and is associated with myxomatous degeneration (mucin-rich weakening/thickening of valve connective tissue), Marfan syndrome, and Ehlers-Danlos syndrome.

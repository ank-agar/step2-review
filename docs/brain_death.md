# Brain death

Brain death = irreversible loss of all brain function, including the brainstem.

Brainstem = the lower part of the brain that controls basic survival reflexes like breathing, pupil response, corneal reflex, gag/cough reflex, and eye movement reflexes.

So brain death is not just:

* coma
* deep sleep
* vegetative state
* severe brain injury

It means the brain has permanently stopped working.

---

### Core idea

The key Step 1 intuition:

Brainstem dead = no drive to breathe.

Why?

The medulla (part of the brainstem) senses high CO2 (carbon dioxide) and tells you to breathe.

If the brainstem is dead, CO2 can rise, but the brain does not respond.

That is why the apnea test is important.

Apnea = not breathing.

---

### What doctors look for

Brain death exam shows:

* unresponsive coma
* absent brainstem reflexes
* no spontaneous breathing

Absent brainstem reflexes means:

* pupils do not react to light
* no corneal reflex
* no gag/cough reflex
* no vestibulo-ocular reflex

Vestibulo-ocular reflex = the brainstem eye movement reflex tested with cold water in the ear.

---

### Why the body can still look "alive"

The heart can keep beating for a while because the heart has its own pacemaker cells.

Pacemaker cells = cells that automatically generate electrical activity.

Also, a ventilator can push air into the lungs.

Ventilator = machine that mechanically moves air into and out of the lungs.

So the chest may rise and the heart may beat, but the person is legally and medically dead because the brain function is gone permanently.

---

## One-line intuition

Brain death = the whole brain, especially the brainstem breathing/reflex center, is permanently off.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Brain death is different from persistent vegetative state.

Persistent vegetative state = loss of awareness with preserved brainstem function.

So vegetative patients may still breathe on their own, but brain-dead patients cannot.

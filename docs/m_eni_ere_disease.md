# Ménière disease

![Image](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0111039.g001&type=large)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Menieres-hearing-loss.png/500px-Menieres-hearing-loss.png)

Ménière disease is a disorder of the inner ear causing recurrent episodes of:

* vertigo (spinning sensation)
* tinnitus (ringing/buzzing in the ear)
* sensorineural hearing loss (hearing loss from inner ear or nerve dysfunction)
* aural fullness (pressure/fullness in the ear)

Core mechanism:

* excess endolymph (fluid inside the membranous labyrinth of the inner ear)
* this causes endolymphatic hydrops (swelling/distension from too much endolymph)

So the inner ear is basically “overfilled with fluid.”

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The inner ear handles both:

* hearing via the cochlea
* balance via the vestibular apparatus (semicircular canals, utricle, and saccule)

Too much endolymph stretches/disrupts these structures:

* **cochlea affected:** hearing loss + tinnitus
* **vestibular apparatus affected:** vertigo + nausea/vomiting
* **pressure buildup:** ear fullness

The hearing loss is classically:

* fluctuating early
* low-frequency sensorineural hearing loss
* may become permanent over time

---

### Diagnosis clue

Think Ménière when you see:

* episodic vertigo lasting minutes to hours
* unilateral ear symptoms
* fluctuating hearing loss
* tinnitus + ear fullness

Unilateral = one-sided.

---

## One-line intuition

Ménière disease is “too much inner-ear fluid,” which messes up both balance and hearing.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Step 1 loves the triad:

* episodic vertigo
* tinnitus
* low-frequency sensorineural hearing loss

Most common treatment concept: low-salt diet + diuretics (drugs that increase urination), because the goal is to reduce fluid buildup.

### Core idea

Salmonella is a Gram-negative rod (bacteria with a thin peptidoglycan cell wall plus an outer membrane) that commonly causes food poisoning.

Classic sources:

* undercooked eggs
* poultry
* reptiles
* contaminated food/water

It is motile because it has flagella (tail-like structures that help bacteria move).

---

### What it causes

Most commonly:

* fever
* abdominal cramps
* inflammatory diarrhea

Why inflammatory?

Salmonella does not just sit in the gut and make toxin. It invades intestinal mucosa (the lining of the intestine), especially through M cells (specialized intestinal immune-sampling cells) over Peyer patches (lymphoid tissue in the ileum).

That invasion triggers neutrophils (acute inflammatory white blood cells), so you get:

* fever
* crampy pain
* diarrhea that can be bloody

---

### Big distinction

There are 2 high-yield buckets:

* **Nontyphoidal Salmonella**: usually gastroenteritis (stomach/intestine infection), often from poultry/eggs
* **Salmonella typhi**: causes typhoid fever, a systemic infection (infection spreading through the body)

Typhoid fever is more severe because Salmonella typhi can survive inside macrophages (immune cells that normally eat bacteria), then spread through the reticuloendothelial system (macrophage-rich organs like liver, spleen, and lymph nodes).

---

### Why it can survive inside cells

Normally, macrophages engulf bacteria and destroy them.

Salmonella can survive intracellularly (inside cells), so it can hide from parts of the immune system and spread deeper.

That is why typhoid fever can cause:

* prolonged fever
* abdominal symptoms
* hepatosplenomegaly (enlarged liver and spleen)
* rose spots (faint pink rash on trunk)

---

### Lab clues

Salmonella is:

* Gram-negative rod
* motile
* lactose non-fermenter (does not ferment lactose sugar)
* H2S producer (hydrogen sulfide producer)

H2S production is why it can make black colonies on certain media, because hydrogen sulfide reacts with iron in the agar.

---

## One-line intuition

Salmonella is an invasive foodborne Gram-negative rod that enters the gut lining, triggers inflammation, and in typhi can live inside macrophages and spread systemically.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In sickle cell disease, Salmonella is classically associated with osteomyelitis (bone infection), while Staphylococcus aureus is still the most common overall cause of osteomyelitis.

### Core idea

Necrotizing enterocolitis, or **NEC (necrotizing enterocolitis)**, is a dangerous bowel disease in newborns, especially premature infants.

Break the word apart:

* **necrotizing** = tissue is dying
* **entero-** = small intestine
* **colitis** = inflammation of the colon

So NEC means:

> inflammation + death of the intestinal wall.

---

### Classic patient

Think:

* premature infant
* after feeding has started
* abdominal distension
* bloody stool
* feeding intolerance
* systemic illness like temperature instability, apnea, or lethargy

**Apnea** means pauses in breathing.  
**Lethargy** means abnormal sleepiness/low responsiveness.

---

### Why it happens

Premature bowel is vulnerable because it has:

* weak mucosal barrier (inner protective lining)
* immature immune control
* abnormal bacterial colonization
* reduced blood flow/ischemia risk

Then feeds + bacteria + fragile bowel wall can trigger inflammation.

That inflammation damages the bowel wall, bacteria enter the wall, and tissue starts dying.

---

### Key X-ray finding

The classic imaging finding is:

* **pneumatosis intestinalis** = gas inside the bowel wall

Why?

Bacteria invade the damaged intestinal wall and produce gas there.

So instead of gas being only inside the bowel lumen (the hollow tube), gas is actually trapped in the bowel wall.

More severe findings:

* **portal venous gas** = gas entering veins that drain toward the liver
* **pneumoperitoneum** = free air in the abdominal cavity, suggesting perforation

**Perforation** means a hole through the bowel wall.

---

## One-line intuition

NEC is when a premature baby’s fragile intestine gets inflamed, invaded by bacteria, and becomes necrotic, causing gas in the bowel wall.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, the buzzword combo is:

**premature infant + bloody stools + abdominal distension + pneumatosis intestinalis = necrotizing enterocolitis.**

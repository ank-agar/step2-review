# Sheehan syndrome

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459247/bin/StatePearls_Pituitary_ElSayed_2016_Kathi.gif)

Sheehan syndrome is postpartum pituitary necrosis.

Postpartum = after delivery  
Pituitary = hormone gland under the brain  
Necrosis = tissue death from loss of blood supply

So the core idea is:

> severe postpartum hemorrhage causes low blood pressure, so the pituitary loses blood flow and infarcts.

Infarct = tissue dies because it is not getting enough oxygenated blood.

---

### Why pregnancy makes this happen

During pregnancy, the anterior pituitary gets bigger because lactotrophs grow.

Lactotrophs = anterior pituitary cells that make prolactin.

Prolactin = hormone that helps make breast milk.

So now the pituitary is:

* bigger
* more metabolically active
* needing more oxygen/blood

But if the patient has major postpartum hemorrhage:

* blood volume drops
* blood pressure drops
* pituitary perfusion drops

Perfusion = blood flow to tissue.

That enlarged pituitary is especially vulnerable, so it undergoes ischemic necrosis.

Ischemia = not enough blood flow.

---

### What gets damaged

Mostly the **anterior pituitary**.

Anterior pituitary hormones include:

* prolactin
* FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone)
* LH (luteinizing hormone)
* ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone)
* TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone)
* GH (growth hormone)

So Sheehan syndrome causes hypopituitarism.

Hypopituitarism = low pituitary hormone output.

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

> woman has severe bleeding during/after delivery, then cannot breastfeed and later does not resume periods.

Key findings:

* failure to lactate because prolactin is low
* amenorrhea because FSH/LH are low
* fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain because TSH is low, causing secondary hypothyroidism
* hypotension, hypoglycemia, weakness because ACTH is low, causing secondary adrenal insufficiency
* loss of pubic/axillary hair because gonadotropins are low

Amenorrhea = no menstrual periods.  
Secondary hypothyroidism = low thyroid hormone because the pituitary is not stimulating the thyroid enough.  
Secondary adrenal insufficiency = low cortisol because the pituitary is not stimulating the adrenal cortex enough.

---

### Why no milk?

Milk production needs prolactin from the anterior pituitary.

If the anterior pituitary infarcts:

* prolactin falls
* breast milk production fails

This is why inability to breastfeed after major postpartum hemorrhage is a huge clue.

---

### Why amenorrhea?

The anterior pituitary normally releases FSH and LH.

FSH and LH stimulate the ovaries.

If FSH/LH are low:

* ovaries are not stimulated
* estrogen/progesterone fall
* ovulation and periods do not resume

---

## One-line intuition

Sheehan syndrome is the pituitary getting “knocked out” after massive postpartum blood loss, causing low anterior pituitary hormones.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 clue:

> postpartum hemorrhage + failure to lactate = Sheehan syndrome until proven otherwise.

Also, diabetes insipidus is uncommon because the posterior pituitary is usually spared.

Diabetes insipidus = inability to concentrate urine due to low ADH (antidiuretic hormone) effect.

### Core idea

Complex regional pain syndrome, or **CRPS** (complex regional pain syndrome), is chronic severe limb pain after an injury/surgery that is way worse than expected.

Usually affects:

* hand/arm
* foot/leg

The pain is **regional**, meaning it does not follow one clean dermatome (skin area supplied by one spinal nerve) or one single nerve distribution.

---

### What happens

After trauma, the nervous system becomes overactive and sensitized.

So the limb can develop:

* burning pain
* allodynia (pain from normally non-painful touch)
* hyperalgesia (exaggerated pain from a painful stimulus)
* swelling
* color/temperature changes
* sweating changes
* stiffness and decreased range of motion
* skin/nail/hair changes

---

### Why the findings happen

Think:

> injury triggers abnormal pain signaling + abnormal autonomic control.

Autonomic nervous system (automatic nerves controlling blood vessels, sweating, temperature) dysfunction explains:

* red/purple color changes
* warm or cold limb
* sweating changes
* swelling

Pain pathway sensitization explains:

* severe burning pain
* pain out of proportion to the injury
* pain with light touch

Disuse explains:

* stiffness
* weakness
* decreased motion
* later osteopenia (low bone density)

---

### Types

* **Type 1:** no clear major nerve injury
* **Type 2:** clear nerve injury

Old names:

* reflex sympathetic dystrophy = usually CRPS type 1
* causalgia = usually CRPS type 2

---

## One-line intuition

CRPS is when a limb injury heals structurally, but the pain/autonomic wiring keeps acting like the limb is still in extreme danger.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Classic Step 1 clue: **pain out of proportion after trauma + allodynia + skin temperature/color/sweating changes** = CRPS.

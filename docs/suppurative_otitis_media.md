# Suppurative otitis media

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/ear-infection/media/images/ear-infection.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19324.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549830/bin/Otitis_media_entdifferenziert2.jpg)

Suppurative otitis media = middle ear infection with pus.

Break it down:

* **suppurative** = pus-forming
* **otitis** = ear inflammation
* **media** = middle ear

So this means:

> pus builds up behind the tympanic membrane (eardrum).

---

### Core idea

Usually after an upper respiratory infection (cold), the Eustachian tube (tube connecting middle ear to nasopharynx) gets swollen/blocked.

Then:

* middle ear cannot drain
* fluid sits behind the eardrum
* bacteria grow in that trapped fluid
* neutrophils rush in
* pus forms

That pus increases pressure behind the tympanic membrane (eardrum), so the eardrum becomes:

* bulging
* red
* painful
* less mobile on pneumatic otoscopy (otoscope test using air pressure)

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Ear pain:** pus/pressure stretches the tympanic membrane (eardrum)
* **Fever:** acute infection causes inflammatory cytokines (immune signaling proteins)
* **Decreased hearing:** fluid blocks sound conduction through the middle ear
* **Otorrhea:** ear drainage if the tympanic membrane (eardrum) ruptures

---

### Common bugs

Classic acute otitis media bacteria:

* Streptococcus pneumoniae
* nontypeable Haemophilus influenzae
* Moraxella catarrhalis

These are the same kinds of organisms that colonize the upper respiratory tract, which is why this often follows a cold.

---

## One-line intuition

A cold blocks the Eustachian tube, trapped middle-ear fluid gets infected, and pus pushes the eardrum outward.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Otitis media causes **deep ear pain without pain when pulling the ear**.

Pain with pulling the pinna (outer ear) or pressing the tragus (small cartilage bump in front of ear canal) points more toward otitis externa (outer ear canal infection).

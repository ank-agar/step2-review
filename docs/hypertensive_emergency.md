# Hypertensive emergency

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Main_complications_of_persistent_high_blood_pressure.svg/1280px-Main_complications_of_persistent_high_blood_pressure.svg.png)

![Image](https://eyewiki-images.s3.us-east-va.perf.cloud.ovh.us/9/9d/HTN_RET_Findings.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.medbullets.com/topic/108081/images/rp_nov_dec_a10_fig01.jpg)

Hypertensive emergency = severely high BP (blood pressure), usually >180/120 mmHg, **plus acute end-organ damage**.

End-organ damage means the high pressure is actively injuring important organs like:

* brain
* heart
* kidneys
* retina
* aorta

So the key idea is:

> high BP alone is not the emergency; high BP + organ damage is.

---

### Hypertensive emergency vs urgency

**Hypertensive urgency**:

* very high BP
* no acute end-organ damage

**Hypertensive emergency**:

* very high BP
* acute end-organ damage

Examples of end-organ damage:

* encephalopathy = brain dysfunction causing confusion, headache, seizures
* pulmonary edema = fluid in lungs from acute heart strain
* acute kidney injury = sudden kidney dysfunction
* MI (myocardial infarction) = heart attack
* aortic dissection = tear in the aortic wall
* papilledema = optic disc swelling from severe pressure/vascular injury

---

### Why organ damage happens

Very high BP causes intense mechanical stress on small arteries/arterioles.

That damages endothelium (inner blood vessel lining), causing:

* leakage of plasma proteins into vessel walls
* platelet activation and microthrombi
* tissue ischemia = poor blood flow/oxygen delivery

Path buzzwords:

* **fibrinoid necrosis** = severe acute vessel wall damage
* **hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis** = concentric “onion-skin” thickening of arterioles

This is why malignant/severe hypertension can cause kidney failure, retinal hemorrhages, cotton-wool spots, papilledema, and brain symptoms.

---

### One-line intuition

Hypertensive emergency is when blood pressure is so high that vessels start failing and organs are actively getting injured.

---

### Treatment idea

Use IV (intravenous) antihypertensives and lower BP carefully.

Do **not** drop it too fast, because chronically hypertensive organs are used to higher pressures; sudden overcorrection can cause hypoperfusion (too little blood flow).

Typical Step 1 idea:

* reduce MAP (mean arterial pressure) by about 10-20% in the first hour

---

## Pearl 🔥

On Step 1, **papilledema + very high BP** should make you think malignant hypertension/hypertensive emergency, with **fibrinoid necrosis and hyperplastic “onion-skin” arteriolosclerosis** in small vessels.

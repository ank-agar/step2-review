# Long QT syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/eb/Electrocardiograph_complex.svg/1280px-Electrocardiograph_complex.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/22/Long_QT_syndrome_type_1.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Torsades_de_Pointes_%28polymorphic_VT%29.svg/960px-Torsades_de_Pointes_%28polymorphic_VT%29.svg.png)

Long QT syndrome is a heart electrical disorder where the **QT interval** (time from ventricular depolarization to ventricular repolarization) is prolonged on an ECG (electrocardiogram).

Translation:

* the ventricles take too long to electrically reset
* that prolonged reset time makes dangerous arrhythmias easier to trigger

---

### Core idea

The QT interval represents:

* QRS complex = ventricular depolarization
* T wave = ventricular repolarization

So a long QT means:

> the ventricles are repolarizing too slowly.

That matters because delayed repolarization can cause early afterdepolarizations (abnormal extra depolarizations during repolarization), which can trigger torsades de pointes (a polymorphic ventricular tachycardia that twists around the baseline).

---

### Why it happens

Most Step 1 causes are about ion channels:

* ↓ K+ efflux = potassium leaves the cardiac cell too slowly
* ↑ Na+ influx = sodium keeps entering too long

Either way, the cardiac action potential lasts longer.

Longer action potential = longer ventricular repolarization = longer QT interval.

---

### Symptoms

Can cause:

* syncope (fainting)
* palpitations
* seizures due to transient cerebral hypoperfusion (brief low blood flow to the brain)
* sudden cardiac death

The “seizures” are not always primary epilepsy. The brain can look like it is seizing because the arrhythmia briefly drops cardiac output.

---

### Acquired causes

Common acquired causes:

* hypokalemia (low potassium)
* hypomagnesemia (low magnesium)
* hypocalcemia (low calcium)
* drugs that prolong QT

Classic QT-prolonging drugs:

* class IA antiarrhythmics, like quinidine
* class III antiarrhythmics, like sotalol
* macrolides, like erythromycin
* fluoroquinolones, like levofloxacin
* antipsychotics, like haloperidol

---

### Inherited forms

Important inherited long QT syndromes:

* Romano-Ward syndrome = autosomal dominant, no deafness
* Jervell and Lange-Nielsen syndrome = autosomal recessive, with congenital sensorineural deafness

Why deafness?

Inner ear hair cells also need ion channels, especially K+ handling, to work normally.

---

## One-line intuition

Long QT syndrome means the ventricle takes too long to electrically reset, so a bad extra beat can land during a vulnerable period and spiral into torsades de pointes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Treat torsades de pointes with IV magnesium, even if serum magnesium is normal.

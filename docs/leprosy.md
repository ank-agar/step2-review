Leprosy, aka Hansen disease, is a chronic infection caused by:

* Mycobacterium leprae
* sometimes Mycobacterium lepromatosis

It mainly affects:

* skin
* peripheral nerves
* nasal mucosa
* eyes

---

### Core idea

Think:

> slow-growing acid-fast bacteria that love cool body areas and peripheral nerves.

That is why leprosy causes:

* hypopigmented skin patches
* loss of sensation
* thickened peripheral nerves
* muscle weakness
* painless ulcers/injuries

Acid-fast means the bacteria have a waxy mycolic acid cell wall, so they hold acid-fast stains.

---

### Why numb skin patches happen

Mycobacterium leprae has tropism for Schwann cells.

Schwann cells = cells that make myelin around peripheral nerves.

So the bacteria/inflammation injure peripheral nerves, causing:

* decreased pain sensation
* decreased temperature sensation
* weakness if motor nerves are affected

That is why patients can get painless burns, cuts, ulcers, and deformities.

---

### Tuberculoid vs lepromatous

The spectrum depends on cell-mediated immunity.

Cell-mediated immunity = T-cell/macrophage immune response against intracellular pathogens.

**Tuberculoid leprosy**

* strong cell-mediated immunity
* few organisms
* few well-demarcated skin lesions
* prominent nerve damage/anesthesia

**Lepromatous leprosy**

* weak cell-mediated immunity
* many organisms
* diffuse symmetric skin lesions/nodules
* leonine facies can occur

Leonine facies = thickened, nodular facial skin that can look lion-like.

---

## One-line intuition

Leprosy is a slow acid-fast infection that damages peripheral nerves, so skin lesions become numb and injuries become painless.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 distinction:

* tuberculoid leprosy = strong TH1 response, granulomas, few bacilli
* lepromatous leprosy = weak TH1 response, many bacilli, worse dissemination

TH1 means T helper 1 cells, which activate macrophages to kill intracellular organisms.

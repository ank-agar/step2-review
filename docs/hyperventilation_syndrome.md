## Hyperventilation syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Hyperventilation_vs_Hyperpnea.jpg/960px-Hyperventilation_vs_Hyperpnea.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Davenport_Fig_11.jpg/120px-Davenport_Fig_11.jpg)

Hyperventilation syndrome is when someone breathes too fast or too deeply for what the body actually needs.

That causes them to blow off too much CO2 (carbon dioxide).

Less CO2 in blood causes:

* increased blood pH (more basic/alkalotic)
* respiratory alkalosis (alkalosis caused by breathing off too much CO2)

---

### Core idea

CO2 acts like acid in the blood.

So:

* more CO2 = more acidic
* less CO2 = more basic

In hyperventilation syndrome:

* breathing too much -> CO2 drops -> pH rises

That high pH is what causes many of the weird symptoms.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Low CO2 causes respiratory alkalosis.

Respiratory alkalosis increases calcium binding to albumin (a blood protein), so free ionized calcium decreases.

That can cause:

* perioral tingling (tingling around the mouth)
* paresthesias (pins-and-needles sensation)
* hand/foot tingling
* muscle cramps
* carpopedal spasm (hand/foot cramping)

Also, low CO2 causes cerebral vasoconstriction (narrowing of brain blood vessels), so less blood flow reaches the brain.

That causes:

* lightheadedness
* dizziness
* feeling like you might faint

---

### Common presentation

Patient may have:

* anxiety or panic
* rapid breathing
* chest tightness
* shortness of breath
* palpitations (feeling the heart racing)
* tingling around mouth/fingers
* dizziness

The key is that they often feel like they are not getting enough air, but the problem is actually overbreathing.

---

## One-line intuition

Hyperventilation syndrome = breathing too much -> CO2 gets too low -> blood becomes alkalotic -> tingling, dizziness, cramps.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On USMLE (United States Medical Licensing Examination), acute anxiety/panic with hyperventilation classically causes respiratory alkalosis:

* pH high
* PaCO2 (arterial carbon dioxide pressure) low

Do not confuse it with asthma/COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease), where severe fatigue or poor ventilation can lead to CO2 retention and respiratory acidosis.

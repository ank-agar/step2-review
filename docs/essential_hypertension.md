### Core idea

Essential hypertension means chronic high blood pressure with **no single identifiable secondary cause**.

Essential = primary/idiopathic (unknown exact cause).

Hypertension = high arterial blood pressure (pressure of blood pushing against artery walls).

---

### Why blood pressure goes up

Blood pressure is basically:

> cardiac output x systemic vascular resistance

Cardiac output = how much blood the heart pumps per minute.

Systemic vascular resistance = how tight/narrow the body's arteries are.

So essential hypertension usually comes from a mix of:

* increased vascular tone
* increased salt/water retention
* increased sympathetic activity
* genetic/environmental factors

No one tumor, kidney artery stenosis, endocrine disease, or drug is causing it.

---

### Why it matters

Long-term high pressure injures small vessels and organs:

* kidney: hyaline arteriolosclerosis (protein leakage into small arteriole walls)
* heart: left ventricular hypertrophy, meaning thickened left heart muscle
* brain: stroke risk
* retina: hypertensive retinopathy

The kidney finding happens because chronic pressure damages small vessel endothelium (inner lining), so plasma proteins leak into the wall and make it pink/glassy on histology.

---

## One-line intuition

Essential hypertension is high blood pressure from many small chronic factors adding up, not from one obvious secondary disease.

---

## Pearl

Essential hypertension is the most common cause of hypertension overall, while secondary hypertension is more suspicious if the patient is young, has sudden severe hypertension, resistant hypertension, or hypokalemia (low potassium).

### Core idea

Pulmonary metastases = cancer that started somewhere else and spread to the lungs.

Pulmonary = lung.

Metastases = distant tumor deposits from a primary cancer.

So pulmonary metastases are not usually "primary lung cancer"; they are lung lesions made of the original cancer type.

Example:

* colon cancer spreading to lung = metastatic colon cancer in the lung
* breast cancer spreading to lung = metastatic breast cancer in the lung

---

### Why the lungs get metastases

The lungs are a common site because venous blood returns to the right heart and then gets pumped into the pulmonary circulation (blood vessels of the lungs).

So tumor cells traveling through blood can get trapped in the lung capillary bed (tiny lung blood vessels), then grow into nodules.

This is why hematogenous spread (spread through blood) often gives:

* multiple lung nodules
* bilateral lesions, meaning both lungs
* rounded "cannonball" lesions on chest imaging

---

### Common primary tumors that spread to lung

Think cancers that like hematogenous spread:

* renal cell carcinoma
* choriocarcinoma
* sarcomas
* colon cancer
* breast cancer
* melanoma

Choriocarcinoma is especially classically associated with early blood-borne metastasis to the lungs.

---

## One-line intuition

The lungs are like a blood filter, so cancer cells traveling in venous blood can lodge there and grow into metastatic nodules.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Multiple well-circumscribed round lung nodules on chest x-ray are called cannonball metastases and are classically associated with renal cell carcinoma and choriocarcinoma.

## Urea cycle disorders

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482363/bin/Figure__1.__Urea__Cycle.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/17167.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Urea_cycle.svg/1920px-Urea_cycle.svg.png)

Urea cycle disorders are inherited enzyme defects where the liver cannot convert ammonia into urea.

Break it down:

* ammonia = toxic nitrogen waste from protein breakdown
* urea = safe water-soluble waste product that gets peed out
* urea cycle = liver pathway that turns ammonia into urea

So the core problem is:

> nitrogen waste cannot exit safely, so ammonia builds up in the blood.

This causes hyperammonemia (high ammonia in the blood).

---

### Why ammonia is dangerous

Ammonia is especially toxic to the brain.

High ammonia causes:

* poor feeding
* vomiting
* lethargy
* seizures
* cerebral edema (brain swelling)
* coma

Why?

Ammonia gets pushed into glutamine (an amino acid made from glutamate + ammonia) inside astrocytes (support cells in the brain). Too much glutamine pulls water into the cells, causing brain swelling.

---

### Classic trigger

Symptoms often happen after:

* high-protein meal
* fasting
* illness
* birth/newborn period

because catabolism (breaking down body tissue for fuel) releases amino acids, and amino acids release nitrogen.

More protein breakdown = more ammonia load.

---

### Important enzyme defects

The urea cycle enzymes include:

* CPS1 (carbamoyl phosphate synthetase 1)
* OTC (ornithine transcarbamylase)
* ASS1 (argininosuccinate synthetase 1)
* ASL (argininosuccinate lyase)
* ARG1 (arginase 1)

The big Step 1 one is:

* OTC deficiency

OTC deficiency is X-linked recessive (mostly affects males because the gene is on the X chromosome).

---

### OTC deficiency intuition

OTC normally helps combine:

* ornithine
* carbamoyl phosphate

to make citrulline.

If OTC is missing:

* carbamoyl phosphate builds up
* ammonia builds up
* citrulline decreases

Extra carbamoyl phosphate gets shunted into pyrimidine synthesis (making DNA/RNA bases), which increases orotic acid.

So OTC deficiency causes:

* hyperammonemia (high ammonia)
* increased orotic acid
* decreased blood urea nitrogen, BUN (blood urea nitrogen)

---

## One-line intuition

Urea cycle disorders are basically a blocked ammonia disposal pathway, so protein waste backs up and poisons the brain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

OTC deficiency vs orotic aciduria:

* OTC deficiency: increased orotic acid + hyperammonemia
* orotic aciduria: increased orotic acid, but no hyperammonemia

Because OTC deficiency blocks ammonia disposal, while orotic aciduria is a pyrimidine synthesis problem.

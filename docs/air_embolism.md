### Core idea

Air embolism = air bubbles enter the bloodstream and block blood flow.

Think of it like a bubble getting stuck in a pipe.

The danger depends on where the air goes:

* venous air embolism = air enters veins and travels to the right heart/lungs
* arterial air embolism = air enters arteries and can travel to brain/heart

---

### Why it is dangerous

If air enters the venous system:

* it goes to the right atrium/right ventricle
* then to the pulmonary arteries
* it can block blood flow to the lungs

This can cause:

* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* chest pain
* hypoxemia (low blood oxygen)
* hypotension (low blood pressure)
* cardiovascular collapse

Why?

Because a large air bubble can create an **air lock**, meaning the right ventricle cannot pump blood forward effectively.

---

### Arterial air embolism

Arterial air embolism is scarier because arteries feed organs directly.

If air reaches:

* brain → stroke-like symptoms, seizure, coma
* coronary arteries (heart arteries) → myocardial ischemia (low oxygen to heart muscle), arrhythmias

This can happen from lung barotrauma, procedures, or paradoxical embolism.

Paradoxical embolism = a venous embolus crosses to the arterial side through a right-to-left shunt.

Classic shunt:

* patent foramen ovale (PFO; persistent fetal opening between right and left atria)

---

### Common causes

Air can enter blood during:

* central venous catheter placement/removal
* IV (intravenous) line errors
* surgery, especially neurosurgery when veins are above the heart
* dialysis circuit problems
* trauma
* scuba diving/barotrauma

---

## One-line intuition

Air embolism is bad because air bubbles mechanically block blood flow, and if they reach the brain or heart, even small bubbles can cause ischemia.

---

## Pearl

For venous air embolism, place the patient in **left lateral decubitus** position (lying on the left side) and **Trendelenburg** position (head down) to trap air in the right atrium/right ventricle and reduce movement into the pulmonary artery.

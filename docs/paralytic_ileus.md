## Paralytic ileus

### Core idea

Paralytic ileus is when the intestines stop moving properly, but there is **no physical blockage**.

Break it down:

* **paralytic** = paralyzed / not moving
* **ileus** = failure of intestinal contents to move forward

So the bowel is basically open, but it is not pushing food, gas, or stool forward.

---

### Why it happens

Normally, the gut moves contents by **peristalsis** (wave-like smooth muscle contraction that pushes food forward).

In paralytic ileus:

* peristalsis slows or stops
* gas and fluid build up
* bowel loops become dilated
* patient gets abdominal distension, pain, nausea/vomiting, constipation, and decreased bowel sounds

The key is:

> the problem is movement, not a plugged tube.

---

### Common causes

Classically happens after:

* abdominal surgery
* opioids
* electrolyte problems, especially hypokalemia (low potassium)
* peritonitis (inflammation of the peritoneum, the lining of the abdominal cavity)
* severe illness or trauma

Why opioids cause it:

* opioids decrease acetylcholine (parasympathetic neurotransmitter that increases gut movement)
* less acetylcholine = slower gut motility
* slower motility = constipation/ileus

---

### How to distinguish from mechanical obstruction

**Mechanical obstruction** = something physically blocks the bowel.

**Paralytic ileus** = bowel is not blocked, but it is not moving.

On imaging:

* paralytic ileus: diffuse dilation of bowel, often small bowel + colon
* mechanical obstruction: dilation before the blockage, decompressed bowel after it, often a transition point

Intuition:

* obstruction = traffic jam from a roadblock
* paralytic ileus = the cars all stopped because the engines shut off

---

## One-line intuition

Paralytic ileus is a functional bowel obstruction where the intestines are open but temporarily "asleep."

# Anal fistula

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Fistula_diag_01.svg/960px-Fistula_diag_01.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/44/Diseases_of_the_rectum_and_anus-_designed_for_students_and_practitioners_of_medicine_%281910%29_%2814597987219%29.jpg)

An anal fistula is an abnormal tunnel connecting:

* the anal canal
* to the skin around the anus

Fistula = abnormal connection/tunnel between two epithelial surfaces (surfaces lined by cells, like skin or mucosa).

---

### Core idea

Usually starts with an infected anal gland.

That infection forms an abscess (pocket of pus).

The pus wants to drain, so it burrows outward toward the skin.

If that drainage tract stays open, it becomes a fistula.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Pain/swelling:** active inflammation or abscess near the anus
* **Pus or foul drainage:** the tunnel is draining infected fluid
* **Recurrent symptoms:** the outside opening can temporarily close, then pus builds up again
* **Skin irritation:** chronic drainage irritates perianal skin (skin around the anus)

---

### Important association

Think about Crohn disease.

Crohn disease causes transmural inflammation (inflammation through the full bowel wall), so it can create abnormal tracts.

That is why Crohn can cause:

* anal fistulas
* enteroenteric fistulas (bowel-to-bowel connections)
* enterovesical fistulas (bowel-to-bladder connections)

---

### Fistula vs fissure

* **Anal fissure:** a tear/cut in the anal mucosa
* **Anal fistula:** a tunnel from the anal canal to nearby skin

So:

> fissure = cut  
> fistula = tunnel

---

## One-line intuition

An anal fistula is what happens when an anal abscess drains outward and leaves behind a persistent infected tunnel.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association: recurrent perianal fistulas should make you think of Crohn disease because Crohn has transmural inflammation, which can form fistulas.

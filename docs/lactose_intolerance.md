# Lactose intolerance

![Image](https://nigms.nih.gov/sites/nigms/files/wp_content_bio/2021/06/BioBeat-6.2.2021.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/49/Lactase_mechanism.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Villi_%26_microvilli_of_small_intestine.svg/1280px-Villi_%26_microvilli_of_small_intestine.svg.png)

Lactose intolerance = inability to digest **lactose** (milk sugar) because of low **lactase** (enzyme that breaks down lactose).

Lactase normally lives on the **brush border** (microvilli surface of small intestinal cells) and splits lactose into:

* glucose
* galactose

These smaller sugars can be absorbed.

---

### Core idea

If lactase is low:

* lactose stays in the gut
* lactose pulls water into the intestine
* bacteria in the colon ferment it

So you get:

* bloating
* flatulence
* abdominal cramps
* watery diarrhea

---

### Why the symptoms happen

**Diarrhea:** undigested lactose is osmotically active, meaning it pulls water into the intestinal lumen.

**Gas/bloating:** colonic bacteria ferment lactose and produce gas, especially hydrogen.

**Cramping:** extra water + gas distend the bowel wall.

---

### Important distinction

Lactose intolerance is **not a milk allergy**.

* lactose intolerance = enzyme deficiency
* milk allergy = immune reaction to milk proteins

So lactose intolerance causes mostly GI (gastrointestinal) symptoms, not anaphylaxis (life-threatening allergic reaction).

---

### Diagnosis idea

Hydrogen breath test:

* patient drinks lactose
* bacteria ferment undigested lactose
* hydrogen rises in breath

So increased breath hydrogen suggests lactose malabsorption.

---

## One-line intuition

Lactose should be cut into absorbable sugars in the small intestine, but without lactase it reaches colon bacteria, which turn it into water-pulling diarrhea + gas.

---

## HY pearl

Lactase is a **brush border disaccharidase** (enzyme that breaks a two-sugar carbohydrate), and lactase deficiency classically causes **osmotic diarrhea** after dairy.

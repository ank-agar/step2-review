### Core idea

Bone metastases are cancer deposits in bone from a cancer that started somewhere else.

Metastasis means spread of cancer from one site to another.

So this is not usually “primary bone cancer.” It is more like:

> breast/lung/prostate/kidney/thyroid cancer cells moved into bone and started growing there.

---

### Why bone is a common target

Bone marrow has rich blood flow, so tumor cells can get trapped there easily.

Common sites:

* spine
* pelvis
* ribs
* proximal femur/humerus

These are red marrow-rich areas, so they get lots of blood supply.

---

### Lytic vs blastic

Bone metastases can be:

* **lytic:** bone is destroyed
* **blastic:** extra abnormal bone is formed
* **mixed:** both happen

Think:

* **prostate cancer:** classically osteoblastic
* **renal cell carcinoma, thyroid cancer, lung cancer:** classically osteolytic
* **breast cancer:** often mixed, but can be lytic

Osteoblastic means osteoblasts (bone-building cells) are overactive.

Osteolytic means osteoclasts (bone-resorbing cells) are overactive.

---

### Why symptoms happen

* **Bone pain:** tumor stretches/damages bone and periosteum (outer bone covering)
* **Pathologic fracture:** weakened bone breaks with minor trauma
* **Hypercalcemia:** lytic destruction releases calcium into blood
* **Spinal cord compression:** vertebral metastases can collapse or press on the cord

---

## One-line intuition

Cancer cells seed the skeleton, then either chew up bone or trigger messy new bone formation.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For USMLE (United States Medical Licensing Examination) Step 1, prostate cancer bone metastases are classically **osteoblastic**, while multiple myeloma causes **punched-out lytic bone lesions** but is not a metastasis.

## Glycogen storage disease

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459277/bin/Glycogen__Storage2.jpg)

![Image](https://www.coriell.org/0/Images/gsdmain2.gif)

![Image](https://www.aasld.org/sites/default/files/2024-05/Figure%202%20LFN%20PathPearls%205.2024.jpeg)

Glycogen storage diseases, or **GSDs (glycogen storage diseases)**, are inherited enzyme defects where the body cannot properly make or break down **glycogen (stored glucose)**.

So the basic problem is:

* glucose gets stored as glycogen
* but the body cannot access or process that glycogen normally
* glycogen builds up in tissues, especially liver and muscle

---

### Core idea

Think of glycogen as the body’s backup glucose battery.

In GSD:

> the battery is full, but the body cannot use it correctly.

That causes:

* **hypoglycemia (low blood glucose)**, especially during fasting
* **hepatomegaly (enlarged liver)** if glycogen accumulates in liver
* **muscle cramps/weakness** if glycogen metabolism is defective in skeletal muscle
* sometimes **cardiomyopathy (heart muscle disease)**, especially in Pompe disease

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Fasting hypoglycemia:** liver cannot release glucose from glycogen well
* **Big liver:** glycogen piles up inside hepatocytes (liver cells)
* **Lactic acidosis (high lactic acid):** trapped glucose-6-phosphate gets shunted into glycolysis (glucose breakdown pathway), making lactate
* **Hyperuricemia (high uric acid):** lactate competes with uric acid for renal excretion, so uric acid stays in blood
* **Muscle pain/exercise intolerance:** muscle cannot efficiently use stored glycogen for energy

---

### High-yield examples

**Von Gierke disease = GSD type I**

* glucose-6-phosphatase deficiency
* severe fasting hypoglycemia
* hepatomegaly
* lactic acidosis
* hyperuricemia
* hyperlipidemia (high blood lipids)

Intuition:

> liver gets glucose almost to the finish line, but cannot remove the phosphate to release free glucose into blood.

---

**Pompe disease = GSD type II**

* lysosomal alpha-1,4-glucosidase deficiency
* glycogen builds up in lysosomes (cellular recycling organelles)
* cardiomegaly (enlarged heart)
* hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (thickened heart muscle)

This one is also a **lysosomal storage disease (disease where material accumulates inside lysosomes)**.

---

**McArdle disease = GSD type V**

* skeletal muscle glycogen phosphorylase deficiency
* exercise intolerance
* muscle cramps
* myoglobinuria (myoglobin in urine, often from muscle breakdown)

Intuition:

> muscle has stored fuel, but cannot break it down during exercise.

---

## One-line intuition

Glycogen storage disease means stored glucose is stuck in the wrong form, so organs look “full of fuel” but the body still acts energy-starved.

---

## Pearl

The classic Step 1 triad for **von Gierke disease (GSD type I)** is:

* fasting hypoglycemia
* lactic acidosis
* hepatomegaly

because glucose-6-phosphate cannot become free glucose.

### Core idea

Obstructive uropathy = urine flow is blocked somewhere in the urinary tract.

Urine normally flows:

* kidney
* renal pelvis (funnel-like collecting area of kidney)
* ureter (tube from kidney to bladder)
* bladder
* urethra (tube from bladder to outside)

If something blocks that path, urine backs up behind the blockage.

---

### What happens upstream?

The backed-up urine increases pressure and causes dilation:

* hydronephrosis = dilation/swelling of the kidney collecting system
* hydroureter = dilation/swelling of the ureter

Think:

> blocked pipe -> fluid backs up -> the pipe and reservoir stretch.

---

### Common causes

High-yield causes include:

* kidney stone stuck in ureter
* benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH; non-cancerous enlargement of prostate)
* prostate cancer
* tumors compressing ureter
* posterior urethral valves (congenital obstructing flaps in male urethra)
* ureteropelvic junction obstruction (UPJ obstruction; blockage where renal pelvis meets ureter)

---

### Why kidney function drops

The kidney filters blood into urine, but if urine cannot drain, pressure backs up into the kidney.

That pressure compresses renal tubules and blood flow, causing:

* decreased glomerular filtration rate (GFR; how much blood the kidney filters per minute)
* acute kidney injury (AKI; sudden kidney dysfunction)
* tubular damage
* eventually cortical atrophy (thinning/loss of kidney tissue) if chronic

So the kidney is not failing because it cannot make urine at first.

It is failing because the urine has nowhere to go.

---

### Symptoms

Depends where and how fast the blockage happens:

* acute ureteral stone: severe flank pain radiating to groin
* bladder outlet obstruction: weak stream, hesitancy, urinary retention
* bilateral obstruction: low urine output and AKI
* chronic obstruction: may be painless until kidney damage is found

---

## One-line intuition

Obstructive uropathy is urinary tract blockage causing urine backup, hydronephrosis, and pressure injury to the kidney.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Bilateral hydronephrosis + AKI in an older male should make you think bladder outlet obstruction from BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia) until proven otherwise.

## Pneumoconiosis

![Image](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/sites/default/files/inline-images/How%20Lungs%20Work%20Illustration_edited.jpg)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/media/images/Petsonk-Miner-x-ray.png)

![Image](https://dlmp.uw.edu/content/page-img/367/spd-img0047__large.jpg)

Pneumoconiosis is an occupational lung disease caused by inhaling inorganic dust that gets stuck in the lungs and causes fibrosis (scarring).

Break the word apart:

* pneumo- = lung/air
* conio- = dust
* -osis = disease process

So pneumoconiosis basically means:

> dusty lung disease.

---

### Core idea

Tiny dust particles get inhaled into the alveoli (small air sacs where gas exchange happens).

Macrophages (immune cells that eat debris) try to eat the dust, but some dusts cannot be cleared well.

This causes:

* chronic inflammation
* fibroblast activation
* collagen deposition
* stiff, scarred lungs

That is why pneumoconiosis causes a restrictive lung disease pattern, meaning the lungs cannot expand well.

---

### Big types to know

* Coal workers' pneumoconiosis: coal dust
* Silicosis: silica dust from mining, sandblasting, stone cutting
* Asbestosis: asbestos fibers from insulation, shipyards, construction
* Berylliosis: beryllium exposure, can mimic sarcoidosis (granulomatous inflammatory disease)

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* Dyspnea (shortness of breath): stiff fibrotic lungs are harder to expand
* Dry cough: chronic lung irritation
* Hypoxemia (low blood oxygen): scarred alveolar walls impair gas exchange
* Pulmonary hypertension (high pressure in lung blood vessels): damaged lung tissue increases resistance to blood flow
* Cor pulmonale (right heart failure from lung disease): the right ventricle has to pump against high lung pressure

---

## One-line intuition

Pneumoconiosis is when occupational dust gets trapped in the lungs, macrophages cannot clear it, and chronic inflammation turns the lung into stiff scar tissue.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Silicosis increases the risk of tuberculosis because silica impairs macrophages, and macrophages are needed to control Mycobacterium tuberculosis (the bacteria that causes TB).

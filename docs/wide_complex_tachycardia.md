## Wide complex tachycardia

![Image](https://nl.ecgpedia.org/images/thumb/1/11/Wide_qrs_tachy_AAM1.jpg/240px-Wide_qrs_tachy_AAM1.jpg)

Wide complex tachycardia means:

* tachycardia = fast heart rhythm, usually >100 beats/min
* wide complex = wide QRS complex on ECG (electrocardiogram), usually >120 ms

QRS complex = ventricular depolarization, meaning the electrical activation of the ventricles.

So the simple definition:

> A fast rhythm where the ventricles are being activated slowly or abnormally, making the QRS look wide.

---

### Core idea

The big Step 1 idea:

* narrow QRS = signal is using the normal His-Purkinje system
* wide QRS = signal is not using the normal fast pathway normally

His-Purkinje system = the fast electrical wiring system that rapidly spreads impulses through the ventricles.

So wide QRS looks wide because ventricular activation is slow and spread-out instead of fast and coordinated.

---

### Most important cause

Wide complex tachycardia is **ventricular tachycardia** until proven otherwise.

VT (ventricular tachycardia) means the rhythm starts in the ventricles.

Why VT causes wide QRS:

* the impulse starts in ventricular muscle
* it spreads cell-to-cell through myocardium
* that is slower than the His-Purkinje system
* slow ventricular activation = wide QRS

---

### Other causes

Wide complex tachycardia can also be:

* SVT with aberrancy
* SVT with accessory pathway conduction
* hyperkalemia
* sodium channel blocker toxicity

SVT (supraventricular tachycardia) = fast rhythm starting above the ventricles.

Aberrancy = abnormal conduction through the ventricles, often because of a bundle branch block.

Accessory pathway = extra electrical pathway, like in WPW (Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome).

---

### Intuition

Think:

* fast + narrow = rhythm is probably coming from above ventricles and using normal wiring
* fast + wide = rhythm may be coming from ventricles OR using abnormal ventricular wiring

Wide means:

> the ventricles are being activated in a messy/slower way.

---

### Why it matters clinically

This matters because VT (ventricular tachycardia) can reduce cardiac output.

Cardiac output = blood pumped by the heart per minute.

If the ventricles beat too fast:

* less filling time
* weaker pumping
* hypotension
* syncope
* can degenerate into VF

VF (ventricular fibrillation) = chaotic ventricular rhythm causing cardiac arrest.

---

## One-line intuition

Wide complex tachycardia = fast rhythm with wide QRS, and on exams you should assume VT because ventricular-origin rhythms spread slowly through muscle.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In a patient with prior MI (myocardial infarction) who has wide complex tachycardia, the answer is usually **ventricular tachycardia** from a re-entry circuit around scar tissue.

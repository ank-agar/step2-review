## Essential tremor

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Cerebello-dentato-thalamo-cortical_pathway.png/960px-Cerebello-dentato-thalamo-cortical_pathway.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3b/VIM_of_thalamus_diagram.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0d/Spiral_drawing_-_essential_tremor.jpg)

Essential tremor is a common movement disorder causing a rhythmic shaking tremor, usually in the hands.

The key feature:

* worse with action/posture
* better at rest

So it shows up when the patient is:

* writing
* holding a cup
* reaching for something
* drawing a spiral

---

### Core idea

Essential tremor is basically an overactive tremor circuit involving the:

* cerebellum (movement coordination center)
* thalamus (relay station)
* motor cortex (movement command area)

The cerebellum normally smooths out movements.

If that circuit fires rhythmically, the hand gets rhythmic shaking.

---

### Why it happens

Think:

> bad movement smoothing = shaky voluntary movement

That is why essential tremor gets worse when the person tries to use the affected body part.

This is different from Parkinson disease, where the classic tremor is a resting tremor.

---

### Classic clues

Essential tremor is often:

* bilateral hand tremor
* action tremor
* family history
* worsened by anxiety, caffeine, or stress
* temporarily improved by alcohol

Bilateral means both sides.

Action tremor means tremor during voluntary movement or while holding a posture.

---

### Treatment

First-line meds:

* propranolol, a beta blocker (blocks beta-adrenergic sympathetic signaling)
* primidone, an antiseizure medication

Severe refractory cases can use deep brain stimulation (DBS, implanted electrical stimulation) targeting the VIM (ventral intermediate nucleus) of the thalamus.

---

## One-line intuition

Essential tremor is a cerebellum-thalamus-motor cortex rhythm problem that makes hands shake when they are being used.

---

## Pearl 🔥

* Parkinson disease = resting tremor, often with bradykinesia (slow movement) and rigidity

# Corneal ulcer

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Cornea.png/330px-Cornea.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK539689/bin/corneal__ulcer.jpg)

### Core idea

A corneal ulcer is an open sore/infected defect in the cornea (clear front covering of the eye).

More specifically:

* damage starts in the epithelium (outer surface layer)
* then involves the stroma (deeper structural layer)
* inflammation/infection causes a white-gray spot, pain, redness, tearing, and photophobia (light sensitivity)

---

### Why it is painful

The cornea has tons of sensory nerves.

So even a small ulcer can cause:

* severe eye pain
* foreign body sensation
* tearing
* blinking/spasm

Intuition:

> the clear “windshield” of the eye gets scratched open, then infected/inflamed.

---

### Common causes

Most are infectious:

* bacterial, especially in contact lens users
* viral, especially herpes simplex virus (HSV; virus that causes cold sores)
* fungal, classically after plant/vegetative trauma
* Acanthamoeba (free-living amoeba), classically contact lens + contaminated water

---

### Diagnosis clue

Fluorescein (yellow-green dye used to highlight corneal surface defects) stains the epithelial defect.

So on exam, the ulcer lights up because dye pools where the surface layer is missing.

---

## One-line intuition

A corneal ulcer is a corneal “crater” where the protective surface is broken and infection/inflammation can dig deeper.

---

## High-yield (HY) pearl 🔥

Contact lens-associated corneal ulcers are classically linked to Pseudomonas aeruginosa, a gram-negative rod that can rapidly damage the cornea.

## Tularemia

![Image](https://perspectivesinmedicine.cshlp.org/content/3/4/a010314/F1.large.jpg)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/tularemia/media/images/2024/05/Tularemia-home-2.jpg)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/tularemia/media/images/tularemiahires.jpg)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/ticks/media/images/PHIL_170-1.jpg)

Tularemia = infection by **Francisella tularensis**, a **gram-negative coccobacillus** (small rod-shaped bacterium).

Classic association:

* rabbits/hares/rodents
* ticks
* deer flies
* hunters or landscapers

So the nickname is:

> rabbit fever

---

### Core idea

Francisella tularensis gets into the body through:

* skin
* eyes
* mouth
* lungs

Then it survives inside macrophages (immune cells that normally eat bacteria).

That is why it is called **facultative intracellular**:

* facultative = can live inside or outside cells
* intracellular = inside cells

---

### Classic presentation

Most classic form = **ulceroglandular tularemia**.

Break it down:

* ulcer = painful skin sore at the entry site
* glandular = swollen lymph nodes

So think:

> tick bite or rabbit exposure -> skin ulcer + painful regional lymphadenopathy

Regional lymphadenopathy means swollen lymph nodes near the infected area.

---

### Why the ulcer + lymph nodes happen

The bacteria enter through a small break in skin, then move through lymphatics (small drainage channels that carry immune fluid).

So:

* local infection causes the ulcer
* lymphatic spread causes swollen painful lymph nodes

It is like the infection starts at the bite/cut, then drains to the nearest lymph node station.

---

### Other forms

Depending on route:

* oculoglandular = eye infection + lymph nodes
* oropharyngeal = throat infection after contaminated food/water
* pneumonic = lung infection after inhalation
* typhoidal = systemic illness without a clear local site

Pneumonic tularemia is especially dangerous because inhaled organisms can cause severe pneumonia.

---

### Diagnosis/treatment Step 1 vibe

Francisella tularensis:

* grows on cysteine-enriched media
* is highly infectious
* can be a bioterrorism agent because aerosolized bacteria can cause severe disease

Treatment:

* aminoglycosides such as streptomycin or gentamicin

Aminoglycosides are antibiotics that block bacterial protein synthesis.

---

## One-line intuition

Tularemia is rabbit/tick-associated Francisella infection that hides inside macrophages and classically causes a skin ulcer with painful nearby lymph nodes.

---

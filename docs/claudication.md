# Claudication

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b3/Atherosclerosis_diagram.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Pad_abi_ENG.svg/1280px-Pad_abi_ENG.svg.png)

Claudication = muscle pain with exercise that improves with rest.

Most classically, this means **intermittent claudication** from **PAD (peripheral artery disease)**.

---

### Core idea

Arteries to the leg are narrowed, usually by **atherosclerosis (cholesterol plaque buildup in an artery wall)**.

At rest, the muscle can still get enough blood.

But during walking/exercise:

* leg muscles need more oxygen
* narrowed arteries cannot increase blood flow enough
* muscle becomes ischemic

**Ischemic** = not enough blood/oxygen.

So the patient gets crampy pain, often in the calf.

Then when they stop walking:

* oxygen demand drops
* limited blood flow becomes enough again
* pain improves

---

### Classic pattern

* calf, thigh, or buttock pain with walking
* predictable distance before pain starts
* relief after a few minutes of rest
* decreased pulses may be present
* cool/shiny/hairless legs can happen in more advanced PAD

Location can hint at where the blockage is:

* calf pain = femoral/popliteal artery disease
* buttock/thigh pain = aortoiliac disease

---

### Diagnosis clue

Use **ABI (ankle-brachial index)**.

ABI = ankle systolic blood pressure / arm systolic blood pressure.

Low ABI suggests PAD because pressure is reduced distal to the arterial narrowing.

---

## One-line intuition

Claudication is like “angina of the leg”: exercise increases oxygen demand, but narrowed arteries cannot deliver enough blood.

---

## Pearl 🔥

**Leriche syndrome** = aortoiliac occlusion causing:

* buttock/thigh claudication
* impotence
* decreased femoral pulses

### Core idea

Neurogenic bladder = bladder dysfunction caused by a nerve problem.

The bladder itself may be structurally okay, but the nerve signals controlling storage and emptying are messed up.

So the patient can have:

* urinary retention
* urinary incontinence
* recurrent UTIs (urinary tract infections)
* hydronephrosis (kidney swelling from backed-up urine)

---

### Normal voiding

To pee normally:

* detrusor muscle (bladder wall muscle) contracts
* urethral sphincter (muscle that holds urine in) relaxes

So:

> bladder squeezes + outlet opens = urine leaves.

---

### What goes wrong

In neurogenic bladder, the brain/spinal cord/peripheral nerves cannot coordinate this.

Two big patterns:

* overactive bladder: detrusor contracts too easily -> urgency/incontinence
* underactive bladder: detrusor does not contract well -> retention/overflow incontinence

Sometimes the detrusor contracts while the sphincter stays closed.

That is called detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia (poor coordination between bladder squeezing and sphincter relaxation).

---

### Why kidney damage can happen

If the bladder squeezes against a closed sphincter, pressure rises.

High bladder pressure can push urine backward:

* bladder -> ureters
* ureters -> kidneys

This can cause vesicoureteral reflux (urine flowing backward from bladder to ureter/kidney), hydronephrosis, and eventually renal damage.

---

### Common causes

Think anything that damages bladder nerves:

* spinal cord injury
* spina bifida
* multiple sclerosis
* diabetes mellitus causing autonomic neuropathy (nerve damage affecting automatic body functions)
* stroke
* Parkinson disease

---

## One-line intuition

Neurogenic bladder is when the bladder and sphincter stop getting coordinated nerve instructions, so urine is either held when it should leave or leaks when it should stay.

---

## HY pearl

Spina bifida is a classic Step 1 association with neurogenic bladder, and recurrent UTIs/hydronephrosis happen because poor emptying or high bladder pressure lets urine stagnate or reflux.

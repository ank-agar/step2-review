# Hypoventilation

Hypoventilation means breathing is not moving enough air in and out of the alveoli.

Alveoli are the tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens.

So the main problem is:

* not enough CO2 (carbon dioxide) is blown off
* CO2 builds up in blood
* blood becomes acidic

This causes respiratory acidosis.

Respiratory acidosis means low blood pH because the lungs are retaining too much CO2.

---

### Core idea

Ventilation is how the lungs remove CO2.

If ventilation goes down:

* CO2 cannot leave the body well
* PaCO2 (arterial partial pressure of carbon dioxide) goes up
* CO2 combines with water to form acid
* pH goes down

Simple equation intuition:

CO2 + H2O → H2CO3 → H+ + HCO3-

H2CO3 means carbonic acid.

HCO3- means bicarbonate.

So more CO2 pushes toward more H+, and more H+ means lower pH.

---

### Hypoventilation vs hyperventilation

Hypoventilation:

* low ventilation
* CO2 retained
* PaCO2 goes up
* respiratory acidosis

Hyperventilation:

* high ventilation
* CO2 blown off
* PaCO2 goes down
* respiratory alkalosis

Alkalosis means blood pH is too high.

---

### Why oxygen can also fall

If the alveoli are not being refreshed with enough new air:

* less fresh O2 (oxygen) gets in
* less CO2 gets out

So hypoventilation usually causes:

* hypercapnia = high CO2 in blood
* hypoxemia = low O2 in blood

But the most direct clue is high PaCO2.

---

### Common causes

Anything that makes breathing too slow, too shallow, or too weak can cause hypoventilation:

* opioids or sedatives depressing the CNS (central nervous system)
* brainstem injury
* neuromuscular disease, like myasthenia gravis or ALS (amyotrophic lateral sclerosis)
* obesity hypoventilation syndrome
* severe COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease)
* chest wall restriction

The shared mechanism is inadequate alveolar ventilation.

Alveolar ventilation means the amount of fresh air actually reaching the gas-exchange alveoli.

---

### ABG pattern

ABG means arterial blood gas.

In acute hypoventilation:

* pH down
* PaCO2 up
* HCO3- only slightly up

In chronic hypoventilation:

* PaCO2 still up
* kidneys retain more HCO3-
* pH becomes closer to normal

Why?

The kidneys compensate by keeping bicarbonate, which buffers the extra acid.

---

## One-line intuition

Hypoventilation is under-breathing: you do not blow off enough CO2, so CO2 rises and causes respiratory acidosis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

High PaCO2 is the key marker of hypoventilation; low O2 alone could come from many lung problems, but CO2 retention specifically tells you ventilation is inadequate.

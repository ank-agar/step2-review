## Diabetes mellitus in pregnancy

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19724.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/2bef/6274679/1ba8bc28c702/ijms-19-03342-g002.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/2bef/6274679/9a3b1daa4b0a/ijms-19-03342-g003.jpg)

Diabetes mellitus in pregnancy means high maternal blood glucose during pregnancy.

This can be:

* pregestational diabetes mellitus = diabetes mellitus (chronic high blood sugar from impaired insulin action/production) that existed before pregnancy
* gestational diabetes mellitus, or GDM (glucose intolerance first recognized during pregnancy)

---

### Core idea

Pregnancy is naturally insulin-resistant.

Why?

The placenta makes hormones that push nutrients toward the fetus, especially:

* human placental lactogen, or hPL (placental hormone that increases maternal insulin resistance)
* progesterone
* cortisol
* placental growth hormone

These hormones make the mother use glucose less efficiently, so more glucose stays in the blood and can cross the placenta.

Normally, the mother compensates by making more insulin.

In diabetes mellitus in pregnancy, she cannot compensate enough, so maternal glucose rises.

---

### Why baby gets big

Glucose crosses the placenta.

Insulin does **not** cross the placenta.

So if mom has high glucose:

* fetal glucose rises
* fetal pancreas makes more insulin
* fetal insulin acts like a growth signal
* baby becomes macrosomic (abnormally large infant)

Think:

> Mom gives glucose, baby makes insulin, insulin makes baby grow.

---

### Main fetal/newborn risks

* macrosomia (large baby)
* shoulder dystocia (shoulder stuck during delivery)
* neonatal hypoglycemia (low newborn blood glucose)
* respiratory distress syndrome (poor newborn breathing from insufficient surfactant effect)
* congenital anomalies, especially if poor glucose control early in pregnancy

Neonatal hypoglycemia happens because after birth:

* maternal glucose supply suddenly stops
* baby still has high insulin
* glucose drops

---

### Main maternal risks

* preeclampsia (hypertension plus end-organ signs during pregnancy)
* polyhydramnios (too much amniotic fluid)
* C-section risk
* future type 2 diabetes mellitus after GDM

Polyhydramnios happens because fetal hyperglycemia causes fetal polyuria (increased urination), and fetal urine contributes to amniotic fluid.

---

## One-line intuition

Pregnancy makes mom insulin-resistant; if mom cannot increase insulin enough, extra glucose goes to the fetus, and fetal insulin drives overgrowth.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In diabetic pregnancy:

* **high fetal insulin causes macrosomia**
* **high fetal insulin also causes neonatal hypoglycemia after birth**

Same mechanism, two classic Step 1 outcomes.

### Core idea

Pelvic organ prolapse is when pelvic organs lose support and drop downward into the vagina.

Usually this involves:

* bladder
* uterus
* rectum
* small bowel

Prolapse = an organ slips or bulges out of its normal position.

---

### Why it happens

The pelvic floor (muscles and connective tissue that support pelvic organs) gets weakened or stretched.

So instead of holding organs up like a hammock, it lets them sag downward.

Common risk factors:

* vaginal delivery
* aging/menopause
* chronic constipation/straining
* chronic cough
* obesity
* connective tissue weakness

---

### Types

* **Cystocele** = bladder prolapse into anterior vaginal wall
* **Rectocele** = rectum prolapse into posterior vaginal wall
* **Uterine prolapse** = uterus descends into vagina
* **Enterocele** = small bowel prolapse into upper vagina

Anterior = front.

Posterior = back.

---

### Symptoms

Patients may feel:

* vaginal pressure/heaviness
* sensation of a bulge
* urinary retention or incontinence
* constipation or incomplete stool emptying
* worse symptoms after standing, lifting, or straining

Reasoning:

When organs bulge into the vagina, they mechanically press on nearby structures like the urethra (tube carrying urine out), bladder, or rectum.

---

## One-line intuition

Pelvic organ prolapse is basically pelvic floor "hammock failure," so the organs sag downward into the vaginal canal.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On Step 1, connect pelvic organ prolapse to damage/weakening of pelvic support structures, especially after childbirth, and remember:

* cystocele = bladder/anterior wall
* rectocele = rectum/posterior wall

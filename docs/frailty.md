### Core idea

Frailty is decreased physiologic reserve.

Physiologic reserve means the body's backup capacity to handle stress, like:

* infection
* surgery
* a fall
* dehydration
* new medications

So a frail patient may look "stable" at baseline, but a small stressor can cause a big decline.

---

### Intuition

Think of frailty as:

> less backup battery.

A healthy older adult can get pneumonia and recover back to baseline.

A frail older adult gets the same pneumonia and may develop:

* delirium
* falls
* loss of independence
* prolonged hospitalization
* inability to return to baseline

---

### What causes it

Frailty is usually multifactorial, meaning many causes add together:

* sarcopenia = loss of skeletal muscle mass and strength
* poor nutrition
* chronic inflammation
* low physical activity
* multiple chronic diseases
* polypharmacy = taking many medications
* cognitive impairment = problems with thinking/memory

The key mechanism is that multiple organ systems lose reserve at the same time.

---

### Classic Fried frailty phenotype

Fried frailty phenotype means a clinical checklist used to identify frailty.

The 5 classic features are:

* unintentional weight loss
* exhaustion
* weakness, often measured by grip strength
* slow gait speed, meaning slow walking speed
* low physical activity

Usually:

* 0 = robust
* 1-2 = pre-frail
* 3 or more = frail

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Weight loss:** poor intake + chronic inflammation + muscle breakdown
* **Weak grip:** sarcopenia reduces muscle strength
* **Slow gait:** weak muscles + poor balance + low energy
* **Exhaustion:** low reserve means normal activities feel metabolically expensive
* **Falls:** weak muscles and impaired balance make small perturbations dangerous

---

## One-line intuition

Frailty means the patient has low physiologic backup, so small medical stressors can cause major functional decline.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

Frailty is strongly linked to sarcopenia, which is loss of skeletal muscle mass and strength; resistance exercise and adequate protein intake are key protective interventions.

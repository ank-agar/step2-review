## Congenital toxoplasmosis

### Core idea

Congenital toxoplasmosis is fetal infection with **Toxoplasma gondii**, an intracellular protozoan parasite (single-celled parasite), usually from a mother who gets a new infection during pregnancy.

Classic Step 1 triad:

* chorioretinitis (inflammation/scarring of the choroid and retina in the eye)
* hydrocephalus (too much CSF, cerebrospinal fluid, in the brain ventricles)
* intracranial calcifications (calcium deposits inside the brain)

---

### How the fetus gets infected

Mom usually gets infected from:

* cat feces-contaminated soil/food/water
* undercooked meat with tissue cysts

Then the parasite can cross the placenta (organ connecting maternal and fetal blood supply) and infect the fetus.

Simple chain:

> cat feces or undercooked meat -> maternal infection -> transplacental spread -> fetal brain and eye damage

---

### Why those findings happen

Toxoplasma likes neural and eye tissue.

So in the fetus, it can damage:

* brain tissue -> calcifications and seizures
* CSF flow pathways -> hydrocephalus
* retina/choroid -> chorioretinitis and later vision problems

This is why the disease is very "brain + eye" heavy.

---

### Timing intuition

Earlier pregnancy infection is usually:

* less likely to transmit
* more severe if it does transmit

Later pregnancy infection is usually:

* more likely to transmit
* often less severe at birth

Reason: early fetal development is when organ formation is most vulnerable, but the placenta becomes easier for organisms to cross later.

---

## One-line intuition

Congenital toxoplasmosis is the cat/undercooked-meat parasite crossing the placenta and damaging the fetal brain and retina.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, distinguish congenital toxoplasmosis from congenital CMV (cytomegalovirus): toxoplasmosis classically has **diffuse intracranial calcifications**, while CMV classically has **periventricular calcifications**.

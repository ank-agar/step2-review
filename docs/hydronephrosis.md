### Core idea

Hydronephrosis = dilation/swelling of the renal pelvis and calyces because urine is backing up.

Break it down:

* hydro = water/fluid
* nephro = kidney
* -osis = condition

So it literally means:

> fluid-filled swelling of the kidney.

The key concept is not that the kidney is making too much urine.

It is that urine cannot drain normally downstream.

---

### Why it happens

Normally urine flows:

* kidney
* ureter (tube from kidney to bladder)
* bladder
* urethra (tube from bladder to outside)

If something blocks that path, pressure backs up into the kidney.

That pressure dilates the collecting system:

* renal pelvis (central urine-collecting area)
* calyces (smaller urine-collecting cups)

If severe/prolonged, the pressure can compress kidney tissue and decrease kidney function.

---

### Common causes

Think obstruction until proven otherwise:

* kidney stone stuck in the ureter
* ureteropelvic junction obstruction, or UPJ obstruction (blockage where renal pelvis meets ureter)
* benign prostatic hyperplasia, or BPH (enlarged prostate)
* tumor compressing the ureter
* pregnancy compressing the ureter
* vesicoureteral reflux, or VUR (urine flows backward from bladder toward kidney)

---

### What it can cause

Urine stasis (urine sitting still) can lead to:

* flank pain
* urinary tract infection, or UTI
* fever if infected
* kidney damage if severe or chronic

---

## One-line intuition

Hydronephrosis is a plumbing backup: urine gets blocked downstream, so the kidney's collecting system balloons upstream.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On ultrasound, hydronephrosis shows dilation of the renal pelvis/calyces; if the patient has fever + obstructed hydronephrosis, that is an emergency because infected trapped urine can rapidly cause sepsis (life-threatening systemic infection).

### Core idea

Follicular thyroid cancer is a malignant tumor from thyroid follicular cells.

Follicular cells are the thyroid cells that normally make thyroid hormone:

* T3 (triiodothyronine)
* T4 (thyroxine)

So this is a differentiated thyroid cancer, meaning it still resembles normal thyroid-type cells.

---

### The key diagnostic idea

The big question is:

> did the follicular tumor invade?

A benign follicular adenoma and follicular carcinoma can look very similar under the microscope.

What makes it cancer is invasion into:

* capsule
* blood vessels

Capsule = fibrous wall around the tumor.

Vascular invasion = tumor cells entering blood vessels.

---

### Why it spreads to bone/lung

Follicular thyroid cancer tends to spread hematogenously.

Hematogenous means through the blood.

That is why classic metastasis sites are:

* bone
* lung

This is different from papillary thyroid cancer, which more classically spreads to cervical lymph nodes (neck lymph nodes).

---

### Intuition

Think:

follicular thyroid cancer = follicular cells + invasion through capsule/blood vessels.

It is not diagnosed just because the cells look weird.

It is diagnosed because the tumor proves it can escape.

---

## One-line intuition

Follicular thyroid cancer is a thyroid follicular-cell tumor that becomes malignant by invading the capsule or blood vessels, so it likes blood-borne spread to bone and lung.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: follicular thyroid carcinoma spreads hematogenously to bone/lung, while papillary thyroid carcinoma spreads via lymphatics to cervical lymph nodes.

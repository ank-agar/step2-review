### Core idea

Medicare is the U.S. federal health insurance program mainly for:

* people age 65 or older
* younger people with certain disabilities
* people with ESRD (end-stage renal disease, permanent kidney failure needing dialysis or transplant)

Think:

> Medicare = federal insurance for elderly/disability/kidney failure.

---

### The parts

* **Part A:** hospital insurance  
  Covers inpatient hospital care, skilled nursing facility care, hospice, some home health.

* **Part B:** medical insurance  
  Covers outpatient care, doctor visits, preventive services, labs, imaging, durable medical equipment.

* **Part C:** Medicare Advantage  
  Private insurance plan that bundles Medicare benefits, usually Parts A + B and often Part D.

* **Part D:** prescription drug coverage  
  Helps pay for outpatient medications.

---

### Why the names are confusing

Original Medicare usually means:

* Part A = hospital/inpatient
* Part B = doctor/outpatient

Then people can add:

* Part D for outpatient prescription drugs
* Medigap (Medicare supplement insurance) to help cover leftover costs like copays/deductibles

Or they can choose:

* Part C/Medicare Advantage instead, which is a private-plan version of Medicare coverage.

---

### Medicare vs Medicaid

* **Medicare:** mainly based on age/disability/ESRD
* **Medicaid:** based mostly on low income

So:

> Medicare = old/disability/kidney failure  
> Medicaid = low income

Some patients can have both.

---

## One-line intuition

Medicare is government health insurance where **A = admitted**, **B = basic doctor/outpatient**, **C = combo private plan**, and **D = drugs**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY public health pearl: **Medicare is federally funded/administered, while Medicaid is jointly federal + state funded/administered**, which is why Medicaid eligibility and coverage can vary more by state.

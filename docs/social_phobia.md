### Core idea

Social phobia, also called social anxiety disorder, is intense fear of social situations where the person may be:

* judged
* embarrassed
* humiliated
* watched closely by others

Classic examples:

* public speaking
* eating in public
* meeting new people
* answering questions in class
* performing in front of others

---

### What the person is actually afraid of

Not just “being around people.”

The fear is more specifically:

> “Other people will notice something bad about me.”

Like:

* “They will see me blush.”
* “They will hear my voice shake.”
* “They will think I am stupid.”
* “I will embarrass myself.”

So the anxiety is tied to negative evaluation.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The brain treats the social situation like danger, so the sympathetic nervous system turns on.

Sympathetic nervous system = fight-or-flight system.

That causes:

* sweating
* tremor
* tachycardia (fast heart rate)
* nausea
* dry mouth
* blushing
* shaky voice

Then the person may avoid the situation.

Avoidance gives short-term relief, but it reinforces the fear long-term.

---

### How to distinguish it

* **Normal shyness:** uncomfortable, but still functional
* **Introversion:** prefers less social stimulation, but not necessarily fear-based
* **Social phobia:** fear is intense enough to cause avoidance or impairment

Impairment = it interferes with school, work, relationships, or daily life.

---

## One-line intuition

Social phobia is fear of being negatively judged in social/performance situations, so the person avoids them even when they want or need to participate.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Performance-only social anxiety can present as fear of public speaking, and beta blockers like propranolol may help physical symptoms such as tremor and tachycardia before a performance.

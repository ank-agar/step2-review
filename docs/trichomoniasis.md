### Core idea

Trichomoniasis is an STI (sexually transmitted infection) caused by **Trichomonas vaginalis**, a flagellated protozoan (single-celled parasite with whip-like tails).

It infects the urogenital tract:

* vagina/cervix in females
* urethra/prostate in males

---

### Classic findings

In females, think:

* foul-smelling green-yellow frothy discharge
* vulvar itching/burning
* dysuria (painful urination)
* dyspareunia (pain with sex)
* strawberry cervix

In males, it is often asymptomatic, but can cause urethritis (urethral inflammation).

---

### Why the findings happen

The parasite attaches to the vaginal/urogenital mucosa and causes local inflammation.

That inflammation explains:

* discharge = inflammatory fluid + organisms
* itching/burning = irritated mucosa
* dysuria = urethral irritation
* strawberry cervix = punctate cervical hemorrhages from inflammation

---

### Diagnosis

Wet mount microscopy can show **motile flagellated trophozoites**.

Trophozoite = active feeding/reproducing form of a parasite.

Key detail:

* Trichomonas has **no cyst form**
* transmission is mainly sexual

---

### Treatment

Treat with:

* metronidazole
* or tinidazole

Also treat sexual partners, because reinfection is common.

---

## One-line intuition

Trichomoniasis is a motile flagellated protozoan STI that inflames the vagina/cervix and causes frothy foul discharge.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Trichomonas vaginalis is a **protozoan**, not a bacterium, so treatment is with metronidazole/tinidazole, which also covers anaerobes (organisms that grow without oxygen).

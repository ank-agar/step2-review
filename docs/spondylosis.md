### Core idea

Spondylosis = degenerative osteoarthritis (wear-and-tear joint degeneration) of the spine.

It usually involves:

* intervertebral discs (cushions between vertebrae)
* facet joints (small joints in the back of the spine)
* vertebral endplates (top/bottom surfaces of vertebral bones)

Basically:

> the spine is aging/degenerating, so discs flatten and bone spurs form.

---

### What happens

As discs degenerate, they lose water and height.

That causes:

* narrower disc space
* more stress on facet joints
* osteophytes (bone spurs)
* possible narrowing of foramina (holes where nerve roots exit)
* possible spinal stenosis (narrowing of the spinal canal)

---

### Why symptoms happen

Many people have spondylosis on imaging with no symptoms.

But if degenerative changes compress nerves:

* neck/back pain
* stiffness
* radiculopathy (nerve root pain shooting into arm/leg)
* numbness/tingling
* weakness

If the spinal cord itself is compressed, especially in cervical spondylosis:

* myelopathy (spinal cord dysfunction)
* gait problems
* hand clumsiness
* hyperreflexia (increased reflexes)

---

### Word breakdown

* spondylo- = vertebra/spine
* -osis = abnormal condition

So spondylosis literally means an abnormal condition of the vertebrae, but clinically it usually means degenerative spine disease.

---

## One-line intuition

Spondylosis is “arthritis of the spine”: discs shrink, joints degenerate, and bone spurs can narrow spaces where nerves travel.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Do not confuse:

* **spondylosis** = degenerative spine arthritis
* **spondylolysis** = pars interarticularis defect (stress fracture in part of the vertebra)
* **spondylolisthesis** = one vertebra slips forward over another

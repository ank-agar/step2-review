### Core idea

Cardiac metastases = cancer spread to the heart from somewhere else.

Metastasis means a tumor left its original site and formed a new tumor in another location.

The cancer can involve:

* pericardium (sac around the heart)
* epicardium (outer heart surface)
* myocardium (heart muscle)
* endocardium (inner lining/chambers)
* coronary arteries (arteries supplying the heart)

---

### Most common pattern

The pericardium is the most commonly involved site.

So the classic clinical problem is:

* malignant pericardial effusion (cancer-related fluid around the heart)
* possible cardiac tamponade (heart compression from fluid, causing poor filling)

---

### How cancer reaches the heart

Main routes:

* direct invasion from nearby lung, breast, esophageal, or mediastinal tumors
* lymphatic spread through lymph vessels
* hematogenous spread through blood
* venous extension through big veins into heart chambers

Venous extension is especially associated with renal cell carcinoma (kidney cancer), which can grow up the inferior vena cava (large vein returning blood from the lower body) into the right atrium.

---

### Why symptoms happen

Symptoms depend on where the tumor lands:

* **Pericardium:** effusion or tamponade
* **Myocardium:** arrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms) or heart failure
* **Endocardium/chambers:** obstruction of blood flow or emboli (pieces breaking off and traveling)
* **Coronary arteries:** ischemia (low blood flow) or myocardial infarction (heart attack)

Many cases are silent because the heart can keep pumping until involvement becomes large or strategically placed.

---

### Common primary cancers

Think:

* lung cancer
* breast cancer
* melanoma
* lymphoma/leukemia
* renal cell carcinoma

Melanoma has a strong tendency to metastasize widely, including to the heart.

---

## One-line intuition

Cardiac metastases are usually not "heart cancer starting in the heart"; they are advanced systemic cancer landing on or inside the heart, most often causing pericardial effusion/tamponade.

---

## HY pearl

Primary cardiac tumors are rare; metastatic tumors to the heart are more common. For Step 1, if a cancer patient has dyspnea (shortness of breath), hypotension (low blood pressure), jugular venous distension (neck vein swelling), and muffled heart sounds, think malignant pericardial effusion causing cardiac tamponade.

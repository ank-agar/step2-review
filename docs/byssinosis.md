## Byssinosis

Byssinosis is an occupational lung disease from inhaling cotton, flax, or hemp dust.

Classic nickname:

* brown lung disease
* Monday fever
* cotton worker lung

---

### Core idea

Cotton dust irritates the airways and causes bronchoconstriction (narrowing of the bronchi/bronchioles, which are the air tubes in the lungs).

So it acts kind of like occupational asthma (work-related reversible airway narrowing).

---

### Classic presentation

Symptoms are worst on the first day back at work:

* chest tightness
* wheezing
* cough
* shortness of breath

Why Monday?

The worker is away from cotton dust over the weekend, then gets re-exposed on Monday, causing acute airway irritation and bronchospasm (airway smooth muscle tightening).

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Cotton dust exposure triggers airway inflammation.

That leads to:

* airway wall swelling
* mucus production
* smooth muscle contraction
* decreased airflow

Less airflow = wheezing + chest tightness.

---

### Diagnosis clue

Think:

> textile worker + cotton dust exposure + Monday chest tightness

Pulmonary function tests (PFTs, tests that measure lung airflow/volume) can show an obstructive pattern, especially decreased FEV1 (forced expiratory volume in 1 second).

---

## One-line intuition

Byssinosis is cotton dust causing asthma-like airway narrowing, classically worse when returning to work after time away.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Byssinosis is classically associated with cotton textile workers and “Monday fever,” while coal workers get black lung and silica exposure causes silicosis.

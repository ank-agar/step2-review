### Core idea

Entamoeba histolytica is a protozoan parasite (single-celled eukaryotic parasite) that causes amebiasis (infection by amoebae).

Classic disease:

* amebic dysentery (bloody diarrhea from invasive colon infection)
* liver abscess

Break the name apart:

* Entamoeba = intestinal amoeba
* histolytica = tissue-dissolving

So the name literally hints at what it does:

> an intestinal amoeba that destroys tissue.

---

### Transmission

You ingest cysts from fecally contaminated food or water.

Important forms:

* cyst = hardy infectious form that survives outside the body
* trophozoite = active invasive form that damages tissue

The cyst gets swallowed, survives stomach acid, then releases trophozoites in the intestine.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Trophozoites invade the colon mucosa (inner lining of the colon).

That causes:

* bloody diarrhea because the mucosa gets ulcerated and bleeds
* abdominal pain because the colon is inflamed
* flask-shaped ulcers because the parasite burrows under the mucosal surface, making a narrow opening with a wider deep base

The trophozoites can also enter portal blood flow (blood draining from intestines to liver), so they can spread to the liver.

That causes:

* liver abscess
* RUQ pain (right upper quadrant abdominal pain)
* fever
* "anchovy paste" pus, classically

---

### Lab clue

On stool microscopy, E. histolytica trophozoites can contain ingested RBCs (red blood cells).

That is the big clue that it is invasive.

Why?

Because it is literally eating host RBCs after damaging the intestinal wall.

---

### Treatment idea

Use:

* metronidazole for invasive tissue disease
* plus paromomycin or iodoquinol to clear cysts from the intestinal lumen (inside space of the gut)

Why both?

Metronidazole kills invasive trophozoites well, but you still need a luminal agent (drug that stays in the gut lumen) to remove remaining cysts.

---

## One-line intuition

E. histolytica is a fecal-oral parasite where cysts are swallowed, trophozoites invade the colon, eat RBCs, and can travel to the liver.

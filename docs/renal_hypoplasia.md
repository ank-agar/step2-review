# Renal hypoplasia

![Image](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/ncbddd/birthdefects/surveillancemanual/quick-reference-handbook/renal-agenesis-hypoplasia_html_files/Fig.-33.-Renal-agenesis-hypoplasia.jpg)

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/11236/B2-Image03-Kidney%2BNephron-FINAL_thumbnail.jpg)

### Core idea

Renal hypoplasia = a kidney that is congenitally small.

Congenital means present from birth.

The key is:

* the kidney is small
* the tissue is organized normally
* there are fewer nephrons

Nephrons are the kidney's filtering units.

So renal hypoplasia is basically:

> a small-but-otherwise-normal kidney with too few filters.

---

### Why the findings happen

Because the kidney did not fully grow during development:

* fewer nephrons means less filtering capacity
* less filtering capacity can cause chronic kidney disease
* if one kidney is hypoplastic, the other kidney may enlarge to compensate
* if both kidneys are hypoplastic, kidney failure is more likely

Compensate means the healthy kidney works harder and often gets bigger.

---

### Important distinction

Do not confuse:

* renal hypoplasia = small kidney, normal organization
* renal dysplasia = disorganized abnormal kidney tissue
* renal agenesis = kidney is absent

Hypoplasia means underdevelopment.

Dysplasia means abnormal development.

Agenesis means failure to form.

---

## One-line intuition

Renal hypoplasia is when the kidney was built with too few nephrons, so it is small and has reduced filtering reserve.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: renal hypoplasia can cause hypertension because fewer nephrons increase pressure/workload in the remaining nephrons, eventually activating RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system).

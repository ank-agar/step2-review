## Cranial nerve injury

Cranial nerve injury means damage to one of the 12 cranial nerves (nerves that come directly from the brain or brainstem).

These nerves control things like:

* smell
* vision
* eye movement
* facial sensation
* facial movement
* hearing/balance
* swallowing/voice
* tongue movement

So the symptoms depend on which cranial nerve is injured.

---

### Core idea

Think:

> cranial nerve injury = a broken wire between the brainstem and a specific head/neck function.

So if the wire to the face is damaged, facial movement is weak.

If the wire to the tongue is damaged, the tongue moves abnormally.

If the wire to the eye muscles is damaged, eye movement is abnormal.

---

### Common examples

* **CN III (oculomotor nerve):** eye looks "down and out," ptosis (droopy eyelid), dilated pupil
* **CN V (trigeminal nerve):** decreased facial sensation, weak chewing
* **CN VII (facial nerve):** facial paralysis, cannot close eye well
* **CN VIII (vestibulocochlear nerve):** hearing loss, vertigo (spinning sensation)
* **CN X (vagus nerve):** hoarseness, dysphagia (difficulty swallowing), uvula deviation
* **CN XII (hypoglossal nerve):** tongue deviation

---

### Why the findings happen

Each cranial nerve has a specific job.

So injury causes loss of that job:

* motor nerve injury -> weakness/paralysis
* sensory nerve injury -> numbness or sensory loss
* parasympathetic nerve injury -> pupil/gland problems
* vestibular nerve injury -> balance/vertigo problems

Example:

CN XII (hypoglossal nerve) controls tongue muscles.

If one side is damaged, that side becomes weak, so the normal side pushes the tongue toward the weak side.

So:

> tongue deviates toward the lesion in a lower motor neuron CN XII injury.

---

### Causes

Common causes include:

* trauma
* tumors compressing nerves
* diabetes causing ischemic nerve injury
* aneurysm compression
* stroke
* infection/inflammation
* increased ICP (intracranial pressure; high pressure inside the skull)

---

## One-line intuition

Cranial nerve injury gives a focal head/neck deficit because each cranial nerve is a specific cable for a specific function.

---

## Pearl 🔥

A CN III palsy with a dilated pupil is concerning for a posterior communicating artery aneurysm compressing CN III, because the parasympathetic fibers that constrict the pupil run superficially on the nerve.

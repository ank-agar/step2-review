## Silicosis

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK592821/bin/ch2f12.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK555902/bin/Eggshell__calcification.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK594245/bin/silicosis.jpg)

Silicosis is a pneumoconiosis (lung disease from inhaled dust) caused by inhaling crystalline silica dust.

Classic exposures:

* sandblasting
* mining
* stone cutting
* countertop fabrication
* construction/concrete drilling

---

### Core idea

Silica dust gets deep into alveoli (tiny air sacs), where macrophages (immune cells that eat debris) try to swallow it.

But silica is toxic to macrophages, so they get damaged and release inflammatory signals.

That causes:

* chronic inflammation
* fibroblast activation
* collagen deposition
* lung scarring/fibrosis

So the lung becomes stiff.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Dyspnea (shortness of breath):** stiff fibrotic lungs cannot expand well
* **Restrictive lung disease:** fibrosis reduces lung compliance (stretchiness)
* **Upper-lobe nodules:** inhaled particles tend to deposit in upper lung zones
* **Eggshell calcification:** hilar lymph nodes (lymph nodes near the lung roots) calcify around the edges
* **Progressive massive fibrosis:** small nodules can merge into large scarred masses

---

### Histology

Classic finding:

* silicotic nodules with whorled collagen

Think:

> silica irritates macrophages → macrophages call in fibrosis → collagen nodules form.

---

### Big association

Silicosis increases risk of tuberculosis, also called TB (infection by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*).

Why?

Macrophages are important for controlling TB, and silica damages macrophages.

---

## One-line intuition

Silicosis is “rock dust gets eaten by macrophages, macrophages die angry, and the lung scars down.”

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, the buzzword is:

* **upper-lobe fibrosis + eggshell calcification of hilar lymph nodes**

Also remember: silica exposure increases TB risk.

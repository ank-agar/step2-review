### Core idea

Bacterial vaginosis, or **BV (bacterial vaginosis)**, is when the normal vaginal bacteria balance shifts:

* decreased **Lactobacillus** bacteria
* increased anaerobic bacteria, especially **Gardnerella vaginalis**

Anaerobic bacteria = bacteria that grow better in low-oxygen environments.

---

### Why symptoms happen

Normally, **Lactobacillus** makes lactic acid, which keeps vaginal **pH (acidity level)** low.

In BV:

* less Lactobacillus → less acid
* vaginal pH rises above 4.5
* anaerobes overgrow
* amines are produced

Amines = smelly nitrogen-containing compounds.

That is why BV classically causes:

* thin gray-white discharge
* fishy odor
* positive whiff test after adding **KOH (potassium hydroxide)**

---

### Microscopy finding

Classic finding:

* **clue cells**

Clue cells = vaginal epithelial cells covered with bacteria, making the cell borders look fuzzy.

They are called “clue” cells because they are a clue that BV is present.

---

## One-line intuition

BV is not one single invading pathogen; it is a vaginal microbiome imbalance where protective Lactobacillus drops and anaerobes take over.

---

## Pearl 🔥

BV is associated with **preterm labor** and increased risk of acquiring **STIs (sexually transmitted infections)**, and treatment is usually **metronidazole** or **clindamycin**.

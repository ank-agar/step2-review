### Core idea

A febrile seizure is a seizure triggered by fever in a young child.

Usually:

* age 6 months to 5 years
* fever present
* no CNS infection
* no electrolyte/metabolic cause
* no prior afebrile seizure

CNS = central nervous system, meaning brain and spinal cord.

Afebrile = without fever.

---

### Why fever causes it

Fever lowers the seizure threshold.

Seizure threshold = how easily brain neurons fire abnormally.

Young kids have immature brains, so their neurons are more excitable. When fever rises, especially early in an illness, that extra excitability can push the brain into a generalized seizure.

Generalized = both sides of the brain are involved.

---

### Simple vs complex

Simple febrile seizure:

* generalized
* lasts less than 15 minutes
* happens once in 24 hours
* child returns to baseline after

Complex febrile seizure:

* focal
* lasts 15 minutes or more
* repeats within 24 hours

Focal = starts in one specific brain area, so symptoms may affect one side/body part more.

---

### One-line intuition

Fever temporarily makes a young child's already-excitable brain more likely to seize.

---

### What Step 1 wants

Simple febrile seizures are usually benign.

Benign = not dangerous long-term in most cases.

They do not usually mean the child has epilepsy.

Epilepsy = tendency to have recurrent unprovoked seizures.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Simple febrile seizure = generalized, less than 15 minutes, once in 24 hours.

If it is focal, prolonged, or recurrent in 24 hours, think complex febrile seizure.

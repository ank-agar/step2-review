### Core idea

Acute myocardial infarction, or AMI (acute myocardial infarction), means:

* acute = sudden
* myocardial = heart muscle
* infarction = tissue death from lack of blood flow

So AMI is basically:

> sudden death of heart muscle because a coronary artery is blocked.

Coronary arteries are the blood vessels that feed the heart muscle itself.

---

### What usually causes it

Most AMIs happen when an atherosclerotic plaque (cholesterol-rich buildup in an artery wall) ruptures.

That rupture makes the body think there is an injury, so platelets and clotting factors rush in and form a thrombus (blood clot).

If the clot blocks the coronary artery:

* blood cannot reach part of the myocardium (heart muscle)
* oxygen delivery drops
* cells switch to anaerobic metabolism (energy production without oxygen)
* ATP (cell energy) falls
* heart muscle cells die

That dead heart muscle is the infarct.

---

### Why chest pain happens

The heart muscle is starving for oxygen.

Ischemia (low blood flow causing low oxygen) triggers pain fibers, causing classic:

* crushing substernal chest pain
* radiation to left arm/jaw
* sweating
* nausea
* shortness of breath

Substernal means under the sternum (breastbone).

---

### STEMI vs NSTEMI

Two big testable categories:

* STEMI (ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction)
* NSTEMI (non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction)

STEMI usually means a complete coronary artery blockage causing full-thickness, or transmural, myocardial injury.

NSTEMI usually means partial blockage causing subendocardial injury, meaning injury mainly in the inner layer of the heart wall.

Why the inner layer first?

The subendocardium (inner heart muscle layer) is farthest from the coronary blood supply and gets squeezed hardest during contraction, so it is most vulnerable to low oxygen.

---

### Key lab marker

Troponin rises.

Troponin is a cardiac muscle protein released into blood when heart muscle cells die.

So:

* chest pain + ECG (electrocardiogram) changes + elevated troponin = myocardial infarction pattern

ECG means electrocardiogram, the test that records the heart's electrical activity.

---

## One-line intuition

Acute myocardial infarction = a coronary artery suddenly clots off, so downstream heart muscle loses oxygen and dies.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Post-MI (post-myocardial infarction) complications follow a timeline:

* 1-3 days: fibrinous pericarditis (inflammation of the heart sac)
* 3-7 days: free wall rupture, papillary muscle rupture, or ventricular septal rupture
* weeks later: ventricular aneurysm or Dressler syndrome (autoimmune pericarditis after myocardial injury)

The most dangerous rupture window is about 3-7 days because macrophages have digested dead tissue, but scar tissue has not formed yet.

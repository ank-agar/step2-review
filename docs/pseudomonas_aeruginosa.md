### Core idea

Pseudomonas aeruginosa is a **Gram-negative rod** that loves wet environments and mainly infects sick/vulnerable patients.

Think:

* hospitals
* ventilators
* catheters
* burns
* cystic fibrosis
* immunocompromised patients

---

### Classic features

Pseudomonas is:

* **Gram-negative rod**
* **oxidase positive**
* **non-lactose fermenter**
* **aerobic** = needs oxygen
* has a **blue-green pigment** called pyocyanin
* has a fruity/grape-like odor

The blue-green color happens because pyocyanin is a pigment made by the bacteria.

---

### Why it is dangerous

It is an opportunist, meaning it usually waits for weak defenses.

It causes:

* pneumonia, especially in cystic fibrosis
* burn wound infections
* urinary tract infections from catheters
* hot tub folliculitis
* malignant otitis externa in diabetics
* sepsis with ecthyma gangrenosum

Ecthyma gangrenosum = necrotic skin ulcers from vascular invasion during severe infection.

---

### Main virulence idea

Pseudomonas is hard to kill because it has:

* biofilms = sticky bacterial communities that block immune cells and antibiotics
* endotoxin LPS (lipopolysaccharide) = Gram-negative outer membrane toxin
* exotoxin A = toxin that inhibits protein synthesis
* elastase = enzyme that destroys tissue proteins

Exotoxin A works like diphtheria toxin:

* ADP-ribosylates EF-2
* EF-2 = elongation factor 2, needed for protein translation
* no EF-2 = no protein synthesis
* no protein synthesis = cell death

---

## One-line intuition

Pseudomonas is the wet-environment, hospital-associated, blue-green, biofilm-forming Gram-negative rod that attacks vulnerable patients.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember: **Pseudomonas + cystic fibrosis = chronic pneumonia due to biofilm formation**, especially with mucoid strains that produce alginate.

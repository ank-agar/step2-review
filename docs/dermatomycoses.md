## Dermatomycoses

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/ringworm/media/images/347136-A_MDB_Clean_Slate_Images_ringworm_symptoms.jpg)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/ringworm/media/images/RingowrmLocations.jpg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil///PHIL_Images/17784/17784_lores.jpg)

Dermatomycoses = fungal infections of the skin.

Break it down:

* dermato- = skin
* mycosis = fungal infection

So dermatomycoses literally means:

> fungal skin infections.

Common examples:

* tinea corporis = ringworm of the body
* tinea pedis = athlete foot
* tinea cruris = jock itch
* tinea capitis = scalp ringworm
* onychomycosis = fungal nail infection

The most classic cause is dermatophytes (fungi that love keratin), especially:

* Trichophyton
* Microsporum
* Epidermophyton

Keratin is the tough structural protein in:

* skin stratum corneum
* hair
* nails

That is why these fungi usually stay superficial instead of invading deep tissue.

---

### Why it looks like a ring

The fungus grows outward along the superficial skin.

The center may start clearing because the immune system and skin shedding catch up there, while the active fungal growth is at the edge.

So you get:

* raised scaly border
* central clearing
* itch
* circular/ring-like lesion

That is why it is called ringworm, even though it is not a worm.

---

### Diagnosis

Classic test:

* KOH prep (potassium hydroxide preparation)

KOH dissolves skin cells but leaves fungal hyphae (branching fungal filaments), so you can see them under the microscope.

---

## One-line intuition

Dermatomycoses are fungi eating keratin on the surface of skin, hair, or nails, causing scaly itchy rashes that often spread outward in rings.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Do not treat suspected tinea with topical steroids alone: steroids can reduce inflammation but let the fungus spread, causing tinea incognito (masked/worsened fungal infection).

## Hereditary spherocytosis

![Image](https://haematologica.org/article/download/4984/20466/21077)

### Core idea

Hereditary spherocytosis is an inherited RBC (red blood cell) membrane skeleton problem.

The RBC loses membrane, so instead of being a flexible biconcave disc, it becomes a small round sphere.

The main defective proteins are:

* spectrin
* ankyrin
* band 3
* protein 4.2

These proteins normally act like the RBC's internal scaffolding.

---

### Why the findings happen

Normal RBCs are squishy discs, so they can squeeze through the spleen.

In hereditary spherocytosis:

* membrane skeleton is weak
* pieces of membrane are lost
* the RBC becomes round
* round RBCs are less deformable
* the spleen traps and destroys them

So this causes extravascular hemolysis (RBC destruction by macrophages outside the blood vessels, mainly in the spleen).

---

### Classic findings

* anemia
* jaundice from increased unconjugated bilirubin
* splenomegaly
* pigment gallstones
* spherocytes on blood smear
* increased MCHC (mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration)
* increased osmotic fragility

Spherocytes look small, dense, and lack central pallor (the pale center normally seen in RBCs).

---

### Why osmotic fragility increases

Osmotic fragility means RBCs burst more easily in hypotonic solution (watery solution with lower solute concentration).

Why?

Spherocytes already have less membrane surface area compared with their volume.

So when water enters, they cannot stretch much before bursting.

---

### Key distinction

Hereditary spherocytosis and autoimmune hemolytic anemia can both show spherocytes.

Difference:

* hereditary spherocytosis: Coombs test negative
* autoimmune hemolytic anemia: Coombs test positive

Coombs test means direct antiglobulin test (test for antibodies or complement stuck to RBCs).

---

## One-line intuition

The RBC membrane skeleton is broken, so RBCs become round, get stuck in the spleen, and are eaten.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Splenectomy improves anemia because it removes the main site of RBC destruction, but the spherocytes are still present because the membrane defect is genetic.

# Vitamin K deficiency

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Vitamin-k-zyklus.svg/1920px-Vitamin-k-zyklus.svg.png)

![Image](https://open.oregonstate.education/app/uploads/sites/289/2019/07/1909_Blood_Clotting.jpg)

### Core idea

Vitamin K deficiency means the liver cannot properly activate clotting factors.

Vitamin K is needed for gamma-carboxylation, which means adding a carboxyl group so clotting factors can bind calcium and work correctly.

The vitamin K-dependent clotting factors are:

* factor II
* factor VII
* factor IX
* factor X
* protein C
* protein S

Easy memory:

> 1972 + C + S

---

### Why bleeding happens

Without vitamin K, the liver still makes clotting factors, but they are underactive.

So the problem is not platelet plugging. The problem is secondary hemostasis, meaning fibrin clot formation.

That causes:

* easy bruising
* mucosal bleeding
* GI bleeding (gastrointestinal bleeding)
* oozing from wounds or IV (intravenous) sites
* severe newborn bleeding, including intracranial hemorrhage (bleeding inside the skull)

---

### Lab pattern

Classically:

* increased PT (prothrombin time)
* increased INR (international normalized ratio)
* normal bleeding time
* normal platelet count

Why PT first?

Factor VII has the shortest half-life, and PT measures the extrinsic pathway, which depends on factor VII.

So early vitamin K deficiency looks like:

> prolonged PT first.

---

### Causes

Think of anything that lowers vitamin K intake, absorption, or recycling:

* newborn state
* malabsorption, especially fat malabsorption
* prolonged antibiotics, because gut bacteria help make vitamin K
* liver disease
* warfarin, which blocks vitamin K epoxide reductase

Vitamin K is fat-soluble, meaning it needs bile and fat absorption to be absorbed well.

So biliary obstruction or pancreatic insufficiency can also cause deficiency.

---

### Newborn connection

Newborns are high risk because:

* low vitamin K crosses the placenta
* breast milk has relatively low vitamin K
* newborn gut bacteria are not established yet

That is why newborns get vitamin K prophylaxis, meaning preventive vitamin K after birth.

---

## One-line intuition

Vitamin K deficiency = clotting factors II, VII, IX, X, protein C, and protein S cannot bind calcium well, so fibrin clots form poorly and bleeding risk goes up.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Vitamin K deficiency and warfarin both prolong PT/INR first because factor VII drops fastest.

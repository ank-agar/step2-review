## Substance-induced psychotic disorder

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d8/Dopaminergic_pathways.svg)

![Image](https://nida.nih.gov/sites/default/files/images/mind_matters_brain.png)

![Image](https://nida.nih.gov/sites/default/files/images/soa_brain_cell_communication.jpg)

Substance-induced psychotic disorder means:

* hallucinations (perceiving something that is not there)
* delusions (fixed false beliefs)

that happen during or soon after substance intoxication (being affected by a drug), withdrawal (body reaction after stopping/reducing a drug), or medication exposure.

---

### Core idea

The drug temporarily pushes brain signaling into a psychosis-like state.

Big offenders:

* amphetamines/cocaine: increase dopamine (reward/threat-salience neurotransmitter)
* cannabis: can trigger psychosis in vulnerable people
* PCP (phencyclidine) or ketamine: block NMDA receptors (glutamate receptors important for normal cognition)
* hallucinogens: alter serotonin signaling
* alcohol withdrawal: can cause hallucinosis or delirium tremens (severe alcohol withdrawal with confusion, autonomic instability, and hallucinations)

---

### Why psychosis happens

Psychosis often comes from the brain assigning too much importance to random stimuli.

High dopamine in the mesolimbic pathway (reward/emotion pathway from the ventral tegmental area to nucleus accumbens) can make ordinary things feel intensely meaningful or threatening.

So the patient may develop:

* paranoia: "people are after me"
* hallucinations: hearing voices
* delusions: fixed false beliefs despite evidence

Intuition:

> the drug turns the brain's "pattern detector" and "threat detector" up too high.

---

### How to distinguish from schizophrenia

Substance-induced psychotic disorder is tied closely to drug use or withdrawal.

Primary psychotic disorder, like schizophrenia, is more likely if:

* psychosis started before substance use
* symptoms persist long after the drug is gone
* there are prominent negative symptoms (reduced emotion, speech, motivation)
* there is a strong family history

---

## One-line intuition

A substance directly disrupts neurotransmitters, so the patient has hallucinations/delusions that are temporally linked to intoxication or withdrawal.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, stimulants like amphetamines and cocaine can cause psychosis because they increase dopamine, and antipsychotics work mainly by blocking dopamine D2 receptors (dopamine receptor subtype 2).

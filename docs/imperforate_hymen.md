## Imperforate hymen

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Imperforate_hymen_svg_hariadhi.svg/960px-Imperforate_hymen_svg_hariadhi.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/98/Gynaecology_for_students_and_practitioners_%281916%29_%2814781000142%29.jpg)

Imperforate hymen = the hymen completely covers the vaginal opening.

So menstrual blood cannot leave the body.

Break it down:

* imperforate = no opening
* hymen = thin tissue at the vaginal opening

---

### Core idea

Normally, the hymen has an opening.

In imperforate hymen, there is no hole, so the vagina becomes a blocked tube.

Once puberty starts and menstrual blood is produced, the blood gets trapped behind the hymen.

This causes:

* primary amenorrhea (no first period by expected age)
* cyclic pelvic pain (monthly crampy pain)
* hematocolpos (blood trapped in the vagina)
* sometimes hematometra (blood trapped in the uterus)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **No visible menstrual bleeding:** blood is made, but the exit is blocked
* **Cyclic pelvic pain:** the uterus still contracts each month
* **Bulging blue hymen:** old trapped blood distends the hymen outward
* **Urinary retention:** the swollen, blood-filled vagina can compress the urethra (tube that carries urine out)
* **Constipation/back pain:** mass effect from the enlarged vagina/uterus

---

### One-line intuition

The patient is menstruating internally, but the outflow tract is blocked at the hymen.

---

### Classic presentation

Teenage girl with:

* normal secondary sex characteristics
* primary amenorrhea
* monthly pelvic pain
* bulging bluish membrane at the vaginal opening

Normal secondary sex characteristics matter because estrogen is present, so the ovaries are working.

The problem is not hormone production.

The problem is outflow obstruction.

---

### Treatment

Hymenotomy or hymenectomy (surgical opening/removal of hymenal tissue) lets the trapped blood drain.

---

## HY pearl

Imperforate hymen causes **primary amenorrhea with cyclic pelvic pain**, while androgen insensitivity syndrome causes **primary amenorrhea without uterus** and with scant/absent pubic hair due to androgen receptor resistance.

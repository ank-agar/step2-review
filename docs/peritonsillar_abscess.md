### Core idea

A peritonsillar abscess is a pocket of pus next to the tonsil.

Break it down:

* peri- = around
* tonsillar = tonsil-related
* abscess = pus collection from infection

So:

> infection spreads around the tonsil and forms a pus pocket.

---

### What it looks like clinically

Classic findings:

* severe sore throat, usually worse on one side
* fever
* dysphagia (trouble swallowing)
* odynophagia (painful swallowing)
* muffled "hot potato" voice
* trismus (difficulty opening the mouth because jaw muscles spasm)
* uvula deviation away from the abscess

The uvula is the little dangling structure in the back of the throat.

---

### Why the uvula deviates

The abscess forms beside one tonsil and pushes inward toward the middle of the throat.

That mass effect shoves the uvula to the opposite side.

So if the abscess is on the right:

* right tonsillar area bulges
* uvula gets pushed left

---

### Why the voice sounds weird

The swollen tonsillar area narrows the oropharynx (back part of the mouth/throat).

The patient talks like there is something bulky in the back of the mouth, hence:

* "hot potato" voice

---

### Treatment idea

Usually:

* drainage of the pus
* antibiotics

Antibiotics alone may not be enough if there is a true abscess, because pus collections have poor antibiotic penetration.

---

## One-line intuition

Peritonsillar abscess = a tonsil infection that walled off into pus and physically pushes the uvula away.

---

## Pearl

Peritonsillar abscess is a complication of tonsillitis and is classically associated with uvula deviation away from the affected side.

### Core idea

Hyponatremia means low serum sodium concentration, usually:

* Na+ less than 135 mEq/L

But the key idea is:

> Hyponatremia is usually too much water relative to sodium, not always too little sodium.

So the blood becomes more dilute.

---

### Why symptoms happen

Sodium is the main extracellular osmole.

Extracellular means outside cells.

Osmole means a particle that pulls water.

If sodium is low outside the cell:

* extracellular fluid becomes hypotonic
* hypotonic means less concentrated
* water moves into cells
* brain cells swell

That is why hyponatremia causes neurologic symptoms:

* headache
* nausea/vomiting
* confusion
* seizures
* coma

The brain is the big problem because the skull is a fixed box, so swelling has nowhere to go.

---

### Acute vs chronic

Acute hyponatremia means sodium drops quickly.

The brain has no time to adapt, so water rushes in and causes cerebral edema.

Cerebral edema means brain swelling.

Chronic hyponatremia means sodium has been low for longer, usually more than 48 hours.

The brain adapts by dumping osmoles:

* Na+ = sodium
* K+ = potassium
* Cl- = chloride
* organic osmolytes = small molecules that hold water inside cells

This lowers the pull of water into brain cells, so symptoms may be milder.

---

### Why correcting too fast is dangerous

In chronic hyponatremia, the brain has already dumped osmoles.

If you rapidly raise the serum sodium, the outside suddenly becomes much more concentrated.

Then water gets pulled out of brain cells too fast.

This can cause osmotic demyelination syndrome.

Osmotic demyelination syndrome means damage to myelin from a rapid osmotic shift.

Myelin means the fatty insulation around nerves.

Classically this affects the pons, called central pontine myelinolysis.

Pons means part of the brainstem.

---

### Common causes

Think of hyponatremia as excess ADH effect until proven otherwise.

ADH means antidiuretic hormone, which makes the kidney reabsorb water.

Common causes:

* SIADH (syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion)
* heart failure
* cirrhosis
* kidney failure
* thiazide diuretics
* adrenal insufficiency
* primary polydipsia

Primary polydipsia means drinking so much water that the kidney cannot excrete it fast enough.

---

## One-line intuition

Hyponatremia makes the blood too watery, so water enters brain cells and causes swelling.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Do not rapidly correct chronic hyponatremia because it can cause osmotic demyelination syndrome, classically central pontine myelinolysis.

### Core idea

**Taenia** is a genus of tapeworms.

The big Step 1 ones are:

* **Taenia solium** = pork tapeworm
* **Taenia saginata** = beef tapeworm

They cause **taeniasis** (intestinal tapeworm infection).

---

### How infection happens

Humans eat undercooked meat containing **cysticerci** (larval cysts):

* pork → Taenia solium
* beef → Taenia saginata

Then the worm attaches to the small intestine using its **scolex** (head/attachment organ).

It grows into a long segmented worm made of **proglottids** (tapeworm body segments that contain reproductive organs/eggs).

---

### Why Taenia solium is scarier

Taenia saginata usually just causes intestinal disease.

Taenia solium can do two different things:

* eating undercooked pork with cysticerci → adult worm in intestine
* ingesting Taenia solium eggs → **cysticercosis** (larvae invade tissues)

If larvae go to the brain, that is **neurocysticercosis** (cysticercosis involving the central nervous system, meaning brain/spinal cord).

That can cause:

* seizures
* headaches
* hydrocephalus (too much cerebrospinal fluid in the brain ventricles)

---

### Key difference

Taenia solium has a scolex with:

* suckers
* hooks

Taenia saginata has:

* suckers
* no hooks

So pork tapeworm is the one with the more aggressive “hooked” head.

---

## One-line intuition

Taenia is a segmented intestinal tapeworm, but **Taenia solium is dangerous because its eggs can turn humans into the intermediate host and form cysts in the brain**.

---

## High-yield pearl

For Step 1: **Taenia solium eggs → cysticercosis/neurocysticercosis**, but **Taenia solium cysticerci in pork → intestinal taeniasis**.

Eggs are the dangerous brain-cyst route.

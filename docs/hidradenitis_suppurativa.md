### Core idea

Hidradenitis suppurativa, or HS (hidradenitis suppurativa), is a chronic inflammatory disease of hair follicles in skin-fold areas.

Common locations:

* axillae (armpits)
* groin
* perineum (area between genitals and anus)
* buttocks
* inframammary folds (under the breasts)

It causes recurrent painful:

* nodules (firm bumps)
* abscesses (pus-filled collections)
* draining sinus tracts (tunnels under the skin that connect to the surface)
* scarring

---

### Why it happens

The key problem is follicular occlusion (blocking of the hair follicle).

Think:

> hair follicle gets plugged -> follicle swells -> follicle ruptures -> immune system reacts strongly -> abscesses and tunnels form.

So despite the name, HS is not mainly a sweat gland infection.

The pus happens because the ruptured follicle triggers inflammation and neutrophils (acute inflammatory white blood cells) pile in.

The tunnels happen because repeated inflammation digs channels under the skin.

The scarring happens because the body keeps trying to heal the same inflamed area over and over.

---

### Why skin folds are affected

HS likes areas with:

* friction
* hair follicles
* apocrine glands (scent-type sweat glands found in armpits/groin)
* moisture

Friction + follicle plugging makes rupture and inflammation more likely.

---

### Risk factors

Associated with:

* smoking
* obesity
* family history
* female sex
* puberty/post-puberty timing
* inflammatory bowel disease, or IBD (inflammatory bowel disease)

---

### Classic clinical clue

Recurrent painful "boils" in the armpit or groin that drain and scar.

If the question says:

* recurrent abscesses
* axilla/groin
* sinus tracts
* scarring

think HS.

---

## One-line intuition

HS is like severe, recurrent follicle blockage in skin folds, where ruptured follicles cause chronic abscesses, tunnels, and scars.

---

## Pearl

HS is part of the follicular occlusion tetrad (four related follicle-blocking diseases):

* hidradenitis suppurativa
* acne conglobata (severe nodular acne)
* dissecting cellulitis of the scalp (deep inflammatory scalp follicle disease)
* pilonidal disease (hair-containing sinus near the gluteal cleft)

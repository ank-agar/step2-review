# Gas exchange

Gas exchange = movement of oxygen and carbon dioxide between air and blood.

In the lungs, this happens mainly in the alveoli (tiny air sacs):

* O2 (oxygen gas) moves from alveoli into pulmonary capillary blood
* CO2 (carbon dioxide) moves from pulmonary capillary blood into alveoli

Then you exhale the CO2.

---

### Core idea

Gas moves down its partial pressure gradient.

Partial pressure = how much "push" a specific gas has in a mixture of gases.

So:

* alveoli have high O2 compared with deoxygenated blood, so O2 enters blood
* blood has high CO2 compared with alveoli, so CO2 enters alveoli

No ATP (adenosine triphosphate, cellular energy currency) is needed.

---

### Why alveoli are good at this

Alveoli are built for fast diffusion (movement from high concentration/pressure to low concentration/pressure):

* huge surface area
* very thin wall
* wrapped in capillaries
* moist surface so gases can dissolve

Think:

> thin wall + lots of surface area + lots of blood flow = fast gas exchange.

---

### Why disease causes low oxygen

Anything that blocks diffusion or separates air from blood can impair gas exchange.

Examples:

* pulmonary edema (fluid in lungs): fluid increases diffusion distance
* pneumonia (infection of lung tissue): alveoli fill with inflammatory material
* emphysema (destruction of alveolar walls): less surface area
* pulmonary embolism (clot in pulmonary artery): ventilation without perfusion

Ventilation = air reaching alveoli.

Perfusion = blood reaching alveoli.

You need both for gas exchange.

---

## One-line intuition

Gas exchange is basically the alveolus trading O2 into blood and dumping CO2 out of blood, driven by pressure gradients.

---

## HY pearl

V/Q mismatch (ventilation/perfusion mismatch) is the most common cause of hypoxemia (low oxygen in blood).

Classic Step 1 idea:

* airway problem = poor ventilation
* blood flow problem = poor perfusion
* either way, gas exchange gets worse

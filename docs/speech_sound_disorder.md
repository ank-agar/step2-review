## Speech sound disorder

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/Student_doing_speech_therapy%28GN08932%29.jpg/500px-Student_doing_speech_therapy%28GN08932%29.jpg?utm_campaign=index&utm_content=thumbnail&utm_source=commons.wikimedia.org)

### Core idea

Speech sound disorder means a child has trouble making speech sounds clearly enough for their age.

Basically:

* the child may know what they want to say
* but the sounds come out wrong, unclear, or immature

This is a **speech** problem, not necessarily a **language** problem.

Language = knowing words/grammar/meaning.

Speech sound = physically and mentally organizing the sounds like **/r/**, **/s/**, **/k/**, **/t/**.

---

### Two big types

**Articulation disorder** = problem making a specific sound.

Example:

* says “wabbit” instead of “rabbit”
* lisps on **/s/**

Intuition:

> the mouth/tongue/lips are not placing the sound correctly.

---

**Phonological disorder** = problem with the sound rules/patterns.

Example:

* drops final consonants: “ca” instead of “cat”
* replaces back sounds with front sounds: “tat” instead of “cat”

Intuition:

> the child’s brain is simplifying whole categories of sounds, not just missing one sound.

---

### Why it happens

Speech sounds require coordination of:

* lips
* tongue
* teeth
* palate (roof of the mouth)
* vocal cords
* hearing feedback
* brain motor planning

So errors can happen if:

* the child has not learned the sound pattern yet
* the tongue/lips are not coordinating well
* hearing loss makes sounds harder to copy
* there is cleft palate (abnormal opening in the roof of the mouth)
* there is motor planning difficulty, like childhood apraxia of speech (difficulty planning speech movements)

---

### One-line intuition

A speech sound disorder is when the “sound-making system” is immature or poorly coordinated, so words are harder to understand even if the child’s thoughts are normal.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Speech sound disorder is different from stuttering:

* speech sound disorder = wrong or unclear sounds
* stuttering = disrupted flow/rhythm of speech

So “tat” for “cat” points to speech sound disorder, while “c-c-c-cat” points to stuttering.

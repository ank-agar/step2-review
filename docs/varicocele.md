### Core idea

A **varicocele** is dilation of the **pampiniform plexus** (network of veins around the testicle in the spermatic cord).

Think:

* varicocele = varicose veins of the scrotum
* dilated veins feel like a **“bag of worms”**

---

### Why it happens

Normally, testicular veins drain blood upward from the testicle.

If venous valves are weak, blood refluxes backward and pools in the pampiniform plexus.

That pooling causes:

* enlarged veins
* scrotal heaviness/aching
* worse symptoms with standing or Valsalva maneuver (bearing down)

---

### Why left-sided is classic

Most varicoceles are on the **left** because:

* left testicular vein drains into the left renal vein at a 90-degree angle
* right testicular vein drains more directly into the IVC (inferior vena cava)

So left-sided venous drainage has higher pressure and more chance for backup.

---

### Why it can cause infertility

The pampiniform plexus normally cools blood before it reaches the testicle.

If those veins are dilated and full of warm pooled blood:

* testicular temperature rises
* spermatogenesis (sperm production) decreases
* sperm count/motility can fall

So the key mechanism is **heat injury to sperm production**.

---

### Classic presentation

* young man
* dull scrotal ache or heaviness
* “bag of worms” above testicle
* worse with standing
* improves when lying down
* possible infertility

---

## One-line intuition

A varicocele is backed-up testicular venous blood that makes worm-like scrotal veins and overheats the testicle, hurting sperm production.

---

## Pearl 🔥

A **new isolated right-sided varicocele** is concerning because the right testicular vein drains into the IVC (inferior vena cava), so think about possible retroperitoneal/renal mass obstructing venous drainage.

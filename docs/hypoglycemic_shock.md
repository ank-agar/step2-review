### Core idea

Hypoglycemic shock = severe hypoglycemia (dangerously low blood glucose) causing brain dysfunction, altered mental status, seizures, coma, or loss of consciousness.

Important: this is not usually "shock" in the classic blood pressure sense. It means the brain is shocked because it is not getting enough glucose.

---

### Why it happens

The brain depends heavily on glucose for ATP (adenosine triphosphate, the cell's energy currency).

So when glucose drops:

* less glucose enters neurons
* less ATP is made
* neurons cannot maintain normal ion gradients
* brain function fails

That gives neuroglycopenic symptoms.

Break the word:

* neuro = nervous system
* glyco = glucose/sugar
* penic = deficient

So neuroglycopenia = the brain is glucose-deficient.

---

### Symptoms

Early symptoms are mostly adrenergic (epinephrine/sympathetic nervous system response):

* sweating
* tremor
* palpitations
* anxiety
* hunger

Severe symptoms are neuroglycopenic:

* confusion
* weakness
* blurry vision
* seizure
* coma

Reason:

Low glucose first triggers epinephrine to warn you, then the brain itself starts malfunctioning.

---

### Common causes

Most HY (high-yield) causes:

* too much insulin
* sulfonylureas (oral diabetes drugs that increase insulin release)
* skipped meal after taking insulin
* alcohol use, especially fasting
* adrenal insufficiency
* insulinoma (insulin-secreting pancreatic beta-cell tumor)

Alcohol causes hypoglycemia because it uses up NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, an electron carrier), which blocks gluconeogenesis (making new glucose), especially in fasting patients.

---

### Treatment intuition

If awake and able to swallow:

* give oral glucose/carbohydrates

If altered, seizing, or unconscious:

* IV (intravenous) dextrose, like D50 (50% dextrose)
* IM (intramuscular) glucagon if no IV access

Glucagon works by telling the liver:

> break down glycogen and release glucose into blood.

---

## One-line intuition

Hypoglycemic shock is when blood sugar gets so low that the brain runs out of usable fuel and starts shutting down.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In a hypoglycemic patient with high insulin, check C-peptide (connecting peptide, released with endogenous insulin):

* high insulin + high C-peptide = insulinoma or sulfonylurea use
* high insulin + low C-peptide = injected insulin

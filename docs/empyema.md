## Empyema

Empyema means pus inside a body cavity.

In Step 1 lung context, it usually means **pleural empyema**:

* pus in the pleural space
* pleural space = the potential space between the visceral pleura (lining on lung surface) and parietal pleura (lining on chest wall)

Most commonly, this happens after pneumonia.

---

### Core idea

Pneumonia starts in the lung tissue.

Then infection/inflammation can spread outward into the pleural space:

* simple parapneumonic effusion = sterile fluid near pneumonia
* complicated parapneumonic effusion = infected/inflammatory fluid
* empyema = frank pus in the pleural space

Parapneumonic means “around pneumonia.”

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Fever/chills:** bacterial infection triggers systemic inflammation
* **Pleuritic chest pain:** inflamed pleura hurts with breathing
* **Dyspnea (shortness of breath):** pus/fluid compresses the lung, so it cannot expand well
* **Dullness to percussion:** fluid/pus is dense, not air-filled
* **Decreased breath sounds:** fluid blocks sound transmission from the lung

---

### What the fluid looks like

Thoracentesis (needle drainage of pleural fluid) shows:

* purulent fluid = pus
* low pH
* low glucose
* high LDH (lactate dehydrogenase, marker of tissue breakdown/inflammation)

Why low glucose?

Bacteria and neutrophils (acute inflammatory white blood cells) are metabolically active, so they consume glucose in the pleural space.

---

### Treatment intuition

Antibiotics alone often are not enough because pus is thick and trapped.

So treatment is usually:

* antibiotics
* drainage with chest tube

If it becomes loculated (walled-off into pockets by fibrin), it may need VATS (video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery) or decortication (removing fibrous peel from the lung).

---

## One-line intuition

Empyema is basically pneumonia spilling infected pus into the pleural space, compressing the lung from the outside.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Empyema classically has **pleural fluid pH < 7.2**, low glucose, and high LDH — and it needs drainage, not just antibiotics.

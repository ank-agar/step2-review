# Intracerebral hemorrhage

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/8813.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470242/bin/Intracerebral_hemorrhage_2.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/images/HemorrhagicStroke_share.jpg)

### Core idea

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) means bleeding directly into the brain tissue.

Break it down:

* intra- = inside
* cerebral = brain
* hemorrhage = bleeding

So:

> blood vessel ruptures inside the brain tissue, and blood forms a clot-like mass called a hematoma (collection of blood).

---

### Why this is bad

Blood is not supposed to be inside brain parenchyma (functional brain tissue).

So the bleed causes damage by:

* directly destroying nearby neurons (brain cells)
* compressing surrounding brain tissue
* increasing ICP (intracranial pressure, pressure inside the skull)
* causing edema (swelling)

The skull is a closed box, so extra blood takes up space and squeezes the brain.

---

### Classic causes

Most important Step 1 cause:

* chronic hypertension (high blood pressure)

Why?

Long-standing hypertension damages small penetrating arteries, making them weak and more likely to rupture.

Common bleed locations:

* basal ganglia
* thalamus
* pons
* cerebellum

Other causes:

* cerebral amyloid angiopathy (amyloid deposition in brain blood vessel walls)
* AVM (arteriovenous malformation, abnormal artery-vein connection)
* anticoagulants (blood thinners)
* trauma

---

### Presentation

Can cause sudden:

* focal neurologic deficits (localized brain-function problems, like weakness or aphasia)
* headache
* vomiting
* decreased consciousness
* seizures

Why vomiting/decreased consciousness?

Because the blood raises ICP (intracranial pressure), which irritates and compresses the brain.

---

### Imaging intuition

On CT (computed tomography), acute blood usually looks bright white.

So ICH is basically:

> a bright white blob inside the brain tissue.

This is different from:

* epidural hematoma (blood between skull and dura mater)
* subdural hematoma (blood between dura mater and arachnoid mater)
* subarachnoid hemorrhage (blood in the subarachnoid space, often from berry aneurysm rupture)

ICH is inside the brain itself.

---

## One-line intuition

Intracerebral hemorrhage is a ruptured brain vessel dumping blood into brain tissue, creating a space-occupying hematoma that crushes and damages nearby neurons.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage is classically due to Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms (tiny aneurysms of small penetrating arteries), especially in the basal ganglia/internal capsule region.

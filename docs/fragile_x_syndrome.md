## Fragile X syndrome

![Image](https://www.frontiersin.org/files/Articles/884424/xml-images/fgene-13-884424-g001.webp)

![Image](https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/images/7/7d/Fragile_x_inheritance.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Fmr1.jpeg/330px-Fmr1.jpeg)

Fragile X syndrome is an X-linked genetic cause of intellectual disability caused by too many CGG repeats in the FMR1 gene (fragile X messenger ribonucleoprotein 1 gene).

Normal:

* FMR1 gene makes FMRP (fragile X mental retardation protein)
* FMRP helps regulate protein production at neuronal synapses (connections between neurons)

In fragile X:

* CGG trinucleotide repeats (3-base DNA repeat: cytosine-guanine-guanine) expand to >200
* the FMR1 gene becomes methylated (chemically silenced)
* FMRP is not made
* neurons develop abnormal synapses → developmental delay/intellectual disability

So the key chain is:

> CGG expansion → methylation → FMR1 silencing → ↓ FMRP → abnormal brain synapse development

Classic features:

* intellectual disability
* autism-like behavior
* long face
* large ears
* large jaw/prominent chin
* macroorchidism (large testes), especially after puberty
* hyperextensible joints (overly flexible joints)

Why males are usually worse:

* males have one X chromosome
* if that X has the full mutation, there is no backup normal FMR1 gene

Females can be milder because they have two X chromosomes, and X-inactivation (random silencing of one X chromosome in each cell) can leave some cells using the normal X.

Important repeat numbers:

* normal: usually <45 CGG repeats
* premutation: 55-200 CGG repeats
* full mutation: >200 CGG repeats

Premutation carriers may not have classic fragile X syndrome, but they are important because the repeat can expand in offspring, especially through maternal transmission.

## One-line intuition

Fragile X is a repeat expansion disease where the FMR1 gene gets "shut off," so the brain loses a protein needed for normal synapse development.

## Pearl 🔥

Fragile X syndrome is the most common inherited cause of intellectual disability and a classic trinucleotide repeat expansion disease.

### Core idea

Unstable angina is chest pain from sudden decreased blood flow to the heart muscle, but without myocardial infarction (MI; heart attack/cell death).

Usually the cause is:

* atherosclerotic plaque (cholesterol plaque in an artery wall) ruptures
* platelets stick to it
* a thrombus (blood clot) forms
* the clot partially blocks a coronary artery (artery supplying heart muscle)

So:

> unstable angina = plaque rupture + partial clot + ischemia, but no heart cell death.

Ischemia means low oxygen delivery to tissue.

---

### Why it is “unstable”

Stable angina is predictable:

* comes with exertion
* improves with rest
* improves with nitroglycerin (vasodilator drug that lowers heart workload)

Unstable angina is more dangerous because it can be:

* new-onset severe chest pain
* chest pain at rest
* chest pain that is getting worse/more frequent
* chest pain not reliably relieved by rest or nitroglycerin

The plaque has ruptured, so the blockage can suddenly worsen.

---

### Why troponin is normal

Troponin is a heart muscle injury marker released when myocardial cells die.

In unstable angina:

* the heart muscle is ischemic
* but there is no infarction (cell death)
* so troponin stays normal

That is the key difference from NSTEMI (non-ST elevation myocardial infarction).

---

### ECG findings

ECG (electrocardiogram) may show:

* ST depression
* T-wave inversion
* or sometimes no clear changes

Why ST depression?

Partial coronary blockage often causes subendocardial ischemia (low oxygen in the inner layer of the heart wall).

Subendocardial ischemia classically gives ST depression, not ST elevation.

---

### Unstable angina vs NSTEMI vs STEMI

ACS (acute coronary syndrome) includes unstable angina, NSTEMI, and STEMI.

| Condition | Main idea | ECG (electrocardiogram) | Troponin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unstable angina | ischemia without cell death | ST depression/T-wave inversion possible | normal |
| NSTEMI (non-ST elevation myocardial infarction) | partial blockage with cell death | ST depression/T-wave inversion possible | elevated |
| STEMI (ST elevation myocardial infarction) | usually complete blockage with full-thickness injury | ST elevation | elevated |

---

## One-line intuition

Unstable angina is a ruptured coronary plaque causing scary, unpredictable ischemic chest pain, but the heart cells have not died yet, so troponin is normal.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

Troponin separates unstable angina from NSTEMI (non-ST elevation myocardial infarction):

* unstable angina = normal troponin
* NSTEMI = elevated troponin

Both can have ST depression or T-wave inversion on ECG (electrocardiogram).

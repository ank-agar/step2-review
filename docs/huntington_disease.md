### Core idea

Huntington disease is an autosomal dominant neurodegenerative disease.

Autosomal dominant means:

* only 1 mutated copy is needed
* it often appears in every generation

Neurodegenerative means neurons progressively die over time.

The key affected area is the caudate nucleus, which is part of the basal ganglia.

Basal ganglia = deep brain motor-control structures that help regulate movement.

---

### Genetic cause

Huntington disease is caused by a CAG trinucleotide repeat expansion in the HTT gene.

Break that down:

* CAG = cytosine-adenine-guanine DNA sequence
* trinucleotide repeat = 3 DNA bases repeated over and over
* HTT gene = gene that makes huntingtin protein

More CAG repeats → abnormal huntingtin protein → neuronal death.

Classically:

* chromosome 4
* autosomal dominant inheritance
* anticipation

Anticipation means the disease can show up earlier and more severely in the next generation because repeat number expands.

This is especially associated with paternal transmission.

---

### Why chorea happens

Chorea means involuntary, irregular, dance-like movements.

In Huntington disease, the caudate degenerates, especially neurons in the indirect pathway.

Indirect pathway = basal ganglia pathway that normally suppresses unwanted movement.

So the logic is:

* indirect pathway normally acts like the movement brake
* Huntington damages that brake
* less brake → too much movement
* too much movement → chorea

That is the main intuition.

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* chorea
* dementia
* psychiatric symptoms

Psychiatric symptoms can include:

* depression
* irritability
* personality changes
* suicidality

Dementia means progressive decline in cognition, memory, judgment, and daily function.

---

### Brain finding

Caudate atrophy is classic.

Atrophy means shrinkage.

As the caudate shrinks, the lateral ventricles can look enlarged.

Lateral ventricles = cerebrospinal fluid-filled spaces in the brain.

So on imaging, Huntington disease can show:

* small caudate
* enlarged frontal horns of the lateral ventricles

---

## One-line intuition

Huntington disease is a CAG repeat disease where the caudate loses the movement brake, causing chorea plus progressive cognitive and psychiatric decline.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Huntington disease causes decreased GABA and acetylcholine in the caudate.

GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) is an inhibitory neurotransmitter.

Acetylcholine is a neurotransmitter important for movement and cognition.

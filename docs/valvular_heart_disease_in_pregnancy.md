# Valvular heart disease in pregnancy

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18147.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/b0fb/7930898/df6157daf4e8/ytab010f6.jpg)

### Core idea

Valvular heart disease in pregnancy means a pregnant patient has a diseased heart valve that is either:

* **stenotic** = too narrow, so blood has trouble moving forward
* **regurgitant** = leaky, so blood flows backward

Pregnancy stresses the heart because it increases:

* blood volume
* heart rate
* cardiac output (CO; amount of blood pumped by the heart per minute)

So a valve problem that was mild before pregnancy can become symptomatic during pregnancy.

---

### Most important Step 1 pattern

The big one is:

* **mitral stenosis (MS; narrowing of the mitral valve)**

Mitral stenosis is especially bad in pregnancy because the mitral valve is between the:

* left atrium (LA; chamber receiving oxygenated blood from lungs)
* left ventricle (LV; main pumping chamber)

If the mitral valve is narrowed, blood backs up into the LA and lungs.

That can cause:

* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* pulmonary edema (fluid in lungs)
* atrial fibrillation (irregular atrial rhythm)
* heart failure

---

### Why pregnancy makes mitral stenosis worse

Pregnancy increases CO, so more blood tries to cross the narrowed mitral valve.

But the valve opening is fixed and tight.

So:

> more flow through a narrow hole = higher pressure behind the valve.

That higher pressure backs up into the pulmonary veins, causing lung congestion.

Also, pregnancy increases heart rate.

High heart rate makes mitral stenosis worse because the mitral valve fills the LV during **diastole** (relaxation/filling phase of the heart).

Faster heart rate = shorter diastole = less time to push blood through the stenotic valve.

---

### Stenosis vs regurgitation in pregnancy

Pregnancy generally worsens **stenotic lesions** more than regurgitant lesions.

Why?

* stenosis = fixed obstruction, so increased blood flow creates a pressure backup
* regurgitation = leaky valve, often better tolerated because pregnancy lowers systemic vascular resistance (SVR; resistance the LV pumps against)

Lower SVR means blood can move forward more easily, so less blood may leak backward.

---

## One-line intuition

Pregnancy is like a volume-and-flow stress test for the heart; stenotic valves, especially mitral stenosis, cannot handle the extra flow.

---

## HY pearl

Mitral stenosis in pregnancy is classically due to rheumatic heart disease and can present with dyspnea/pulmonary edema because increased pregnancy blood volume backs up behind the narrowed mitral valve into the lungs.

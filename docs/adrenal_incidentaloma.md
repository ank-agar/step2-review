### Core idea

An adrenal incidentaloma is an adrenal mass found accidentally on imaging done for some other reason.

Break the word apart:

* adrenal = adrenal gland, which sits on top of the kidney
* incidental = found by accident
* -oma = mass/tumor

So it means:

> "Oops, we found an adrenal mass while looking for something else."

Most are benign adrenal adenomas (benign glandular tumors), but the key is to ask 2 questions:

* Is it making hormones?
* Does it look malignant?

---

### Why we care

Even if the patient has no symptoms, adrenal tumors can secretly produce hormones:

* cortisol -> Cushing syndrome features
* aldosterone -> hypertension + hypokalemia
* catecholamines (epinephrine/norepinephrine-like hormones) -> pheochromocytoma symptoms

Catecholamines are made by the adrenal medulla, so a pheochromocytoma can cause:

* episodic headache
* sweating
* palpitations
* hypertension

---

### Imaging intuition

Doctors look at CT (computed tomography) features like:

* size
* smooth vs irregular borders
* homogeneous vs heterogeneous appearance
* HU (Hounsfield units, a CT density measurement)

Low HU usually means more fat inside the lesion.

Why that matters:

* lipid-rich adrenal adenomas are usually benign
* malignant tumors, metastases, and pheochromocytomas are usually denser/more irregular

So:

> fatty + small + smooth = reassuring  
> large + irregular + heterogeneous = concerning

---

### Workup idea

Adrenal incidentaloma workup is basically:

* check if it is functional (hormone-secreting)
* check if it is cancer-looking

Common screening:

* dexamethasone suppression test for cortisol excess
* plasma free metanephrines or urine metanephrines for pheochromocytoma
* aldosterone-renin ratio if hypertensive or hypokalemic

Metanephrines are breakdown products of catecholamines, so they help detect pheochromocytoma.

---

## One-line intuition

An adrenal incidentaloma is an accidentally discovered adrenal mass that must be ruled out for hormone secretion and malignancy.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 point: never biopsy an adrenal mass until pheochromocytoma is ruled out, because poking a catecholamine-secreting tumor can trigger a dangerous hypertensive crisis.

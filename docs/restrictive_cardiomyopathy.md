### Core idea

Restrictive cardiomyopathy is a heart muscle disease where the ventricles become stiff and cannot relax/fill well during diastole.

Diastole = the filling phase of the heart.

So the main problem is:

* normal squeeze
* bad filling

This gives diastolic heart failure, often with preserved EF (ejection fraction; percent of blood pumped out of the ventricle per beat).

---

### Why the findings happen

The ventricle is stiff, so it resists incoming blood.

That causes:

* decreased ventricular filling
* increased filling pressure
* blood backs up into the atria
* atria enlarge

That is why restrictive cardiomyopathy classically has:

* biatrial enlargement
* normal or near-normal ventricular size
* preserved systolic function early
* signs of heart failure

---

### Symptoms

Because blood backs up behind the heart:

* pulmonary congestion = shortness of breath
* systemic venous congestion = edema, ascites, hepatomegaly
* elevated JVP (jugular venous pressure; neck vein pressure)

Think:

> stiff ventricle → pressure backs up → atria and veins get congested.

---

### Causes

High-yield causes are infiltrative or fibrotic diseases:

* amyloidosis
* hemochromatosis
* sarcoidosis
* endomyocardial fibrosis
* radiation-induced fibrosis

Infiltrative means abnormal material is deposited into tissue.

Amyloidosis is especially classic: amyloid protein deposits in the myocardium (heart muscle), making it stiff.

---

### Key comparison

Restrictive cardiomyopathy:

* myocardium is stiff
* pericardium is usually normal

Constrictive pericarditis:

* myocardium may be okay
* pericardium is stiff/calcified

Pericardium = sac around the heart.

So:

> restrictive = stiff heart muscle  
> constrictive = stiff heart wrapper

---

## One-line intuition

Restrictive cardiomyopathy is a stiff, noncompliant ventricle that can squeeze okay but cannot fill okay.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Restrictive cardiomyopathy causes diastolic dysfunction with preserved EF early because the ventricle can still eject a normal percentage of the smaller amount of blood it receives.

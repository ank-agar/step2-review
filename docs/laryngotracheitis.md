## Laryngotracheitis

![Image](https://www.medicalgraphics.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/croup.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/12/Croup_steeple_sign.jpg)

Laryngotracheitis means inflammation of the:

* larynx (voice box)
* trachea (windpipe)

In kids, this is basically **croup**.

---

### Core idea

Usually a viral infection causes swelling right below the vocal cords.

That area is called the **subglottic region** (the airway just below the glottis/vocal cords).

Because children have small airways, even a little swelling makes the airway much narrower.

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

* barking cough
* hoarse voice
* inspiratory stridor (high-pitched noisy breathing during inhalation)
* worse at night
* often after an upper respiratory infection (infection of the nose/throat area)

Most common cause:

* parainfluenza virus

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Barking cough:** swollen larynx changes the sound of coughing
* **Hoarseness:** vocal cord area is inflamed
* **Inspiratory stridor:** air has trouble entering through the narrowed upper airway
* **Steeple sign:** X-ray shows tapering/narrowing of the upper trachea

The X-ray looks like a church steeple because the swollen airway narrows into a point.

---

## One-line intuition

Laryngotracheitis is viral swelling of the child’s upper airway, especially below the vocal cords, causing a barky cough and stridor.

---

## Pearl

Croup = **steeple sign** on neck X-ray.

Epiglottitis (inflammation of the epiglottis, the flap that covers the airway during swallowing) = **thumbprint sign** and drooling/toxic appearance.

Inflammatory disease of the breast basically means **mastitis**: inflammation of breast tissue.

Most classically, this happens during breastfeeding.

Core findings:

* breast pain
* warmth
* redness
* swelling
* fever/chills if infection is present

---

### Why it happens

Milk can get trapped in ducts.

That stagnant milk becomes a good place for bacteria to grow, especially if bacteria enter through a cracked nipple.

Most common organism:

* **Staphylococcus aureus**

So the sequence is:

> milk stasis → inflammation → possible bacterial infection → painful red swollen breast

---

### Abscess complication

If the infection gets walled off, it can form a **breast abscess** (localized pus collection).

That causes:

* more focal tenderness
* fluctuant mass
* persistent fever/pain despite antibiotics

Abscess often needs drainage, not just antibiotics.

---

### Important mimic

Inflammatory breast cancer can look like mastitis.

It can cause:

* red swollen breast
* peau d'orange (orange-peel skin from lymphatic obstruction)
* nipple inversion
* skin thickening

Key clue:

> mastitis should improve with antibiotics; inflammatory breast cancer does not.

So non-lactating patient + persistent "mastitis" symptoms = biopsy/imaging concern.

---

## One-line intuition

Breast tissue gets inflamed, usually from milk stasis plus bacterial infection, making the breast hot, red, swollen, and painful.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Mastitis is usually due to **Staph aureus**, while inflammatory breast cancer causes peau d'orange because tumor cells block dermal lymphatics (skin lymph drainage vessels).

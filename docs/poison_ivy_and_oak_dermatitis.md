Poison ivy and poison oak dermatitis is **allergic contact dermatitis**.

That means:

* allergic = immune system reaction
* contact = triggered where something touches the skin
* dermatitis = skin inflammation

The trigger is **urushiol** (the oily resin in poison ivy, poison oak, and poison sumac).

---

### Core idea

Urushiol sticks to the skin, binds skin proteins, and makes them look foreign to the immune system.

Then **T cells** attack the area.

T cells = immune cells involved in cell-mediated immunity.

So this is a:

* type IV hypersensitivity reaction
* delayed reaction
* T-cell-mediated reaction

---

### Why the rash is delayed

Type IV hypersensitivity is not antibody-mediated.

It takes time for T cells to recognize the antigen and recruit inflammation, so the rash usually appears hours to days after exposure.

That is why someone can touch poison ivy on Monday and feel like the rash "randomly appeared" later.

---

### What it looks like

Classic findings:

* intense itching
* erythema (redness)
* vesicles (small fluid-filled blisters)
* linear streaks

The linear streaks are HY because the plant brushes across the skin in lines.

So if you see itchy vesicles in a line after hiking/gardening, think poison ivy/oak.

---

### Why it can seem like it is spreading

The blister fluid itself usually does **not** spread the rash.

It looks like spreading because:

* different skin areas got different amounts of urushiol
* thinner skin reacts earlier
* urushiol can stay on clothes, tools, shoes, or pets and re-expose the skin

So the real spread is usually from leftover oil, not from the blisters.

---

### Treatment idea

Main goal: remove the oil early and calm inflammation.

* wash skin with soap and water as soon as possible
* wash contaminated clothes/tools
* topical corticosteroids for mild/local disease
* oral corticosteroids if severe, widespread, or involving face/genitals

Corticosteroids reduce inflammation by suppressing immune activation.

---

## One-line intuition

Poison ivy/oak dermatitis is a delayed T-cell allergic reaction to urushiol oil stuck on the skin.

---

## Pearl

Type IV hypersensitivity = delayed, T-cell-mediated.

Classic Step 1 examples include:

* poison ivy/oak dermatitis
* PPD test (purified protein derivative test for tuberculosis exposure)
* contact dermatitis from nickel

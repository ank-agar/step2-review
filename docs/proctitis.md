### Core idea

Proctitis = inflammation of the rectum.

Break it down:

* proct- = rectum/anus area
* -itis = inflammation

So proctitis literally means:

> inflamed rectal lining.

---

### What symptoms does that cause?

The rectum is the final storage area before stool exits, so when it gets inflamed, it becomes irritated and sensitive.

Common symptoms:

* rectal pain or burning
* rectal bleeding
* mucus or pus discharge
* diarrhea
* tenesmus, meaning the constant feeling that you need to poop even when little or nothing comes out

Why tenesmus happens:

* inflamed rectal mucosa falsely signals "fullness"
* the rectum spasms/irritates sensory nerves
* the brain interprets that as needing to defecate

---

### Common causes

Major buckets:

* IBD (inflammatory bowel disease), especially UC (ulcerative colitis)
* STI (sexually transmitted infection), like gonorrhea, chlamydia, HSV (herpes simplex virus), or syphilis
* radiation injury after pelvic radiation
* infection, like C. difficile (Clostridioides difficile)
* ischemia, meaning low blood flow

---

### Step 1 intuition

Think:

> rectum inflamed = urgency + bleeding + pain + mucus.

If the question says inflammation is limited to the rectum, that is proctitis.

If it spreads into the sigmoid colon, that becomes proctosigmoiditis.

---

### Why bleeding/mucus happens

Inflammation damages the superficial mucosa, meaning the inner lining.

That causes:

* fragile blood vessels -> bleeding
* irritated goblet cells -> mucus
* exposed/ulcerated surface -> pain and discharge

---

## Pearl

UC (ulcerative colitis) usually starts in the rectum and extends proximally in a continuous pattern.

So isolated rectal UC = ulcerative proctitis.

### Core idea

Hyperlipidemia means too much lipid/fat in the blood.

Usually this means high:

* LDL (low-density lipoprotein)
* triglycerides
* or both

LDL (low-density lipoprotein) is the “bad cholesterol” because it delivers cholesterol into tissues and artery walls.

HDL (high-density lipoprotein) is the “good cholesterol” because it helps carry cholesterol away from tissues back to the liver.

---

### Why it matters

Extra LDL (low-density lipoprotein) can enter the artery wall.

Macrophages (immune cells that eat debris) then eat the LDL (low-density lipoprotein) and become foam cells.

Foam cells build up into a fatty plaque.

That plaque can:

* narrow the artery
* reduce blood flow
* rupture and form a clot

So hyperlipidemia increases risk of:

* coronary artery disease
* myocardial infarction, meaning heart attack
* stroke
* peripheral artery disease

---

### High triglycerides

Triglycerides are the storage form of fat.

Very high triglycerides can cause pancreatitis because excess triglycerides are broken down into free fatty acids, which irritate/injure pancreatic tissue.

Skin clue:

* eruptive xanthomas = small yellow-red lipid deposits in the skin

---

### Classic physical findings

Can see:

* xanthomas = lipid deposits in skin/tendons
* xanthelasma = yellow cholesterol deposits on eyelids
* corneal arcus = cholesterol ring around the cornea

These are basically visible lipid deposition.

---

## One-line intuition

Hyperlipidemia is too much fat cargo floating in blood, so it can deposit in arteries, skin, tendons, and sometimes trigger pancreatitis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Familial hypercholesterolemia is commonly due to defective LDL receptor function, so the liver cannot clear LDL (low-density lipoprotein) from blood well.

Result:

* very high LDL (low-density lipoprotein)
* tendon xanthomas
* premature atherosclerosis

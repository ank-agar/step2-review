HIV (human immunodeficiency virus) is an enveloped RNA (ribonucleic acid) retrovirus that infects CD4 T cells (helper T lymphocytes).

Core idea:

* HIV mainly attacks CD4 T cells
* CD4 T cells coordinate immune responses
* losing CD4 T cells makes the patient vulnerable to opportunistic infections

So the name makes sense:

* human = infects humans
* immunodeficiency = causes weak immune function
* virus = uses host cells to replicate

---

### Why HIV is a retrovirus

Most cells go:

> DNA → RNA → protein

HIV goes backward first:

> RNA → DNA

It does this using reverse transcriptase (enzyme that makes DNA from RNA).

Then HIV uses integrase (enzyme that inserts viral DNA into host DNA) to hide inside the CD4 cell genome.

That is why HIV is hard to cure:

* the virus is not just floating around
* it can become integrated into host DNA
* latent reservoirs (hidden infected cells not actively making virus) can persist

---

### HIV life cycle

Simplified steps:

1. HIV binds CD4 plus a coreceptor
2. HIV fuses into the cell
3. reverse transcriptase converts viral RNA into viral DNA
4. integrase inserts viral DNA into host DNA
5. host cell makes viral proteins and RNA
6. new virus particles assemble and bud out
7. protease (enzyme that cuts viral proteins into mature pieces) makes the virus infectious

Intuition:

> HIV turns the CD4 T cell into a virus factory, then slowly destroys the immune command system.

---

### Important receptors

HIV binds:

* CD4 receptor
* CCR5 or CXCR4 coreceptor

CCR5 is especially high-yield because early HIV infection usually uses CCR5.

Patients with CCR5-delta32 mutation have resistance to many HIV strains because the virus has trouble entering cells.

---

### What HIV causes

Untreated HIV can progress to AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome).

AIDS is defined by either:

* CD4 count < 200 cells/mm3
* or an AIDS-defining illness

Low CD4 count means poor T-cell help, so patients become vulnerable to infections that normal immunity usually controls.

Examples:

* Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia
* Candida esophagitis
* toxoplasmosis
* cryptococcal meningitis
* Kaposi sarcoma

---

## One-line intuition

HIV is an RNA retrovirus that infects CD4 T cells, integrates into host DNA, and weakens cell-mediated immunity.

---

## Pearl

HIV is treated with ART (antiretroviral therapy), usually multiple drugs together, because the virus mutates rapidly and combination therapy blocks several life-cycle steps at once.

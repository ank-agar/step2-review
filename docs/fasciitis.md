## Fasciitis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/ba/PF-PainAreas.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19568.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6a/Necrotizing_fasciitis_left_leg.JPEG)

Fasciitis = inflammation of fascia.

Fascia (fibrous connective tissue sheet around muscles, nerves, blood vessels, and organs) is basically the body’s “wrapping layer.”

So fasciitis means:

* fascia gets irritated/inflamed
* swelling builds up in that tight tissue plane
* pain happens because fascia is stretched and nearby nerves get irritated

---

### Common example: plantar fasciitis

Plantar fasciitis = inflammation/degeneration of the plantar fascia (thick connective tissue band on the bottom of the foot).

Classic:

* heel pain
* worse with first steps in the morning
* pain near the medial calcaneal tubercle (inner heel bone attachment site)

Why morning pain?

Overnight, the plantar fascia tightens/shortens. The first steps suddenly stretch the irritated fascia, so it hurts most at the beginning.

---

### Dangerous example: necrotizing fasciitis

Necrotizing fasciitis = rapidly spreading infection that destroys fascia and surrounding soft tissue.

Necrotizing = tissue death.

This is scary because bacteria spread along fascial planes:

* fascia has relatively poor blood supply
* infection can move quickly under the skin
* skin may look less severe than the deep infection early on

Classic clue:

* severe pain out of proportion to exam

That means the patient has way more pain than the skin appearance seems to explain.

---

## One-line intuition

Fasciitis is inflammation of the body’s connective-tissue wrapping layer; mild forms cause localized pain, but necrotizing fasciitis spreads fast through fascial planes and can be fatal.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Necrotizing fasciitis needs urgent surgical debridement (cutting away dead/infected tissue) plus broad-spectrum antibiotics; do not wait for imaging if the patient is unstable or suspicion is high.

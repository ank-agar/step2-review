### Core idea

Separation anxiety disorder is excessive fear or anxiety about being away from an attachment figure (someone the child is strongly bonded to, usually a parent/caregiver).

Normal kids can get upset when separated.

This becomes a disorder when it is:

* too intense for the child's developmental age
* persistent
* impairing school, sleep, or daily function

---

### Classic presentation

Child is very distressed when separated from parent/caregiver:

* refuses to go to school
* clings to parent
* refuses to sleep alone
* has nightmares about separation
* worries something bad will happen to parent
* gets headaches, stomachaches, nausea, or vomiting before separation

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The brain treats separation like danger.

So the child gets:

* **fear/worry:** "What if mom does not come back?"
* **avoidance:** refuses school or sleepovers
* **body symptoms:** sympathetic nervous system activation (fight-or-flight response), causing abdominal pain, nausea, palpitations, sweating

The key cycle:

* separation causes anxiety
* avoidance gives short-term relief
* relief reinforces avoidance
* next separation feels even scarier

---

### Timing clue

Normal separation anxiety can happen in infancy/toddler years.

Separation anxiety disorder is when the fear is developmentally inappropriate and persistent:

* at least 4 weeks in children/adolescents
* usually 6 months or more in adults

---

### Treatment

First-line is CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy), especially gradual exposure (slowly practicing separation in manageable steps).

SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors) can be used if symptoms are severe or not improving.

---

## One-line intuition

The child is not just "clingy" -- their threat system overfires when separated from their safe person.

---

## HY pearl

Separation anxiety disorder commonly presents as school refusal with physical complaints like stomachache or headache, especially right before school.

### Core idea

Intellectual disability is a neurodevelopmental disorder, meaning a brain-development condition that starts early in life.

It involves problems with both:

* intellectual functioning: reasoning, learning, problem-solving, judgment
* adaptive functioning: everyday life skills like communication, self-care, school/work functioning, and social independence

So it is not just "low IQ (intelligence quotient)."

The key idea is:

> learning/thinking ability is impaired enough that daily independent functioning is also affected.

---

### Why adaptive functioning matters

Someone can score low on testing but still function pretty well day-to-day.

Someone else may have major trouble with:

* managing money
* understanding danger
* communicating needs
* learning basic school skills
* living independently

That daily-life impact is what makes the diagnosis clinically meaningful.

Severity is based more on adaptive functioning/support needs than just IQ (intelligence quotient).

---

### Common causes

Intellectual disability can come from anything that disrupts brain development, such as:

* genetic/chromosomal disorders, like Down syndrome or fragile X syndrome
* prenatal toxin exposure, like fetal alcohol exposure
* congenital infections, like CMV (cytomegalovirus)
* birth complications causing hypoxic injury, meaning low oxygen injury
* severe malnutrition or brain injury early in life

Many cases are multifactorial or idiopathic, meaning no single clear cause is found.

---

### Important distinction

Intellectual disability is different from a specific learning disorder.

Specific learning disorder:

* one academic area is impaired, like reading or math
* overall intellectual ability may be normal

Intellectual disability:

* global intellectual functioning is impaired
* adaptive daily-life functioning is impaired too

---

## One-line intuition

Intellectual disability = the brain has difficulty with learning/reasoning broadly, and that spills over into real-life independence.

---

## Pearl

For Step 1, think: **intellectual disability requires deficits in both intellectual functioning and adaptive functioning, with onset during development.**

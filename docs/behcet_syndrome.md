### Core idea

Behcet syndrome is a multisystem vasculitis (inflammation of blood vessels) that classically causes recurrent:

* oral ulcers
* genital ulcers
* uveitis (inflammation of the uvea, the middle layer of the eye)

Think:

> painful ulcers + angry eyes + vessel inflammation everywhere.

---

### Why it causes so many random findings

The key problem is vasculitis (blood vessel inflammation).

Blood vessels go everywhere, so Behcet can affect many organs:

* **Mouth:** recurrent aphthous ulcers (canker sore-like ulcers)
* **Genitals:** painful genital ulcers
* **Eyes:** uveitis, which can threaten vision
* **Skin:** erythema nodosum (tender red nodules), acne-like lesions
* **Joints:** arthritis (joint inflammation)
* **CNS:** central nervous system involvement, meaning brain/spinal cord inflammation
* **GI tract:** gastrointestinal tract ulcers, which can mimic IBD (inflammatory bowel disease)

---

### Pathergy

Pathergy means the skin overreacts to minor trauma.

So if you prick the skin with a needle, the patient may form a papule or pustule (small raised inflamed bump).

Intuition:

> the immune system is so inflamed that even a tiny poke triggers a big local reaction.

---

### Association

Behcet syndrome is associated with:

* HLA-B51 (human leukocyte antigen B51)
* people from the Silk Road region, especially Turkey, the Middle East, and East Asia

HLA (human leukocyte antigen) genes help present antigens to immune cells, so certain HLA types increase risk for autoimmune/autoinflammatory disease.

---

## One-line intuition

Behcet is a relapsing vasculitis where inflamed blood vessels cause recurrent mouth/genital ulcers, eye inflammation, and skin lesions.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, the classic triad is:

* recurrent oral ulcers
* recurrent genital ulcers
* uveitis

And the buzzword test finding is **positive pathergy**.

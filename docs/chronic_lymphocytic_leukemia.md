### Core idea

Chronic lymphocytic leukemia, or **CLL (chronic lymphocytic leukemia)**, is a slow-growing cancer of mature-looking **B lymphocytes (B cells, a type of white blood cell)**.

Basically:

* too many abnormal B cells build up
* they look mature, but they do not work well
* they accumulate in blood, bone marrow, lymph nodes, and spleen

So CLL is usually an older-adult leukemia with lots of small lymphocytes in the blood.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Lymphocytosis (high lymphocyte count):** malignant B cells keep accumulating in the blood
* **Lymphadenopathy (enlarged lymph nodes):** CLL cells collect in lymph nodes
* **Splenomegaly (enlarged spleen):** CLL cells collect in the spleen
* **Fatigue/anemia (low red blood cells):** bone marrow gets crowded, so red blood cell production can drop
* **Infections:** the abnormal B cells do not make good antibodies, so immunity is weak

The classic blood smear finding is:

* **smudge cells**

Smudge cells are fragile CLL lymphocytes that break apart while the blood smear is being made.

---

### Key markers

CLL is a B-cell cancer, but the cells weirdly express:

* **CD20 (B-cell marker)**
* **CD23**
* **CD5**

Important intuition:

CD5 is usually a **T-cell marker (marker found on T lymphocytes)**, but in CLL the malignant B cells express CD5 too.

---

## One-line intuition

CLL is a slow buildup of fragile, mature-looking but poorly functioning B cells, causing lymphocytosis, lymph node/spleen enlargement, and weak immunity.

---

## HY pearl

CLL can transform into an aggressive lymphoma called **Richter transformation (CLL turning into diffuse large B-cell lymphoma)**, which presents with rapidly enlarging lymph nodes and worsening systemic symptoms.

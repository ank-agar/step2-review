## Parotitis

![Image](https://visualsonline.cancer.gov/retrieve.cfm?dpi=72&fileformat=jpg&imageid=9435)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK560735/bin/Gray1024.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/80/Mumps_PHIL_130_lores.jpg)

Parotitis = inflammation of the parotid gland.

The parotid gland is the big salivary gland near the ear/angle of the jaw.

So clinically it gives:

* cheek/jaw swelling
* pain near the ear
* pain with chewing
* sometimes fever
* sometimes pus from Stensen duct (the parotid duct opening inside the cheek)

---

### Core idea

The parotid gland makes saliva.

If saliva flow gets blocked or infected, the gland swells and hurts.

Common causes:

* mumps virus
* bacterial infection, especially Staphylococcus aureus
* dehydration causing thick saliva/stasis
* sialolithiasis (salivary stone)
* autoimmune disease like Sjogren syndrome (autoimmune dry eyes + dry mouth)

---

### Why swelling happens

Inflammation brings:

* fluid
* immune cells
* pressure inside the gland

That pressure causes pain, especially when eating because eating stimulates saliva production.

If the duct is blocked, saliva tries to come out but cannot drain well, so the gland gets even more swollen.

---

### Mumps association

Mumps classically causes bilateral parotitis.

Bilateral = both sides.

Think:

* fever
* malaise (feeling sick/tired)
* painful swollen cheeks
* unvaccinated patient

Mumps can also cause:

* orchitis (testicular inflammation)
* aseptic meningitis (meningitis without typical bacterial infection)
* pancreatitis (pancreas inflammation)

---

## One-line intuition

Parotitis is basically an angry swollen saliva gland near the ear, often from mumps, bacteria, or blocked saliva flow.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Mumps is a Paramyxovirus and is prevented by the MMR vaccine (measles, mumps, rubella vaccine).

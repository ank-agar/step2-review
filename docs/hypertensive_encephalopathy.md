### Core idea

Hypertensive encephalopathy = brain dysfunction from a sudden severe rise in blood pressure.

Encephalopathy means diffuse brain dysfunction, usually causing:

* headache
* confusion/altered mental status
* nausea/vomiting
* blurry vision
* seizures

---

### Why it happens

Normally, brain arterioles constrict when blood pressure rises.

That protects the brain from too much blood flow.

But if blood pressure gets too high too fast:

* autoregulation fails
* cerebral vessels leak
* BBB (blood-brain barrier) breaks down
* fluid enters brain tissue
* vasogenic edema develops

Vasogenic edema means fluid leaks out of blood vessels into the extracellular space because the BBB is damaged.

So the brain is not primarily dying at first; it is swelling from leaky vessels.

---

### Important association

Hypertensive encephalopathy is closely related to PRES (posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome).

PRES often causes edema in the posterior brain, especially:

* parietal lobes
* occipital lobes

That explains the visual symptoms.

Posterior areas are more vulnerable because they have relatively weaker sympathetic protection of blood vessels.

---

### Why not drop BP too fast?

Chronic hypertension shifts the brain's autoregulation curve upward.

So if you suddenly lower BP (blood pressure) too aggressively, the brain may become underperfused.

Underperfused means not enough blood flow.

That can cause ischemia, meaning tissue injury from low oxygen delivery.

---

## One-line intuition

The pressure gets so high that the brain's vessels cannot protect themselves anymore, so the BBB leaks and the brain swells.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Hypertensive encephalopathy is a hypertensive emergency because there is end-organ damage: CNS (central nervous system) symptoms from cerebral edema.

Treat with controlled IV (intravenous) BP lowering, commonly nicardipine or labetalol, but avoid overly rapid BP reduction.

## Constipation

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17483/N00033-H_thumbnail.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9e/BristolStoolChart.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/96/Journal.pmed.1000092.g002_fecal_impaction.png)

Constipation = stool is moving too slowly through the colon, so bowel movements become:

* infrequent
* hard
* dry
* painful/difficult to pass

The key idea:

> The longer stool sits in the colon, the more water the colon absorbs from it.

So stool becomes dry and hard.

---

### Why it happens

The colon normally absorbs water and pushes stool forward by peristalsis (wave-like smooth muscle contraction).

If transit is slow:

* stool sits longer
* more water gets absorbed
* stool gets harder
* harder stool is harder to push out
* this causes straining and incomplete emptying

So constipation is basically:

> slow stool movement + excess water removal = hard stool.

---

### Common causes

* low fiber intake
* dehydration
* inactivity
* opioids
* anticholinergic drugs
* hypothyroidism
* hypercalcemia
* irritable bowel syndrome (IBS = irritable bowel syndrome)
* pelvic floor dysfunction

---

### Important complication

Severe constipation can cause fecal impaction (a hard stool mass stuck in the rectum or colon).

This can cause:

* abdominal pain/distension
* overflow diarrhea
* urinary retention sometimes

Overflow diarrhea happens because liquid stool leaks around the hard impacted stool.

---

## One-line intuition

Constipation is when stool moves too slowly, so the colon dries it out into a hard mass that is difficult to pass.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Opioids commonly cause constipation because they decrease gut motility by activating mu opioid receptors in the GI tract (gastrointestinal tract).

## Thyroid nodule

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/39/Thyroid_ultrasound_110322134324_1356420.jpg)

A thyroid nodule is a lump inside the thyroid gland.

The thyroid gland is the butterfly-shaped endocrine gland (hormone-secreting gland) in the front of the neck that makes T3 (triiodothyronine) and T4 (thyroxine).

So a thyroid nodule is basically:

> a focal bump/growth within the thyroid.

Most thyroid nodules are:

* benign (non-cancerous)
* asymptomatic (no symptoms)
* found incidentally on exam or imaging

---

### What it can be made of

A nodule can be:

* solid tissue
* cystic fluid
* mixed solid + cystic
* a benign overgrowth of thyroid follicles
* less commonly, thyroid cancer

Follicles are the small thyroid units that store colloid (thyroid hormone precursor material).

---

### Why we care

The big questions are:

* Is it making thyroid hormone?
* Is it suspicious for cancer?
* Is it compressing nearby structures?

If it makes too much hormone, it can cause hyperthyroidism (too much thyroid hormone), leading to:

* weight loss
* heat intolerance
* palpitations
* tremor

If it is large, it can cause compressive symptoms:

* trouble swallowing
* hoarseness
* neck pressure

---

### Hot vs cold nodules

On radioactive iodine uptake scan:

* hot nodule = takes up iodine and makes hormone
* cold nodule = does not take up iodine well

Intuition:

* hot = active thyroid tissue
* cold = inactive/nonfunctioning tissue

Cold nodules are more concerning for malignancy (cancer), but most are still benign.

---

### Workup logic

First check TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone).

* low TSH means the thyroid is already overactive, so scan for a hot nodule
* normal/high TSH means evaluate the nodule mainly by ultrasound

Ultrasound looks for suspicious features:

* solid
* hypoechoic (darker than surrounding tissue)
* irregular margins
* microcalcifications (tiny calcium spots)
* taller-than-wide shape

If suspicious or large enough, do FNA (fine needle aspiration), which means sampling cells with a small needle.

---

## One-line intuition

A thyroid nodule is a localized lump in the thyroid; most are benign, but Step-style thinking is: check TSH, decide hot vs cold, then use ultrasound/FNA to rule out cancer.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Hot thyroid nodules are usually benign because they are functioning thyroid tissue; cold nodules are the ones that raise more concern for cancer.

# Granulomatous enterocolitis

Granulomatous enterocolitis means inflammation of the small intestine and colon with granulomas.

Break it down:

* granulomatous = forming granulomas (organized clusters of macrophages, which are immune cells that "wall off" chronic inflammation)
* entero- = small intestine
* colitis = colon inflammation

In Step 1 terms, this basically points you toward Crohn disease, especially when you see:

* skip lesions (patchy diseased areas with normal bowel in between)
* transmural inflammation (inflammation through the full bowel wall)
* noncaseating granulomas (granulomas without central necrosis/dead tissue)

Why does Crohn cause these findings?

The immune system overreacts to gut bacteria and gut contents. Instead of causing only surface irritation, the inflammation goes deep through the bowel wall.

That explains:

* transmural inflammation → fistulas (abnormal connections between organs), strictures (narrowed bowel), and obstruction
* skip lesions → patchy involvement rather than one continuous area
* granulomas → macrophages clump together because the immune system is trying to contain chronic inflammation
* diarrhea and abdominal pain → inflamed bowel absorbs poorly and spasms

Important distinction:

* Crohn disease = transmural, skip lesions, noncaseating granulomas, can affect mouth to anus
* Ulcerative colitis = mucosa/submucosa only, continuous disease, starts at rectum, no granulomas

One-line intuition:

Crohn is like a deep, patchy immune attack on the bowel wall, and granulomas are the immune system's attempt to wall off that chronic inflammation.

## HY Step 1 pearl

Noncaseating granulomas are classic for Crohn disease, but they are not always present, so absence of granulomas does not rule it out.

### Core idea

Diarrhea = stool has too much water, usually causing:

* loose/watery stool
* increased stool frequency
* urgency

Normal colon job:

* absorb water
* absorb electrolytes (charged particles like sodium and chloride)
* turn liquid intestinal contents into formed stool

So diarrhea happens when the gut either:

* cannot absorb enough water
* secretes too much water
* moves stool too fast for water to be absorbed

---

### Why it becomes watery

Think:

> water follows solute.

Solute = dissolved particles, like salts, sugars, or toxins.

If extra solute stays inside the intestinal lumen (inside space of the gut), water gets pulled into the stool.

That gives watery diarrhea.

---

### Main mechanisms

**Osmotic diarrhea**

Non-absorbed solutes pull water into the gut.

Example:

* lactose intolerance

Why:

* lactose stays in the gut
* water follows lactose
* diarrhea improves with fasting

---

**Secretory diarrhea**

The gut actively secretes electrolytes and water.

Example:

* cholera toxin
* ETEC (enterotoxigenic Escherichia coli)

Why:

* toxin increases chloride secretion
* sodium and water follow chloride
* diarrhea continues even with fasting

---

**Inflammatory diarrhea**

The intestinal lining is damaged/inflamed.

Findings:

* blood
* pus
* fever
* abdominal pain

Why:

* damaged mucosa leaks fluid, protein, and blood into stool

Examples:

* Shigella
* Salmonella
* IBD (inflammatory bowel disease)

---

### Big danger

The main danger is dehydration and electrolyte loss.

This is especially dangerous in:

* infants
* elderly patients
* severe cholera-like watery diarrhea

Treatment idea:

* ORS (oral rehydration solution) uses glucose + sodium together

Why it works:

* glucose helps sodium get absorbed
* water follows sodium into the body

---

## One-line intuition

Diarrhea is basically the colon losing the race: water stays in the gut instead of getting absorbed back into the body.

---

## HY pearl

Secretory diarrhea persists during fasting, while osmotic diarrhea improves during fasting.

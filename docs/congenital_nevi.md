### Core idea

Congenital nevi are moles present at birth.

A nevus is a benign cluster of melanocytes, which are pigment-producing cells that make melanin, the brown-black pigment in skin.

So congenital nevi are:

> birthmarks made of melanocytes that formed during development.

They can be small, medium, large, or giant.

---

### Why they are dark

Melanocytes make melanin.

So if a patch of skin has extra melanocytes grouped together, that area can look:

* tan
* brown
* black
* sometimes blue-gray if pigment is deeper in the dermis, meaning the deeper skin layer

The bigger the nevus, the more melanocytes are present in that lesion.

---

### Why giant congenital nevi matter

Most small congenital nevi are benign.

The important Step 1 concern is a giant congenital melanocytic nevus.

Why?

* it contains a large number of melanocytes
* melanocytes can transform into melanoma, which is malignant cancer of melanocytes
* more melanocytes = more cells that could potentially acquire dangerous mutations

So giant congenital nevi have increased melanoma risk compared with ordinary small moles.

---

### What they can look like

Congenital nevi may be:

* flat or raised
* smooth, pebbly, or wart-like
* hairy
* sharply bordered
* very large, sometimes covering a broad body region

Hair can grow from them because the nevus can involve deeper skin structures around hair follicles.

---

## One-line intuition

A congenital nevus is a mole present at birth: a developmental cluster of melanocytes that makes extra pigment.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Giant congenital melanocytic nevus is associated with increased melanoma risk because it contains many melanocytes that can undergo malignant transformation.

### Core idea

Aspiration pneumonia = lung infection after something from the mouth/stomach goes into the airway instead of the esophagus (food pipe).

Aspiration means "breathing in something that should not enter the lungs."

So the sequence is:

* food/saliva/vomit goes down the wrong pipe
* bacteria from the mouth enter the lungs
* alveoli (tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens) fill with inflammatory fluid/pus
* patient gets pneumonia symptoms

---

### Why it happens

Normally, the epiglottis (flap that covers the airway during swallowing) protects the trachea (windpipe).

If airway protection is weak, material can enter the lungs.

Common risk factors:

* stroke
* seizures
* alcohol intoxication
* anesthesia
* dementia
* dysphagia (difficulty swallowing)
* impaired cough/gag reflex

---

### Symptoms

Usually looks like pneumonia:

* fever
* cough
* shortness of breath
* low oxygen
* crackles on lung exam
* infiltrate/consolidation on CXR (chest x-ray)

Consolidation means the alveoli are filled with fluid/pus instead of air, so that area looks whiter on x-ray.

---

### Aspiration pneumonia vs aspiration pneumonitis

Important distinction:

* aspiration pneumonia = infection from aspirated bacteria
* aspiration pneumonitis = chemical inflammation from aspirated gastric acid

Pneumonitis often happens quickly after vomiting/aspiration.

Pneumonia is more about bacteria growing after aspiration.

---

### Why the right lung is common

The right main bronchus is more:

* vertical
* wide
* direct

So aspirated material more easily falls into the right lung, especially the right lower lobe.

---

## One-line intuition

Aspiration pneumonia is basically "wrong pipe" material carrying mouth bacteria into dependent lung areas, causing infection and pus-filled alveoli.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1: aspiration pneumonia classically affects dependent lung segments:

* upright patient: lower lobes, especially right lower lobe
* supine patient: posterior upper lobes or superior lower lobes

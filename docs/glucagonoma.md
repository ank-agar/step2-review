### Core idea

A **glucagonoma** is a rare pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (hormone-producing tumor from endocrine cells) of **alpha cells** in the pancreatic islets.

Alpha cells normally make **glucagon** (hormone that raises blood glucose).

So glucagonoma = too much glucagon.

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* **diabetes mellitus** (high blood glucose from insulin problems or excess glucose production)
* **necrolytic migratory erythema** (red, blistering, crusting rash that moves around)
* weight loss
* anemia (low red blood cells or hemoglobin)
* diarrhea
* glossitis (inflamed tongue)
* venous thrombosis (blood clots in veins)

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Glucagon tells the body:

> “We are fasting. Make more glucose and break down stored fuel.”

So excess glucagon causes:

* **Hyperglycemia:** liver makes/release more glucose, causing diabetes-like symptoms
* **Weight loss:** glucagon promotes protein and fat breakdown
* **Rash:** amino acids (protein building blocks), zinc, and fatty acids can become depleted, so skin cannot repair normally
* **Glossitis/anemia:** nutritional depletion affects fast-turnover tissues like tongue and blood cells
* **Thrombosis:** malignancy and glucagonoma syndrome increase clot risk

---

### Rash clue

The high-yield rash is **necrolytic migratory erythema**:

* necrolytic = skin cells are dying
* migratory = moves/spreads
* erythema = redness

It often affects:

* mouth area
* groin/perineum (area between genitals and anus)
* buttocks
* legs

---

## One-line intuition

Glucagonoma is an alpha-cell pancreatic tumor that makes the body act like it is starving, so glucose rises while fat/protein stores and skin integrity get wrecked.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Glucagonoma is classically associated with **MEN1** (multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1), a tumor syndrome involving the **3 Ps**:

* parathyroid
* pituitary
* pancreatic endocrine tumors

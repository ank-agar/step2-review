Dyspnea = subjective feeling of difficult or uncomfortable breathing.

In simple terms:

> “I feel like I cannot get enough air.”

It is a symptom, not a diagnosis.

---

### Core idea

Dyspnea happens when the body/brain senses a mismatch between:

* how much breathing effort is needed
* how much oxygen delivery or ventilation is actually happening

Ventilation means moving air in and out of the lungs.

So dyspnea can come from:

* lung problems
* airway narrowing
* heart problems
* anemia
* anxiety/panic
* weak breathing muscles

---

### Why it happens

Common mechanisms:

* **Airway obstruction:** asthma or COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) makes air harder to move
* **Poor gas exchange:** pneumonia, pulmonary edema, or fibrosis makes oxygen transfer harder
* **Low oxygen-carrying capacity:** anemia means less hemoglobin to carry oxygen
* **Heart failure:** heart cannot pump blood forward well, so fluid backs up into lungs
* **Pulmonary embolism:** PE (pulmonary embolism) blocks blood flow to part of the lung
* **Pneumothorax:** air in the pleural space collapses part of the lung

Pleural space = thin potential space between lung and chest wall.

---

### What it can look like

Signs of respiratory distress include:

* tachypnea (fast breathing)
* accessory muscle use
* nasal flaring
* intercostal retractions
* cyanosis (blue skin/lips from low oxygen)
* diaphoresis (sweating)
* inability to speak full sentences

Intercostal retractions = skin pulling inward between ribs because the patient is working hard to breathe.

---

## One-line intuition

Dyspnea is the brain saying: “breathing feels too hard for the amount of air/oxygen I am getting.”

---

## Pearl 🔥

Orthopnea = dyspnea when lying flat.

This is HY for left-sided heart failure because lying down increases venous return to the heart, worsening pulmonary congestion/fluid in the lungs.

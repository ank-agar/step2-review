### Core idea

Conjunctivitis = inflammation of the conjunctiva (thin clear membrane covering the white part of the eye and the inside of the eyelids).

That is why it is called “pink eye”:

* conjunctiva gets inflamed
* blood vessels dilate
* the eye looks red/pink

---

### Main causes

* **Viral:** most common; watery discharge, often with URI (upper respiratory infection) symptoms
* **Bacterial:** thicker/purulent discharge; eyelids may be stuck shut in the morning
* **Allergic:** itchy, watery eyes; usually bilateral (both eyes)

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Red eye:** inflammation causes vasodilation (widening of blood vessels)
* **Watery tearing:** irritated conjunctiva stimulates tear production
* **Discharge:** inflammatory cells/mucus/pus collect on the eye surface
* **Itching:** especially allergic conjunctivitis, due to histamine release from mast cells

---

### Contagious?

Viral and bacterial conjunctivitis can spread easily by:

* touching the eye
* hand-to-eye contact
* shared towels/pillows
* contaminated surfaces

Allergic conjunctivitis is **not contagious** because it is an immune reaction, not an infection.

---

## One-line intuition

The clear “saran wrap” layer over the white of the eye gets irritated/infected, so it becomes red, watery, itchy, and sometimes goopy.

---

## Pearl 🔥

USMLE loves: **itching strongly suggests allergic conjunctivitis**, while **purulent discharge suggests bacterial conjunctivitis**.

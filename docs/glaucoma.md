### Core idea

Glaucoma is damage to the optic nerve, usually from increased IOP (intraocular pressure).

Think:

* eye makes fluid
* fluid cannot drain well
* pressure builds up
* pressure damages the optic nerve
* vision is slowly lost

The fluid is called aqueous humor (clear fluid in the front of the eye).

---

### Why pressure goes up

Aqueous humor normally drains through the trabecular meshwork (drainage tissue near the cornea-iris angle).

If drainage is blocked or impaired:

* fluid accumulates
* IOP (intraocular pressure) rises
* optic nerve fibers get compressed/damaged
* optic disc cupping happens

Optic disc cupping means the center of the optic nerve head looks enlarged/hollowed out.

---

### Classic finding

Glaucoma usually causes:

* painless vision loss
* peripheral vision loss first
* optic disc cupping

Why peripheral vision first?

Because glaucoma damages retinal ganglion cell axons (nerve fibers carrying visual information from retina to brain), and the early damage often shows up as peripheral visual field defects.

---

### Open-angle vs angle-closure

**Open-angle glaucoma**

* most common
* chronic
* painless
* gradual peripheral vision loss
* drainage angle is open, but outflow is poor

**Angle-closure glaucoma**

* acute emergency
* painful red eye
* headache/nausea
* halos around lights
* mid-dilated pupil
* drainage angle is physically blocked

---

## One-line intuition

Glaucoma is like a clogged sink in the eye: fluid cannot leave well, pressure rises, and the optic nerve slowly gets crushed.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Acute angle-closure glaucoma can be triggered by pupillary dilation, because the iris can bunch up and block aqueous humor drainage through the trabecular meshwork.

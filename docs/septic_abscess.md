## Septic abscess

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3b/Cutaneous_abscess_MRSA_staphylococcus_aureus_7826_lores.jpg/960px-Cutaneous_abscess_MRSA_staphylococcus_aureus_7826_lores.jpg)

A septic abscess is a localized pocket of pus caused by infection.

Break it down:

* septic = infected, usually bacterial
* abscess = walled-off collection of pus
* pus = dead neutrophils, bacteria, dead tissue, and inflammatory fluid

So the core idea is:

> bacteria get trapped in tissue, neutrophils rush in, pus forms, and the body walls it off.

---

### Why it becomes walled off

The immune system tries to contain the infection so it does not spread.

That creates a capsule-like barrier around the pus.

This is why abscesses often feel:

* swollen
* tender
* warm
* fluctuant, meaning squishy because there is fluid inside

---

### Why antibiotics alone may not fix it

Antibiotics may not penetrate well into the center of an abscess because the center is full of dead tissue and pus, not great blood flow.

So treatment often requires:

* incision and drainage, meaning cutting it open and letting pus out
* antibiotics if there is surrounding cellulitis, systemic illness, immunocompromise, or high-risk location

Cellulitis means spreading infection of the skin and soft tissue without a clear pus pocket.

---

### Common bugs

Common causes include:

* Staphylococcus aureus
* MRSA, meaning methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus
* Streptococcus species
* anaerobes, meaning bacteria that grow in low-oxygen environments, especially in dental, perirectal, or intra-abdominal abscesses

---

## One-line intuition

A septic abscess is the immune system trapping bacteria in a pus-filled pocket.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, abscess pus is mainly dead neutrophils, which is why abscesses are classic examples of acute inflammation.

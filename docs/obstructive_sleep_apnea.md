## Obstructive sleep apnea

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is repeated pauses in breathing during sleep because the upper airway physically collapses or gets blocked.

Break it down:

* obstructive = something is blocking airflow
* sleep = happens during sleep
* apnea = no breathing

So OSA means:

> during sleep, the person tries to breathe, but air cannot move because the airway is blocked.

---

### Core idea

During sleep, muscles in the throat relax.

In some people, that relaxation lets the:

* tongue fall backward
* soft palate collapse
* tonsils/adenoids narrow the airway
* pharynx (throat airway) close off

That blocks airflow even though the chest is still trying to breathe.

---

### Why oxygen drops

The brain is still sending signals to breathe.

The diaphragm (main breathing muscle) and chest wall keep pulling, but the airway is closed.

So:

* air cannot enter lungs
* blood oxygen falls
* carbon dioxide rises
* brain briefly wakes the person up
* airway tone returns
* breathing restarts

Then the person falls back asleep and it can happen again.

That cycle causes fragmented sleep, even if the patient does not remember waking up.

---

### Classic symptoms

Think:

* loud snoring
* witnessed apneas (someone sees them stop breathing)
* gasping/choking during sleep
* morning headache
* daytime sleepiness
* poor concentration
* resistant hypertension

Snoring happens because partially blocked soft tissues vibrate.

Silent pauses are more concerning because that can mean complete obstruction.

---

### Big risk factors

Common risk factors:

* obesity
* large neck circumference
* male sex
* older age
* alcohol/sedatives before sleep
* enlarged tonsils, especially in kids
* craniofacial anatomy that narrows the airway

Obesity matters because extra soft tissue around the neck/pharynx makes the airway easier to collapse.

---

### Obstructive vs central sleep apnea

OSA (obstructive sleep apnea):

* respiratory effort is present
* airway is blocked
* chest/abdomen still try to move

Central sleep apnea (CSA) means the brainstem is not sending enough respiratory drive, so there is reduced effort to breathe.

Easy distinction:

* obstructive = effort but blocked pipe
* central = no signal, so low effort

---

### Diagnosis

Diagnosed with polysomnography (sleep study).

Polysomnography means a sleep test that measures breathing, oxygen, brain waves, heart rhythm, and muscle activity.

Severity is often measured by AHI (apnea-hypopnea index), which means the number of apnea or hypopnea events per hour of sleep.

Hypopnea means abnormally shallow breathing, not a full pause.

---

### Treatment idea

Main treatment is CPAP (continuous positive airway pressure).

CPAP works like an air splint:

* it pushes air pressure into the airway
* keeps the pharynx open
* prevents collapse during sleep

Weight loss, avoiding alcohol/sedatives, side-sleeping, oral appliances, or surgery can help depending on the cause.

---

## One-line intuition

OSA is like trying to breathe through a soft straw that collapses during sleep: the brain says “breathe,” the chest tries, but the airway is physically closed.

---

## Pearl 🔥

OSA causes intermittent hypoxia (repeated low oxygen episodes), which increases sympathetic tone and can lead to hypertension, pulmonary hypertension, arrhythmias, and right heart strain.

## Azotemia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Renal_corpuscle.svg/960px-Renal_corpuscle.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Urinary_system.svg/500px-Urinary_system.svg.png)

Azotemia means increased nitrogen-containing waste products in the blood, especially:

* BUN (blood urea nitrogen)
* creatinine

Core idea:

> the kidneys are not clearing waste well enough.

---

### Why it happens

Normally, the kidney filters blood at the glomerulus.

If GFR (glomerular filtration rate) drops, less blood gets filtered, so waste builds up in the blood.

So:

* lower GFR -> higher BUN
* lower GFR -> higher creatinine

Creatinine comes from muscle metabolism.

Urea/BUN comes from protein breakdown in the liver.

---

### 3 big categories

Azotemia is classified by where the problem is:

* **Prerenal azotemia:** before the kidney
* **Intrinsic renal azotemia:** inside the kidney
* **Postrenal azotemia:** after the kidney

---

### Prerenal azotemia

This is decreased blood flow to the kidney.

Examples:

* dehydration
* blood loss
* heart failure
* shock

The kidney itself is not damaged at first.

It just is not getting enough blood to filter.

Because the body thinks volume is low, it tries to save salt and water:

* RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system) turns on
* ADH (antidiuretic hormone) turns on
* urine sodium becomes low
* urine becomes concentrated

HY pattern:

* BUN/Cr (blood urea nitrogen to creatinine ratio) > 20
* FeNa (fractional excretion of sodium) < 1%

Why BUN rises more than creatinine:

slow kidney flow gives the tubules more time to reabsorb urea with water, but creatinine is not reabsorbed much.

---

### Intrinsic renal azotemia

This is direct kidney damage.

Examples:

* ATN (acute tubular necrosis)
* glomerulonephritis
* acute interstitial nephritis

Now the tubules are injured, so they cannot reabsorb sodium and water normally.

HY pattern in ATN:

* BUN/Cr around 10-15
* FeNa > 2%
* muddy brown granular casts

Intuition:

prerenal = kidney is thirsty but tubules still work.

intrinsic renal = kidney tissue itself is damaged.

---

### Postrenal azotemia

This is obstruction after the kidney.

Examples:

* kidney stone
* BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia)
* tumor compressing the ureter

Urine cannot drain, so pressure backs up into the kidney.

That back-pressure lowers GFR, so BUN and creatinine rise.

If severe, it can cause hydronephrosis (dilation of the kidney collecting system from backed-up urine).

---

### Azotemia vs uremia

Azotemia = abnormal lab finding.

Uremia = azotemia plus symptoms from retained toxins.

Uremia can cause:

* nausea/vomiting
* confusion
* pericarditis
* platelet dysfunction/bleeding

---

## One-line intuition

Azotemia means the kidney filter is not clearing nitrogen waste, either because blood is not reaching it, the kidney is damaged, or urine cannot drain.

---

## Pearl

Prerenal azotemia classically has BUN/Cr > 20 because low flow increases urea reabsorption, while creatinine stays mostly in the tubule.

### Core idea

Basal cell carcinoma is a slow-growing skin cancer that comes from basal cells.

Basal cells = cells in the deepest layer of the epidermis (outer skin layer).

So the name literally means:

* basal cell = bottom-layer skin cell
* carcinoma = cancer from epithelial cells (lining/surface cells)

---

### Classic appearance

Basal cell carcinoma often looks like a:

* pearly papule (shiny raised bump)
* rolled border
* central ulceration sometimes
* telangiectasias (small visible blood vessels)

Classic Step 1 phrase:

> pearly papule with telangiectasias

Most common location:

* sun-exposed skin, especially face/nose

---

### Why it happens

UV light (ultraviolet light from sun exposure) damages DNA in basal cells.

If DNA repair and growth-control genes are damaged, the basal cells keep dividing when they should stop.

That gives you a locally invasive tumor.

Important intuition:

* basal cell carcinoma grows locally
* it can invade nearby tissue
* but it rarely metastasizes (spreads to distant organs)

So it is common and destructive locally, but usually not a big metastasis cancer.

---

### Histology

On biopsy, basal cell carcinoma shows:

* nests of basaloid cells
* peripheral palisading
* clefting around tumor islands

Peripheral palisading = tumor cells line up around the edge like a fence.

That is why histology images often show dark blue tumor islands with the edge cells neatly arranged.

---

## One-line intuition

Basal cell carcinoma is a sun-related cancer of the bottom layer of the epidermis that classically forms a pearly, rolled, blood-vessel-covered bump and almost never metastasizes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Basal cell carcinoma is linked to PTCH1 mutation in the hedgehog signaling pathway.

HY association: Gorlin syndrome = inherited PTCH1 mutation causing multiple basal cell carcinomas, jaw cysts, and skeletal abnormalities.

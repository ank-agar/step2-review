### Core idea

A nasal polyp is a benign, soft, noncancerous growth of inflamed mucosa (mucous membrane lining) in the nose or paranasal sinuses (air-filled spaces around the nose).

Think:

> chronic inflammation makes the nasal lining swell outward like a grape.

---

### Why it happens

Chronic inflammation causes:

* edema (fluid swelling)
* mucosal overgrowth
* blockage of airflow and sinus drainage

So symptoms are usually:

* nasal obstruction
* decreased smell
* rhinorrhea (runny nose)
* postnasal drip (mucus dripping down the throat)
* recurrent sinusitis (sinus inflammation/infection)

---

### Important associations

Nasal polyps are classically associated with:

* asthma
* allergic rhinitis (allergic inflammation of the nose)
* chronic rhinosinusitis (long-term inflammation of the nose and sinuses)
* cystic fibrosis (CF; inherited chloride channel disease causing thick secretions)
* aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD; asthma + nasal polyps + aspirin sensitivity)

---

## One-line intuition

Nasal polyps are basically chronically inflamed nasal/sinus lining that balloons into the airway and blocks smell/airflow.

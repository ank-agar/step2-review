### Core idea

Cardiac arrest = the heart suddenly stops pumping blood effectively.

So the problem is not just “heart pain” — it is:

* no effective heartbeat
* no pulse
* no blood flow to brain/organs
* rapid loss of consciousness

This is immediately life-threatening.

---

### Why it happens

Most commonly, cardiac arrest happens because of a dangerous arrhythmia (abnormal heart rhythm).

The key Step 1 idea:

* the heart’s electrical system becomes chaotic
* the ventricles (main pumping chambers) do not squeeze normally
* blood stops moving forward

So even if the heart has electrical activity, it may not be producing a useful pulse.

---

### Classic rhythms

Important cardiac arrest rhythms:

* ventricular fibrillation = chaotic ventricular electrical activity
* pulseless ventricular tachycardia = very fast ventricular rhythm with no pulse
* asystole = flatline/no meaningful electrical activity
* PEA (pulseless electrical activity) = electrical activity is present, but there is no pulse

Ventricular fibrillation and pulseless ventricular tachycardia are “shockable” rhythms because defibrillation can reset the chaotic electrical activity.

---

### Cardiac arrest vs heart attack

Heart attack = MI (myocardial infarction), meaning blocked blood flow causes heart muscle injury.

Cardiac arrest = heart stops pumping effectively.

Intuition:

* heart attack = plumbing problem
* cardiac arrest = electrical/pump failure problem

A heart attack can cause cardiac arrest, but they are not the same thing.

---

### Why CPR helps

CPR (cardiopulmonary resuscitation) manually compresses the chest to squeeze the heart.

This does not “fix” the heart rhythm directly.

It buys time by pushing some blood to:

* brain
* heart muscle
* other vital organs

Then an AED (automated external defibrillator) can check the rhythm and shock if it is shockable.

---

## One-line intuition

Cardiac arrest is when the heart’s pump suddenly fails, so circulation stops and the brain loses oxygen within minutes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In cardiac arrest, defibrillation helps ventricular fibrillation and pulseless ventricular tachycardia, but not asystole or PEA (pulseless electrical activity).

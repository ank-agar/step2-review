### Core idea

Premature atrial contractions, or PACs (premature atrial contractions), are early heartbeats that start in the atria (top chambers of the heart) instead of waiting for the SA node (sinoatrial node, the normal pacemaker) to fire.

So the atrium basically says:

> "I fired too early."

That early atrial impulse usually travels through the AV node (atrioventricular node, the electrical gate between atria and ventricles) and creates a mostly normal-looking QRS complex (ventricular depolarization on ECG).

---

### What you see on ECG

Classic PAC findings:

* early beat
* abnormal-looking P wave (atrial depolarization wave)
* narrow QRS complex
* brief pause afterward

The QRS is usually narrow because the impulse still uses the normal His-Purkinje system (fast ventricular conduction pathway).

---

### Why the P wave looks weird

Normal P wave:

* starts in SA node
* spreads through atria in the usual direction

PAC P wave:

* starts from an ectopic atrial focus (random irritated atrial spot)
* spreads through atria from a different direction

Different direction = different P-wave shape.

---

### Why there is a pause after

The early atrial beat can reset the SA node.

So the SA node has to restart its timing, causing a short pause before the next normal beat.

Usually this pause is noncompensatory, meaning the total timing is not exactly equal to 2 normal cycles.

---

### PAC vs PVC

PAC (premature atrial contraction):

* early abnormal P wave
* narrow QRS
* starts above the ventricles

PVC (premature ventricular contraction):

* no preceding normal P wave
* wide QRS
* starts in the ventricles

Think:

* PAC = atria start early, ventricles still conduct normally
* PVC = ventricles start early, so QRS is wide and bizarre

---

## One-line intuition

A PAC is an early atrial "extra beat" that makes a weird P wave, but usually a normal narrow QRS because ventricular conduction is still normal.

---

## Pearl

PACs are usually benign, but frequent PACs can be a risk marker for atrial fibrillation, or AFib (irregularly irregular rhythm from chaotic atrial electrical activity).

## Adhesive capsulitis

Adhesive capsulitis = frozen shoulder.

It is inflammation + fibrosis (scar-like thickening) of the shoulder joint capsule.

Break it down:

* adhesive = sticky / stuck down
* capsule = connective tissue sleeve around the joint
* -itis = inflammation

So adhesive capsulitis literally means:

> inflamed shoulder capsule that becomes stiff, thick, and stuck down.

---

### Core idea

The shoulder joint capsule normally has enough loose space to let the humeral head (top of upper arm bone) move freely.

In adhesive capsulitis, the capsule:

* gets inflamed
* thickens
* contracts/shrinks
* forms adhesions (scar-like bands)

So the shoulder becomes painful and hard to move.

---

### Classic finding

Loss of both:

* active range of motion = patient moves it themselves
* passive range of motion = examiner moves it for them

That is the key.

If only active motion is limited but passive motion is okay, think more about rotator cuff tear.

If both active and passive motion are limited, the joint capsule itself is physically tight.

---

### Most limited motion

External rotation is usually hit hardest.

External rotation = rotating the arm outward, like opening your arm away from your belly.

Why?

Because the anterior/superior capsule and coracohumeral ligament (ligament from coracoid process to humerus) get tight, so the humeral head cannot rotate normally.

---

### Associations

Higher risk with:

* diabetes mellitus (chronic high blood sugar disease)
* thyroid disease
* age 40-60
* female sex
* prolonged shoulder immobilization after injury/surgery

Diabetes is the big Step 1 association.

---

## One-line intuition

The shoulder capsule turns from a loose flexible sleeve into a tight scarred sleeve, so the arm cannot rotate normally.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Adhesive capsulitis causes decreased active and passive range of motion, especially external rotation; rotator cuff tears mainly decrease active range of motion while passive range of motion is relatively preserved.

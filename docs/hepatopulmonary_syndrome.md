### Core idea

Hepatopulmonary syndrome is:

* liver disease
* low oxygen in the blood
* dilated blood vessels in the lungs

So basically:

> bad liver disease causes lung blood vessels to dilate too much, so blood passes through the lungs without picking up enough oxygen.

---

### Why oxygen drops

Normally, red blood cells move close to alveoli (air sacs) and pick up oxygen.

In hepatopulmonary syndrome:

* pulmonary vessels dilate
* blood flows too far from the alveolar oxygen
* blood moves through too quickly or bypasses good gas exchange

So you get a right-to-left shunt-like effect.

Right-to-left shunt means blood goes from the right side circulation to the left side circulation without being properly oxygenated.

---

### Classic clue

Symptoms can be worse when standing:

* platypnea = shortness of breath when upright
* orthodeoxia = low oxygen when upright

Why upright makes it worse:

* gravity sends more blood to the lower lungs
* the lower lungs have more dilated vessels
* more blood gets poorly oxygenated

So standing can drop oxygen saturation.

---

### Important distinction

Hepatopulmonary syndrome is different from portopulmonary hypertension.

* hepatopulmonary syndrome = pulmonary vasodilation (vessels too wide)
* portopulmonary hypertension = pulmonary vasoconstriction/remodeling (high pressure in lung vessels)

One-line contrast:

* hepatopulmonary syndrome makes lung vessels too open
* portopulmonary hypertension makes lung vessels too tight/high pressure

---

## One-line intuition

The liver fails, lung vessels dilate, blood cannot grab enough oxygen, and the patient becomes hypoxemic (low blood oxygen).

---

## HY pearl

Hepatopulmonary syndrome classically causes increased A-a gradient (alveolar-arterial gradient, the difference between oxygen in alveoli and oxygen in arterial blood) due to impaired oxygen transfer from lung to blood.

Toxic epidermal necrolysis, or TEN (toxic epidermal necrolysis), is a severe drug reaction where the epidermis (top layer of skin) dies and peels off.

Think:

* body reacts badly to a drug
* cytotoxic T cells (killer immune cells) attack keratinocytes (main skin cells)
* keratinocytes die
* epidermis separates from the dermis (deeper skin layer)
* skin sloughs off like a severe burn

---

### Why the skin peels

The key event is massive keratinocyte apoptosis (programmed cell death).

The immune system releases cytotoxic proteins like:

* granulysin
* perforin
* granzyme B
* Fas ligand

These make epidermal cells die in sheets.

So the skin does not peel because of infection directly.

It peels because the immune system kills the epidermis.

---

### Classic triggers

Common drug triggers:

* sulfonamides, like TMP-SMX (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole)
* allopurinol
* carbamazepine
* phenytoin
* lamotrigine
* NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs)

Usually starts 1-3 weeks after starting the medication.

---

### Presentation

TEN causes:

* fever and flu-like prodrome (early nonspecific symptoms)
* painful red/purplish rash
* bullae (blisters)
* skin sloughing
* mucosal involvement, especially mouth, eyes, and genitals
* positive Nikolsky sign (skin slips off with gentle pressure)

TEN involves more than 30% body surface area.

Compare:

* SJS (Stevens-Johnson syndrome) = less than 10%
* SJS/TEN overlap = 10-30%
* TEN = more than 30%

---

## One-line intuition

TEN is basically a life-threatening drug-triggered immune attack that makes the epidermis die and detach like a burn.

---

## Pearl

TEN is a type IV hypersensitivity reaction (delayed T-cell-mediated immune reaction), and the biggest immediate step is stopping the offending drug.

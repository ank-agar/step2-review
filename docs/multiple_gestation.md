Multiple gestation means pregnancy with more than one fetus, like:

* twins
* triplets
* higher-order multiples

The big idea:

> more than one fetus is growing in the uterus at the same time.

---

### Key words

**Zygosity** = whether the fetuses came from the same fertilized egg.

* **monozygotic** = one zygote (fertilized egg) splits → genetically identical twins
* **dizygotic** = two separate eggs fertilized by two separate sperm → fraternal twins

**Chorionicity** = number of chorions/placentas.

* **dichorionic** = two chorions, usually two placentas
* **monochorionic** = one chorion, shared placenta

**Amnionicity** = number of amniotic sacs.

* **diamniotic** = each fetus has its own amniotic sac
* **monoamniotic** = fetuses share one amniotic sac

---

### Why this matters

Multiple gestation increases risk of:

* **preterm labor** because the uterus gets stretched earlier
* **fetal growth restriction** because nutrients/placental space are shared
* **preeclampsia** because there is more placental mass
* **gestational diabetes mellitus** because placental hormones increase insulin resistance
* **postpartum hemorrhage** because an overdistended uterus may not contract well after delivery

Monochorionic twins are especially important because they share one placenta.

That can cause **TTTS (twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome)**:

* one twin donates too much blood
* the other twin receives too much blood

So one twin can become small/anemic, while the other can become volume overloaded.

---

## One-line intuition

Multiple gestation = more babies sharing the same pregnancy space, so complications come from crowding, shared placenta, and uterine overdistension.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On Step 1, multiple gestation can cause elevated **MSAFP (maternal serum alpha-fetoprotein)** because more fetuses make more **AFP (alpha-fetoprotein)**.

# Psychogenic nonepileptic seizures

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK557655/bin/nonepileptic__seizures.jpg)

Psychogenic nonepileptic seizures, or **PNES (psychogenic nonepileptic seizures)**, are seizure-like episodes that look like epileptic seizures but are **not caused by abnormal cortical electrical discharges**.

Key idea:

* looks like a seizure
* but **EEG (electroencephalogram, a test that records brain electrical activity)** does not show epileptic seizure activity during the event

---

### Break down the name

* **psychogenic** = arising from psychological/functional brain mechanisms
* **nonepileptic** = not due to epilepsy
* **seizure** = sudden episode of abnormal movement, awareness, sensation, or behavior

So:

> PNES = real seizure-like episodes, but the mechanism is functional/psychological rather than epileptic electrical firing.

Important: this does **not** mean the patient is faking.

---

### Why it happens

Think of the brain like:

* hardware = brain structure
* software = brain function/signaling

In PNES, the “hardware” may be structurally okay, but the “software” is misfiring under stress, trauma, emotion, or abnormal brain-network regulation.

That produces real physical symptoms:

* shaking
* unresponsiveness
* abnormal movements
* staring
* collapse-like episodes

but without the epileptic electrical storm seen in true epileptic seizures.

---

### How to diagnose

Best test:

* **video EEG monitoring**

This means the patient is recorded on video while EEG is also recording.

If the patient has their typical seizure-like event and the EEG does **not** show epileptic activity, that supports PNES.

---

### PNES vs epileptic seizure

Epileptic seizure:

* abnormal synchronized electrical firing in cortex
* EEG often shows epileptiform activity
* treated with antiseizure meds

PNES:

* functional/psychological brain-network problem
* EEG does not show epileptic seizure activity during the event
* treated with explanation, therapy, and addressing triggers

---

### Treatment intuition

Antiseizure drugs usually do **not** help PNES because the problem is not epileptic electrical discharge.

Treatment often involves:

* clear diagnosis explanation
* CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy, a structured therapy that changes thought-behavior patterns)
* treating anxiety, depression, PTSD (post-traumatic stress disorder), or trauma if present

---

## One-line intuition

PNES are real seizure-like attacks caused by dysfunctional brain signaling, not by epileptic electrical firing.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1/clinical pearl: the best diagnostic clue is a **typical event captured on video EEG with no epileptiform EEG correlate**.

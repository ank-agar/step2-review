### Core idea

Vibrio cholerae is a:

* gram-negative comma-shaped rod
* oxidase-positive
* motile with a polar flagellum
* water/seafood-associated bacterium

It causes cholera: massive watery diarrhea, classically "rice-water stool."

---

### Why the diarrhea happens

Vibrio cholerae releases cholera toxin, an AB toxin:

* A subunit = active/toxic part
* B subunit = binding part

The toxin ADP-ribosylates Gs protein (stimulatory G protein), which keeps adenylate cyclase turned on.

That causes:

* increased cAMP (cyclic adenosine monophosphate)
* increased CFTR (cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator) chloride secretion
* water follows chloride into the gut lumen

So the intestine is basically forced to dump salt and water outward.

---

### Why stool is watery, not bloody

Vibrio cholerae does not invade and destroy the intestinal mucosa.

It mainly changes ion transport.

So you get:

* watery diarrhea
* minimal fever
* no major blood/pus in stool

---

### Treatment intuition

Main treatment is rehydration:

* ORS (oral rehydration solution)
* IV (intravenous) fluids if severe

ORS works because glucose-sodium cotransport stays intact, so giving glucose helps pull sodium and water back into the body.

Antibiotics can shorten illness, but fluids are the lifesaving part.

---

## One-line intuition

Vibrio cholerae makes a toxin that locks Gs on, raises cAMP, opens chloride channels, and makes the gut pour out water.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Cholera toxin has the same cAMP-increasing mechanism as heat-labile toxin from ETEC (enterotoxigenic Escherichia coli): both cause watery diarrhea by increasing cAMP.

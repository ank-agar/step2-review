### Core idea

Discitis = inflammation/infection of the intervertebral disc (the cushion between vertebral bones in the spine).

Most of the time, think:

* bacteria seed the spine through the blood
* the disc/endplates get infected
* patient gets focal back pain

Endplates = the thin bone/cartilage borders between the vertebral body and the disc.

---

### Why it happens

In adults, the disc itself has poor blood supply.

So infection usually starts in the nearby vertebral endplate first, then spreads into the disc.

That is why discitis is often paired with vertebral osteomyelitis (infection of the vertebral bone).

Together this is called:

* spondylodiscitis = infection of vertebra + disc

Break the word:

* spondylo- = vertebra/spine
* discitis = inflamed/infected disc

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

* severe localized back pain
* pain with movement
* fever may or may not be present
* elevated ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate) and CRP (C-reactive protein)

ESR and CRP are inflammatory markers.

---

### Diagnosis

Best imaging:

* MRI (magnetic resonance imaging)

Why MRI?

* shows disc inflammation
* shows vertebral bone involvement
* shows epidural abscess if present

Epidural abscess = pus in the epidural space, which is the space outside the dura mater (outer covering of the spinal cord).

---

### Common organisms

Most common:

* Staphylococcus aureus

Risk associations:

* bloodstream infection
* recent spine surgery/procedure
* IV drug use (intravenous drug use)
* diabetes mellitus
* immunosuppression

---

## One-line intuition

Discitis is basically a spine infection centered on the disc space, usually spreading from infected adjacent vertebral endplates.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Back pain + fever + high ESR/CRP = think vertebral osteomyelitis/discitis, and get MRI because the feared complication is epidural abscess causing spinal cord compression.

### Core idea

A stasis ulcer is a chronic, poorly healing skin ulcer caused by venous stasis (blood pooling in veins).

Most commonly, it happens in the lower leg, especially near the medial malleolus (inner ankle bone).

---

### Why it happens

Normally, leg veins use valves to push blood upward back to the heart.

If the valves are damaged:

* blood falls backward
* pressure builds up in leg veins
* fluid leaks into tissue
* skin gets inflamed and poorly oxygenated
* eventually the skin breaks down into an ulcer

So the problem is not lack of arterial blood coming in.

It is poor venous drainage going out.

---

### Classic findings

Venous stasis ulcers are usually:

* shallow
* irregularly shaped
* near the medial malleolus (inner ankle bone)
* surrounded by edema (swelling)
* surrounded by brown hyperpigmentation

The brown color is from hemosiderin (iron pigment from broken-down red blood cells) leaking out of congested veins.

---

### Stasis dermatitis

Stasis dermatitis means skin inflammation from chronic venous stasis.

It can cause:

* itchy, red, scaly skin
* swelling
* brown discoloration
* eventual ulcer formation

Think:

> venous congestion irritates the skin until it breaks down.

---

## One-line intuition

A stasis ulcer is what happens when blood cannot drain well from the leg, so pressure and inflammation slowly damage the skin near the ankle.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Venous ulcers usually occur near the medial malleolus, while arterial ulcers are often painful, punched-out ulcers on distal toes/feet due to poor arterial blood supply.

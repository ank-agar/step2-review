## Pathologic fracture

### Core idea

A pathologic fracture is a bone fracture that happens because the bone was already weakened by disease.

So the trauma can be:

* minor
* low-energy
* sometimes basically absent

The key idea:

> the bone breaks too easily because the bone itself is abnormal.

---

### Why it happens

Normally, bone can tolerate a lot of force.

But if something weakens the bone structure, the same small force can now break it.

Common causes:

* metastatic cancer = cancer spread to bone
* multiple myeloma = plasma cell cancer that causes punched-out bone lesions
* osteoporosis = low bone mass
* Paget disease = abnormal bone remodeling
* bone cysts or benign bone tumors
* osteomyelitis = bone infection

---

### Intuition

Think of bone like a wall.

Normal wall + small push = fine.

Wall with a hole in it + small push = cracks.

That "hole" can be a lytic lesion (bone-destroying lesion), tumor, cyst, infection, or severe loss of bone density.

---

### What it looks like clinically

Classic presentation:

* sudden localized bone pain
* fracture after minimal trauma
* fracture through an abnormal-looking bone lesion on X-ray

Common sites:

* vertebrae
* femur
* humerus
* ribs

---

### Step 1 framing

If the stem says:

* older patient
* history of cancer
* bone pain
* fracture after a small fall
* lytic or blastic bone lesions

think:

> pathologic fracture from bone metastasis until proven otherwise.

---

## One-line intuition

A pathologic fracture is when the bone breaks because disease weakened it first, not because the trauma was strong.

---

## Pearl

Multiple myeloma classically causes lytic bone lesions, hypercalcemia, anemia, renal dysfunction, and bone pain/fractures.

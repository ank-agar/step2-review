## Mycoplasma pneumoniae

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil///PHIL_Images/22880/22880_lores.jpg)

![Image](https://phil.cdc.gov//PHIL_Images/21528/21528_lores.jpg)

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/PHIL_Images/24528/24528_lores.jpg)

Mycoplasma pneumoniae is a tiny atypical bacterium that causes:

* walking pneumonia
* tracheobronchitis
* upper respiratory infection

Atypical pneumonia means pneumonia that does not look like classic lobar bacterial pneumonia.

---

### Core idea

Mycoplasma pneumoniae has **no cell wall**.

So it is resistant to beta-lactams (antibiotics that attack bacterial cell walls), like:

* penicillin
* amoxicillin
* cephalosporins

Why?

Because beta-lactams need a peptidoglycan cell wall target, and Mycoplasma does not have one.

---

### What it causes

Classically:

* young adult or school-age patient
* dry cough
* mild fever
* gradual symptoms
* patient looks less sick than the chest x-ray suggests

That is why it is called **walking pneumonia**:

> the patient has pneumonia but is still “walking around.”

---

### Important associations

Mycoplasma pneumoniae can cause extrapulmonary findings, meaning symptoms outside the lungs:

* cold agglutinin autoimmune hemolytic anemia
* erythema multiforme
* Stevens-Johnson syndrome

Cold agglutinins are antibodies, usually IgM (immunoglobulin M), that bind red blood cells in colder temperatures and can cause hemolysis (red blood cell destruction).

---

### Treatment idea

Use antibiotics that do **not** target the cell wall, like:

* macrolides, such as azithromycin
* doxycycline
* respiratory fluoroquinolones

---

## One-line intuition

Mycoplasma pneumoniae is the “no cell wall” atypical pneumonia bug that causes a dry cough in young people and does not respond to beta-lactams.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Mycoplasma pneumoniae is associated with **cold agglutinin disease**, which can cause autoimmune hemolytic anemia after pneumonia.

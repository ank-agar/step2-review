A **post-term infant** is a baby born at **42 weeks gestation or later**.

Gestation = how far along the pregnancy is.

So:

* term = normal timing
* late-term = close to post-term
* post-term = **too long in utero**

---

### Core idea

The baby stayed in the uterus longer than usual, so the placenta can start to become less efficient.

Placenta = organ that gives the fetus oxygen and nutrients.

If the placenta gets old/less effective:

* less oxygen delivery
* less nutrient delivery
* less fetal fat/glycogen storage
* more stress on the fetus

Glycogen = stored glucose.

---

### What the baby may look like

Post-term infants can have:

* dry, peeling, wrinkled skin
* long fingernails
* less subcutaneous fat
* alert, mature appearance
* meconium staining

Subcutaneous fat = fat under the skin.

Meconium = the baby’s first stool.

---

### Why the peeling/wrinkled skin happens

The baby has been sitting in amniotic fluid longer.

Amniotic fluid = fluid surrounding the fetus.

Normally, vernix protects the skin.

Vernix = white, greasy protective coating on newborn skin.

But post-term babies have **less vernix**, so the skin is more exposed to fluid and can look:

* dry
* cracked
* peeling
* “old-looking”

---

### Main risks

The big complications are:

* **meconium aspiration syndrome** = baby inhales meconium-stained fluid into the lungs
* **hypoglycemia** = low blood glucose
* **perinatal asphyxia** = low oxygen around birth
* **oligohydramnios** = low amniotic fluid

Why hypoglycemia?

Because placental insufficiency causes poor nutrient delivery, so the baby may have low glycogen stores. After birth, they cannot maintain glucose well.

---

## One-line intuition

A post-term infant is an “overdue” baby: more mature-looking outside, but at risk because the placenta may be aging and under-delivering oxygen/nutrients.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Post-term infant on exams = think **dry peeling skin + long nails + meconium aspiration risk + neonatal hypoglycemia**.

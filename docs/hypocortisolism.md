# Hypocortisolism

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/8720.jpg)

### Core idea

Hypocortisolism means **too little cortisol**.

Cortisol is a glucocorticoid (steroid hormone from the adrenal cortex) that helps the body:

* maintain blood pressure
* maintain blood glucose
* respond to stress
* regulate inflammation

So if cortisol is low, the patient cannot handle stress well.

---

### The axis

Think:

**Hypothalamus → pituitary → adrenal cortex → cortisol**

More specifically:

* hypothalamus releases **CRH** (corticotropin-releasing hormone)
* anterior pituitary releases **ACTH** (adrenocorticotropic hormone)
* adrenal cortex makes cortisol

Cortisol then gives negative feedback, meaning it tells the brain/pituitary to slow down CRH and ACTH.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Low cortisol causes:

* **fatigue/weakness:** low cortisol = poor stress-energy response
* **hypoglycemia:** cortisol normally helps raise blood glucose
* **hypotension:** cortisol helps blood vessels respond to catecholamines (epinephrine/norepinephrine)
* **weight loss/anorexia:** chronic adrenal hormone deficiency makes the body catabolic and low-energy

If it is **primary adrenal insufficiency** (adrenal gland problem), aldosterone is often low too.

Low aldosterone causes:

* **hyponatremia:** low sodium because aldosterone normally retains sodium
* **hyperkalemia:** high potassium because aldosterone normally excretes potassium
* **salt craving/hypotension:** body is trying to replace lost sodium and volume

---

### Primary vs secondary

**Primary hypocortisolism** = adrenal gland cannot make cortisol.

Classic example:

* Addison disease = primary adrenal insufficiency, often autoimmune destruction of adrenal cortex

Labs:

* low cortisol
* high ACTH
* often low aldosterone
* high potassium

Why high ACTH?

The adrenal is failing, so cortisol is low. Low cortisol removes negative feedback, so the pituitary keeps yelling louder with ACTH.

---

**Secondary hypocortisolism** = pituitary does not make enough ACTH.

Labs:

* low cortisol
* low ACTH
* aldosterone usually normal
* potassium usually normal

Why is aldosterone usually normal?

Aldosterone is controlled mostly by the RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system), not ACTH.

---

### Hyperpigmentation

Hyperpigmentation means darkening of skin or mucosa.

This is mainly in **primary** adrenal insufficiency.

Reason:

Low cortisol → high ACTH.

ACTH comes from **POMC** (pro-opiomelanocortin), the same precursor that can also increase **MSH** (melanocyte-stimulating hormone).

MSH stimulates melanocytes (pigment-producing skin cells), causing darker skin, especially:

* palmar creases
* gums/oral mucosa
* elbows/knees
* scars

---

## One-line intuition

Hypocortisolism = the body loses its stress hormone, so it becomes weak, hypotensive, hypoglycemic, and bad at handling illness.

---

## HY pearl

Primary adrenal insufficiency has **high ACTH + hyperpigmentation + hyperkalemia**.

Secondary adrenal insufficiency has **low ACTH**, so usually **no hyperpigmentation** and **no hyperkalemia**.

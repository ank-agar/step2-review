### Core idea

Bacterial endocarditis is infection of the endocardium (inner lining of the heart), usually involving the heart valves.

The classic thing it forms is a:

* vegetation = clump of bacteria + fibrin (clot protein) + platelets on a valve

Think:

> bacteria stick to a damaged valve and build a dirty clot.

---

### Why it happens

Valves usually do not have great blood supply, so once bacteria attach there, immune cells and antibiotics have a harder time reaching the infection.

The usual setup is:

* damaged valve surface
* bacteria enter the blood
* bacteria stick to the valve
* clot material builds around them
* vegetation grows

Common ways bacteria enter blood:

* dental work or poor dentition
* IV (intravenous) drug use
* indwelling catheters
* bacteremia (bacteria in the blood)

---

### Big organisms to know

* Staph aureus: acute, aggressive; common in IV (intravenous) drug use
* Strep viridans: after dental procedures; sticks to damaged valves
* Enterococcus: after GI (gastrointestinal) or GU (genitourinary) procedures
* Strep bovis: associated with colon cancer
* HACEK organisms: slow-growing gram-negative oral flora

HACEK = Haemophilus, Aggregatibacter, Cardiobacterium, Eikenella, Kingella.

---

### Why the findings happen

* Fever: infection in the blood
* New murmur: vegetation damages the valve, causing regurgitation (backward blood flow)
* Stroke or splenic infarct: pieces of vegetation break off and embolize (travel through blood and block vessels)
* Janeway lesions: painless palm/sole lesions from septic emboli (infected clots)
* Osler nodes: painful finger/toe nodules from immune complex deposition
* Roth spots: retinal hemorrhages with pale centers
* Glomerulonephritis: immune complexes deposit in kidneys

---

### Acute vs subacute

Acute bacterial endocarditis:

* fast and destructive
* often Staph aureus
* can infect even normal valves

Subacute bacterial endocarditis:

* slower course
* often Strep viridans
* usually needs a previously damaged valve

---

## One-line intuition

Bacterial endocarditis is bacteria hiding in clot-like vegetations on heart valves, causing fever, murmurs, emboli, and immune-complex findings.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember:

* IV (intravenous) drug use → Staph aureus → tricuspid valve endocarditis
* dental procedure → Strep viridans → damaged valve endocarditis
* Strep bovis endocarditis → think colon cancer

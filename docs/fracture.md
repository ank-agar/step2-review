A fracture means a break or crack in a bone.

It can be:

* complete: bone breaks all the way through
* incomplete: bone cracks/bends but does not fully break
* open/compound: bone communicates with outside through skin
* closed/simple: skin stays intact
* comminuted: bone breaks into multiple pieces
* displaced: bone pieces are no longer aligned

---

### Core idea

Bone is strong, but it fails when force exceeds its ability to absorb stress.

That force can be:

* acute trauma, like a fall or crash
* repetitive stress, causing a stress fracture
* weak bone, causing a pathologic fracture (fracture through diseased or fragile bone)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Pain:** periosteum (outer bone covering) has pain fibers
* **Swelling/bruising:** broken bone and nearby soft tissue bleed
* **Deformity:** displaced fragments pull out of alignment
* **Loss of function:** muscle contraction and weight bearing move the broken pieces, causing pain/instability
* **Crepitus:** broken fragments can grind against each other

---

### Healing

Bone heals by forming:

* hematoma (blood clot)
* soft callus
* hard callus
* remodeled bone

So the body first stabilizes the break with clot/inflammation, then slowly replaces it with stronger bone.

---

## One-line intuition

A fracture is bone losing the mechanical fight against stress, causing a crack, break, or shattered segment.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Fat embolism syndrome can occur after long-bone fractures, especially femur fractures, and classically causes:

* respiratory distress
* neurologic symptoms
* petechial rash

Think: broken marrow fat enters blood and embolizes to the lungs/brain.

# Selective mutism

### Core idea

Selective mutism is an anxiety disorder where a child **can speak normally in some settings** but **cannot speak in specific social settings**, like school.

So the issue is not:

* lack of speech ability
* defiance
* not knowing the language

It is more like:

> anxiety becomes so high that speech “freezes.”

---

### Classic presentation

A child:

* talks normally at home
* stays silent at school
* may use gestures, nodding, pointing, or whispering
* often looks very shy or “stuck”

Key point:

* they are **not choosing silence**
* they are **unable to speak in that situation**

---

### Why it happens

Selective mutism is strongly related to **social anxiety disorder** (fear/anxiety around social interaction or being judged).

In scary social settings:

* sympathetic nervous system activates
* child feels watched or pressured
* avoidance lowers anxiety short-term
* the silence gets reinforced

So the brain learns:

> “If I do not speak, the anxiety drops.”

That is why forcing the child to speak often makes it worse.

---

### Diagnosis idea

DSM (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders) concept:

* consistent failure to speak in specific social situations
* despite speaking in other situations
* interferes with school/social functioning
* lasts at least 1 month
* not just due to language unfamiliarity or another communication disorder

---

### Treatment

Main treatment is CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy), especially gradual exposure.

This means:

* start with low-pressure communication
* reward small progress
* slowly move toward harder speaking situations

SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors), like fluoxetine, can be used if anxiety is severe.

---

## One-line intuition

Selective mutism = **the child has the ability to talk, but anxiety blocks speech in specific settings.**

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: selective mutism is classified as an **anxiety disorder**, not a speech production disorder.

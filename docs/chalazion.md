### Core idea

A chalazion is a chronic, usually painless eyelid lump from blockage of a meibomian gland (oil gland in the eyelid).

The meibomian gland normally secretes oily material into the tear film.

If the duct gets blocked:

* oil backs up
* the gland swells
* inflammation forms a firm nodule

So think:

> blocked eyelid oil gland = retained lipid lump.

---

### Why it is usually painless

A chalazion is usually chronic granulomatous inflammation.

Granulomatous inflammation means macrophages (immune cells that clean up debris) wall off material that is hard to clear.

Here, the hard-to-clear material is trapped lipid/oil.

That is why it tends to be:

* slow-growing
* firm
* less tender than an acute infection

---

### Chalazion vs stye

* Chalazion = blocked meibomian gland, usually painless, chronic
* Stye/hordeolum = acute infection of an eyelid gland, usually painful and tender

Easy intuition:

* chalazion = clogged oil
* stye = infected gland

---

### Treatment intuition

Warm compresses help because heat softens the thickened oil so it can drain more easily.

If it persists, it can be treated with steroid injection or incision and drainage.

---

## One-line intuition

A chalazion is basically a clogged eyelid oil gland that turns into a firm, usually painless inflammatory bump.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Recurrent chalazion in an older adult should raise concern for sebaceous carcinoma (malignant tumor of sebaceous/oil glands), especially if it keeps coming back in the same spot.

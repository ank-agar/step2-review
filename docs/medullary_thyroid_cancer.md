### Core idea

Medullary thyroid cancer is a thyroid cancer that comes from **parafollicular C cells** (calcitonin-secreting cells in the thyroid).

These cells make **calcitonin** (a hormone that lowers blood calcium, though usually not enough to cause major hypocalcemia).

So the big association is:

* medullary thyroid cancer
* increased calcitonin
* amyloid deposits in the tumor

---

### Why calcitonin matters

C cells normally secrete calcitonin.

So when C cells become cancerous:

* the tumor can release lots of calcitonin
* serum calcitonin becomes a tumor marker
* calcitonin-derived protein can deposit as amyloid

Amyloid means misfolded protein deposits that look pink/eosinophilic on histology.

---

### Classic pathology

On histology, think:

* nests/sheets of neuroendocrine tumor cells
* amyloid stroma
* positive calcitonin staining

Neuroendocrine means hormone-secreting cells with nervous-system-like secretory granules.

---

### MEN association

Medullary thyroid cancer is strongly associated with **MEN 2** (multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2).

MEN 2 is caused by activating mutations in **RET** (a proto-oncogene, meaning a normal growth gene that can become cancer-driving when mutated).

MEN 2A:

* medullary thyroid cancer
* pheochromocytoma
* parathyroid hyperplasia

MEN 2B:

* medullary thyroid cancer
* pheochromocytoma
* mucosal neuromas
* marfanoid habitus

Marfanoid habitus means a tall, thin body shape with long limbs/fingers, similar to Marfan syndrome.

---

## One-line intuition

Medullary thyroid cancer is a **C-cell tumor**, so it makes **calcitonin**, deposits **amyloid**, and makes you think **MEN 2/RET**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Medullary thyroid cancer does **not** come from thyroid follicular cells, so it does **not** take up radioactive iodine well.

That is why Step 1 loves contrasting it with papillary/follicular thyroid cancers, which are follicular-cell derived.

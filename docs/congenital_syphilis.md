### Core idea

Congenital syphilis is syphilis passed from pregnant parent to fetus across the placenta.

Syphilis is caused by **Treponema pallidum**.

Break it down:

* congenital = present from birth
* syphilis = sexually transmitted infection caused by a spirochete
* spirochete = spiral-shaped bacterium

So congenital syphilis = baby gets infected before birth.

---

### What happens

Early congenital syphilis can look like secondary syphilis in a newborn:

* snuffles = syphilitic rhinitis, meaning runny/congested nose from infection
* rash, especially palms and soles
* hepatosplenomegaly = enlarged liver and spleen
* jaundice = yellow skin/eyes from bilirubin buildup
* anemia = low red blood cells
* bone inflammation

Late congenital syphilis causes the classic chronic findings:

* Hutchinson teeth = notched incisors
* saddle nose = collapsed nasal bridge
* saber shins = anterior bowing of tibia
* interstitial keratitis = corneal inflammation
* CN VIII deafness = vestibulocochlear nerve-related hearing loss

---

### Why the findings happen

Treponema pallidum spreads through the fetal bloodstream and causes widespread inflammation.

That inflammation damages:

* nasal mucosa -> snuffles
* liver/spleen -> hepatosplenomegaly and jaundice
* skin -> diffuse rash, often palms/soles
* bone/periosteum -> saber shins and pseudoparalysis
* teeth development -> Hutchinson teeth and mulberry molars
* eye/ear structures -> keratitis and deafness

Pseudoparalysis means the baby does not move a limb because it hurts, not because the nerve is actually paralyzed.

---

## One-line intuition

Untreated maternal syphilis crosses the placenta and causes a baby-wide inflammatory infection that hits skin, bones, liver, nose, teeth, eyes, and ears.

## Hyperthermia

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459311/bin/Cooling_Techniques_For_Hyperthermia_-01.jpg)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/media/images/2024/04/beat-the-heat.JPG)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/media/images/2024/08/Heat-Related-Illness.jpg)

Hyperthermia means abnormally high body temperature because the body cannot get rid of heat fast enough.

Break the word apart:

* hyper- = high
* therm = heat
* -ia = condition

So:

> high-heat body state.

Key idea:

* **hyperthermia** = increased body temperature from failed heat loss
* **fever** = increased body temperature because the hypothalamic set point is raised

Hypothalamus = brain temperature-control center.

---

### Why it happens

Normally, the body cools itself by:

* sweating
* vasodilation (widening blood vessels near the skin)
* moving heat from core organs to the skin

In hyperthermia, heat production or heat exposure overwhelms cooling.

Examples:

* hot environment
* intense exercise
* dehydration
* anticholinergic drugs (drugs that block parasympathetic sweating signals)
* malignant hyperthermia (dangerous inherited reaction to certain anesthetics)

---

### Why it is dangerous

High heat damages proteins and cell membranes.

That can cause:

* confusion/seizures from brain dysfunction
* rhabdomyolysis (skeletal muscle breakdown)
* acute kidney injury from myoglobin (muscle protein released into blood)
* disseminated intravascular coagulation, or DIC (widespread clotting factor activation)

---

## One-line intuition

Hyperthermia is not the body “choosing” a higher temperature like fever; it is the body losing control of heat balance.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For heat stroke, the board-style clue is hyperthermia plus CNS dysfunction (central nervous system dysfunction), like confusion, seizures, or coma.

# Vaginismus

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f9/1116_Muscle_of_the_Female_Perineum.png)

![Image](https://dccdn.de/www.doccheck.com/data/ne/mj/96/dq/g7/rn/beckenbodenmuskulatur-der-frau_lg.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Plastic_and_Silicone_vaginal_dilators.jpg/1280px-Plastic_and_Silicone_vaginal_dilators.jpg)

Vaginismus is involuntary tightening/spasm of the pelvic floor muscles around the vagina, especially when penetration is attempted.

Pelvic floor muscles = muscles that support pelvic organs and surround openings like the urethra (pee tube), vagina, and anus.

---

### Core idea

The vagina itself is not usually “blocked.”

Instead, the surrounding pelvic floor muscles reflexively clamp down, making penetration:

* painful
* difficult
* sometimes impossible

Think:

> the muscles around the vaginal opening act like a guard reflex.

---

### Why the pain happens

When penetration is anticipated, the nervous system can trigger pelvic floor contraction.

That contraction narrows the vaginal opening and increases pressure/tension, so insertion feels like:

* burning
* stinging
* hitting a wall
* severe tightness

Pain then reinforces the fear/guarding reflex:

pain -> anxiety/anticipation -> muscle spasm -> more pain

So it becomes a self-reinforcing loop.

---

### Important distinction

Vaginismus is different from dyspareunia (painful sex/intercourse).

* **Dyspareunia:** pain with sex, many possible causes
* **Vaginismus:** pain/difficulty mainly from involuntary pelvic floor muscle tightening

They can overlap.

---

### Treatment intuition

Treatment is about teaching the pelvic floor to relax instead of reflexively guarding.

Common approaches:

* pelvic floor physical therapy
* gradual vaginal dilators
* relaxation/breathing training
* CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy, therapy that changes fear/thought-behavior loops), especially if anxiety or trauma is involved

---

## One-line intuition

Vaginismus = the pelvic floor involuntarily “closes the gate” around the vagina during attempted penetration.

---

## Pearl 🔥

DSM-5 (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th edition) groups vaginismus under **genito-pelvic pain/penetration disorder**, along with penetration pain and fear/anxiety about penetration.

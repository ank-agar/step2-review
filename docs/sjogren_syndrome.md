# Sjogren syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/df/Blausen_0780_SalivaryGlands.png)

![Image](https://iowaprotocols.medicine.uiowa.edu/sites/iowaprotocols.medicine.uiowa.edu/files/styles/large/public/2025-06/wysiwyg_uploads/sjogren%20lobule_0.jpg?itok=olI-X4OI)

### Core idea

Sjogren syndrome is an autoimmune disease where immune cells attack exocrine glands (glands that secrete fluid onto body surfaces).

Main glands hit:

* lacrimal glands (tear glands)
* salivary glands (spit glands)

So the classic symptoms are:

* dry eyes
* dry mouth

This is called sicca symptoms (dryness symptoms).

---

### Why the findings happen

Immune cells, especially lymphocytes (white blood cells involved in adaptive immunity), infiltrate and damage the glands.

So:

* lacrimal gland damage -> less tears -> dry, gritty eyes
* salivary gland damage -> less saliva -> dry mouth
* less saliva -> more dental caries (tooth cavities), because saliva normally protects teeth
* salivary gland inflammation -> parotid gland enlargement

One-line mechanism:

> immune attack on tear + saliva glands = dry eyes + dry mouth.

---

### Antibodies

Classic antibodies:

* anti-SSA/Ro (autoantibody against Sjogren syndrome antigen A/Ro)
* anti-SSB/La (autoantibody against Sjogren syndrome antigen B/La)

It can be primary or secondary.

Secondary means it occurs with another autoimmune disease, especially:

* rheumatoid arthritis
* systemic lupus erythematosus

---

### Big complication

Sjogren syndrome increases risk of B-cell lymphoma (cancer of B lymphocytes).

Why?

Chronic immune stimulation keeps B cells activated for years, which increases the chance of malignant transformation.

---

## One-line intuition

Sjogren syndrome is basically autoimmune "dry gland disease": the immune system attacks glands that make tears and saliva.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association: Sjogren syndrome can cause renal tubular acidosis type 1 (distal renal tubular acidosis), which means the kidney cannot acidify urine properly.

### Core idea

Anal polyps are small tissue growths in or near the anal canal.

Polyp just means:

* a projecting growth from a mucosal surface

In the anal canal, these can be:

* benign inflammatory tissue
* hypertrophied anal papillae (enlarged normal anal papillae near the dentate line)
* skin tags
* adenomatous polyps (gland-forming precancerous polyps)

---

### Why they happen

Anal polyps often form because of chronic irritation/inflammation.

Common causes:

* chronic anal fissure (small tear in the anal lining)
* hemorrhoids
* repeated trauma from hard stool/constipation
* inflammatory bowel disease, or IBD (Crohn disease or ulcerative colitis)

The idea is:

> repeated irritation makes tissue overgrow into a little bump.

---

### Symptoms

They may cause:

* rectal bleeding
* feeling of a lump
* mucus discharge
* itching/irritation
* prolapse, meaning the tissue sticks out through the anus

But many are found incidentally on anoscopy (scope exam of the anal canal) or colonoscopy (scope exam of the colon).

---

### Why doctors care

Most anal polyps are benign, but they need to be distinguished from:

* hemorrhoids
* anal warts from HPV (human papillomavirus)
* rectal/anal cancer
* adenomatous colorectal polyps

If the polyp is adenomatous, it matters because adenomas are premalignant, meaning they can progress toward cancer over time.

---

## One-line intuition

Anal polyps are little tissue overgrowths near the anus, usually from irritation, but biopsy/removal may be needed because some polyps can be precancerous.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: adenomatous polyps are neoplastic (new abnormal growth) and can progress to colorectal adenocarcinoma through APC → KRAS → p53 mutations.

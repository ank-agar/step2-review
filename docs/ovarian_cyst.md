An ovarian cyst is a fluid-filled sac on or inside the ovary.

Most are functional cysts, meaning they come from the normal menstrual cycle:

* follicular cyst: follicle does not rupture and release the egg
* corpus luteum cyst: corpus luteum seals back up and fills with fluid or blood

Break it down:

* ovarian = related to the ovary
* cyst = fluid-filled sac

So:

> ovarian cyst = a fluid-filled sac involving the ovary.

### Why it happens

Normally, a follicle grows, releases an egg, then shrinks.

If that process gets stuck:

* follicle keeps enlarging → follicular cyst
* corpus luteum bleeds or traps fluid → corpus luteum cyst / hemorrhagic cyst

That is why many ovarian cysts are benign and self-resolve.

### Symptoms

Often asymptomatic.

If symptomatic:

* unilateral pelvic pain
* bloating/fullness
* pain with sex
* menstrual irregularity

If rupture happens:

* sudden sharp pelvic pain
* free fluid/blood can irritate the peritoneum (abdominal lining)

### Big complication to know

Large ovarian cysts can predispose to ovarian torsion.

Torsion means the ovary twists around its blood supply.

Why painful?

* twisting blocks venous drainage first
* ovary becomes swollen/congested
* arterial flow can later be compromised
* ischemia (low blood flow injury) causes severe acute pain

### One-line intuition

An ovarian cyst is usually a normal ovulation structure that failed to regress properly and ballooned into a fluid-filled sac.

## Pearl 🔥

Functional ovarian cysts are usually benign and related to ovulation, but acute unilateral pelvic pain + nausea/vomiting + enlarged ovary should make you think ovarian torsion.

# Carpal tunnel syndrome in pregnancy

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Carpal-Tunnel.svg/960px-Carpal-Tunnel.svg.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/1081.jpg)

![Image](https://d38sso7f6qz01j.cloudfront.net/original_images/Carpal_Tunnell_YPM_600.jpg)

Carpal tunnel syndrome in pregnancy is compression of the **median nerve** inside the **carpal tunnel**.

Median nerve = nerve that gives sensation to the:

* thumb
* index finger
* middle finger
* lateral half of ring finger

Carpal tunnel = narrow space in the wrist made of bones + the transverse carpal ligament.

---

### Core idea

Pregnancy causes increased fluid retention and swelling.

That swelling increases pressure inside the tight carpal tunnel.

Since the tunnel cannot expand much, the median nerve gets squeezed.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Numbness/tingling:** compressed median nerve cannot carry sensory signals normally
* **Pain worse at night:** wrist flexion during sleep further narrows the carpal tunnel
* **Weak grip or clumsiness:** median nerve also helps control thenar muscles (thumb muscles)
* **Symptoms in thumb/index/middle fingers:** those are the classic median nerve sensory areas

Important: the **thenar eminence** (thumb muscle mound on the palm) is usually spared sensory-wise because the palmar cutaneous branch of the median nerve branches off before the carpal tunnel.

---

### Pregnancy connection

This is common in late pregnancy because edema (fluid swelling) is higher.

Usually it improves after delivery as pregnancy-related fluid retention goes down.

Treatment is usually conservative:

* nighttime wrist splint in neutral position
* avoiding prolonged wrist flexion
* elevation/cold packs if swollen

---

## One-line intuition

Pregnancy makes the wrist tunnel “too crowded” from fluid swelling, so the median nerve gets pinched.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Carpal tunnel syndrome affects the **median nerve**, so think:

* numbness in digits 1-3
* possible thenar weakness
* symptoms often worse at night

But sensory loss over the central palm/thenar eminence points away from isolated carpal tunnel syndrome because that branch bypasses the tunnel.

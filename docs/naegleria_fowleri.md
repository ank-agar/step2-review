## Naegleria fowleri

Naegleria fowleri is a free-living amoeba found in warm freshwater.

It causes primary amebic meningoencephalitis (PAM), which is a rapidly fatal infection of the brain and meninges (protective coverings of the brain and spinal cord).

---

### Core idea

This organism enters through the nose, not by drinking water.

Route:

* warm freshwater enters nose
* amoeba crosses nasal mucosa (lining of the nose)
* travels along the olfactory nerve (cranial nerve I, smell nerve)
* reaches the brain
* destroys brain tissue

That is why it is called the "brain-eating amoeba."

---

### Why the symptoms happen

It causes acute meningoencephalitis:

* meningitis = inflammation of meninges
* encephalitis = inflammation of brain tissue

So symptoms look like severe bacterial meningitis:

* fever
* headache
* nausea/vomiting
* stiff neck
* confusion
* seizures
* coma

The key difference is the exposure history:

* recent swimming/diving in warm freshwater
* water forced up the nose

---

### Life cycle forms

Naegleria fowleri has 3 forms:

* trophozoite = active feeding form
* flagellate = temporary motile form with flagella (tail-like structures)
* cyst = dormant environmental form

The trophozoite is the infective form in humans.

Important: cysts are environmental and are not usually seen in brain tissue.

---

### Diagnosis

Look for trophozoites in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF, fluid around the brain and spinal cord).

CSF can show:

* high neutrophils
* high protein
* low glucose
* motile amoebae on wet mount

That can mimic bacterial meningitis, so the exposure history matters a lot.

---

## One-line intuition

Naegleria fowleri is a warm freshwater amoeba that shoots up the nose, follows the smell nerve to the brain, and causes rapidly fatal meningoencephalitis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Naegleria fowleri infection is from water going up the nose, not from drinking contaminated water.

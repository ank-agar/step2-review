## Male breast cancer

### Core idea

Male breast cancer is cancer arising from the small amount of breast tissue behind the nipple.

Even though males do not have developed milk-producing breasts, they still have:

* ducts
* fatty tissue
* connective tissue
* lymph vessels/nodes

So those cells can still become malignant (cancerous).

---

### Most common type

Most male breast cancers are:

* invasive ductal carcinoma

Meaning:

* ductal = starts in breast ducts
* carcinoma = cancer from epithelial cells (lining cells)
* invasive = it has broken out of the duct into surrounding tissue

Lobular carcinoma (cancer from milk-producing glands called lobules) is rare in males because males have very few lobules.

---

### Classic presentation

Usually:

* painless firm lump
* under or near the nipple
* one-sided

Other clues:

* nipple retraction (nipple pulled inward)
* bloody nipple discharge
* skin dimpling
* ulceration (open sore)
* axillary lymph node swelling (swollen lymph nodes in the armpit)

---

### Why it often presents near the nipple

Male breast tissue is concentrated right behind the nipple/areola (darker skin around the nipple).

So if cancer forms in that small ductal tissue, it is often felt as a subareolar mass (mass under the areola).

---

### Risk factors

Big ones:

* older age
* BRCA2 mutation (inherited DNA repair gene mutation)
* family history of breast/ovarian cancer
* Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY chromosome pattern)
* increased estrogen (female-type sex hormone) exposure
* liver disease
* obesity
* prior chest radiation

Reasoning:

More estrogen stimulation or impaired DNA repair increases the chance that ductal cells accumulate mutations and become cancerous.

---

### Diagnosis/treatment idea

Workup usually uses:

* mammogram (breast X-ray)
* ultrasound (sound-wave imaging)
* biopsy (tissue sample to look for cancer cells)

Treatment often includes:

* surgery, often mastectomy (removal of breast tissue)
* radiation
* chemotherapy
* endocrine therapy (hormone-blocking treatment)

Many male breast cancers are estrogen receptor positive, meaning estrogen can stimulate their growth.

---

## One-line intuition

Men have a small amount of breast duct tissue behind the nipple, and that duct tissue can become breast cancer, usually as a firm painless subareolar lump.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Male breast cancer is strongly associated with BRCA2 mutation, and most cases are estrogen receptor positive, so hormone-blocking therapy like tamoxifen is commonly useful.

### Core idea

Tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy means:

* a persistently fast heart rhythm
* weakens the myocardium (heart muscle)
* causing a dilated cardiomyopathy pattern
* that can improve after controlling the rhythm/rate.

Tachycardia = fast heart rate.  
Cardiomyopathy = disease/weakness of heart muscle.

---

### Why the heart gets weak

If the heart is beating too fast for too long, it has less time to:

* fill during diastole (relaxation phase)
* perfuse itself through coronary arteries
* generate efficient contractions
* recover energy stores

So the myocardium gets chronically overworked.

Over time, this causes:

* LV dilation (left ventricular enlargement)
* decreased EF (ejection fraction; percent of blood pumped out per beat)
* systolic dysfunction (poor contraction)
* heart failure symptoms

Common triggers include:

* atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response
* atrial flutter
* supraventricular tachycardia
* ventricular tachycardia
* frequent PVCs (premature ventricular contractions)

---

### Key clue

The big clue is:

> weak heart + persistent tachyarrhythmia, then EF improves after rhythm or rate control.

That reversibility is the point.

If the heart gets stronger after fixing the fast rhythm, the fast rhythm was likely causing the cardiomyopathy.

---

## One-line intuition

The heart is like an engine forced to redline nonstop, so it eventually dilates and pumps weakly.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy is an important reversible cause of dilated cardiomyopathy, so always think of it when unexplained low EF occurs with chronic tachyarrhythmia.

# Thalassemia in pregnancy

Thalassemia in pregnancy means a pregnant patient has thalassemia, an inherited defect in hemoglobin production.

Hemoglobin is the oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells.

Main types:

* alpha-thalassemia = decreased alpha-globin chain production
* beta-thalassemia = decreased beta-globin chain production

Pregnancy matters because there are 2 separate issues:

* maternal anemia
* fetal inheritance risk

---

### Maternal issue

Pregnancy normally causes hemodilution.

Hemodilution means plasma volume increases more than red blood cell mass, so hemoglobin concentration looks lower.

So if someone already has thalassemia-related anemia, pregnancy can make the anemia look worse.

Labs often show:

* low MCV (mean corpuscular volume; average red blood cell size)
* low MCH (mean corpuscular hemoglobin; hemoglobin amount per red blood cell)
* normal or high RBC count (red blood cell count), especially in thalassemia trait

This is why thalassemia trait can look like iron deficiency anemia.

Key distinction:

* iron deficiency = low iron stores
* thalassemia = globin chain production problem

So do not automatically give iron unless iron deficiency is actually present.

---

### Fetal issue

Thalassemia is inherited, so partner testing matters.

If both parents are carriers, the fetus can inherit a severe form.

For beta-thalassemia:

* both parents carriers = 25% chance of beta-thalassemia major

Beta-thalassemia major means severe beta-globin deficiency causing severe anemia after birth.

For alpha-thalassemia:

* the scary fetal outcome is deletion of all 4 alpha-globin genes
* this causes Hb Bart hydrops fetalis

Hb Bart means hemoglobin made of 4 gamma chains because alpha chains are absent.

Hydrops fetalis means severe fetal edema from heart failure, often due to profound anemia.

---

### Why hydrops happens

No alpha chains means the fetus cannot make functional fetal hemoglobin.

Fetal hemoglobin is normally alpha2 gamma2.

Without alpha chains:

* oxygen delivery drops
* fetal tissues become hypoxic
* the fetal heart pumps harder
* high-output heart failure develops
* fluid accumulates in fetal compartments

That fluid accumulation is hydrops.

---

## One-line intuition

Thalassemia in pregnancy is dangerous less because of the mild carrier anemia, and more because two carrier parents can make a fetus with severe globin-chain deficiency.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Thalassemia trait classically causes microcytic anemia with a normal/high RBC count, while iron deficiency anemia usually has a low RBC count.

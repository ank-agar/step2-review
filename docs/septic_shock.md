Septic shock is severe sepsis where infection causes dangerous circulatory collapse.

Basically:

* infection triggers a massive immune response
* blood vessels dilate and become leaky
* blood pressure drops
* organs do not get enough oxygenated blood
* lactate rises because tissues switch toward anaerobic metabolism

Anaerobic metabolism means making energy without enough oxygen.

---

### Core idea

Septic shock is **distributive shock**.

Distributive shock means the blood volume is not being distributed properly because vessels are too dilated.

So the problem is not initially "no blood."

The problem is:

> the pipes are too wide and leaky, so pressure collapses.

---

### Why hypotension happens

Infection activates immune cells, which release cytokines.

Cytokines are inflammatory signaling proteins.

These cytokines cause:

* **vasodilation** = blood vessels widen
* **increased vascular permeability** = vessels become leaky
* **third spacing** = fluid leaves blood vessels and enters tissues

So even if total body fluid is present, the effective circulating volume is low.

That causes hypotension (low blood pressure).

---

### Why lactate rises

Low blood pressure means poor perfusion.

Perfusion means blood delivery to tissue.

Poor perfusion means cells do not get enough oxygen.

So cells switch toward anaerobic metabolism, producing lactate.

That is why septic shock has:

* hypotension
* high lactate
* organ dysfunction

---

### Classic findings

Common findings:

* fever or hypothermia
* tachycardia, meaning fast heart rate
* tachypnea, meaning fast breathing
* hypotension
* altered mental status, meaning confusion or decreased alertness
* decreased urine output
* elevated lactate

qSOFA (quick Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) screening clues:

* systolic BP (blood pressure) less than or equal to 100 mmHg
* RR (respiratory rate) greater than or equal to 22/min
* altered mental status

---

### Warm shock vs cold shock

Early septic shock can be "warm":

* low SVR (systemic vascular resistance)
* high cardiac output
* warm flushed skin
* bounding pulses

Later septic shock can become "cold":

* poor cardiac output
* cool extremities
* weak pulses
* worse organ perfusion

Intuition:

Early on, the vessels are wide open. Later, the whole system starts failing.

---

### Treatment logic

Main treatment:

* antibiotics to kill the infection
* IV fluids to refill the vascular space
* vasopressors if BP stays low

Vasopressors are drugs that tighten blood vessels and raise blood pressure.

The classic first-line vasopressor is:

* norepinephrine

Reason:

Norepinephrine increases vascular tone, which helps restore MAP (mean arterial pressure).

MAP is the average arterial pressure driving blood into organs.

---

## One-line intuition

Septic shock is an infection-driven cytokine storm that makes vessels wide and leaky, dropping blood pressure so organs cannot get oxygen.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 pearl: septic shock is a **low SVR (systemic vascular resistance), high cardiac output shock early**, unlike most other shock types where SVR rises as compensation.

### Core idea

ADHD (attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder) is a neurodevelopmental disorder where the brain has trouble with:

* attention
* impulse control
* sitting still / regulating activity level

Think:

> weak "executive control" from the prefrontal cortex.

Executive control means the brain's ability to plan, focus, inhibit impulses, and stay organized.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The prefrontal cortex is like the brain's "brake + organizer."

If that system is underactive, you get:

* **inattention:** hard to filter distractions
* **impulsivity:** weak braking system, so actions happen before thinking
* **hyperactivity:** difficulty regulating motor activity/restlessness
* **disorganization:** trouble planning and finishing tasks

---

### Neurotransmitter idea

ADHD involves abnormal signaling of:

* dopamine
* norepinephrine

These neurotransmitters help the prefrontal cortex maintain attention and control behavior.

So stimulant meds help because they increase catecholamines (dopamine and norepinephrine) in the CNS (central nervous system).

Examples:

* methylphenidate
* amphetamines

---

### One-line intuition

ADHD is not just "too much energy"; it is poor prefrontal cortex regulation of attention, impulses, and activity.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Stimulants treat ADHD by increasing dopamine and norepinephrine, but common side effects are decreased appetite and insomnia.

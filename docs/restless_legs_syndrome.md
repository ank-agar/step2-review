## Restless legs syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/RLS_sleep_patterns_diagram_-_en.svg/1280px-RLS_sleep_patterns_diagram_-_en.svg.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/images/RestlessLeg.jpg)

### Core idea

Restless legs syndrome, or RLS (restless legs syndrome), is a neurologic sleep/movement disorder where the patient has an irresistible urge to move the legs.

Classically:

* worse at rest
* worse in the evening/night
* relieved by movement
* causes trouble falling asleep or staying asleep

Patients often describe it as:

* crawling
* tingling
* pulling
* aching
* uncomfortable deep leg sensations

---

### Why it happens

The key idea is abnormal dopamine signaling and low iron availability in the brain.

Dopamine (a neurotransmitter important for movement control) helps regulate smooth, controlled movement circuits.

Iron is needed for normal dopamine production/function.

So low iron states can make dopamine signaling worse, causing abnormal sensory discomfort + urge to move.

That is why RLS is associated with:

* iron deficiency anemia
* pregnancy
* chronic kidney disease
* peripheral neuropathy
* Parkinson disease

---

### Why movement helps

Movement temporarily overrides the uncomfortable sensory signal.

So walking, stretching, or shaking the legs gives relief, but the symptoms come back when the patient rests again.

---

### Related finding

Many patients also have PLMD (periodic limb movement disorder), meaning repetitive leg jerks during sleep.

RLS = awake urge to move legs.

PLMD = involuntary leg movements during sleep.

---

## One-line intuition

RLS is when resting makes the legs feel unbearable, so the patient has to move them, especially at night.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association: RLS can be caused or worsened by iron deficiency, so check ferritin (iron storage protein) and treat iron deficiency if present.

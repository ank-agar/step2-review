## Central retinal vein occlusion

Central retinal vein occlusion, or **CRVO (central retinal vein occlusion)**, is blockage of the main vein that drains blood out of the retina.

Break it down:

* central retinal vein = main vein draining the retina
* occlusion = blockage

So:

> blood can enter the retina, but it cannot drain out normally.

---

### Core idea

Think of it like a clogged sink drain.

Arterial blood keeps coming in, but venous blood cannot leave, so the retina becomes:

* congested
* swollen
* full of hemorrhages

That is why the fundus has the classic **"blood and thunder"** appearance.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Sudden painless vision loss:** retina cannot function normally when it is swollen/ischemic
* **Diffuse retinal hemorrhages:** backed-up venous pressure makes vessels leak blood
* **Dilated tortuous veins:** veins are engorged because blood is trapped
* **Macular edema:** fluid leaks into the macula, the central vision area
* **Cotton-wool spots:** small areas of retinal ischemia (low oxygen injury)

---

### Risk factors

Usually associated with vascular disease:

* hypertension
* diabetes mellitus
* hyperlipidemia
* glaucoma
* older age

Rarely, think hypercoagulable states (increased tendency to clot), especially in a younger patient.

---

## One-line intuition

CRVO is a blocked retinal drain: blood backs up, veins get engorged, retina swells, and hemorrhages appear everywhere.

---

## Pearl

Compare:

* **CRVO (central retinal vein occlusion):** "blood and thunder" retina with diffuse hemorrhages
* **CRAO (central retinal artery occlusion):** pale retina with cherry-red spot

Vein blockage = backup and bleeding.  
Artery blockage = no inflow and retinal pallor.

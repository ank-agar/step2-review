### Core idea

Giardia is a flagellated protozoan parasite (single-celled parasite with whip-like motility structures) that causes giardiasis, a small-intestine infection.

Classic setting:

* camping/hiking
* daycare outbreaks
* contaminated water
* fecal-oral transmission

The infectious form is the **cyst** (hardy dormant form).

---

### What it causes

Giardia classically causes:

* foul-smelling watery diarrhea
* bloating/flatulence
* abdominal cramps
* weight loss
* greasy, floating stools from steatorrhea (fat in stool)

Usually **no blood** and **no major fever**.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

After you swallow Giardia cysts, they release trophozoites (active feeding form) in the small intestine.

The trophozoites attach to the duodenal mucosa (lining of the first part of the small intestine) with a ventral sucking disk.

They do **not** usually invade deeply.

So the key problem is:

* they coat/irritate the small bowel surface
* brush border enzymes (digestive enzymes on intestinal cells) work poorly
* fat and nutrients are absorbed poorly

That is why the stool is classically greasy, foul-smelling, and floating.

---

### Diagnosis

High-yield diagnosis:

* stool antigen test
* ova and parasite exam, often multiple stool samples

Microscopy clue:

* trophozoites look “face-like” because they have 2 nuclei
* cysts are oval and environmentally resistant

---

### Treatment

Treat with:

* metronidazole
* tinidazole
* nitazoxanide

---

## One-line intuition

Giardia is a waterborne cyst-forming parasite that sticks to the duodenum and causes malabsorption, especially greasy foul-smelling diarrhea.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Giardia = **foul-smelling fatty diarrhea after camping**; the infectious form is the **cyst**, and the active intestinal form is the **trophozoite**.

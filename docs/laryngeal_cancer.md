### Core idea

Laryngeal cancer = cancer of the larynx (voice box).

Most commonly, it is squamous cell carcinoma (cancer from flat epithelial cells lining mucosal surfaces).

The larynx has 3 main regions:

* supraglottis = above the vocal cords
* glottis = vocal cords
* subglottis = below the vocal cords

---

### Classic presentation

The big symptom is:

* hoarseness

Why?

Because the tumor often involves the glottis (vocal cord region), so the vocal cords cannot vibrate normally.

Other possible symptoms:

* dysphagia (difficulty swallowing)
* odynophagia (painful swallowing)
* stridor (high-pitched breathing sound from upper airway narrowing)
* neck mass from lymph node spread
* weight loss

---

### Major risk factors

Big ones:

* smoking
* alcohol use

They act synergistically, meaning together they increase risk more than either one alone.

Also associated with HPV (human papillomavirus), especially in some head and neck squamous cell cancers.

---

### Why location matters

Glottic cancer usually presents early because even a small lesion on the vocal cords causes hoarseness.

Supraglottic cancer may present later because it has more lymphatic drainage, so it can spread to cervical lymph nodes (neck lymph nodes) before causing obvious voice symptoms.

Subglottic cancer is less common.

---

## One-line intuition

A tumor grows on/near the voice box, so the first clue is often a smoker with persistent hoarseness.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Persistent hoarseness in a smoker is laryngeal cancer until proven otherwise, especially if it lasts more than a few weeks.

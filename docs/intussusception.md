## Intussusception

Intussusception is when one part of the intestine telescopes into the next part.

Think:

> bowel segment sliding into another bowel segment, like a collapsible telescope.

The inner sliding part is the **intussusceptum**.

The outer receiving part is the **intussuscipiens**.

Most commonly, the ileum (last part of the small intestine) slides into the colon.

---

### Core idea

The bowel folds into itself, causing:

* obstruction
* venous compression
* swelling/edema
* decreased blood flow
* possible ischemia (low oxygen injury from poor blood supply)

This is why it can become dangerous if not treated.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Intermittent severe abdominal pain:** peristalsis (wave-like gut contraction) pulls the bowel further inward
* **Child drawing knees to chest:** classic pain response in infants
* **Vomiting:** bowel obstruction backs things up
* **Sausage-shaped abdominal mass:** folded bowel creates a palpable mass
* **Currant jelly stool:** blood + mucus from ischemic, irritated bowel lining

Currant jelly stool is classic, but it is usually a later finding.

---

### Imaging

Classic ultrasound finding:

* **target sign** or **doughnut sign**

Why?

Because you are looking at bowel-within-bowel in cross-section, so it makes concentric rings.

On longitudinal ultrasound, it can look like a:

* **pseudokidney sign**

because the folded bowel can resemble the shape/layers of a kidney.

---

### Important association

In kids, it is often idiopathic (no clear cause), but can follow viral infection.

Reason:

viral infection can enlarge Peyer patches (lymphoid tissue in the ileum), creating a lead point.

A **lead point** means something the bowel can grab onto and drag forward.

In adults, intussusception is more concerning for a tumor acting as the lead point.

---

## One-line intuition

Intussusception is bowel swallowing bowel, causing episodic pain and obstruction.

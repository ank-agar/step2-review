### Core idea

Night blindness = poor vision in the dark or dim light.

Medical word:

* nyctalopia = night blindness

This usually means a problem with:

* rods = retinal photoreceptors (light-sensing cells) used for dim-light vision
* rhodopsin = rod visual pigment made from opsin (protein part) + retinal (vitamin A-derived light-sensing part)

---

### Why it happens

Rods are the main cells for seeing in low light.

So if rods or rhodopsin do not work well, the patient struggles most when light is low:

* driving at night
* walking in a dark room
* adjusting after entering a movie theater

Vitamin A matters because vitamin A is needed to make retinal, which is part of rhodopsin.

So:

> low vitamin A → low retinal → poor rhodopsin function → rods cannot detect dim light well → night blindness

---

### Classic causes

Important causes include:

* vitamin A deficiency
* retinitis pigmentosa = inherited retinal degeneration where rods often die first
* congenital stationary night blindness = inherited night blindness that is usually nonprogressive

Retinitis pigmentosa classically starts with:

* night blindness
* peripheral vision loss

because rods are concentrated more in the peripheral retina.

---

## One-line intuition

Night blindness is usually a rod problem, and rods need vitamin A-derived rhodopsin to see in the dark.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Vitamin A deficiency causes night blindness first because rods are especially dependent on rhodopsin for low-light vision.

## Pyoderma gangrenosum

Pyoderma gangrenosum is a rare inflammatory skin disease where painful bumps/pustules rapidly turn into deep ulcers.

Key idea:

* it is **not primarily an infection**
* it is a **neutrophilic dermatosis** (skin disease driven by neutrophils, which are acute inflammatory white blood cells)

So the name is kind of misleading:

* pyo- = pus
* derma = skin
* gangrenosum = gangrene-like tissue death

But the main problem is immune overactivation, not bacteria eating the skin.

---

### Classic look

Think:

* very painful ulcer
* irregular border
* violaceous border (purple-colored border)
* undermined border (skin edge hangs over because tissue underneath is destroyed)
* often on legs

---

### Why it happens

The immune system sends too many neutrophils into the skin.

Neutrophils release inflammatory enzymes and signals, which damage tissue.

That creates:

* necrosis (tissue death)
* ulceration (loss of skin surface)
* pus-like drainage even without true infection

---

### Pathergy

Pathergy means skin trauma triggers a worse inflammatory lesion.

So in pyoderma gangrenosum, things like:

* biopsy
* surgery
* debridement (removing dead tissue)
* minor cuts

can make the ulcer worse.

Intuition:

The skin is immunologically “overreactive,” so injury calls in neutrophils and the immune system accidentally expands the wound.

---

### Associations

Commonly associated with:

* IBD (inflammatory bowel disease, like Crohn disease or ulcerative colitis)
* rheumatoid arthritis
* hematologic malignancy (blood cancer)

---

## One-line intuition

Pyoderma gangrenosum is an immune-driven neutrophil attack on skin that causes rapidly expanding, painful, purple-bordered ulcers.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Do **not** think “bad infection = debride aggressively.”

In pyoderma gangrenosum, aggressive debridement can worsen the lesion because of **pathergy**.

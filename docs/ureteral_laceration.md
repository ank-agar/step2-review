## Ureteral laceration

A **ureteral laceration** is a tear/cut in the **ureter** (the tube that carries urine from the kidney to the bladder).

Core idea:

* urine is supposed to stay inside the ureter
* if the ureter is cut, urine can leak into nearby tissue
* that leak can form a **urinoma** (a collection of leaked urine outside the urinary tract)

---

### Why it matters

The ureter is a small retroperitoneal tube.

**Retroperitoneal** means behind the peritoneum (the abdominal lining), so injury can be easy to miss.

If urine leaks out, you can get:

* flank/abdominal pain
* fever
* hematuria (blood in urine)
* urinoma
* infection or sepsis (dangerous body-wide inflammatory response to infection)
* later stricture (scar-narrowing of the ureter)

---

### Common causes

Most commonly:

* pelvic surgery injury, especially gynecologic surgery
* ureteroscopy (scope procedure inside the ureter)
* penetrating trauma, like stab/gunshot injury
* blunt trauma with sudden deceleration

The ureter is vulnerable because it runs close to pelvic vessels and reproductive organs.

---

### Classification intuition

AAST (American Association for the Surgery of Trauma) grades ureter injury by how much of the ureter is cut:

* Grade II: laceration less than 50% through the ureter
* Grade III: laceration 50% or more through the ureter
* Grade IV: complete transection (fully cut through), with small devascularization
* Grade V: avulsion (torn away) or major devascularization

**Devascularization** means loss of blood supply.

So the big idea is:

> more wall cut + worse blood supply = higher grade injury.

---

### Diagnosis clue

CT urography (computed tomography imaging focused on urine flow) may show contrast leaking out of the ureter.

Contrast leaking outside the urinary tract = think ureter injury.

---

### Treatment intuition

Small injuries may be treated with:

* ureteral stent (tube placed inside the ureter to keep urine flowing)
* nephrostomy tube (tube from kidney to outside the body for urine drainage)

Severe injuries often need surgical repair.

The goal is simple:

> divert urine away from the tear so the ureter can heal, or surgically reconnect it if badly cut.

---

## One-line intuition

A ureteral laceration is basically a cut in the kidney-to-bladder tube, causing urine to leak where it should not.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In pelvic surgery, remember: **“water under the bridge.”**

The ureter carries “water” and passes under the uterine artery, so it can be injured during hysterectomy or uterine artery ligation.

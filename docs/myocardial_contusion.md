## Myocardial contusion

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532267/bin/Blunt__Cardiac__Injury__Algorithm.jpg)

### Core idea

Myocardial contusion = a bruise of the myocardium (heart muscle), usually after blunt chest trauma.

Think:

* car crash
* steering wheel injury
* fall
* CPR (cardiopulmonary resuscitation)
* direct blow to the chest

The heart sits right behind the sternum (breastbone), so a strong blow can compress it between the sternum and spine.

---

### What actually happens

The trauma damages heart muscle cells, causing:

* edema (swelling)
* small hemorrhage (bleeding into tissue)
* myocyte injury (heart muscle cell injury)

Because heart muscle is electrically active, bruising it can mess with conduction.

So the big issue is often:

* arrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms)

not just "pain from a bruise."

---

### What you may see

After blunt chest trauma, suspect myocardial contusion if there is:

* chest pain
* palpitations (feeling abnormal heartbeats)
* shortness of breath
* unexplained tachycardia (fast heart rate)
* abnormal ECG (electrocardiogram)
* elevated troponin (blood marker of heart muscle injury)

ECG can show:

* ST-segment changes
* premature ventricular contractions
* atrial fibrillation
* bundle branch block

---

### Why troponin rises

Troponin is inside cardiac myocytes (heart muscle cells).

If the blunt trauma injures those cells, troponin leaks into blood.

So elevated troponin does not always mean MI (myocardial infarction, heart attack from blocked coronary artery).

Here, it can mean:

> the heart muscle got physically bruised.

---

### Diagnosis intuition

Use:

* ECG (electrocardiogram) to check rhythm/conduction
* troponin to check heart muscle injury
* echocardiography (ultrasound of the heart) if unstable or concern for structural damage

If ECG and troponin are both normal, clinically significant blunt cardiac injury is much less likely.

---

## One-line intuition

Myocardial contusion is a traumatic bruise of the heart muscle that matters because bruised heart muscle can leak troponin and trigger arrhythmias.

---

## HY pearl

The right ventricle is commonly injured because it is the most anterior heart chamber, sitting closest to the sternum.

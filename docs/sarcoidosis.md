### Core idea

Sarcoidosis is a multisystem inflammatory disease where the immune system forms **noncaseating granulomas**.

Break that down:

* granuloma = organized ball of immune cells
* noncaseating = no cheesy central necrosis/dead tissue

So the classic biopsy finding is:

> tight clusters of immune cells without central tissue death.

---

### Most common organs

Classically affects:

* lungs
* hilar lymph nodes (lymph nodes near where the bronchi and vessels enter the lungs)
* skin
* eyes

Classic chest imaging finding:

* bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy

Meaning:

* enlarged lymph nodes on both sides of the lung hilum.

---

### Why the findings happen

Sarcoidosis is mainly driven by **CD4+ T cells** (helper T cells) activating macrophages (immune cells that eat debris/pathogens).

That macrophage activation causes granulomas.

Granulomas can:

* collect in lungs → dry cough, dyspnea (shortness of breath), restrictive lung disease
* collect in hilar lymph nodes → bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy
* activate vitamin D → hypercalcemia (high blood calcium)
* produce ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) → elevated serum ACE sometimes

Why hypercalcemia?

Activated macrophages have extra **1-alpha hydroxylase** (enzyme that activates vitamin D), so they make more active vitamin D.

More active vitamin D means:

* more calcium absorption from gut
* higher serum calcium

---

### Classic associations

Think:

* bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy
* noncaseating granulomas
* elevated ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme)
* hypercalcemia
* erythema nodosum (tender red nodules, usually on shins)
* uveitis (inflammation of the uvea/eye)

---

## One-line intuition

Sarcoidosis is your immune system building granuloma “walls” in organs, especially lungs and hilar lymph nodes, even though there is no obvious infection.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Sarcoidosis is a **Th1-type** (T helper 1-type) immune response, but patients can have **anergy** (poor skin test response), because T cells are trapped/activated in granulomas instead of responding normally in the skin.

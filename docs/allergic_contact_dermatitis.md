## Allergic contact dermatitis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/19/Blausen_0014_AllergicDermatitis.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/2387.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Urushiol-induced_contact_dermatitis_7_days_after_contact.jpg/1280px-Urushiol-induced_contact_dermatitis_7_days_after_contact.jpg)

Allergic contact dermatitis is an itchy eczema-like rash that happens after skin touches an allergen.

Important: this is a **type IV hypersensitivity reaction**.

Type IV hypersensitivity = delayed, T-cell-mediated immune reaction.

So this is **not** mainly IgE (immunoglobulin E) or histamine like anaphylaxis/hives.

---

### Core idea

The skin touches something the immune system has already learned to dislike, such as:

* poison ivy/oak
* nickel jewelry
* fragrances
* cosmetics
* latex
* topical antibiotics

Then T cells attack that skin area and cause:

* redness
* itching
* vesicles (small fluid-filled blisters)
* scaling/crusting

---

### Why it is delayed

First exposure:

* allergen enters skin
* antigen-presenting cells show it to T cells
* body becomes sensitized

Re-exposure:

* memory T cells recognize it
* cytokines (immune signaling proteins) recruit inflammation
* rash appears about 24-72 hours later

That delay is why poison ivy rash often shows up the next day or a few days later.

---

### Allergic vs irritant contact dermatitis

Allergic contact dermatitis:

* immune-mediated
* needs prior sensitization
* delayed
* can spread beyond exact contact site
* classic: poison ivy, nickel

Irritant contact dermatitis:

* direct skin damage
* no immune memory needed
* often happens faster
* usually stays where the irritant touched
* classic: soaps, detergents, acids/bases

---

## One-line intuition

Allergic contact dermatitis is your T cells recognizing something on the skin and causing a delayed itchy rash.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Poison ivy dermatitis is a classic **type IV hypersensitivity** reaction, like the **PPD (purified protein derivative) skin test** for tuberculosis.

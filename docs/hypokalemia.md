# Hypokalemia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/65/Sodium-potassium_pump.svg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615888/bin/psnet074f1.jpg)

### Core idea

Hypokalemia means low potassium in the blood.

Usually:

* serum K+ (potassium ion) < 3.5 mEq/L (milliequivalents per liter)

Potassium is mainly inside cells, and it helps set the resting membrane potential (baseline electrical charge across the cell membrane).

So when blood potassium is low, excitable cells get weird:

* muscle cells become weak
* cardiac cells become arrhythmia-prone
* GI (gastrointestinal) smooth muscle slows down

---

### Why low K+ causes weakness

Normally, potassium helps keep the inside of cells electrically negative.

When extracellular K+ (potassium outside cells) is too low:

* K+ wants to leave the cell more strongly
* the inside becomes even more negative
* this is called hyperpolarization

Hyperpolarization means the cell is farther from firing.

So skeletal muscle is harder to activate:

* weakness
* cramps
* hyporeflexia
* paralysis if severe

---

### Big causes

Think of hypokalemia as either:

* K+ lost from the body
* K+ shifted into cells

Common losses:

* vomiting
* diarrhea
* loop diuretics
* thiazide diuretics
* hyperaldosteronism

Common shifts into cells:

* insulin
* beta-2 agonists
* alkalosis

Why alkalosis causes hypokalemia:

* H+ (hydrogen ion) leaves cells to buffer the blood
* K+ shifts into cells to maintain charge balance
* serum K+ falls

---

### ECG findings

ECG (electrocardiogram) changes classically include:

* flattened T waves
* ST depression
* prominent U waves
* apparent QT prolongation
* arrhythmias

The U wave is the big test clue.

---

## One-line intuition

Low potassium makes muscle and heart cells too electrically unstable or hard to activate, so you get weakness plus dangerous ECG changes.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Hypokalemia and hyperaldosteronism go together because aldosterone increases Na+ (sodium ion) reabsorption while increasing K+ and H+ secretion in the collecting duct, causing:

* hypokalemia
* metabolic alkalosis
* hypertension

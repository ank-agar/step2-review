### Core idea

Epididymitis = inflammation of the epididymis.

The epididymis is the coiled tube sitting on the back of the testis that stores and carries sperm.

So epididymitis is basically:

> painful inflammation/infection of the sperm-storage tube behind the testicle.

---

### Why it happens

Usually bacteria ascend backward from the urethra/bladder area into the reproductive tract.

Path:

* urethra
* prostate/ejaculatory ducts
* vas deferens
* epididymis

So the infection travels "upstream" into the epididymis.

Common causes depend on age:

* younger sexually active men: Chlamydia trachomatis or Neisseria gonorrhoeae
* older men: enteric gram-negative rods, especially E. coli (Escherichia coli)

---

### What it looks like clinically

Classic findings:

* gradual scrotal pain
* tender/swollen epididymis
* dysuria (painful urination)
* urethral discharge sometimes
* fever sometimes

Pain may improve when lifting the testis:

* Prehn sign = pain relief with testicular elevation

But do not rely on this alone, because testicular torsion can look similar.

---

### Epididymitis vs torsion

Epididymitis:

* gradual onset
* infectious/urinary symptoms more likely
* Doppler ultrasound shows increased blood flow from inflammation

Testicular torsion:

* sudden severe pain
* high-riding testis
* absent cremasteric reflex
* Doppler ultrasound shows decreased blood flow

Why increased blood flow in epididymitis?

Inflammation causes vasodilation, meaning blood vessels open up to bring immune cells to the infected area.

---

## One-line intuition

Epididymitis is an ascending infection that inflames the epididymis, causing gradual scrotal pain with increased blood flow on Doppler ultrasound.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On USMLE Step 1, testicular torsion is the emergency diagnosis to rule out in acute scrotal pain because ischemia (low blood flow causing tissue injury) can kill the testis.

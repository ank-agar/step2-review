### Core idea

Medulloblastoma is a malignant embryonal brain tumor (cancer from primitive developing nervous tissue) that usually occurs in children.

Classic location:

* cerebellum
* midline posterior fossa
* often near the 4th ventricle

Posterior fossa means the back/lower compartment of the skull that contains the cerebellum and brainstem.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Ataxia:** the cerebellum coordinates movement, so tumor there causes clumsy gait and poor coordination
* **Headache/vomiting:** tumor can block cerebrospinal fluid (CSF; fluid around the brain and spinal cord), causing hydrocephalus (too much CSF buildup)
* **Papilledema:** increased intracranial pressure (pressure inside the skull) pushes on the optic disc
* **Drop metastases:** tumor cells can spread through CSF down the spinal cord

---

### Histology clue

Medulloblastoma is a small round blue cell tumor.

Classic microscopic finding:

* Homer Wright rosettes

Homer Wright rosettes are tumor cells arranged in a circle around central neuropil (neural tissue/processes).

---

## One-line intuition

A fast-growing childhood cerebellar tumor blocks CSF and spreads through CSF.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association: medulloblastoma can be linked to abnormal SHH signaling (Sonic hedgehog signaling; a developmental pathway), especially in some molecular subtypes.

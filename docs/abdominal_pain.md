# Abdominal pain

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19578.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK459220/bin/Gray1220.jpg)

### Core idea

Abdominal pain means discomfort anywhere between the ribs and pelvis.

It is a **symptom**, not a diagnosis.

So the key question is:

> Which organ or layer is irritated?

The pain can come from:

* GI (gastrointestinal) tract: stomach, intestines, appendix
* hepatobiliary system (liver, gallbladder, bile ducts)
* GU (genitourinary) system: kidneys, ureters, bladder, reproductive organs
* abdominal wall muscles/skin
* peritoneum (thin lining of the abdominal cavity)

---

### Why location matters

The abdomen is divided into quadrants:

* RUQ (right upper quadrant): liver/gallbladder
* LUQ (left upper quadrant): stomach/spleen/pancreas
* RLQ (right lower quadrant): appendix
* LLQ (left lower quadrant): sigmoid colon

So:

* RUQ pain after fatty meals = think gallstones
* RLQ pain = think appendicitis
* epigastric pain (upper middle abdomen) radiating to back = think pancreatitis
* suprapubic pain (above pubic bone) = think bladder/UTI (urinary tract infection)

---

### Visceral vs parietal pain

**Visceral pain** means pain from internal organs.

It is usually:

* dull
* crampy
* poorly localized

Why?

Because organs have fewer pain fibers, so the brain has a harder time pinpointing the exact spot.

**Parietal pain** means pain from the parietal peritoneum (body-wall lining of the abdominal cavity).

It is usually:

* sharp
* localized
* worse with movement/coughing

Why?

Because the parietal peritoneum has somatic nerves (body-wall nerves), which localize pain well.

---

### Classic Step 1 example

Appendicitis often starts as vague periumbilical pain (pain around the belly button).

Then it migrates to the RLQ (right lower quadrant).

Why?

Early appendicitis irritates visceral nerves, so pain is vague and referred to the umbilicus.

Later, inflammation reaches the parietal peritoneum, so pain becomes sharp and localized over the appendix.

---

## One-line intuition

Abdominal pain is the body telling you that an abdominal organ, abdominal wall, or peritoneal lining is being stretched, inflamed, blocked, infected, or ischemic (low blood flow).

---

## Pearl

Pain out of proportion to exam is HY (high-yield) for mesenteric ischemia, meaning low blood flow to the intestines.

## Protein C deficiency

### Core idea

Protein C deficiency = not enough functional protein C, which is a natural anticoagulant (a protein that slows clotting).

So the patient becomes hypercoagulable (too likely to form clots).

---

### What protein C normally does

Protein C is activated by:

* thrombin
* thrombomodulin (an endothelial cell surface protein that helps thrombin activate protein C)

Activated protein C (APC) then works with protein S to inactivate:

* factor Va
* factor VIIIa

Factor Va and factor VIIIa normally help make thrombin, which helps make fibrin clots.

So:

> Protein C turns off Va and VIIIa, which turns down clot formation.

---

### Why deficiency causes clots

If protein C is low:

* factor Va stays active longer
* factor VIIIa stays active longer
* more thrombin is made
* more fibrin is made
* venous clots form more easily

Most common issue:

* DVT (deep vein thrombosis)
* PE (pulmonary embolism, a clot that travels to the lungs)

---

### Warfarin connection

Protein C is vitamin K-dependent, like factors II, VII, IX, and X.

Warfarin blocks vitamin K recycling, so it lowers all of these.

But protein C has a short half-life (it drops quickly), so early after starting warfarin:

* anticoagulant protein C drops first
* pro-clotting factors are still around
* patient can become temporarily more hypercoagulable

That is why warfarin can rarely cause skin necrosis (skin tissue death from small-vessel clotting), especially in protein C deficiency.

---

### Severe form

Severe protein C deficiency in newborns can cause purpura fulminans (life-threatening widespread clotting and skin bleeding/necrosis).

Think:

* lots of tiny vessel clots
* tissue ischemia (low blood flow)
* purple skin lesions
* possible DIC (disseminated intravascular coagulation, widespread clotting that consumes clotting factors)

---

## One-line intuition

Protein C is one of the body's clot brakes; deficiency removes the brake, so factors Va and VIIIa keep pushing thrombin production.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Do not start warfarin alone in a high-risk patient: bridge with heparin because early warfarin lowers protein C first and can transiently increase clot risk.

### Core idea

A **spermatocele** is a benign (noncancerous), fluid-filled cyst that forms in the **epididymis** (coiled tube on the back/top of the testis that stores and transports sperm).

The fluid often contains:

* sperm
* proteinaceous fluid (protein-rich fluid)
* cellular debris (broken-down cells)

So think:

> spermatocele = sperm-containing cyst near the epididymis

---

### Why it happens

Usually, it forms when an epididymal duct gets blocked.

That blockage traps sperm-containing fluid, so the tube balloons out into a cyst.

Like:

> clogged sperm drainage tube -> backup of fluid/sperm -> cystic swelling

---

### What it feels like

Classically:

* painless
* smooth
* cystic/fluid-filled
* located above or behind the testis
* separate from the testis itself
* may transilluminate (light passes through because it is fluid-filled)

Large ones can cause:

* dull scrotal ache
* heaviness
* swelling

---

### Diagnosis

If unsure, use **US (ultrasound)**.

US shows a cystic/fluid-filled lesion near the epididymis, often with internal echoes from sperm/debris.

Important intuition:

* spermatocele is usually **extratesticular** (outside the testis)
* testicular cancer is usually **intratesticular** (inside the testis)

That location difference is why ultrasound is helpful.

---

### Do you treat it?

Usually no treatment if asymptomatic (not causing symptoms).

Treat only if:

* painful
* very large
* bothersome

Treatment can be surgical removal, but surgery can injure the epididymis or vas deferens (tube carrying sperm from epididymis toward urethra), so fertility goals matter.

---

## One-line intuition

A spermatocele is basically a benign sperm-filled epididymal cyst sitting above/behind the testicle.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1-style thinking: **painless scrotal mass that transilluminates** suggests a fluid-filled lesion like hydrocele or spermatocele, but **spermatocele is localized to the epididymis**, while hydrocele is fluid around the testis.

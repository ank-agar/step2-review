### Core idea

Necrotizing fasciitis is a rapidly spreading bacterial infection of the fascia.

Break the word apart:

* necrotizing = causing tissue death
* fasciitis = inflammation/infection of fascia
* fascia = connective tissue layer around muscles and deep soft tissues

So:

> bacteria spread along the fascial plane and destroy soft tissue very fast.

---

### Why it is so dangerous

The infection spreads deep under the skin, so early skin findings can look less dramatic than the actual damage underneath.

Bacteria release toxins and enzymes that cause:

* vessel thrombosis (blood clots in small vessels)
* ischemia (low blood flow)
* necrosis (tissue death)
* systemic toxicity/sepsis (body-wide inflammatory shock response)

That is why patients can go from “skin infection” to shock very quickly.

---

### Classic clues

Think:

* severe pain out of proportion to exam
* fever/toxic appearance
* rapidly spreading redness/swelling
* bullae (fluid-filled blisters)
* skin necrosis
* crepitus sometimes (crackling from gas in tissue)
* anesthesia later (loss of sensation because nerves die)

The key phrase is:

> pain out of proportion.

---

### Common causes

Can be:

* polymicrobial (multiple bacteria together), especially in diabetics or after surgery
* Group A Streptococcus, aka Streptococcus pyogenes
* Clostridium species, which can make gas
* Vibrio vulnificus after seawater/raw oyster exposure

---

### Treatment intuition

Antibiotics alone are not enough because dead tissue has poor blood flow, so antibiotics cannot reach it well.

Treatment needs:

* emergency surgical debridement (cutting out dead/infected tissue)
* broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (intravenous antibiotics)
* hemodynamic support if septic (blood pressure/organ support)

---

## One-line intuition

Necrotizing fasciitis is a deep soft-tissue infection that races along fascia, cuts off blood supply, kills tissue, and needs immediate surgery.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For USMLE Step 1, the highest-yield clue is **severe pain out of proportion to physical exam**, followed by rapid progression and systemic toxicity.

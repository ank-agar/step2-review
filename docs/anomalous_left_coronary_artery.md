### Core idea

Anomalous left coronary artery usually means **ALCAPA** (anomalous left coronary artery from the pulmonary artery).

The left coronary artery is supposed to come off the **aorta**.

In ALCAPA, it comes off the **pulmonary artery** instead.

So the heart muscle supplied by the left coronary artery gets:

* lower-pressure blood
* lower-oxygen blood
* poor perfusion, especially after birth

---

### Why this becomes a problem after birth

Before birth, pulmonary artery pressure is high, so flow can still be okay.

After birth:

* pulmonary vascular resistance (resistance in lung blood vessels) drops
* pulmonary artery pressure drops
* blood in the left coronary artery can flow backward into the pulmonary artery

This is called **coronary steal**.

Meaning:

> blood gets "stolen" away from the heart muscle and drains into the low-pressure pulmonary artery.

So the left ventricle becomes ischemic.

**Ischemia** = not enough blood/oxygen to tissue.

---

### What it causes

The left coronary artery supplies a big chunk of the left ventricle.

So ALCAPA can cause:

* myocardial infarction (heart attack) in an infant
* heart failure
* poor feeding
* sweating with feeds
* tachypnea (fast breathing)
* mitral regurgitation

**Mitral regurgitation** = blood leaks backward through the mitral valve, often because ischemia damages the papillary muscles that help the valve close.

---

### Classic Step 1 association

Infant with:

* signs of heart failure
* poor feeding/sweating with feeds
* ECG (electrocardiogram) showing anterolateral infarction

Think:

> anomalous left coronary artery from the pulmonary artery.

---

## One-line intuition

The left coronary artery is plugged into the wrong vessel, so after birth the left ventricle gets under-oxygenated, low-pressure blood and can infarct.

---

## Pearl

ALCAPA is also called **Bland-White-Garland syndrome**.

High-yield clue: it can mimic **dilated cardiomyopathy** (enlarged weak heart), but the real issue is abnormal coronary artery origin causing ischemia.

### Core idea

Brucella is a **Gram-negative coccobacillus** (short rod-shaped bacterium) that causes **brucellosis**.

It is classically from:

* unpasteurized dairy
* infected livestock
* animal tissue exposure

Think:

> farm animals + unpasteurized milk/cheese + fever that comes and goes.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Brucella is **facultative intracellular** (can live inside cells if it wants to), especially inside **macrophages** (immune cells that eat microbes).

Normally, macrophages engulf bacteria and kill them inside lysosomes (acidic digestive compartments).

But Brucella can survive inside macrophages by avoiding normal killing pathways.

So it spreads through the **reticuloendothelial system** (macrophage-rich organs like liver, spleen, bone marrow, and lymph nodes).

That explains:

* **undulant fever:** fever rises and falls in waves
* **hepatosplenomegaly:** enlarged liver and spleen
* **malaise/fatigue:** chronic intracellular infection
* **arthralgia/osteomyelitis:** joints and bone can get seeded

---

### Classic Step 1 associations

* Gram-negative coccobacillus
* intracellular in macrophages
* transmitted by unpasteurized dairy
* causes undulant fever
* commonly linked to goats, sheep, cattle, pigs, and dogs

---

## One-line intuition

Brucella is a farm-animal intracellular bug that hides in macrophages and causes a waxing/waning systemic fever.

---

## Pearl

Brucella is treated with **doxycycline + rifampin** because intracellular organisms need antibiotics that can get inside cells.

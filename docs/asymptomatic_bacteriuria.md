## Asymptomatic bacteriuria

![Image](https://domf5oio6qrcr.cloudfront.net/medialibrary/13972/9f24ff46-02b4-41f7-91e4-6bef89ae2542.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557479/bin/UTI.jpg)

### Core idea

Asymptomatic bacteriuria means:

* bacteria are present in the urine
* urine culture is positive
* but the patient has **no urinary symptoms**

Asymptomatic = no symptoms.

Bacteriuria = bacteria in urine.

So the key idea is:

> Positive urine culture does **not automatically** mean urinary tract infection.

---

### Why it is not always treated

A true urinary tract infection (UTI, infection anywhere in the urinary system) causes symptoms like:

* dysuria (painful urination)
* frequency
* urgency
* suprapubic pain
* fever/flank pain if pyelonephritis (kidney infection)

But in asymptomatic bacteriuria, bacteria are just present without causing tissue invasion/inflammation severe enough to make symptoms.

Treating everyone would mostly just:

* select for resistant bacteria
* cause medication side effects
* disrupt normal flora (normal bacteria living in the body)

So most patients do **not** need antibiotics.

---

### When you DO treat

Treat asymptomatic bacteriuria in:

* pregnancy
* before urologic procedures that cause mucosal bleeding

Pregnancy matters because untreated asymptomatic bacteriuria can progress to pyelonephritis (kidney infection), which can harm both mom and fetus.

Urologic procedures matter because bacteria can enter the blood when the urinary tract lining is disrupted.

---

### Classic test clue

The stem may say:

* urine culture grows bacteria
* maybe nitrites/leukocyte esterase are positive
* patient feels fine
* no dysuria, urgency, frequency, fever, or flank pain

That is asymptomatic bacteriuria, not symptomatic UTI.

---

## One-line intuition

Bacteria in urine without symptoms is usually colonization, not an infection that needs treatment.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On Step 1, asymptomatic bacteriuria is usually **do not treat**, except in **pregnancy** or before **invasive urologic procedures**.

### Core idea

A spinal artery infarct means part of the spinal cord dies from loss of blood flow.

Infarct = tissue death from ischemia (low blood flow/oxygen).

Most HY version:

* anterior spinal artery infarct
* also called anterior cord syndrome

The anterior spinal artery supplies the anterior 2/3 of the spinal cord.

---

### What gets damaged

Anterior spinal artery infarct damages:

* corticospinal tract = motor pathway
* spinothalamic tract = pain and temperature pathway
* anterior horn cells = lower motor neurons (LMNs, neurons that directly stimulate muscle)

So the patient gets:

* bilateral weakness/paralysis below the lesion
* loss of pain and temperature below the lesion
* possible bowel/bladder dysfunction

---

### What is spared

Dorsal columns are usually spared.

Dorsal columns carry:

* vibration
* proprioception (sense of body position)
* fine touch

Why spared?

Because dorsal columns are posterior, and they are supplied mainly by the posterior spinal arteries, not the anterior spinal artery.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

If the anterior spinal artery is blocked:

* motor tracts lose oxygen -> weakness/paralysis
* pain/temp tracts lose oxygen -> loss of pain and temperature
* dorsal columns still get blood -> vibration/proprioception preserved

So the pattern is:

> motor + pain/temp lost, but vibration/proprioception preserved.

That contrast is the key.

---

### Common causes

Think of anything that cuts off blood flow to the spinal cord:

* aortic surgery
* aortic dissection
* severe hypotension
* embolus/thrombus
* artery of Adamkiewicz injury

Artery of Adamkiewicz = major radicular artery that helps supply the lower spinal cord.

---

## One-line intuition

Anterior spinal artery infarct is basically a spinal cord stroke that knocks out movement and pain/temp, while sparing dorsal column senses.

---

## HY pearl

Anterior spinal artery infarct classically causes bilateral motor paralysis + loss of pain/temperature with preserved vibration/proprioception.

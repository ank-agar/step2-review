## Achalasia

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17881/N01011-H.jpg)

![Image](https://www.medicalgraphics.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/esophageal-achalasia.jpg)

![Image](https://obgynkey.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/A321246_1_En_17_Fig2_HTML.jpg)

Achalasia is when the lower esophageal sphincter, or LES (lower esophageal sphincter), does not relax properly.

So food gets stuck in the esophagus instead of passing smoothly into the stomach.

Core problem:

* LES fails to relax
* esophagus loses peristalsis (coordinated squeezing that pushes food downward)

Why?

Achalasia is due to degeneration of inhibitory neurons in the myenteric plexus (nerve network in the gut wall).

Those inhibitory neurons normally release:

* nitric oxide
* VIP (vasoactive intestinal peptide)

These relax the LES.

So if those neurons are lost:

* less relaxation signal
* LES stays tight
* food backs up
* esophagus dilates above the tight sphincter

Classic symptoms:

* dysphagia (trouble swallowing) to both solids and liquids
* regurgitation (food coming back up) of undigested food
* chest pain
* weight loss

Important contrast:

* solids only first = think mechanical obstruction, like cancer
* solids and liquids from the start = think motility problem, like achalasia

Barium swallow finding:

* dilated esophagus
* narrow tapering at the LES
* “bird-beak” appearance

Manometry (pressure test of the esophagus) shows:

* high LES pressure
* incomplete LES relaxation
* absent peristalsis

One-line intuition:

The esophagus is trying to drain into the stomach, but the exit valve is clamped shut and the esophageal pump is weak.

HY pearl 🔥

Chagas disease can cause secondary achalasia because Trypanosoma cruzi destroys the myenteric plexus.

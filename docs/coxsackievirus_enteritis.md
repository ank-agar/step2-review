### Core idea

Coxsackievirus enteritis means inflammation of the small intestine caused by Coxsackievirus.

Break it down:

* Coxsackievirus = an enterovirus (virus that often enters through the GI tract)
* enteritis = inflammation of the small intestine

So basically:

> Coxsackievirus infects the gut lining and causes a viral diarrhea-type illness.

---

### Why it causes diarrhea

Coxsackievirus spreads mainly fecal-oral, meaning virus from stool gets into the mouth through contaminated hands, food, or surfaces.

Then it reaches the intestine and infects enterocytes (intestinal lining cells).

That causes:

* inflammation of the intestinal mucosa (gut lining)
* decreased absorption of water/nutrients
* increased fluid in the lumen (inside of the gut tube)

Result:

* watery diarrhea
* abdominal cramps
* fever
* nausea/vomiting sometimes

---

### Why Coxsackievirus is a Step 1 virus

Coxsackievirus is a:

* Picornavirus (small RNA virus family)
* positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus
* nonenveloped virus
* fecal-oral spread virus

Positive-sense RNA means the viral RNA can act like mRNA (messenger RNA), so host ribosomes can translate it directly.

Nonenveloped matters because nonenveloped viruses are tougher in the environment and can survive GI acid better.

---

### Associated diseases

Coxsackievirus can cause way more than enteritis:

* hand-foot-mouth disease
* herpangina (painful mouth ulcers)
* aseptic meningitis (meningitis without typical bacteria)
* myocarditis (heart muscle inflammation)
* pericarditis (inflammation around the heart)

The enteritis part is just the gut manifestation.

---

## One-line intuition

Coxsackievirus is a hardy fecal-oral enterovirus that can infect the gut lining, so it can cause a viral diarrhea/enteritis picture.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Coxsackie B is classically associated with myocarditis and dilated cardiomyopathy, while Coxsackie A is classically associated with hand-foot-mouth disease and herpangina.

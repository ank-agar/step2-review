## Left ventricular hypertrophy

![Image](https://s3.amazonaws.com/static.wd7.us/a/a1/Left_Ventricular_Hypertrophy.png)

![Image](https://www.ecgguru.com/sites/default/files/styles/medium/public/ecg-heart-art/Chicken%20heart%20showing%20ventricles.jpg?itok=Q06vUZ3g)

Left ventricular hypertrophy, or LVH (left ventricular hypertrophy), means the muscle wall of the left ventricle gets thick.

The left ventricle is the main chamber that pumps blood to the whole body.

---

### Core idea

LVH is basically:

* left ventricle has to pump against too much resistance
* muscle responds by getting bigger/thicker
* thick muscle becomes stiff

So the heart may still squeeze okay, but it has trouble relaxing and filling.

---

### Why it happens

Think of the left ventricle like a weightlifter.

If it keeps pumping against high pressure, it gets muscular.

Common causes:

* hypertension (high blood pressure)
* aortic stenosis (narrowed aortic valve)
* hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (genetic thick heart muscle disease)

The main mechanism is increased afterload (pressure the ventricle must pump against).

---

### Why this causes problems

The thick LV wall:

* needs more oxygen
* relaxes poorly
* fills poorly during diastole (heart relaxation/filling phase)

That can cause:

* diastolic heart failure
* angina (chest pain from myocardial oxygen demand exceeding supply)
* arrhythmias (abnormal rhythms)
* sudden cardiac death risk in severe cases

---

### ECG idea

On ECG (electrocardiogram), more muscle mass often means bigger electrical signals.

So LVH can cause:

* tall R waves in left-sided leads, especially V5/V6
* deep S waves in right-sided leads, especially V1/V2
* sometimes LV strain pattern: ST depression/T-wave inversion in lateral leads

---

## One-line intuition

LVH is the left ventricle getting thick because it has been lifting against too much pressure for too long.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Hypertension is the most common cause of LVH, and LVH classically causes diastolic dysfunction because the thick stiff ventricle cannot relax/fill well.

### Core idea

Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) is a herpesvirus that infects B cells and epithelial cells.

Big facts:

* also called HHV-4 (human herpesvirus 4)
* double-stranded DNA virus
* spread mainly through saliva
* causes infectious mononucleosis, aka "mono"
* establishes lifelong latency (dormant infection) in B cells

---

### How it infects cells

EBV infects B cells by binding:

* CD21, also called CR2 (complement receptor 2)

So the Step 1 association is:

> EBV binds CD21 on B cells.

Once inside B cells, EBV can make them proliferate. Your immune system responds by activating CD8+ T cells (cytotoxic T cells), which attack the infected B cells.

That is why the blood smear shows:

* atypical lymphocytes

These are mostly reactive CD8+ T cells, not the infected B cells themselves.

---

### Why mono symptoms happen

Classic mono symptoms:

* fever
* sore throat/pharyngitis (throat inflammation)
* posterior cervical lymphadenopathy (swollen lymph nodes in the back of the neck)
* fatigue
* splenomegaly (enlarged spleen)

Why:

* **Sore throat:** EBV replicates in the oropharynx (mouth/throat region)
* **Lymphadenopathy:** immune cells are proliferating in lymph nodes
* **Fatigue:** systemic viral immune response
* **Splenomegaly:** immune activation expands lymphoid tissue in the spleen

Important consequence:

* avoid contact sports because an enlarged spleen can rupture

---

### Diagnosis clue

The classic screening test is the heterophile antibody test.

Heterophile antibodies = antibodies that react with antigens from a different species.

The old-school name is:

* Monospot test

Also remember:

* EBV can cause a positive heterophile antibody test

---

### Ampicillin rash

If someone with EBV mono gets ampicillin or amoxicillin, they can develop a diffuse maculopapular rash.

Maculopapular means:

* macules = flat skin lesions
* papules = raised skin lesions

This does not automatically mean a true penicillin allergy.

---

### Cancer associations

EBV is associated with:

* Burkitt lymphoma
* Hodgkin lymphoma
* nasopharyngeal carcinoma
* some gastric carcinomas
* post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder

Intuition:

EBV pushes B cells toward proliferation and survival, so if immune control is weak or other mutations occur, cancer risk increases.

---

## One-line intuition

EBV is the saliva-spread herpesvirus that hides in B cells, triggers a big CD8+ T-cell response, and classically causes mono with fever, sore throat, posterior cervical lymphadenopathy, fatigue, and splenomegaly.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In infectious mononucleosis, the "atypical lymphocytes" are reactive CD8+ T cells responding to EBV-infected B cells.

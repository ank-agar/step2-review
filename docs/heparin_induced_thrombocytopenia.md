## Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia

### Core idea

Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, or HIT (heparin-induced thrombocytopenia), is when heparin triggers an immune reaction that:

* lowers platelets
* increases clotting risk

So the confusing part is:

> low platelets, but dangerous thrombosis.

Thrombosis means clot formation inside a blood vessel.

---

### Mechanism

Heparin binds PF4 (platelet factor 4), a protein released by platelets.

Then the body makes IgG (immunoglobulin G) antibodies against the heparin-PF4 complex.

Those IgG antibodies bind platelets through Fc receptors (antibody-binding receptors), which activates platelets.

Activated platelets then:

* clump together
* get consumed, causing thrombocytopenia (low platelet count)
* release pro-clotting signals, causing thrombosis

---

### Why platelets go down

The platelets are not just being destroyed passively.

They are being activated and used up.

So:

* platelets get consumed in clots
* platelet count falls
* clotting risk rises

That is why HIT causes thrombocytopenia but usually does not present mainly with bleeding.

---

### Timing

Classic HIT usually happens:

* 5-10 days after starting heparin

But it can happen faster if the patient was recently exposed to heparin before, because antibodies may already be present.

---

### What clots can happen?

HIT can cause:

* DVT (deep vein thrombosis)
* PE (pulmonary embolism)
* limb ischemia
* stroke or MI (myocardial infarction), less commonly

Venous clots are especially common.

---

### Treatment intuition

Stop all heparin immediately.

Then give a non-heparin anticoagulant (blood thinner), such as:

* argatroban
* bivalirudin
* fondaparinux

Do not give warfarin first by itself, because early warfarin lowers protein C (a natural anticoagulant) and can worsen clotting/skin necrosis.

---

## One-line intuition

Heparin makes an immune complex with PF4, antibodies activate platelets, and the platelets get consumed while making clots.

---

## Pearl

HIT is a hypercoagulable state (increased clotting tendency), not a bleeding disorder, even though the platelet count is low.

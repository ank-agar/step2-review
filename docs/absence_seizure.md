### Core idea

An absence seizure is a brief generalized seizure where the patient suddenly "spaces out" and becomes unresponsive.

Generalized means both sides of the brain are involved from the start.

Usually seen in children.

---

### What it looks like

Classically:

* sudden staring spell
* lasts only a few seconds
* no falling or major shaking
* may have eyelid fluttering or lip smacking
* ends abruptly
* no postictal confusion

Postictal means after the seizure.

So the kid can stop mid-sentence, stare, then immediately continue like nothing happened.

---

### Why it happens

The cortex and thalamus start firing in a synchronized rhythm.

Cortex = outer brain layer involved in awareness/thinking.

Thalamus = relay center that helps regulate consciousness and sensory information.

That abnormal thalamocortical rhythm causes a brief "pause" in awareness.

---

### EEG finding

Classic EEG (electroencephalogram, brain electrical activity test):

* generalized 3-Hz spike-and-wave discharges

3-Hz means 3 cycles per second.

This is very high-yield.

---

### Treatment

First-line drug:

* ethosuximide

Ethosuximide blocks T-type calcium channels in the thalamus.

That matters because T-type calcium channels help generate the abnormal thalamocortical rhythm.

---

## One-line intuition

Absence seizure = a child briefly "disconnects" because both hemispheres get stuck in a 3-Hz thalamocortical rhythm.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Hyperventilation can trigger absence seizures, and the classic EEG finding is generalized 3-Hz spike-and-wave activity.

### Core idea

Squamous cell lung cancer is a type of NSCLC (non-small cell lung cancer) that comes from squamous cells (flat epithelial cells).

In the lung, it classically:

* is strongly associated with smoking
* arises centrally near the bronchi (large airways)
* can cavitate (form a hollow space)
* can cause hypercalcemia (high blood calcium)

---

### Why it is central

Smoking irritates the bronchial epithelium (airway lining).

That irritation causes:

* squamous metaplasia (normal airway cells change into squamous cells)
* dysplasia (abnormal precancerous growth)
* carcinoma (invasive cancer)

So the cancer tends to show up near the bronchi because that is where smoke exposure hits the airway lining the most.

---

### Classic histology

On biopsy, squamous cell carcinoma can show:

* keratin pearls (round pink whorls of keratin)
* intercellular bridges (connections between tumor cells)

Break the word apart:

* squamous = flat cell type
* carcinoma = cancer from epithelial cells (lining/covering cells)

So:

> squamous cell carcinoma = cancer of flat epithelial-type cells.

---

### Why hypercalcemia happens

Squamous cell lung cancer can secrete PTHrP (parathyroid hormone-related peptide).

PTHrP acts like PTH (parathyroid hormone), so it increases blood calcium.

High calcium can cause:

* constipation
* confusion
* kidney stones
* bone pain

---

## One-line intuition

Smoking damages central bronchi, the airway lining turns squamous, then malignant squamous cells form a central lung tumor with keratin pearls and possible PTHrP-mediated hypercalcemia.

---

## HY pearl

Squamous cell carcinoma of the lung is the lung cancer most classically associated with PTHrP (parathyroid hormone-related peptide) causing hypercalcemia (high calcium).

### Core idea

Human herpesvirus 8, or **HHV-8 (human herpesvirus 8)**, is a **double-stranded DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) herpesvirus** that is best known for causing:

* **Kaposi sarcoma**
* **primary effusion lymphoma** (a lymphoma, meaning cancer of lymphocytes, that grows as fluid in body cavities)
* **multicentric Castleman disease** (a lymph node overgrowth disorder involving many lymph node regions)

It is also called **KSHV (Kaposi sarcoma-associated herpesvirus)**.

---

### What type of virus is it?

HHV-8 is a **herpesvirus**, so it has the classic herpesvirus traits:

* enveloped virus
* linear double-stranded DNA genome
* icosahedral capsid (20-sided protein shell)
* lifelong latency

**Latency** means the virus can hide quietly inside cells without making tons of new virus.

For HHV-8, the important latent cells are mainly:

* endothelial cells (cells lining blood vessels and lymph vessels)
* B cells (antibody-producing immune cells)

---

### Why does it cause Kaposi sarcoma?

Kaposi sarcoma is a vascular tumor, meaning it comes from cells related to blood vessels or lymphatic vessels.

HHV-8 infects endothelial cells and makes viral proteins that push the cell toward:

* survival
* proliferation
* angiogenesis (new blood vessel formation)
* immune evasion

So the intuition is:

> HHV-8 makes blood-vessel-type cells grow and survive when they should not, creating purple vascular lesions.

That is why Kaposi sarcoma lesions often look red-purple: they are full of abnormal vascular spaces and blood.

---

### When does HHV-8 become clinically important?

Most important setting:

* immunosuppression

Classic Step 1 association:

* **AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome)** with low **CD4+ T cells (helper T lymphocytes)**

Why?

CD4+ T cells help keep latent viruses controlled. When CD4+ T cells drop, HHV-8 can reactivate and drive tumor growth.

---

### Kaposi sarcoma presentation

Think:

* purple-red macules, plaques, or nodules
* skin or mucous membranes
* can involve GI tract (gastrointestinal tract), lungs, lymph nodes, or other organs

Histology often shows:

* spindle cells
* slit-like vascular spaces
* red blood cell extravasation (red blood cells leaking out of vessels)
* positive HHV-8 staining

---

## One-line intuition

HHV-8 is a latent herpesvirus that becomes dangerous with immunosuppression because it drives abnormal vascular and B-cell proliferation.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Kaposi sarcoma is strongly associated with **HHV-8**, especially in **AIDS**, and classically presents with **purple vascular skin or mucosal lesions**.

### Core idea

Brucellosis is an infection caused by **Brucella**, a **Gram-negative intracellular coccobacillus**.

Break that down:

* **Gram-negative** = has an outer membrane and stains pink/red on Gram stain
* **intracellular** = survives inside host cells
* **coccobacillus** = short, oval rod-shaped bacterium

It is a **zoonosis** (infection transmitted from animals to humans).

---

### How you get it

Classic sources:

* unpasteurized milk or cheese
* infected cattle, goats, sheep, or pigs
* animal tissues/body fluids
* lab exposure

Think:

> farm animals + unpasteurized dairy = Brucella.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Brucella gets eaten by macrophages (immune cells that engulf microbes), but it can survive inside them.

So instead of being killed, it spreads through the **reticuloendothelial system** (macrophage-rich organs like liver, spleen, bone marrow, and lymph nodes).

That causes:

* recurrent fever
* sweats
* malaise (general sick feeling)
* muscle/joint pain
* hepatosplenomegaly (enlarged liver and spleen)

The fever can rise and fall over time, which is why brucellosis is also called:

* **undulant fever**

Undulant = wave-like.

---

### Step 1 associations

Brucella is:

* Gram-negative
* facultative intracellular (can live inside or outside cells, but survives well inside cells)
* transmitted by unpasteurized dairy or animal exposure
* associated with undulant fever

Treatment is usually:

* doxycycline + rifampin

because intracellular organisms often need antibiotics that penetrate cells well.

---

## One-line intuition

Brucellosis is an animal/dairy-transmitted intracellular infection that hides in macrophages and causes recurring wave-like fevers.

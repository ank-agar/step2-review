# Neuroleptic malignant syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Dopamine_pathways.svg/960px-Dopamine_pathways.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Basal_ganglia_circuits.png)

### Core idea

Neuroleptic malignant syndrome, or **NMS (neuroleptic malignant syndrome)**, is a rare but life-threatening reaction to **dopamine-blocking drugs**, especially antipsychotics.

Neuroleptic = antipsychotic drug.

The big idea:

* dopamine gets blocked
* muscles become extremely rigid
* body temperature rises
* autonomic nervous system becomes unstable

Autonomic nervous system = the automatic body system controlling heart rate, blood pressure, sweating, temperature, etc.

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* **Fever**
* **Lead-pipe rigidity**
* **Altered mental status**
* **Autonomic instability**

Lead-pipe rigidity = constant stiff muscle tone throughout the whole movement, like trying to bend a metal pipe.

Altered mental status = confusion, agitation, delirium, stupor, or coma.

Autonomic instability can look like:

* tachycardia
* labile blood pressure
* sweating
* fever

Tachycardia = fast heart rate.

Labile blood pressure = blood pressure that swings up and down.

---

### Why it happens

Most commonly, NMS happens after:

* starting an antipsychotic
* increasing an antipsychotic dose
* using a high-potency antipsychotic like haloperidol

Haloperidol is a first-generation antipsychotic that strongly blocks **D2 receptors (dopamine type 2 receptors)**.

Dopamine normally helps regulate:

* movement through the basal ganglia
* temperature control through the hypothalamus

Basal ganglia = deep brain structures that help control movement.

Hypothalamus = brain region that helps control temperature, hunger, hormones, and autonomic function.

So when dopamine signaling drops too much:

* basal ganglia dysfunction causes severe rigidity
* hypothalamic dysfunction contributes to fever/autonomic instability

---

### Why CK gets high

CK (creatine kinase) is an enzyme released when muscle breaks down.

In NMS, the muscles are rigid for a long time, so they get injured and break down.

That causes:

* high CK
* myoglobinuria
* acute kidney injury

Myoglobinuria = myoglobin in urine from muscle breakdown.

Acute kidney injury = sudden drop in kidney function.

This muscle breakdown is called **rhabdomyolysis**.

---

### NMS vs serotonin syndrome

Both can cause fever, confusion, sweating, and tachycardia.

But the key difference:

* **NMS:** dopamine blockade, slower onset over days, lead-pipe rigidity, high CK
* **Serotonin syndrome:** too much serotonin, faster onset over hours, hyperreflexia/clonus, diarrhea

Hyperreflexia = exaggerated reflexes.

Clonus = rhythmic involuntary muscle contractions.

---

### Treatment idea

First steps:

* stop the offending drug
* ICU-level supportive care
* cooling
* IV fluids for rhabdomyolysis

IV fluids = intravenous fluids given through a vein.

Specific drugs sometimes used:

* **dantrolene** to reduce muscle rigidity
* **bromocriptine** to increase dopamine activity

Bromocriptine is a dopamine agonist, meaning it activates dopamine receptors.

---

## One-line intuition

NMS is what happens when antipsychotics block dopamine too strongly: the brain loses normal movement/temperature control, so the patient becomes **hot, rigid, confused, sweaty, and CK-high**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, the classic clue is:

* **antipsychotic exposure + fever + lead-pipe rigidity + very high CK**

Differentiate it from serotonin syndrome by remembering:

* NMS = **rigid and slow**
* serotonin syndrome = **clonus and fast**

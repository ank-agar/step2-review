### Core idea

**Prenatal radiation exposure** means the embryo/fetus is exposed to **ionizing radiation** while in the uterus.

Ionizing radiation = radiation strong enough to knock electrons off atoms and damage DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid).

Examples:

* X-rays
* CT (computed tomography)
* radiation therapy
* nuclear/radiation emergencies
* radioactive material swallowed or inhaled by the pregnant person

---

### Why the fetus is vulnerable

Radiation mainly hurts cells by damaging DNA.

The fetus is extra sensitive because fetal cells are:

* dividing rapidly
* differentiating into organs
* building the CNS (central nervous system), especially the brain

So the same injury that might be mild for an adult cell can be a bigger deal for a developing fetal cell.

---

### What can happen

Possible effects depend mostly on:

* **dose** = how much radiation
* **timing** = what gestational age (pregnancy age) the fetus is

High enough exposure can cause:

* miscarriage
* growth restriction
* congenital malformations
* intellectual disability
* increased childhood cancer risk

But routine diagnostic imaging is usually far below the dose that causes fetal malformations.

---

### Timing intuition

The most important window is early development.

* **Very early pregnancy:** severe injury may cause miscarriage
* **Organogenesis** = organ formation, especially weeks 3-8: malformations are the big concern
* **Weeks 8-15:** brain/CNS effects are especially important
* **Later pregnancy:** fetus is less sensitive to major malformations, but cancer risk can still increase

Think:

> early fetus = rapidly dividing blueprint stage, so DNA damage can disrupt the whole plan.

---

### External vs internal exposure

**External exposure:** radiation source is outside the body, like an X-ray beam or radiation emergency source.

The pregnant abdomen partially shields the fetus, so fetal dose is often lower than the pregnant person's dose.

**Internal exposure:** radioactive material is swallowed or inhaled.

Then it can enter maternal blood and may reach the fetus through the placenta/umbilical cord.

Placenta = organ that exchanges oxygen, nutrients, and waste between maternal blood and fetal blood.

Umbilical cord = fetal connection to the placenta.

---

## One-line intuition

Prenatal radiation exposure is dangerous when enough ionizing radiation hits rapidly dividing fetal cells, especially during early organ and brain development.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember: **radiation is a teratogen**.

Teratogen = anything that can disrupt fetal development and cause birth defects.

The classic high-yield idea is:

* early exposure → miscarriage/malformations
* later exposure → growth restriction, CNS problems, and increased cancer risk

## Seasonal affective disorder

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Circadian_rhythm.svg/1280px-Circadian_rhythm.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/22/Light_Therapy_for_SAD.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8d/Bright_light_lamp.jpg)

Seasonal affective disorder (SAD) is depression that comes back in a seasonal pattern, classically in fall/winter.

Think:

* less sunlight
* circadian rhythm disruption (circadian = daily body clock)
* serotonin changes (serotonin = mood-regulating neurotransmitter)
* melatonin changes (melatonin = sleep hormone)

---

### Core idea

Winter has shorter days, so the brain gets less light input through the retina (light-sensing layer of the eye).

That can confuse the body clock in the hypothalamus (brain area that controls homeostasis), especially the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN; the body's master circadian clock).

Result:

* low mood
* low energy
* hypersomnia (sleeping too much)
* increased appetite/carbohydrate craving
* weight gain

---

### Why light matters

Light normally tells the brain:

> "It is daytime, stay awake and regulate mood/energy."

In winter-pattern SAD:

* less light → more melatonin effect → sleepiness/fatigue
* less light → lower serotonin activity → depressed mood
* shifted circadian rhythm → sleep and mood feel "out of sync"

So the problem is not just "being sad about winter."

It is a seasonal mismatch between light exposure and brain rhythm.

---

### Treatment intuition

Bright light therapy uses a light box to replace missing morning sunlight.

Usually:

* used in the morning
* around 10,000 lux (lux = unit of light intensity)
* helps reset circadian rhythm and improve mood

Do not stare directly into it; it is usually placed off to the side while you sit nearby.

---

## One-line intuition

SAD is winter depression from low light throwing off serotonin, melatonin, and the circadian clock.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, associate SAD with **atypical depression features**:

* hypersomnia
* hyperphagia (increased eating)
* carbohydrate craving
* weight gain

Also be careful with light therapy in bipolar disorder because it can precipitate mania (abnormally elevated/irritable mood with increased energy).

### Core idea

Gilbert syndrome is a benign inherited problem where the liver has decreased activity of:

* UGT1A1 (uridine diphosphate glucuronosyltransferase 1A1)

That enzyme conjugates bilirubin.

Conjugation means:

> making bilirubin water-soluble so it can leave through bile.

So in Gilbert syndrome:

* less conjugation
* unconjugated bilirubin builds up
* mild intermittent jaundice can happen

---

### Why unconjugated bilirubin increases

Bilirubin starts from old red blood cell breakdown.

Before the liver conjugates it, bilirubin is:

* unconjugated
* indirect
* lipid-soluble
* albumin-bound in blood

Normally, hepatocytes (liver cells) use UGT1A1 to add glucuronic acid, making bilirubin conjugated/direct and easier to excrete.

In Gilbert syndrome, UGT1A1 activity is mildly reduced, so bilirubin gets stuck in the unconjugated form.

---

### Classic presentation

Usually:

* asymptomatic
* mild jaundice or scleral icterus (yellowing of the white part of the eyes)
* normal liver enzymes
* increased unconjugated bilirubin

Triggers that can bring it out:

* fasting
* illness
* stress
* strenuous exercise

Why fasting triggers it:

Fasting increases bilirubin production/handling stress, and the already-slow UGT1A1 pathway cannot keep up.

---

### What makes it benign

The enzyme is not absent.

It is just reduced.

So bilirubin rises mildly, but the liver itself is not being damaged.

That is why labs usually show:

* normal AST (aspartate aminotransferase)
* normal ALT (alanine aminotransferase)
* normal alkaline phosphatase
* isolated indirect hyperbilirubinemia (high unconjugated bilirubin)

---

## One-line intuition

Gilbert syndrome = a slow bilirubin-conjugating enzyme, causing mild unconjugated jaundice during stress/fasting, with otherwise normal liver function.

---

## Pearl

* Gilbert syndrome = mild decreased UGT1A1, benign
* Crigler-Najjar syndrome = severe UGT1A1 deficiency, much more dangerous unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia

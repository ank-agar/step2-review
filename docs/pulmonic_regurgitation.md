### Core idea

Pulmonic regurgitation means the pulmonary valve does not close tightly, so blood leaks backward from the pulmonary artery into the right ventricle during diastole (heart relaxation).

Break it down:

* pulmonic = pulmonary valve, the valve between the right ventricle and pulmonary artery
* regurgitation = backward leaking

So:

> blood that was supposed to go to the lungs falls back into the right ventricle.

---

### Normal flow

Normally:

* right ventricle pumps blood through the pulmonary valve
* blood enters the pulmonary artery
* blood goes to the lungs to get oxygen
* pulmonary valve closes so blood cannot fall backward

In pulmonic regurgitation:

* the valve is leaky
* some blood returns to the right ventricle
* the right ventricle gets extra volume

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Right ventricular dilation:** extra blood keeps returning to the right ventricle, so it stretches over time
* **Right-sided heart failure:** chronic volume overload eventually weakens the right ventricle
* **Diastolic murmur:** the leak happens during diastole (heart relaxation), when blood flows backward from pulmonary artery to right ventricle
* **Shortness of breath/fatigue in severe cases:** poor forward flow to the lungs and right heart dysfunction reduce effective circulation

---

### Common causes

Often due to:

* pulmonary hypertension (high pressure in the pulmonary arteries)
* repaired Tetralogy of Fallot (congenital heart disease with 4 defects)
* infective endocarditis (infection of the heart lining/valves)
* carcinoid syndrome (serotonin-secreting tumor syndrome that can scar right-sided valves)
* congenital pulmonary valve abnormality

Pulmonary hypertension can stretch the pulmonary artery/valve ring, so the valve leaflets cannot meet properly.

---

### Murmur

Pulmonic regurgitation causes an early diastolic decrescendo murmur.

Decrescendo means it starts louder and fades.

If due to pulmonary hypertension, it is called a Graham Steell murmur.

---

## One-line intuition

Pulmonic regurgitation is like a leaky exit door from the right ventricle: blood goes toward the lungs, but some slips back and overloads the right ventricle.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember: pulmonic regurgitation is a **diastolic** murmur because semilunar valves, aortic and pulmonary, are supposed to be closed during diastole.

### Core idea

Enuresis = repeated involuntary urination after the age when bladder control is expected.

Most commonly, this means:

* nocturnal enuresis = bedwetting during sleep
* diurnal enuresis = daytime wetting

In kids, especially nighttime bedwetting, it is usually a developmental bladder-control issue, not the child being lazy or doing it on purpose.

---

### Why it happens

To stay dry at night, the child needs 3 things to line up:

* bladder can hold the urine
* kidneys do not make too much urine overnight
* brain wakes up when the bladder is full

Enuresis happens when one or more of these fails.

Common mechanisms:

* **Low nighttime ADH (antidiuretic hormone):** kidneys keep making too much urine at night
* **Small/overactive bladder:** bladder fills or contracts too early
* **Deep sleep/poor arousal:** full-bladder signals do not wake the child up

So the basic equation is:

> too much nighttime urine + not enough bladder capacity + not waking up = bedwetting

---

### Types

**Primary enuresis**

The child has never had a long dry period.

Think:

* delayed maturation of nighttime bladder control

**Secondary enuresis**

The child was dry for at least 6 months, then starts wetting again.

Think about triggers like:

* urinary tract infection
* constipation
* diabetes mellitus
* diabetes insipidus
* stress or major life change
* sleep disorders

---

### Why constipation matters

The rectum sits right behind the bladder.

If the rectum is full of stool, it can press on the bladder and make it:

* hold less urine
* contract more easily
* leak urine

So constipation can cause both daytime wetting and nighttime enuresis.

---

### Treatment idea

First-line basics:

* avoid caffeine
* treat constipation
* scheduled voiding
* limit excess fluids right before bed
* bedwetting alarm

Medication:

* desmopressin, a synthetic ADH (antidiuretic hormone), can reduce nighttime urine production

---

## One-line intuition

Enuresis is a mismatch between nighttime urine production, bladder storage, and the brain waking up to a full bladder.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: desmopressin is an ADH (antidiuretic hormone) analog, so it helps nocturnal enuresis by making the collecting ducts reabsorb more water and produce less urine overnight.

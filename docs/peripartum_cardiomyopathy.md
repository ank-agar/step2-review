# Peripartum cardiomyopathy

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18082.jpg)

![Image](https://www.heart.org/-/media/Images/Health-Topics/Cardiomyopathy/pregnant-woman-in-bed.jpg?sc_lang=en)

### Core idea

Peripartum cardiomyopathy (PPCM) is **new systolic heart failure** that happens near the end of pregnancy or in the months after delivery.

Systolic heart failure = the heart cannot squeeze well.

So the main problem is:

* pregnancy/postpartum patient
* weak dilated left ventricle
* low ejection fraction
* no other clear cause of heart failure

Ejection fraction (EF) = the percent of blood the left ventricle pumps out with each beat.

---

### What is happening?

The left ventricle (LV), the main pumping chamber of the heart, becomes weak and often dilated.

That means:

* less blood gets pumped forward to the body
* blood backs up into the lungs
* fluid backs up into the legs and abdomen

This is why symptoms look like regular heart failure:

* shortness of breath
* orthopnea, meaning shortness of breath when lying flat
* fatigue
* leg swelling
* cough or pulmonary edema, meaning fluid in the lungs

---

### Why pregnancy triggers it

Pregnancy already increases blood volume and cardiac workload.

In susceptible patients, that extra stress plus postpartum hormonal/vascular injury can damage the heart muscle.

One proposed mechanism:

* prolactin, a lactation hormone, can be broken into a toxic fragment
* that fragment injures blood vessels and heart muscle cells
* the LV becomes weak

This is why bromocriptine, a drug that lowers prolactin, is sometimes discussed in severe cases.

---

### Diagnosis

Think of it when a pregnant or recently postpartum patient has heart failure symptoms that seem too severe to be "normal pregnancy."

Key test:

* echocardiogram, meaning heart ultrasound

Classic finding:

* reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), usually less than 45%

LVEF = left ventricular ejection fraction.

---

### Complications

Because the LV is weak and dilated, blood can stagnate inside the heart.

Stagnant blood can clot, so PPCM increases risk of:

* LV thrombus, meaning clot inside the left ventricle
* embolic stroke, meaning a clot travels to the brain
* arrhythmias, meaning abnormal heart rhythms

---

## One-line intuition

Peripartum cardiomyopathy is **pregnancy-associated dilated cardiomyopathy**: the postpartum heart becomes too weak and stretched out to pump forward well.

---

## Pearl 🔥

PPCM classically presents in the **last month of pregnancy or within 5 months postpartum** with heart failure and **LVEF less than 45%** after other causes are excluded.

## Temporal arteritis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Giant_cell_arteritis_--_intermed_mag.jpg/250px-Giant_cell_arteritis_--_intermed_mag.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Cerebral_Giant-Cell_Vasculitis.jpg/250px-Cerebral_Giant-Cell_Vasculitis.jpg)

Temporal arteritis = **giant cell arteritis**, a **large-vessel vasculitis** (inflammation of large blood vessels), usually in older adults.

It classically affects branches of the **external carotid artery**, especially the **temporal artery** near the temple.

---

### Core idea

The immune system attacks the artery wall.

That causes:

* inflammation
* wall thickening
* narrowed lumen (inside opening of a vessel)
* decreased blood flow

So the artery becomes painful and can’t deliver enough blood.

---

### Classic symptoms

Think: **old patient + new headache + jaw pain + vision risk**

* new temporal headache
* scalp tenderness, like pain with combing hair
* jaw claudication (jaw pain/fatigue with chewing due to ischemia)
* fever, fatigue, weight loss
* visual symptoms, like blurry vision or sudden vision loss

---

### Why jaw claudication happens

Chewing uses jaw muscles.

If inflamed arteries can’t increase blood flow to those muscles, the jaw becomes ischemic (low oxygen from low blood supply).

So chewing causes pain, similar to leg claudication in peripheral artery disease.

---

### Why vision loss happens

The same inflammatory process can affect arteries supplying the eye, especially branches related to the ophthalmic artery.

Less blood to the optic nerve can cause ischemic optic neuropathy (optic nerve damage from poor blood flow).

That’s why temporal arteritis is a medical emergency.

---

### Diagnosis clues

Labs often show:

* high ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate)
* high CRP (C-reactive protein)
* anemia of chronic disease

Biopsy can show:

* granulomatous inflammation (organized immune-cell inflammation)
* multinucleated giant cells
* fragmentation of the internal elastic lamina (elastic layer inside the artery wall)

---

### Treatment

Give high-dose glucocorticoids (steroids like prednisone) immediately if suspected.

Do not wait for biopsy if vision symptoms are present, because blindness can be irreversible.

---

## One-line intuition

Temporal arteritis is an immune attack on scalp/cranial arteries in older adults, causing headache and jaw pain, with the big danger being sudden permanent vision loss.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Temporal arteritis is strongly associated with polymyalgia rheumatica, which causes proximal muscle pain/stiffness in the shoulders and hips.

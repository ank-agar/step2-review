# Post-traumatic stress disorder

![Image](https://www.brainline.org/sites/default/files/styles/full_view_image/public/migrated/6-23-2016%2011-41-24%20AM.jpg?itok=-nz7dtj5)

![Image](https://www.postinfographics.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/5-Signs-You-May-Have-PTSD.jpg)

Post-traumatic stress disorder, or PTSD (post-traumatic stress disorder), is a trauma-related disorder where the brain keeps reacting as if danger is still present after a traumatic event.

Trauma can include:

* combat
* assault
* abuse
* serious accident
* natural disaster
* witnessing death or threatened death

---

### Core idea

PTSD is basically:

> the fear system gets stuck in “threat mode.”

So reminders of the trauma can trigger the same body/brain response as the original event.

---

### Main symptom groups

PTSD symptoms fall into 4 big buckets:

* **Intrusion:** flashbacks, nightmares, unwanted memories
* **Avoidance:** avoiding people, places, thoughts, or feelings linked to the trauma
* **Negative mood/thoughts:** guilt, shame, detachment, low mood, distorted blame
* **Hyperarousal:** being jumpy, irritable, hypervigilant, poor sleep, poor concentration

Hypervigilant means overly alert for danger.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Think of 3 key brain areas:

* **Amygdala:** fear alarm
* **Hippocampus:** context/memory organizer
* **Prefrontal cortex:** rational “brake” on fear

In PTSD:

* the **amygdala** is overactive, so danger signals feel intense
* the **hippocampus** has trouble placing the memory in the past, so triggers can feel like “it is happening again”
* the **prefrontal cortex** does not calm the fear response well enough

That is why a smell, sound, place, or date can suddenly cause flashbacks, panic, sweating, tachycardia (fast heart rate), or avoidance.

---

### Timing

Symptoms must last **more than 1 month** after the trauma.

If symptoms last **3 days to 1 month**, that is acute stress disorder.

---

## One-line intuition

PTSD is when trauma memories stay emotionally “live,” so the brain keeps sounding the danger alarm even when the person is currently safe.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: PTSD is classically associated with **increased sympathetic arousal** and an exaggerated startle response, while the key diagnostic timing is **symptoms >1 month** after trauma.

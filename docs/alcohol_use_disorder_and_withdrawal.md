## Alcohol use disorder and withdrawal

![Image](https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/sites/default/files/styles/large/public/Three_Stages_Alcohol_Disorder_Cycle_Brain_Graphic_English.jpg?itok=kXZnXDYS)

![Image](https://infographicplaza.com/wp-content/uploads/Alcohol-Withdrawals-Timeline-Infographic-plaza-thumb.png)

Alcohol use disorder (AUD) means a person has impaired control over alcohol use despite harm.

Basically:

* they crave alcohol
* cannot reliably cut down
* keep using despite problems
* may develop tolerance and withdrawal

Tolerance means needing more alcohol to get the same effect.

Withdrawal means symptoms that happen when alcohol is stopped after chronic heavy use.

---

### Core idea

Alcohol is a CNS depressant (central nervous system depressant).

It mainly:

* increases GABA activity
* decreases glutamate activity

GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) is the brain's main inhibitory neurotransmitter.

Glutamate is the brain's main excitatory neurotransmitter.

So alcohol is like:

> more brake + less gas.

That causes sedation, relaxation, poor coordination, and impaired judgment.

---

### Why withdrawal happens

With chronic alcohol use, the brain adapts by doing the opposite:

* decreases GABA signaling
* increases glutamate/NMDA receptor signaling

NMDA (N-methyl-D-aspartate) receptors are glutamate receptors that increase neuronal excitation.

So when alcohol is suddenly removed:

* the alcohol "brake" is gone
* but the brain still has extra "gas"

Result:

> CNS hyperexcitability.

That explains:

* tremor
* anxiety
* insomnia
* tachycardia (fast heart rate)
* hypertension (high blood pressure)
* sweating
* seizures
* hallucinations
* delirium tremens

Delirium tremens (DTs) = severe alcohol withdrawal with confusion, autonomic instability, hallucinations, and risk of death.

---

### Timeline

Withdrawal often starts within hours after the last drink.

Typical pattern:

* 6-24 hours: tremor, anxiety, nausea, insomnia
* 12-48 hours: seizures can occur
* 48-96 hours: hallucinations and delirium tremens can occur

The big dangerous things are:

* seizures
* delirium tremens

---

### Treatment logic

Treat withdrawal with benzodiazepines.

Benzodiazepines are drugs that increase GABA-A receptor activity.

Why?

Because alcohol withdrawal is basically:

> not enough inhibitory GABA effect + too much excitatory glutamate effect.

So benzodiazepines restore the missing "brake" and prevent seizures.

Also give thiamine before glucose.

Thiamine is vitamin B1.

Why before glucose?

Because giving glucose to a thiamine-deficient patient can worsen Wernicke encephalopathy.

Wernicke encephalopathy = confusion, ophthalmoplegia, and ataxia.

Ophthalmoplegia means weak/paralyzed eye movements.

Ataxia means uncoordinated movement.

---

## One-line intuition

Alcohol pushes the brain's brake pedal; chronic use makes the brain compensate by pressing the gas, so stopping alcohol suddenly leaves the brain overexcited.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Alcohol withdrawal and benzodiazepine withdrawal can both be life-threatening because both involve loss of GABA-mediated inhibition, causing CNS hyperexcitability and seizures.

# Cord prolapse

![Image](https://americanpregnancy.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/08/umbilical_cord_prolapse.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK542241/bin/Cord.prolaps.jpg)

Cord prolapse means the umbilical cord slips down through the cervix before the baby’s presenting part.

Presenting part = the part of the baby coming out first, usually the head.

So instead of:

* baby’s head first
* cord safely above

you get:

* cord first or next to the head
* baby’s head can compress the cord

---

### Why this is dangerous

The umbilical cord is the baby’s lifeline:

* oxygen
* nutrients
* blood flow between placenta and baby

If the baby’s head presses on the cord, blood flow drops.

Less cord blood flow = less fetal oxygen.

That causes fetal distress, usually seen as:

* sudden fetal bradycardia
* variable decelerations on fetal heart tracing

Fetal bradycardia = abnormally slow fetal heart rate.

Variable decelerations = sudden drops in fetal heart rate from cord compression.

---

### When to suspect it

Classic setup:

* rupture of membranes
* then sudden fetal heart rate drop

Rupture of membranes = “water breaks.”

Risk factors are things that leave extra space for the cord to slip down:

* breech presentation
* transverse lie
* prematurity
* multiple gestation
* polyhydramnios

Breech = butt/feet first.

Transverse lie = baby is sideways.

Polyhydramnios = too much amniotic fluid.

Multiple gestation = twins/triplets/etc.

---

### Management intuition

This is an obstetric emergency.

Main goal:

* relieve pressure off the cord
* deliver fast

Temporary moves:

* lift the presenting fetal part off the cord manually
* knee-chest or Trendelenburg position
* keep exposed cord warm and moist

Trendelenburg position = lying with the head lower than the pelvis.

Definitive treatment is usually emergency C-section unless vaginal delivery is immediately happening.

C-section = cesarean section, surgical delivery through the abdomen and uterus.

---

## One-line intuition

Cord prolapse is when the cord falls below the baby, then the baby compresses its own oxygen supply.

---

## Pearl 🔥

After rupture of membranes, sudden fetal bradycardia or recurrent variable decelerations should make you think cord prolapse until proven otherwise.

### Core idea

Chylothorax = chyle in the pleural space.

* **Chyle** = milky lymph fluid full of dietary fat, especially triglycerides (fat molecules)
* **Pleural space** = space between the lung and chest wall
* **Thoracic duct** = big lymphatic tube that carries fat-rich lymph from the gut back to the blood

So chylothorax is basically:

> the thoracic duct leaks, and fatty lymph fills the chest around the lung.

---

### Why it looks milky

After you eat fat:

* small intestine absorbs fat
* fat is packaged into chylomicrons (fat-carrying particles)
* chylomicrons enter lymph, not directly blood
* lymph travels up the thoracic duct

If the thoracic duct is injured or blocked, that fat-rich lymph leaks into the pleural space.

That is why the fluid can look white/milky.

---

### Common causes

Think damage or obstruction of the thoracic duct:

* surgery or trauma in the chest/neck
* malignancy (cancer), especially lymphoma
* congenital lymphatic malformations in babies

**Lymphoma** is high yield because enlarged lymph nodes can block lymph drainage.

---

### Diagnosis

Pleural fluid shows:

* high triglycerides, classically >110 mg/dL
* chylomicrons present
* often lymphocyte-predominant fluid

Chest X-ray (radiograph using X-rays) just shows a pleural effusion (fluid around the lung), but it cannot tell you the fluid is chyle.

You need fluid analysis.

---

### One-line intuition

Chylothorax = a fatty lymph leak from the thoracic duct into the chest, causing a milky pleural effusion.

---

## Pearl

Thoracic duct injury location predicts side:

* below T5 (5th thoracic vertebra) -> usually right-sided chylothorax
* above T5 -> usually left-sided chylothorax

Reason: the thoracic duct crosses from right to left around T5.

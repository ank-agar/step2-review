### Core idea

Medication overuse headache is a chronic daily-ish headache caused by taking acute headache meds too often.

Acute meds = meds used to stop pain right now, not prevent future headaches.

It is also called rebound headache.

---

### The cycle

The patient has migraines or tension headaches, so they take pain meds.

The meds help briefly.

Then the headache comes back, so they take more meds.

Over time, the brain becomes more pain-sensitive, so headaches happen more often.

So the medicine that used to fix the headache starts maintaining the headache cycle.

---

### Diagnostic idea

Think:

* headache on 15 or more days per month
* for more than 3 months
* with frequent use of headache-relief medication

Common triggers:

* NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs), like ibuprofen or naproxen
* acetaminophen
* triptans
* opioids
* butalbital-containing meds
* combo meds with caffeine

---

### Why it happens

Frequent pain-med use can cause central sensitization.

Central sensitization = the CNS (central nervous system) becomes easier to activate by pain signals.

So instead of needing a big trigger to feel headache pain, the brain starts reacting to smaller and smaller triggers.

That is why the headache becomes more frequent and harder to treat.

---

### Treatment intuition

The main treatment is stopping or tapering the overused medication.

Headache may get worse at first because the brain is used to the medication pattern.

Then headaches usually improve after the rebound cycle breaks.

Preventive meds may be added to reduce the original migraine/tension headache frequency.

---

## One-line intuition

Medication overuse headache = the headache medicine is being taken so often that it turns episodic headaches into chronic headaches.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY distinction:

* simple analgesics like NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) or acetaminophen: risk when used 15 or more days/month
* triptans, opioids, butalbital, ergotamines, or combination analgesics: risk when used 10 or more days/month

So if a migraine patient is taking abortive meds more than 2 days/week, think medication overuse headache.

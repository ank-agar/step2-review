Hyperinsulinism = too much insulin activity.

Insulin is the hormone from pancreatic beta cells that pushes glucose (blood sugar) out of the blood and into tissues.

So the core problem is:

* too much insulin
* glucose gets pulled out of blood
* blood glucose drops
* hypoglycemia (low blood glucose) symptoms happen

---

### Main idea

Insulin is basically the body's "store glucose" signal.

It tells the body:

* take glucose into muscle/fat
* store glucose as glycogen (stored glucose) in liver and muscle
* stop making new glucose
* stop making ketones (fat-derived backup fuel)

So if insulin is high when it should be low, the brain gets hit twice:

* low glucose
* low ketones

because insulin blocks ketogenesis (ketone production).

---

### Why symptoms happen

Low brain fuel causes neuroglycopenic symptoms (brain glucose deprivation symptoms):

* sweating
* shakiness
* irritability
* lethargy
* seizures
* coma in severe cases

This is especially dangerous in newborns because the brain depends heavily on a steady fuel supply.

---

### Congenital hyperinsulinism

Congenital hyperinsulinism = a baby is born with beta cells that secrete too much insulin.

Common severe causes involve:

* **ABCC8**
* **KCNJ11**

These genes code for parts of the ATP-sensitive potassium channel (a potassium channel in beta cells that helps control insulin release).

If this channel does not work properly, beta cells stay too depolarized, calcium enters, and insulin keeps getting released.

In simple terms:

> the beta cell's insulin-release switch gets stuck "on."

---

### Focal vs diffuse

Congenital hyperinsulinism can be:

* **focal:** one small area of pancreas over-secretes insulin
* **diffuse:** the whole pancreas is affected

This matters because focal disease can sometimes be cured by removing that small lesion, while diffuse disease is harder.

---

## One-line intuition

Hyperinsulinism means insulin is inappropriately high, so glucose gets stored instead of staying in the blood, causing hypoglycemia.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia, ketones are low because insulin inhibits lipolysis (fat breakdown) and ketogenesis (ketone production).

That is why low glucose + low ketones points toward excess insulin.

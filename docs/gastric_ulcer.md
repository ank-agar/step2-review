## Gastric ulcer

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8a/Gastric_Ulcer.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7d/Deep_gastric_ulcer.png)

![Image](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/mmanual-ssq1ci05/media/professional/images/g/a/s/gastric_ulcer_high.jpg?mw=2048&sc_lang=en)

A **gastric ulcer** is an open sore in the stomach lining.

Break it down:

* **gastric** = stomach
* **ulcer** = erosion/open sore in a mucosal surface

So a gastric ulcer is basically:

> stomach acid has eaten through the protective stomach lining and made a crater-like wound.

---

### Core idea

Normally, the stomach protects itself from acid using:

* mucus
* bicarbonate
* good blood flow
* prostaglandins (protective lipid signals that help maintain mucus and blood flow)

A gastric ulcer happens when the damaging forces beat the protective forces.

Damaging forces:

* stomach acid
* pepsin (protein-digesting enzyme)
* **H. pylori** (*Helicobacter pylori*, a spiral bacteria that infects stomach mucosa)
* **NSAIDs** (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, like ibuprofen/naproxen/aspirin)

---

### Why H. pylori causes ulcers

**H. pylori** damages the mucus barrier and causes inflammation.

Once the mucus barrier is weakened, acid can reach deeper tissue.

That causes:

* epithelial injury
* inflammation
* ulcer crater formation

Intuition:

The acid was always there, but the stomach usually has a raincoat. H. pylori damages the raincoat.

---

### Why NSAIDs cause ulcers

NSAIDs block prostaglandin production.

Less prostaglandin means:

* less mucus
* less bicarbonate
* less mucosal blood flow

So the stomach lining becomes easier for acid to injure.

---

### Classic symptom pattern

Gastric ulcer pain often gets worse with meals.

Why?

Eating stimulates acid secretion and stretches/irritates the already injured stomach lining.

Contrast:

* gastric ulcer: pain worse with food
* duodenal ulcer (ulcer in the first part of the small intestine): pain better with food, then returns later

---

### Important complication clues

Bleeding ulcer:

* melena (black tarry stool from digested blood)
* hematemesis (vomiting blood)
* iron deficiency anemia (low iron from chronic blood loss)

Perforation:

* sudden severe abdominal pain
* rigid abdomen
* free air under diaphragm on x-ray

---

### Diagnosis/treatment

Diagnosis is usually by upper endoscopy (camera test of esophagus, stomach, and duodenum).

Treatment:

* **PPI** (proton pump inhibitor; acid-lowering drug) like omeprazole
* stop NSAIDs if possible
* test for and eradicate **H. pylori** with antibiotics

---

## One-line intuition

A gastric ulcer is a punched-out wound in the stomach lining because acid overwhelms mucosal protection.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Gastric ulcers should be biopsied to rule out gastric cancer, because a malignant gastric cancer can look like an ulcer.

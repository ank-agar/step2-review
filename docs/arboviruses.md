Arboviruses are **arthropod-borne viruses**.

Arthropod-borne = spread by arthropods, especially:

* mosquitoes
* ticks
* sandflies

So the name is basically:

> **ARBO = arthropod-borne**

Important examples:

* West Nile virus
* dengue virus
* yellow fever virus
* Zika virus
* chikungunya virus
* Eastern equine encephalitis virus

Most are **RNA viruses** (ribonucleic acid viruses), often from families like **Flaviviridae** (flavivirus family) or **Togaviridae** (togavirus family).

---

### Core idea

The virus cycles between:

* an arthropod vector (organism that transmits disease), like a mosquito
* an animal reservoir (organism that maintains the infection), like birds or primates

Humans often get infected when the mosquito/tick accidentally bites us.

For many arboviruses, humans are **dead-end hosts** (hosts that do not make enough virus in blood to keep spreading it back to mosquitoes).

Classic example:

* West Nile virus cycles between birds and mosquitoes
* humans and horses can get sick
* but humans/horses usually do not infect new mosquitoes

---

### Why they cause fever, rash, and brain disease

After the bite, the virus enters blood and causes **viremia** (virus in the bloodstream).

That can cause:

* fever
* headache
* myalgias (muscle aches)
* rash
* arthralgias (joint pains)

Some arboviruses can invade the CNS (central nervous system), causing:

* meningitis (inflammation of meninges, the brain/spinal cord coverings)
* encephalitis (inflammation of brain tissue)
* seizures or altered mental status

---

## One-line intuition

Arboviruses are viruses that use biting bugs as the delivery system.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, think **mosquito + encephalitis** when you hear arbovirus, especially **West Nile virus**, which is associated with **flaccid paralysis** from anterior horn cell involvement.

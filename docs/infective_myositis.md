### Core idea

Infective myositis = infection of skeletal muscle.

Myositis means muscle inflammation:

* myo- = muscle
* -itis = inflammation

So infective myositis is when bacteria, viruses, fungi, or parasites directly infect muscle and make it inflamed.

The most classic Step 1 version is bacterial pyomyositis.

Pyomyositis means pus-forming muscle infection:

* pyo- = pus
* myo- = muscle
* -itis = inflammation

So think:

> bacteria seed muscle → muscle becomes inflamed → pus/abscess can form inside the muscle.

---

### What causes it?

Most commonly:

* **Staphylococcus aureus**

Especially if there is:

* trauma
* immunosuppression
* diabetes mellitus
* HIV (human immunodeficiency virus)
* IV (intravenous) drug use
* bacteremia (bacteria in the blood)

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The infected muscle gets swollen and inflamed, so patients can have:

* localized muscle pain
* tenderness
* swelling
* fever
* difficulty moving that area

If pus collects inside the muscle, that is an abscess.

Abscess = walled-off pocket of pus.

That is why antibiotics alone may not be enough sometimes:

* antibiotics treat the bacteria
* drainage removes the pus collection

---

### Big distinction

Do not confuse infective myositis with autoimmune myositis.

Autoimmune myositis:

* immune system attacks muscle
* usually causes symmetric proximal muscle weakness

Infective myositis:

* organism infects muscle
* often causes focal pain, fever, and sometimes abscess

---

## One-line intuition

Infective myositis is basically a muscle infection; if it is bacterial, especially Staph aureus, it can turn into a pus-filled abscess inside the muscle.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, pyomyositis is classically associated with **Staphylococcus aureus** and presents as a painful, tender muscle infection that may require both antibiotics and drainage if an abscess forms.

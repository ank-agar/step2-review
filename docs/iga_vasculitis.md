# IgA vasculitis

![Image](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/instance/2874920/bin/GRP2010-597648.007.jpg)

![Image](https://dermnetnz.org/assets/Uploads/vascular/hsp1.jpg)

![Image](https://www.hopkinsvasculitis.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/05/hsp_clip_image010.jpg)

IgA vasculitis is a small-vessel vasculitis caused by IgA (immunoglobulin A) immune complex deposition.

Old name:

* Henoch-Schonlein purpura

Core idea:

* IgA immune complexes deposit in small vessels
* this activates inflammation
* inflamed vessels leak blood/fluid
* so you get purpura, joint pain, abdominal pain, and kidney findings

---

### Classic tetrad

Think:

* palpable purpura
* arthralgia/arthritis
* abdominal pain
* renal involvement

Renal involvement usually means:

* hematuria (blood in urine)
* proteinuria (protein in urine)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Palpable purpura:** inflamed small skin vessels leak RBCs (red blood cells), especially in dependent areas like legs/buttocks
* **Joint pain:** immune complex inflammation around joints
* **Abdominal pain:** small-vessel vasculitis in the GI (gastrointestinal) tract causes bowel wall edema/bleeding
* **Kidney disease:** IgA deposits in the glomerular mesangium, similar to IgA nephropathy

---

### Key Step 1 association

Often occurs in children after an upper respiratory infection.

So the pattern is:

> kid + recent URI (upper respiratory infection) + palpable purpura on legs/buttocks + abdominal pain + hematuria = IgA vasculitis

---

## One-line intuition

IgA gets stuck in small vessels, especially skin/GI/kidney vessels, causing inflammation and leakage.

---

## Pearl 🔥

IgA vasculitis and IgA nephropathy both involve IgA deposition, but IgA vasculitis is systemic:

* skin purpura
* abdominal pain
* joint pain
* kidney involvement

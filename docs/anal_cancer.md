### Core idea

Anal cancer is a malignant tumor that starts in the anus or anal canal.

Most commonly, it is:

* squamous cell carcinoma (cancer from flat epithelial surface cells)
* strongly associated with HPV (human papillomavirus), especially HPV 16

So think:

> anal cancer = HPV-driven squamous cancer near the anal canal.

---

### Why HPV causes it

High-risk HPV makes viral proteins:

* E6
* E7

These knock out tumor suppressors:

* E6 inhibits p53 (tumor suppressor that triggers DNA damage arrest/apoptosis)
* E7 inhibits Rb (retinoblastoma protein, a tumor suppressor that blocks excess cell cycle progression)

So the cell loses its brakes:

* damaged DNA is not repaired well
* abnormal squamous cells keep dividing
* dysplasia (disordered growth) can progress to carcinoma (invasive cancer)

---

### Symptoms

Can present with:

* rectal bleeding
* anal pain or pressure
* anal mass/lump
* itching or discharge
* change in bowel habits

Important: it can mimic hemorrhoids, so persistent bleeding or a mass needs evaluation.

---

### Risk factors

Major risk factors:

* HPV (human papillomavirus)
* HIV (human immunodeficiency virus) or other immunosuppression
* receptive anal intercourse
* multiple sexual partners
* smoking
* history of cervical/vulvar/vaginal dysplasia or cancer

Why immunosuppression matters:

* the immune system normally helps clear HPV-infected abnormal cells
* weaker immunity lets persistent HPV infection drive dysplasia longer

---

### Diagnosis

Diagnosis is confirmed by biopsy (taking tissue to look under a microscope).

Screening in high-risk patients may use:

* anal Pap test (Papanicolaou test looking for abnormal squamous cells)
* anoscopy (scope exam of the anus/anal canal)

---

## One-line intuition

Anal cancer is basically the anal-canal version of HPV-related cervical cancer: persistent HPV damages squamous epithelium, knocks out cell-cycle brakes, and can progress to invasive squamous cell carcinoma.

---

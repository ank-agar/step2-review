## Visceral leishmaniasis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Leishmaniasis_life_cycle_diagram_en.svg/1280px-Leishmaniasis_life_cycle_diagram_en.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Leishmania_Amastigote_%28NIH_BioArt_287%29.svg/1280px-Leishmania_Amastigote_%28NIH_BioArt_287%29.svg.png)

![Image](https://haematologica.org/article/download/10340/73529/75856)

Visceral leishmaniasis is a systemic parasitic infection caused by **Leishmania donovani complex** (protozoa that live inside macrophages).

"Visceral" means it mainly affects internal organs:

* spleen
* liver
* bone marrow
* lymph nodes

It is transmitted by the **sandfly**.

---

### Core idea

The sandfly injects **promastigotes** (flagellated parasite form), then macrophages eat them.

Inside macrophages, they become **amastigotes** (non-flagellated intracellular parasite form).

Then they multiply inside macrophages and spread through the **reticuloendothelial system** (macrophage-rich organs like spleen, liver, bone marrow, and lymph nodes).

---

### Classic findings

* **Fever**
* **Weight loss**
* **Massive splenomegaly** (enlarged spleen)
* **Hepatomegaly** (enlarged liver)
* **Pancytopenia** (low red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets)
* Hyperpigmented skin sometimes, called **kala-azar** ("black fever")

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Big spleen/liver:** infected macrophages pile up in spleen and liver
* **Pancytopenia:** bone marrow gets infiltrated, so it cannot make blood cells normally
* **Fever/weight loss:** chronic macrophage/immune activation
* **Infections/bleeding:** low white blood cells increase infection risk, and low platelets increase bleeding risk

---

### Diagnosis clue

Look for **amastigotes inside macrophages**.

These are also called **Leishman-Donovan bodies**.

Break that down:

* Leishman-Donovan bodies = intracellular amastigotes
* intracellular = inside cells
* macrophage = immune cell that eats pathogens

So the image to remember is:

> macrophages stuffed with tiny parasites.

---

## One-line intuition

Visceral leishmaniasis is a sandfly-transmitted parasite that hides and multiplies inside macrophages, so it spreads to macrophage-heavy organs like spleen, liver, and bone marrow.

---

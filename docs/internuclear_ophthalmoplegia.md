### Core idea

Internuclear ophthalmoplegia, or INO (internuclear ophthalmoplegia), is a problem with horizontal eye movement caused by damage to the MLF (medial longitudinal fasciculus).

The MLF (medial longitudinal fasciculus) is the brainstem "wire" that connects:

* CN VI (cranial nerve 6, abducens nerve) nucleus
* CN III (cranial nerve 3, oculomotor nerve) nucleus

This connection lets both eyes move together side-to-side.

---

### What happens

If the right MLF (medial longitudinal fasciculus) is damaged:

* right eye cannot adduct well
* adduct = move toward the nose
* left eye abducts, but has nystagmus
* abduct = move away from the nose
* nystagmus = rhythmic jerking eye movement

So the affected eye is the eye that cannot adduct.

---

### Why this happens

To look left:

* left CN VI (cranial nerve 6) activates the left lateral rectus muscle
* lateral rectus = pulls the eye outward
* left CN VI also sends a signal through the right MLF (medial longitudinal fasciculus)
* that signal reaches right CN III (cranial nerve 3)
* right CN III activates the right medial rectus muscle
* medial rectus = pulls the eye inward

If the right MLF is broken, the right medial rectus never gets the message.

So:

* left eye moves left
* right eye fails to move left
* patient gets diplopia (double vision)

---

### Common causes

Think:

* multiple sclerosis in young patients
* stroke in older patients

Multiple sclerosis causes demyelination, meaning loss of myelin insulation around CNS (central nervous system) nerve fibers.

The MLF is heavily myelinated, so it is a classic place for MS (multiple sclerosis) to cause problems.

---

## One-line intuition

INO is when the brainstem wire between CN VI and CN III is damaged, so one eye turns outward normally but the other eye cannot follow inward.

---

## Pearl 🔥

INO classically has impaired adduction of the ipsilateral eye with nystagmus of the abducting contralateral eye.

Ipsilateral = same side as the lesion.

Contralateral = opposite side of the lesion.

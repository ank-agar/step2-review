### Core idea

Emphysema is destruction of alveolar walls.

Alveoli (tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens) normally look like many small grapes.

In emphysema, the walls between the grapes get destroyed, so you get:

* fewer alveoli
* bigger air spaces
* less surface area for gas exchange
* loss of elastic recoil

---

### Why air gets trapped

Normal lung tissue has elastic recoil, meaning it springs back during expiration (breathing out).

In emphysema:

* alveolar walls are destroyed
* elastic tissue is lost
* small airways collapse during expiration

So air can get in, but it has trouble getting out.

That causes:

* hyperinflation
* barrel chest
* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* prolonged expiration

---

### Why DLCO is decreased

DLCO (diffusing capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide) measures how well gas crosses from alveoli into blood.

In emphysema, alveolar walls are destroyed, so there is less surface area for gas exchange.

Less surface area = lower DLCO.

That is a big contrast with chronic bronchitis, where DLCO is usually normal because the alveolar-capillary membrane is not the main thing being destroyed.

---

### Causes

Most common:

* smoking

Classic genetic cause:

* alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency

Alpha-1 antitrypsin normally inhibits elastase (an enzyme that breaks down elastin).

If alpha-1 antitrypsin is low, elastase goes unchecked and destroys elastic tissue in the lung.

---

### Types

* Centriacinar emphysema: upper lobes, smoking
* Panacinar emphysema: lower lobes, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency

Acinus means the respiratory unit distal to the terminal bronchiole.

---

## One-line intuition

Emphysema = alveolar walls are destroyed, so the lung becomes floppy, overinflated, and bad at gas exchange.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Emphysema is an obstructive lung disease with increased TLC (total lung capacity), increased RV (residual volume), and decreased DLCO (diffusing capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide).

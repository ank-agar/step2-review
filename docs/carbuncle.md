# Carbuncle

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/PHIL_Images/25645/25645_lores.jpg)

A **carbuncle** is a cluster of connected boils.

A **boil**, also called a **furuncle** (deep infection of a hair follicle), is basically:

* bacteria enter a hair follicle
* neutrophils come in
* pus forms
* a painful red bump develops

A **carbuncle** is when multiple nearby furuncles connect under the skin.

---

### Core idea

Think:

> folliculitis = small infection of a hair follicle  
> furuncle = one deep infected follicle/boil  
> carbuncle = multiple connected boils

So carbuncle is deeper and more extensive than a single furuncle.

---

### Why it happens

Usually caused by **Staphylococcus aureus** (gram-positive cocci in clusters).

Reason:

* hair follicles can trap bacteria
* bacteria multiply deeper in the dermis (middle skin layer)
* the immune system sends neutrophils
* dead neutrophils + bacteria + tissue debris = pus
* nearby follicles can merge into one larger infected area

That merging is what makes it a **carbuncle**.

---

### Classic findings

Carbuncles are usually:

* painful
* red/swollen
* pus-filled
* draining from multiple openings
* commonly on the back of the neck, back, buttocks, or thighs

They can also cause systemic symptoms like fever or malaise (feeling generally unwell), because the infection is deeper.

---

### Risk factors

Big Step 1 associations:

* diabetes mellitus
* immunosuppression (weakened immune system)
* poor hygiene/crowding
* friction or occlusion of hair-bearing skin
* nasal carriage of **S. aureus** (Staphylococcus aureus living in the nose)

Diabetes matters because high glucose and impaired neutrophil function make bacterial skin infections easier to get.

---

## One-line intuition

A carbuncle is a **deep staph abscess made of multiple connected infected hair follicles**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Carbuncles are classically due to **Staphylococcus aureus**, but if the question mentions recurrent skin abscesses, outbreaks, or resistance, think **MRSA** (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus).

### Core idea

Neonatal acne is a benign newborn rash with small acne-like bumps on the face.

Neonatal = newborn period.

It usually shows up around:

* 2-4 weeks after birth
* cheeks, nose, forehead, chin

It looks like:

* papules = small raised bumps
* pustules = small bumps with pus

Usually no comedones.

Comedones = clogged pores, like blackheads or whiteheads.

---

### Why it happens

Think:

> newborn oil glands are temporarily being stimulated.

Androgens (testosterone-like hormones) from mom and the baby can stimulate sebaceous glands.

Sebaceous glands = oil glands in the skin.

More oil + irritated follicles can cause little bumps/pustules.

So the intuition is:

> baby skin is adjusting after birth, and the oil glands are briefly overactive.

---

### Important distinction

Neonatal acne:

* before about 6 weeks old
* usually mild
* no scarring
* goes away on its own

Infantile acne:

* starts later, usually after 6 weeks to a few months
* can have comedones
* can sometimes be more persistent

That timing matters because later/severe acne can suggest stronger androgen stimulation.

---

### Treatment idea

Usually reassurance + gentle cleansing.

Do not squeeze the lesions.

Avoid oily lotions because they can clog pores more.

---

## One-line intuition

Neonatal acne is temporary newborn "pimples" from hormone-stimulated oil glands, usually harmless and self-resolving.

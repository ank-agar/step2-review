### Core idea

Birth asphyxia means the newborn does not get enough oxygen around the time of birth.

This causes:

* hypoxemia (low oxygen in blood)
* ischemia (low blood flow)
* acidosis (too much acid in blood)

The biggest concern is injury to the brain, called HIE (hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, meaning brain dysfunction from low oxygen and low blood flow).

---

### Why it happens

Common causes include:

* umbilical cord compression
* placental abruption (placenta separates too early)
* uterine rupture
* maternal hypotension (low maternal blood pressure)
* prolonged difficult delivery
* severe fetal distress

Basically:

> baby cannot oxygenate well before/during delivery, so organs switch into survival mode.

---

### Why the brain gets injured

Low oxygen means cells cannot make enough ATP (adenosine triphosphate, the cell's energy molecule).

Low ATP causes:

* Na+/K+ pump failure (sodium-potassium pump failure)
* cell swelling
* calcium influx
* glutamate release
* free radical injury
* neuronal death

Glutamate is an excitatory neurotransmitter (a chemical signal that stimulates neurons).

Too much glutamate overstimulates neurons, so calcium floods in and damages the cell.

This is called excitotoxicity (cell injury from too much excitatory signaling).

---

### Clinical clues

Think birth asphyxia when a newborn has:

* low Apgar score (quick newborn score for appearance, pulse, grimace, activity, respiration)
* poor breathing or no breathing at birth
* low tone
* seizures
* metabolic acidosis (acid buildup from poor oxygen delivery)
* need for resuscitation

Severe cases can cause multiorgan injury:

* brain injury
* kidney injury
* myocardial dysfunction (heart muscle dysfunction)
* liver injury

---

## One-line intuition

Birth asphyxia is a delivery-time oxygen crash: low oxygen + low blood flow → low ATP → brain cells swell, fire abnormally, and die.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Therapeutic hypothermia (controlled cooling) is used for moderate-to-severe neonatal HIE because cooling slows the delayed secondary brain injury cascade after the initial oxygen-loss event.

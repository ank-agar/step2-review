## Persistent complex bereavement disorder

![Image](https://i0.wp.com/griefcompass.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/dual-model-of-grief-schut-stroebe-copy.jpg?resize=604%2C382&ssl=1)

![Image](https://i0.wp.com/griefcompass.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/istock-489112236.jpg?resize=380%2C253&ssl=1)

Persistent complex bereavement disorder, or PCBD (persistent complex bereavement disorder), is a condition where grief after the death of someone close stays intense, disabling, and hard to adapt to for a long time.

Bereavement means grief after death.

Important naming point:

* **PCBD** was the older DSM-5 (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th edition) term
* DSM-5-TR (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th edition, text revision) now uses **prolonged grief disorder**

So for exams, think:

> prolonged, impairing grief that does not gradually integrate into life

---

### Core idea

Normal grief hurts, but over time the person can usually start moving between:

* missing the person
* slowly re-engaging with life

In PCBD/prolonged grief disorder, the brain gets stuck mostly on the loss side.

Classic symptoms:

* intense yearning or longing for the deceased
* preoccupation with the person who died
* difficulty accepting the death
* avoidance of reminders that the person is gone
* emotional numbness
* feeling that life is meaningless without them
* difficulty returning to relationships, work, or goals

---

### Why this happens

Grief normally involves adapting to a new reality:

* the loved one is gone
* the relationship still matters
* life has to reorganize around the loss

In PCBD, the attachment system stays highly activated.

Attachment system means the emotional system that bonds us to close people.

So the person keeps experiencing:

* **yearning:** “I need them back”
* **avoidance:** “I cannot face reminders that they are gone”
* **identity disruption:** “I do not know who I am without them”

That is why the grief becomes disabling instead of slowly becoming integrated.

---

### Timing

For prolonged grief disorder:

* adults: death occurred at least **12 months** ago
* children/adolescents: death occurred at least **6 months** ago

And the symptoms must cause significant distress or functional impairment.

Functional impairment means trouble doing normal life roles, like school, work, relationships, or self-care.

---

### Distinguish from depression and PTSD

Major depressive disorder, or MDD (major depressive disorder), is more generalized low mood/anhedonia.

Anhedonia means loss of interest or pleasure.

PCBD is more specifically centered on the deceased person:

* longing for them
* preoccupation with them
* inability to adapt to life without them

Post-traumatic stress disorder, or PTSD (post-traumatic stress disorder), is more fear-based after trauma:

* flashbacks
* hypervigilance
* exaggerated startle
* avoidance of trauma reminders

PCBD is more attachment/yearning-based.

---

## One-line intuition

PCBD is when grief gets “stuck” around the lost attachment, so the person cannot emotionally accept the death or rebuild life around the loss.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: differentiate **normal bereavement** from **prolonged grief disorder** by duration + impairment: intense grief lasting **>12 months in adults** or **>6 months in children/adolescents** with major functional impairment.

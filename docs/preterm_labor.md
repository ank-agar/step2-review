# Preterm labor

### Core idea

Preterm labor is labor that starts before 37 weeks of gestation (pregnancy age).

It means the uterus is contracting too early AND causing cervical change:

* cervical dilation (opening)
* cervical effacement (thinning/shortening)

Contractions alone are not enough. The cervix has to be changing.

---

### Why it matters

The fetus may be delivered before organs are fully mature, especially the lungs.

That is why preterm birth increases risk of:

* respiratory distress syndrome (breathing trouble from low surfactant)
* intraventricular hemorrhage (bleeding into brain ventricles)
* necrotizing enterocolitis (intestinal ischemia/inflammation in premature infants)
* infection

---

### Why the cervix changes

Normally, the cervix stays thick and closed to keep the pregnancy inside.

In labor:

* uterine contractions push downward
* the cervix thins out
* the cervix opens

So preterm labor is basically:

> the normal labor program turning on too early.

---

### Common risk factors

Think of anything that irritates the uterus or weakens the cervix:

* prior preterm birth
* multiple gestation (twins or more)
* infection
* short cervix
* uterine abnormalities
* smoking or substance use
* PPROM (preterm premature rupture of membranes, meaning the amniotic sac breaks before labor and before 37 weeks)

---

### Treatment idea

Management tries to buy time.

Why?

* corticosteroids help fetal lung maturation
* magnesium sulfate helps fetal neuroprotection before very early delivery
* tocolytics (drugs that reduce uterine contractions) may delay delivery briefly

The goal is not always to stop labor forever. Often it is to delay delivery long enough to help the fetus.

---

## One-line intuition

Preterm labor = the uterus starts contracting before 37 weeks and the cervix starts opening/thinning too early.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Give antenatal corticosteroids to speed up fetal lung maturity because premature babies are at risk for neonatal respiratory distress syndrome from low surfactant.

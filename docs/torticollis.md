### Core idea

Torticollis means “twisted neck.”

It is an abnormal neck posture where the head is:

* tilted to one side
* rotated to the opposite side

Most classic Step 1 version:

* congenital muscular torticollis
* due to shortening/fibrosis of one sternocleidomastoid muscle

Sternocleidomastoid muscle = neck muscle running from the sternum/clavicle to the mastoid process behind the ear.

---

### Why the head position happens

One sternocleidomastoid muscle has 2 main actions:

* tilts the head toward the same side
* rotates the chin toward the opposite side

So if the right sternocleidomastoid is shortened:

* right ear tilts toward right shoulder
* chin points left

That is the classic “wry neck” posture.

---

### Common causes

Torticollis can be:

* congenital = present from birth
* acquired = develops later

Congenital muscular torticollis is usually from unilateral sternocleidomastoid fibrosis/shortening.

Acquired torticollis can happen from:

* muscle spasm
* trauma
* infection/inflammation
* drugs causing dystonia
* eye problems causing compensatory head tilt

Dystonia = sustained involuntary muscle contraction.

---

### Why it matters

If untreated in babies, chronic head tilt can cause:

* plagiocephaly, meaning asymmetric flattening of the skull
* facial asymmetry
* limited neck range of motion

Treatment is usually:

* stretching
* physical therapy
* treating the underlying cause

Surgery is rare and usually only for severe persistent congenital muscular torticollis.

---

## One-line intuition

Torticollis is when one side of the neck pulls too hard/too short, so the head tilts toward that side and the chin rotates away.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Congenital muscular torticollis is associated with developmental dysplasia of the hip, so babies with torticollis should also have their hips checked.

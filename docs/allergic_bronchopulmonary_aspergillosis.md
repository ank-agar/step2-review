Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA) is an allergic lung reaction to Aspergillus, especially Aspergillus fumigatus.

Key idea:

* Aspergillus colonizes the airways
* the patient is usually asthmatic or has cystic fibrosis (CF; inherited disease causing thick mucus)
* the immune system overreacts
* this causes mucus plugging, wheezing, and bronchiectasis (permanent airway dilation)

---

### What is actually happening?

This is not mainly invasive fungus eating through lung tissue.

It is more like:

> fungus sitting in the mucus + immune system freaking out about it.

The immune response is mostly Th2 (T helper 2 cell) driven:

* IL-4 (interleukin-4) increases IgE (immunoglobulin E; allergy antibody)
* IL-5 (interleukin-5) activates eosinophils (allergy/parasite-type white blood cells)
* eosinophils damage airways
* mucus gets thick and plugs bronchi

That repeated inflammation causes central bronchiectasis.

---

### Classic clues

Think ABPA when you see:

* asthma that is hard to control
* recurrent pulmonary infiltrates
* eosinophilia (high eosinophils)
* very high IgE
* brown mucus plugs
* central bronchiectasis on CT (computed tomography)

Aspergillus-specific IgE or skin test is usually positive.

---

### Why bronchiectasis happens

Inflammation damages the bronchial wall.

Damaged bronchi become floppy and widened, so mucus clearance gets worse.

So the cycle is:

> mucus retention -> Aspergillus colonization -> allergic inflammation -> airway damage -> more mucus retention.

---

## One-line intuition

ABPA is asthma/CF lungs overreacting allergically to Aspergillus living in airway mucus, causing eosinophils, high IgE, mucus plugs, and central bronchiectasis.

---

## HY pearl

ABPA is associated with asthma or cystic fibrosis and classically has high IgE, eosinophilia, and central bronchiectasis.

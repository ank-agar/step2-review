# Nystagmus

### Core idea

Nystagmus is involuntary rhythmic eye movement.

The eyes repeatedly move:

* side-to-side = horizontal nystagmus
* up-and-down = vertical nystagmus
* rotating = torsional nystagmus

---

### Why it happens

Your eyes are normally stabilized by the vestibulo-ocular reflex, or VOR (inner ear reflex that keeps your gaze steady when your head moves).

Example:

* head turns right
* eyes automatically move left
* the image stays fixed on the retina (light-sensitive back layer of the eye)

If the vestibular system (inner ear balance system), brainstem, cerebellum (coordination center), or eye-movement pathways are off-balance, the eyes can drift one way and then rapidly correct back.

That repeated drift + correction = nystagmus.

---

### Jerk vs pendular

**Jerk nystagmus:**

* slow drift in one direction
* fast corrective snap back

Named by the fast phase.

So “right-beating nystagmus” means the fast snap is to the right.

**Pendular nystagmus:**

* equal-speed back-and-forth movement
* no obvious fast phase

---

### Why patients feel dizzy or see shaking

The eyes are supposed to keep the visual world still.

If the eyes keep moving involuntarily, the image keeps sliding across the retina.

That can cause:

* blurry vision
* oscillopsia (the sensation that the world is bouncing or moving)
* vertigo (spinning sensation)
* imbalance

---

### Common causes

Can come from:

* congenital causes (present from birth or infancy)
* vestibular neuritis (inflammation of the vestibular nerve)
* benign paroxysmal positional vertigo, or BPPV (brief vertigo from displaced inner ear crystals)
* cerebellar lesions
* brainstem lesions
* multiple sclerosis, or MS (autoimmune demyelinating central nervous system disease)
* Wernicke encephalopathy (thiamine/vitamin B1 deficiency affecting the brain)
* drugs/toxins, especially alcohol, sedatives, and antiseizure drugs

---

## One-line intuition

Nystagmus = the eye-stabilizing system is malfunctioning, so the eyes keep drifting and correcting instead of holding still.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Vertical nystagmus is especially concerning for a central nervous system cause, meaning brainstem/cerebellar pathology rather than a simple peripheral inner ear problem.

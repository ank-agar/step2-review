## Congenital methemoglobinemia

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/7915/4010018/0715731bbbf7/CRIM.CRITICAL.CARE2013-980589.002.jpg)

Congenital methemoglobinemia is an inherited disorder where too much hemoglobin gets stuck as **methemoglobin**.

Methemoglobin = hemoglobin with iron in the **Fe3+ ferric state** instead of the normal **Fe2+ ferrous state**.

Key idea:

* **Fe2+ hemoglobin** can bind oxygen
* **Fe3+ methemoglobin** cannot bind oxygen well

So the blood has oxygen around, but hemoglobin cannot carry/deliver it properly.

---

### Why cyanosis happens

Cyanosis means bluish discoloration from poor oxygen delivery.

In methemoglobinemia:

* hemoglobin iron gets oxidized from **Fe2+ → Fe3+**
* Fe3+ cannot bind oxygen
* remaining normal hemoglobin holds onto oxygen more tightly
* tissues get less oxygen

That causes:

* cyanosis
* low pulse oximetry reading
* chocolate-brown colored blood

But the **PaO2 (partial pressure of oxygen dissolved in arterial blood)** can be normal, because oxygen is dissolved in plasma normally.

The problem is not getting oxygen into the blood.

The problem is hemoglobin cannot carry/release it correctly.

---

### Congenital causes

The 2 big inherited causes are:

* **cytochrome b5 reductase deficiency**
* **hemoglobin M disease**

Cytochrome b5 reductase is the enzyme that normally reduces methemoglobin back to normal hemoglobin:

* Fe3+ → Fe2+

So if that enzyme is deficient, methemoglobin builds up.

Hemoglobin M disease is when the hemoglobin itself is abnormal and more easily stays in the Fe3+ state.

---

### Types

**Type I cytochrome b5 reductase deficiency**

* only affects RBCs (red blood cells)
* causes lifelong cyanosis
* usually mild

**Type II cytochrome b5 reductase deficiency**

* affects all tissues
* much more severe
* causes cyanosis + neurologic problems

Why neuro problems?

Because cytochrome b5 reductase is needed outside RBCs too, especially for normal cellular metabolism in tissues like the brain.

---

## One-line intuition

Congenital methemoglobinemia = hemoglobin iron gets stuck as **Fe3+**, so it cannot carry oxygen well, causing cyanosis despite often normal PaO2.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Classic Step 1 clue: **cyanosis that does not improve much with oxygen + normal PaO2 + chocolate-brown blood** should make you think **methemoglobinemia**.

Also: methylene blue treatment needs **NADPH (reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate)** from **G6PD (glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase)**, so avoid methylene blue in severe G6PD deficiency.

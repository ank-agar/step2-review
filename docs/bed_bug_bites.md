Bed bug bites are itchy skin reactions caused by bed bugs feeding on blood while a person sleeps.

Bed bugs are small, flat insects that hide in:

* mattress seams
* bed frames
* cracks/crevices
* furniture
* luggage

They come out at night to feed.

---

### What the bites look like

Classically:

* small red itchy bumps
* often on exposed skin
* face, neck, arms, hands
* may appear in a line or cluster

The famous pattern is:

> “breakfast, lunch, and dinner”

meaning 3-ish bites in a row.

But important: bite appearance is not perfectly diagnostic. Mosquitoes, fleas, scabies, and allergic rashes can look similar.

---

### Why they itch

When the bed bug feeds, it injects saliva that contains:

* anesthetic = numbs the bite, so you do not feel it right away
* anticoagulant = prevents blood from clotting

Your immune system reacts to proteins in the saliva.

That immune reaction causes:

* redness
* swelling
* itching
* sometimes blisters

So the bump is not the bug “poisoning” you; it is mostly your body reacting to the bug saliva.

---

### Key clue

Bites alone are not enough to prove bed bugs.

Look for infestation evidence:

* live bed bugs
* shed skins
* tiny white eggs
* rusty blood spots on sheets
* dark fecal spots near mattress seams
* sweet/musty odor in heavy infestations

---

### Treatment

Usually supportive:

* avoid scratching
* topical hydrocortisone for itching
* oral antihistamines for itch/sleep
* treat secondary bacterial infection if scratching causes infection

The real treatment is removing the infestation, often with professional pest control.

---

## One-line intuition

Bed bug bites are delayed itchy allergic reactions to saliva injected by a blood-feeding insect while you sleep.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

Bed bugs are not known to transmit human disease; the main issues are pruritus (itching), sleep loss, anxiety, and secondary skin infection from scratching.

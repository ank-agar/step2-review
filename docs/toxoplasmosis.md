### Core idea

Toxoplasmosis is infection by **Toxoplasma gondii**, an intracellular protozoan parasite (single-celled parasite).

Big picture:

* cats are the definitive host (host where sexual reproduction happens)
* humans get infected from cat feces contamination or undercooked meat
* it especially matters in pregnancy and immunocompromised patients

---

### How infection happens

Humans usually get it by:

* eating undercooked meat with tissue cysts
* ingesting oocysts (parasite eggs) from cat feces-contaminated soil/food/water
* transplacental spread (mother to fetus across placenta)

Why cats matter:

* T. gondii sexually reproduces in cats
* cats shed oocysts in feces
* those oocysts become infectious in the environment

So the simple chain is:

> cat feces or undercooked meat → parasite enters human → spreads through blood → forms cysts in brain/eye/muscle

---

### In normal adults

Most immunocompetent people (normal immune system) are asymptomatic.

If symptomatic, think:

* mild fever
* malaise
* cervical lymphadenopathy (swollen neck lymph nodes)

Basically it can look like a mild mono-like illness.

---

### In AIDS/immunocompromised patients

In immunocompromised patients, especially AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome), toxoplasmosis can reactivate in the brain.

Classic finding:

* multiple ring-enhancing brain lesions
* seizures
* focal neurologic deficits

Why?

T. gondii can stay latent as tissue cysts, especially in the brain. When T cells weaken, the parasite reactivates and causes necrotizing brain abscesses.

---

### Congenital toxoplasmosis

Congenital toxoplasmosis means fetal infection acquired during pregnancy.

Classic triad:

* chorioretinitis (inflammation/scarring of retina and choroid in the eye)
* hydrocephalus (too much CSF, cerebrospinal fluid, in the brain ventricles)
* intracranial calcifications (calcium deposits inside the brain)

Intuition:

The parasite likes neural and eye tissue, so the fetus gets brain and retinal damage.

---

### Diagnosis idea

Common testing uses:

* serology (blood antibody testing)
* PCR (polymerase chain reaction, DNA detection), especially for fetal/congenital infection

IgM (immunoglobulin M) suggests more recent infection, but it can persist and be confusing, so pregnancy cases often need confirmatory testing.

---

## One-line intuition

Toxoplasmosis is the “cat feces/undercooked meat parasite” that is usually mild, but causes fetal brain-eye disease and AIDS ring-enhancing brain lesions.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, toxoplasmosis in AIDS classically presents with **multiple ring-enhancing brain lesions**, and prophylaxis is considered when CD4 count (cluster of differentiation 4 T-cell count) is **<100 cells/mm³** with positive Toxoplasma IgG (immunoglobulin G).

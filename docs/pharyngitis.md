### Core idea

Pharyngitis means inflammation of the pharynx (back of the throat).

So basically:

* pharynx = back of throat
* -itis = inflammation

Most cases are viral, but the big Step 1 bacterial cause is group A Streptococcus, also called Streptococcus pyogenes.

---

### What it feels/looks like

Common findings:

* sore throat
* painful swallowing
* red throat
* swollen tonsils
* fever
* tender anterior cervical lymph nodes (painful lymph nodes in the front of the neck)

If it is strep pharyngitis, you may also see:

* tonsillar exudates (white pus-like patches on tonsils)
* palatal petechiae (tiny red spots on the roof of the mouth)
* no cough

---

### Why the findings happen

Inflammation brings immune cells and blood flow into the throat tissue.

That causes:

* redness from increased blood flow
* swelling from leaky inflamed vessels
* pain with swallowing because the irritated pharynx gets stretched/moved
* fever from cytokines (immune signaling molecules)
* swollen lymph nodes because they are filtering infection from the throat

White tonsillar patches happen when dead immune cells + bacteria/debris collect on the tonsils.

---

### Viral vs strep intuition

Viral pharyngitis usually comes with other “cold” symptoms:

* cough
* runny nose
* hoarseness
* conjunctivitis (pink eye)

Strep pharyngitis is more classically:

* sudden sore throat
* fever
* tonsillar exudates
* tender anterior cervical lymph nodes
* no cough

---

## One-line intuition

Pharyngitis is an inflamed back-of-throat; viral causes usually look like a cold, while strep looks more like fever + pus on tonsils + tender neck nodes without cough.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Group A Streptococcus pharyngitis can lead to acute rheumatic fever (immune-mediated heart/joint/brain disease), but post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (kidney inflammation after strep) is not prevented by antibiotics.

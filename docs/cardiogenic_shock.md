### Core idea

Cardiogenic shock = shock caused by the heart failing as a pump.

Shock means poor tissue perfusion (not enough blood/oxygen reaching organs).

So in cardiogenic shock:

* heart cannot pump forward well
* cardiac output (amount of blood pumped by heart per minute) decreases
* blood pressure falls
* organs get underperfused

Most classic cause:

* acute MI (myocardial infarction, meaning heart attack)

---

### Intuition

Think:

> The tank has blood, but the pump is broken.

This is different from hypovolemic shock (shock from low blood volume), where the pump may work but the tank is empty.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Hypotension (low blood pressure):** low cardiac output means less blood is pushed into arteries
* **Cool, clammy skin:** sympathetic nervous system causes vasoconstriction (blood vessel narrowing) to preserve blood flow to brain/heart
* **Low urine output:** kidneys are underperfused
* **Altered mental status:** brain is underperfused
* **Pulmonary edema (fluid in lungs):** left ventricle (main pumping chamber to the body) fails, so blood backs up into pulmonary veins/lungs

---

### Hemodynamics

Classic pattern:

* decreased cardiac output
* increased pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (pressure estimate of left-sided heart filling/back-up)
* increased systemic vascular resistance (arteriolar constriction trying to maintain blood pressure)

Why increased pulmonary capillary wedge pressure?

Because the failing left heart cannot move blood forward, so pressure backs up behind it into the lungs.

---

## One-line intuition

Cardiogenic shock is low organ perfusion because the heart pump fails, causing low forward flow plus backup into the lungs.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Step 1 clue: cardiogenic shock after MI often has **hypotension + cold clammy extremities + pulmonary edema/crackles** because the left ventricle fails and blood backs up into the lungs.

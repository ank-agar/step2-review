### Core idea

Mesenteric ischemia = decreased blood flow to the bowel through the mesenteric vessels.

Mesenteric vessels = blood vessels in the mesentery, which is the tissue that anchors intestines and carries their blood supply.

So the intestine is basically being starved of oxygen.

---

### Why it hurts so much

The classic presentation is:

* severe abdominal pain
* pain out of proportion to exam

Meaning:

* patient looks like they are in extreme pain
* but the abdomen may initially feel soft, without much guarding or rebound

Why?

Early ischemia injures the bowel wall before there is full-thickness necrosis/peritonitis.

Peritonitis = inflammation of the peritoneum, the lining of the abdominal cavity.

So early on:

* bowel is dying and painful
* but the peritoneum is not irritated yet
* so the physical exam can look deceptively mild

---

### Main causes

Think: anything that blocks or severely lowers bowel blood flow.

* embolus to the SMA (superior mesenteric artery)
* thrombosis (clot forming in place), usually on atherosclerosis
* low-flow state/shock causing nonocclusive ischemia
* SMV (superior mesenteric vein) thrombosis causing venous congestion

The big Step 1 association:

* AFib (atrial fibrillation) → clot forms in left atrium → embolizes to SMA → acute mesenteric ischemia

---

### Why bowel can die fast

The mucosa (inner lining) of the bowel has high metabolic demand.

If blood flow drops:

* low oxygen → ATP (adenosine triphosphate) depletion
* cells swell and die
* mucosal barrier breaks down
* bacteria/toxins can enter blood
* full-thickness bowel necrosis can happen

Necrosis = tissue death.

---

### Imaging clues

CTA (computed tomography angiography) is the key test clinically.

Scary findings:

* pneumatosis intestinalis = gas inside the bowel wall
* portal venous gas = gas in the portal venous system going toward the liver
* absent/decreased bowel wall enhancement

These suggest severe ischemia and possible bowel infarction.

Infarction = tissue death from loss of blood supply.

---

## One-line intuition

Mesenteric ischemia is a “heart attack of the bowel”: blocked or low blood flow causes sudden severe abdominal pain and can rapidly progress to bowel necrosis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Acute mesenteric ischemia classically causes pain out of proportion to physical exam, especially in an older patient with AFib (atrial fibrillation).

### Core idea

Usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) is a **pattern of chronic lung scarring** where the interstitium (support tissue between alveoli, or air sacs) becomes fibrotic.

Most classic association:

* idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) = progressive lung fibrosis with no known cause

So:

> UIP is the biopsy/CT pattern; IPF is the clinical disease when no cause is found.

---

### What it looks like

Classic UIP has:

* patchy fibrosis
* subpleural involvement = near the pleura (outer lung lining)
* basilar predominance = worse at lung bases
* honeycombing = cystic air spaces from destroyed, scarred lung
* fibroblast foci = active little areas of collagen-making fibroblasts

Break the name apart:

* usual = the classic/common chronic fibrosing pattern
* interstitial = in the lung support tissue between alveoli
* pneumonia = inflammation/injury pattern in lung tissue, not necessarily bacterial infection

---

### Why symptoms happen

Fibrosis makes the lung:

* stiff
* hard to expand
* poor at gas exchange

That causes:

* progressive dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* dry cough
* restrictive lung disease pattern
* decreased DLCO (diffusing capacity of lung for carbon monoxide), meaning poor gas diffusion

Why restrictive?

The scarred lung cannot stretch well, so total lung capacity decreases.

---

### One-line intuition

UIP is a patchy, lower-lobe, subpleural scarring pattern that turns soft alveoli into stiff honeycomb-like cystic spaces.

---

### Pearl

HY Step 1 association: **UIP/IPF causes restrictive lung disease with decreased lung compliance and decreased DLCO**, and CT classically shows **subpleural, basilar honeycombing**.

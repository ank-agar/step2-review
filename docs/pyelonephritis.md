# Pyelonephritis

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17823/N00858-H_thumbnail.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459158/bin/kidney.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK613065/bin/ch8renal-Image029.gif)

Pyelonephritis = infection of the kidney.

Break the word apart:

* pyelo- = renal pelvis (the urine-collecting funnel of the kidney)
* nephritis = kidney inflammation

So pyelonephritis means:

> infected, inflamed kidney + renal pelvis.

Most cases happen from an ascending UTI (urinary tract infection):

* bacteria enter urethra
* go to bladder = cystitis (bladder infection)
* travel up ureter
* reach kidney = pyelonephritis

Most common bug:

* E. coli (Escherichia coli)

Why the symptoms happen:

* fever/chills: bacteria reach kidney tissue, so it becomes a systemic infection
* flank pain: kidney sits in the back/side, so inflamed kidney hurts there
* dysuria (painful urination), frequency, urgency: often starts as lower UTI first
* pyuria (white blood cells in urine): neutrophils fight the bacterial infection
* WBC casts (white blood cell casts): inflammation is inside kidney tubules, not just bladder

Key Step 1 distinction:

* cystitis = lower UTI, usually no fever or flank pain
* pyelonephritis = upper UTI, classically fever + flank pain + WBC casts

Risk factors:

* female sex
* pregnancy
* vesicoureteral reflux (urine flows backward from bladder toward kidney)
* urinary obstruction, like kidney stones
* diabetes mellitus

One-line intuition:

It is basically a bladder infection that climbed upstairs into the kidney.

HY pearl 🔥

Pyelonephritis classically has WBC casts because the infection involves renal tubules; simple cystitis should not make casts because it is limited to the bladder.

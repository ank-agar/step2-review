# Anemia of pregnancy

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK568790/bin/irondeficiency.jpg)

### Core idea

Anemia of pregnancy = low hemoglobin (oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells) during pregnancy.

But there are 2 big ideas:

* **physiologic anemia:** normal dilution from pregnancy
* **true anemia:** usually iron deficiency

---

### Why pregnancy causes “physiologic anemia”

Pregnancy increases both:

* plasma volume = liquid part of blood
* red blood cell mass = actual oxygen-carrying cells

But plasma increases **more** than red blood cells.

So the blood gets diluted:

> more fluid + not enough matching red cells = lower hemoglobin concentration

This is called **hemodilution** (blood dilution).

---

### Why true anemia happens

Most common cause is:

* **iron deficiency**

Why?

Pregnancy needs more iron because mom is making:

* more red blood cells
* fetal blood
* placenta

Iron is needed to make hemoglobin, so low iron causes:

* low hemoglobin
* small red blood cells
* pale red blood cells

Small = **microcytic** (small cell size)  
Pale = **hypochromic** (less color from less hemoglobin)

---

### Key labs

Iron deficiency anemia usually shows:

* low hemoglobin
* low MCV (mean corpuscular volume; average red blood cell size)
* low ferritin (stored iron)
* high TIBC (total iron-binding capacity; transferrin availability)

MCV is low because without enough hemoglobin, the red blood cell divides extra times and ends up smaller.

---

## One-line intuition

Pregnancy makes the blood more watery, and if iron supply cannot keep up, the mom cannot make enough hemoglobin-rich red blood cells.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Physiologic anemia of pregnancy is mainly from plasma volume increasing more than red blood cell mass, so **hematocrit (percent of blood made of red blood cells) falls even though total red blood cell mass actually increases**.

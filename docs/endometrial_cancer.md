Endometrial cancer is cancer of the **endometrium** (inner lining of the uterus).

Most commonly, it is an **adenocarcinoma** (cancer from gland-forming epithelial cells).

---

### Core idea

Think:

> too much unopposed estrogen stimulation makes the endometrium keep proliferating, which increases mutation risk.

So the classic setup is:

* postmenopausal bleeding
* obesity
* chronic anovulation (no ovulation)
* polycystic ovary syndrome, or PCOS (hyperandrogenism and chronic anovulation)
* estrogen therapy without progesterone
* Lynch syndrome (inherited DNA mismatch repair defect causing colon/endometrial cancer risk)

---

### Why estrogen matters

Estrogen tells the endometrium:

* grow
* thicken
* proliferate

Progesterone normally balances this by making the lining more stable/secretory.

So if estrogen is **unopposed** (not balanced by progesterone), the endometrium keeps dividing.

More cell division = more chances for DNA mutations = cancer risk.

---

### Classic presentation

Most important clue:

* **postmenopausal bleeding**

This is cancer until proven otherwise because after menopause the endometrium should be thin and quiet.

Also can present with:

* abnormal uterine bleeding
* watery/bloody discharge
* enlarged uterus in some cases

---

### Histology clue

Endometrioid adenocarcinoma often forms abnormal crowded glands.

Break it down:

* endometrioid = resembling endometrial tissue
* adeno- = gland
* carcinoma = malignant epithelial tumor

So:

> malignant gland-forming tumor from the uterine lining.

---

## One-line intuition

Endometrial cancer is usually the “too much estrogen, too much endometrial growth” cancer that presents with postmenopausal bleeding.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Endometrial cancer is associated with **Lynch syndrome** (DNA mismatch repair defect), especially mutations affecting **MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, or PMS2** (mismatch repair genes).

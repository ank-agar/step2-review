## Anuria

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/30/Urinary_system.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/98/Kidney_Nephron.png)

### Core idea

Anuria means almost no urine output.

Classically:

* **anuria:** less than 100 mL urine/day
* **oliguria:** low urine output, but not as severe

So think:

> oliguria = small amount of urine  
> anuria = basically no urine

---

### Why anuria happens

Urine can disappear for 3 big reasons:

* **prerenal:** not enough blood reaches the kidneys
* **intrinsic renal:** the kidney tissue itself is damaged
* **postrenal:** urine is made but blocked from leaving

Break those down:

* **pre-renal** = before the kidney, like shock or severe dehydration
* **intrinsic renal** = inside the kidney, like acute tubular necrosis
* **post-renal** = after the kidney, like bilateral ureter obstruction or bladder outlet obstruction

---

### Reasoning

The nephron is the kidney's filtering unit.

To make urine, it needs:

* blood flow into the glomerulus
* working tubules
* an open path out through ureter, bladder, and urethra

So anuria can happen if:

* blood never gets there
* the nephron cannot filter
* urine gets trapped behind a blockage

---

### What you worry about

Anuria is a red flag for severe AKI (acute kidney injury) or obstruction.

Labs may show:

* increased creatinine
* increased BUN (blood urea nitrogen)
* hyperkalemia (high potassium)
* metabolic acidosis (too much acid in the blood)

The dangerous one to remember fast is **hyperkalemia**, because it can cause arrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms).

---

## One-line intuition

Anuria means the kidney system is producing or passing almost no urine, either because flow in is bad, the kidney is damaged, or flow out is blocked.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Postrenal anuria usually requires obstruction of both kidneys/ureters or obstruction below the bladder, because one working kidney can still make urine.

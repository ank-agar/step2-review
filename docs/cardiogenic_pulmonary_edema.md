### Core idea

Cardiogenic pulmonary edema = fluid in the lungs caused by a heart problem.

Break it down:

* cardio = heart
* genic = caused by
* pulmonary = lungs
* edema = excess fluid in tissue

So the whole phrase means:

> lung fluid buildup because the heart is failing to move blood forward well.

---

### Why it happens

Usually the left ventricle (main pumping chamber of the heart) is weak or overloaded.

If the left ventricle cannot pump blood forward:

* blood backs up into the left atrium (upper left heart chamber)
* pressure backs up into the pulmonary veins (veins carrying blood from lungs to heart)
* pressure rises in lung capillaries (tiny blood vessels)
* fluid gets pushed out into the interstitium (space around cells) and alveoli (air sacs)

So the lungs become wet, and wet alveoli cannot exchange oxygen well.

---

### What you see clinically

Classic symptoms:

* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* orthopnea (shortness of breath when lying flat)
* paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, or PND (sudden nighttime shortness of breath)
* crackles on lung exam
* frothy sputum in severe cases

The key idea:

* air should fill alveoli
* fluid fills alveoli instead
* oxygen diffusion drops

---

### CXR findings

CXR (chest X-ray) can show:

* cardiomegaly (enlarged heart)
* Kerley B lines (short peripheral lines from interstitial fluid)
* cephalization (blood vessels in upper lungs become more prominent)
* pleural effusions (fluid around the lungs)
* bat-wing opacities (central fluffy white edema around the hila)

Hila means the central lung area where vessels and bronchi enter/exit.

---

## One-line intuition

The left heart backs up blood pressure into the lungs, so fluid gets squeezed into the air spaces.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Cardiogenic pulmonary edema is due to increased hydrostatic pressure (fluid pushed out by pressure), while ARDS (acute respiratory distress syndrome) is due to increased permeability (leaky capillaries).

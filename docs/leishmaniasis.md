## Leishmaniasis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Leishmaniasis_life_cycle_diagram_en.svg/960px-Leishmaniasis_life_cycle_diagram_en.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/dpdx/leishmaniasis/images/2/Leish_amast_WBC1_DPDx.jpg?_=00709)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/leishmaniasis/media/images/cutaneous-leishmaniasis-ulcer.jpg)

Leishmaniasis is an infection caused by **Leishmania** protozoa (single-celled parasites).

It is transmitted by the bite of an infected **sand fly**.

---

### Core idea

Think:

* sand fly bite
* parasite enters macrophages
* chronic skin ulcer or visceral disease

Macrophages are immune cells that normally eat pathogens, but Leishmania survives inside them.

So the cell that is supposed to kill the parasite becomes the parasite's home.

---

### Forms

The big forms are:

* **Cutaneous leishmaniasis** = skin disease
* **Mucocutaneous leishmaniasis** = mucous membrane disease, especially nose/mouth
* **Visceral leishmaniasis** = internal organ disease, also called kala-azar

Visceral means involving internal organs.

---

### Why the findings happen

After the sand fly injects the parasite:

* the parasite is taken up by macrophages
* it changes into the **amastigote** form inside macrophages
* it multiplies inside macrophages
* infected macrophages spread locally or through the reticuloendothelial system

The reticuloendothelial system means macrophage-rich organs like:

* spleen
* liver
* bone marrow
* lymph nodes

That is why visceral leishmaniasis causes:

* fever
* weight loss
* hepatosplenomegaly (enlarged liver and spleen)
* pancytopenia (low red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets)

The bone marrow gets involved, so blood cell production drops.

---

### What you see on microscopy

In humans, Leishmania is seen as **amastigotes** inside macrophages.

Amastigotes have:

* nucleus
* kinetoplast

Kinetoplast means a small DNA-containing structure inside the parasite's mitochondrion.

Step 1 wording often describes these as:

> macrophages filled with amastigotes.

---

## One-line intuition

Leishmaniasis is a sand-fly parasite that hides inside macrophages, causing either skin ulcers or systemic liver/spleen/bone marrow disease.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Leishmania is associated with **Th1 immune response** protection.

Th1 cells activate macrophages using **IFN-gamma** (interferon-gamma), which helps macrophages kill intracellular organisms.

So weak cellular immunity increases risk for severe disease.

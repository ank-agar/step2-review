## Disc degeneration

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/Degenerated_disk.svg/960px-Degenerated_disk.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK535373/bin/716_Intervertebral_Disk.jpg)

Disc degeneration means the intervertebral disc (the cushion between two vertebrae, which are spine bones) starts wearing down.

The disc normally has:

* nucleus pulposus = soft, gel-like center
* annulus fibrosus = tough outer ring

With degeneration, the disc loses water and proteoglycans (water-holding cartilage molecules), so it becomes:

* drier
* flatter
* less flexible
* more likely to bulge or tear

---

### Core idea

Think of a young disc like a juicy cushion.

As it degenerates, it becomes more like a dried-out cushion:

* less height
* less shock absorption
* more stress on nearby joints and nerves

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Disc desiccation:** desiccation means drying out. The nucleus pulposus loses water, so it looks darker on MRI (magnetic resonance imaging), especially on T2-weighted images (MRI images where water usually looks bright).
* **Disc height loss:** the disc collapses because it cannot hold water/pressure well anymore.
* **Disc bulge/herniation:** the weakened annulus fibrosus can stretch or tear, letting disc material push outward.
* **Back pain:** the degenerating disc and surrounding joints can become inflamed or mechanically stressed.
* **Radiculopathy:** radiculopathy means nerve root symptoms, like shooting pain, numbness, or weakness. This can happen if a bulging disc compresses a spinal nerve root.

---

### Important clarification

Disc degeneration is common with aging and does **not always mean symptoms**.

Someone can have disc degeneration on MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) and feel totally fine.

Symptoms matter most when degeneration causes:

* nerve compression
* inflammation
* spinal canal narrowing
* foraminal narrowing, meaning narrowing of the hole where the nerve exits

---

## One-line intuition

Disc degeneration = the spine's cushion dries out and flattens, so it absorbs shock poorly and can irritate nearby nerves.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On MRI (magnetic resonance imaging), a degenerated disc often appears as a **dark disc on T2-weighted imaging** because T2 brightness usually reflects water, and degeneration causes water loss.

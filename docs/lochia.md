# Lochia

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615369/bin/ch11f2.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615369/bin/ch11f3.jpg)

Lochia is the normal vaginal discharge after delivery.

It is made of:

* blood
* mucus
* uterine lining
* tissue/fluid from the placental site

Placental site = the area in the uterus where the placenta was attached.

---

### Core idea

After the placenta detaches, the uterus has to:

* shrink back down
* seal bleeding vessels
* clear out leftover pregnancy tissue

That "cleaning out" discharge is lochia.

---

### Stages

Think:

**red -> watery/pink-brown -> white/yellow**

* **Lochia rubra:** red discharge, first few days  
  Rubra = red. Mostly fresh blood from the placental site.

* **Lochia serosa:** pink/brown watery discharge, around days 4-10  
  Serosa = serum-like fluid. Less fresh blood, more fluid, mucus, and white blood cells (immune cells).

* **Lochia alba:** yellow-white discharge, after about day 10  
  Alba = white. Mostly mucus, white blood cells, and epithelial cells (surface lining cells).

---

### Why the color changes

Early on, the placental site is still fresh, so there is more blood.

As the uterus contracts and the placental site heals, there is less red blood and more healing debris/mucus.

So the discharge naturally fades from:

* red
* to pink/brown
* to yellow-white

---

### When it is concerning

Lochia should gradually get lighter.

Red bleeding that stays heavy, comes back suddenly, smells foul, or happens with fever can suggest:

* retained products of conception (placental/fetal tissue left inside)
* endometritis (infection/inflammation of the uterine lining)
* subinvolution (uterus not shrinking back normally)

---

## One-line intuition

Lochia is the uterus "shedding and cleaning out" after delivery as the placental attachment site heals.

---

## HY pearl

Persistent **lochia rubra** beyond about 1 week is concerning for **subinvolution** or retained placental tissue.

### Core idea

A cough is a protective reflex that forcefully pushes air out of the lungs to clear the airway.

It helps remove:

* mucus
* irritants
* microbes
* aspirated material, meaning stuff that accidentally enters the airway

---

### How it happens

Think:

> irritated airway → vagus nerve → medulla → respiratory muscles → cough

Step-by-step:

* Irritant stimulates cough receptors in the larynx, trachea, or bronchi
* Signal travels up the vagus nerve, which is cranial nerve X
* The medulla, which is part of the brainstem, coordinates the reflex
* Motor signals go to respiratory muscles
* You take a deep breath, close the glottis, then forcefully open it
* Air shoots out and clears the airway

Glottis means the opening between the vocal cords.

---

### Why cough can be wet or dry

* **Wet/productive cough:** mucus or fluid is in the airway
* **Dry cough:** irritation without much mucus

So a wet cough sounds “gunky” because the body is trying to move secretions out.

A dry cough is more like the airway alarm system firing from irritation.

---

### Common causes

* Viral upper respiratory infection, meaning common cold-type infection
* Asthma, meaning reversible airway narrowing
* Gastroesophageal reflux disease, meaning acid reflux irritating the airway
* Pneumonia, meaning infection of lung tissue
* Postnasal drip, meaning mucus dripping from the nose/sinuses into the throat
* ACE inhibitor use, meaning angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor blood pressure meds

---

## One-line intuition

Cough is the airway’s “eject button” when something is irritating or blocking the breathing tubes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

ACE inhibitors, meaning angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, can cause dry cough because they increase bradykinin, which irritates airway sensory nerves.

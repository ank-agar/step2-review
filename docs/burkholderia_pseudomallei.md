### Core idea

**Burkholderia pseudomallei** is a **Gram-negative rod** that causes **melioidosis** (a severe infection classically from soil/water exposure).

Think:

* tropical soil/water
* Southeast Asia + northern Australia
* pneumonia + abscesses + sepsis

---

### What it looks like

It is a:

* **Gram-negative rod** (pink rod-shaped bacterium on Gram stain)
* **oxidase-positive** (has cytochrome c oxidase enzyme)
* **motile** (can move)
* often has **bipolar staining** (darker staining at both ends), giving a "safety-pin" look

---

### How infection happens

People get exposed through contaminated:

* soil
* standing water
* rice paddies

Entry can happen by:

* skin inoculation (bacteria entering through cuts)
* inhalation (breathing it in)
* ingestion (swallowing contaminated material)

---

### Why it causes so many findings

This bug can survive inside cells and spread through blood, so it can hit multiple organs.

That explains:

* **pneumonia:** inhaled organism infects lung tissue
* **skin abscesses:** direct entry through skin creates pus collections
* **liver/spleen/prostate abscesses:** bloodstream spread seeds organs
* **sepsis:** widespread infection triggers systemic inflammation and shock
* **latent infection/reactivation:** it can persist quietly, then come back later

---

### Big risk factor

The biggest classic risk factor is:

* **diabetes mellitus** (chronically high blood glucose causing immune dysfunction)

Other risks:

* chronic kidney disease
* heavy alcohol use
* chronic lung disease

---

## One-line intuition

**Burkholderia pseudomallei is a tropical soil-water Gram-negative rod that causes melioidosis: pneumonia, abscesses, and sepsis, especially in diabetics.**

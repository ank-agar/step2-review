Phocomelia is a congenital limb malformation where the proximal limb parts are absent or severely underdeveloped.

So the hand or foot may look like it is attached very close to the trunk.

Break the word apart:

* phoco- = seal
* -melia = limb

So phocomelia literally means “seal-like limbs.”

---

### Core idea

Normally, limbs grow outward in segments:

* upper limb: shoulder → arm → forearm → hand
* lower limb: hip → thigh → leg → foot

In phocomelia, the middle/proximal limb segments fail to develop properly, but the distal part, like the hand or foot, may still be present.

That is why it can look like:

* hands close to shoulders
* feet close to hips
* very short or absent long bones

---

### Why it happens

During embryology, the limb bud has to grow outward in an organized proximal-to-distal pattern.

The AER (apical ectodermal ridge) helps drive this outward limb growth using FGF (fibroblast growth factor) signals.

If limb development is disrupted early, especially vascular growth and limb-bud signaling, the long bones may not form correctly.

Classic cause:

* thalidomide exposure during pregnancy

Thalidomide is teratogenic (causes birth defects), especially for limb development.

---

### Important comparison

* Amelia = complete absence of a limb
* Meromelia = partial absence of a limb
* Phocomelia = proximal limb parts are absent/short, with distal hands/feet closer to the body

---

## One-line intuition

Phocomelia is when the limb “skips” much of the arm/leg segment, so the hand or foot ends up attached close to the body.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Thalidomide is high-yield because it is anti-angiogenic (blocks new blood vessel formation), and disrupted embryonic blood vessel growth can cause severe limb defects like phocomelia.

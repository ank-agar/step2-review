## Bladder rupture

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK470226/bin/Gray402.jpg)

![Image](https://learningradiology.com/caseofweek/caseoftheweekpix/cow101.jpg)

![Image](https://learningradiology.com/caseofweek/caseoftheweekpix2006/cow187arr.jpg)

Bladder rupture = a tear in the urinary bladder wall, usually after pelvic/lower abdominal trauma.

Big idea:

* urine leaks out of the bladder
* patient often has gross hematuria (visible blood in urine)
* suprapubic pain (pain above the pubic bone)
* difficulty urinating or low urine output

---

### Two main types

#### Extraperitoneal rupture

Urine leaks around the bladder, but not into the peritoneal cavity (space containing abdominal organs).

Usually from:

* pelvic fracture
* bone fragments tearing the bladder wall

Why?

The bladder sits deep in the pelvis, so pelvic trauma can rip the lower/anterior bladder wall.

Usually treated with:

* Foley catheter drainage

because keeping the bladder empty lets the tear heal.

---

#### Intraperitoneal rupture

Urine leaks into the peritoneal cavity.

Classic mechanism:

* direct blow to a full bladder
* tear at the bladder dome

Dome = top of the bladder.

Why the dome?

When the bladder is full, the dome stretches upward and becomes the weakest exposed part. A sudden hit increases pressure inside the bladder, so it bursts at the top.

Usually treated with:

* surgical repair

because urine in the abdomen can cause peritonitis (inflammation of the peritoneal lining), infection, electrolyte problems, and renal failure (kidney failure).

---

### How to remember

* Pelvic fracture = extraperitoneal rupture
* Full bladder + blow to abdomen = intraperitoneal dome rupture

---

### Diagnosis

Best test:

* retrograde cystography (contrast is pushed backward through a catheter into the bladder)
* often CT cystography (computed tomography cystography)

Reason:

You need to fill the bladder with contrast to see where urine/contrast leaks out.

---

## One-line intuition

Bladder rupture is basically a popped urine balloon: pelvic fractures usually leak urine around the bladder, while a full bladder hit at the dome leaks urine into the abdomen.

---

## Pearl 🔥

If there is blood at the urethral meatus (opening of the urethra), do a retrograde urethrogram (RUG; imaging test for urethral injury) before placing a Foley catheter, because forcing a catheter through a torn urethra can worsen the injury.

### Core idea

An arteriovenous malformation, or AVM (arteriovenous malformation), is an abnormal direct connection between:

* arteries
* veins

without a normal capillary bed in between.

Normally:

* artery → capillaries → vein

In AVM:

* artery → tangled abnormal vessels → vein

That tangled abnormal vessel area is called the nidus (central knot of abnormal vessels).

---

### Why this is dangerous

Arteries are high-pressure vessels.

Veins are low-pressure vessels.

Capillaries normally act like a pressure buffer between them.

So if you skip the capillaries, arterial pressure gets dumped straight into veins.

That causes:

* dilated fragile vessels
* risk of rupture
* bleeding into the brain if the AVM is cerebral (in the brain)

---

### Classic symptoms

Brain AVMs can present with:

* seizures
* headache
* intracranial hemorrhage (bleeding inside the skull)
* focal neurologic deficits (weakness, numbness, vision changes, etc.)

The big fear is hemorrhage because those abnormal vessels are under high flow and high stress.

---

### Intuition

Think:

> AVM = high-pressure artery shortcuts directly into a fragile vein.

The capillary bed is supposed to slow blood down and lower pressure.

Without it, the vein gets blasted with pressure it was not built to handle.

---

### Important association

AVMs are usually congenital (present from birth), but they may not cause symptoms until later.

They are not the same as an aneurysm (balloon-like dilation of a vessel), but both can cause intracranial bleeding.

---

## One-line intuition

An AVM is a tangled artery-to-vein shortcut that bypasses capillaries, making fragile vessels prone to bleeding.

---

## Pearl 🔥

AVM causes a high-flow vascular lesion because blood bypasses the high-resistance capillary bed and flows directly from artery to vein.

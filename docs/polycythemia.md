# Polycythemia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/1901_Composition_of_Blood.jpg/250px-1901_Composition_of_Blood.jpg)

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17879/N00810-H_thumbnail.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Polycythemia_vera%2C_blood_smear.jpg/960px-Polycythemia_vera%2C_blood_smear.jpg)

Polycythemia means too many RBCs (red blood cells) in the blood.

This usually shows up as increased:

* hemoglobin
* hematocrit
* RBC count

Hematocrit means the percentage of blood volume made up by RBCs (red blood cells).

---

### Core idea

Blood becomes more packed with RBCs (red blood cells).

More RBCs can carry more oxygen, but the downside is:

* thicker blood
* slower flow
* increased clot risk

So the big danger is thrombosis (clot formation).

---

### Types

**Relative polycythemia**

The RBC number is not truly increased.

Instead, plasma volume is low, so the blood looks more concentrated.

Classic example:

* dehydration

Think:

> same RBCs, less water.

---

**Absolute polycythemia**

There is a true increase in RBC mass.

This can be:

* primary
* secondary

---

### Primary polycythemia

Primary means the problem starts in the bone marrow.

Classic example:

* polycythemia vera

Polycythemia vera is a myeloproliferative neoplasm (bone marrow cancer where myeloid blood cell lines overgrow).

Most cases are due to a JAK2 mutation.

JAK2 (Janus kinase 2) is part of a growth-signaling pathway.

If JAK2 is always active, the marrow acts like it is constantly being told:

> make more blood cells.

So polycythemia vera can increase:

* RBCs (red blood cells)
* WBCs (white blood cells)
* platelets

EPO (erythropoietin, the kidney hormone that stimulates RBC production) is usually low because the kidney senses there are already too many RBCs.

---

### Secondary polycythemia

Secondary means something outside the marrow is telling the marrow to make more RBCs.

Usually this is increased EPO (erythropoietin).

Common causes:

* chronic hypoxia (low oxygen)
* COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease)
* high altitude
* obstructive sleep apnea
* smoking
* renal cell carcinoma producing EPO

Reasoning:

If tissues are not getting enough oxygen, the kidney releases EPO (erythropoietin), which tells bone marrow to make more RBCs.

So secondary polycythemia is often the body trying to compensate for low oxygen.

---

### Symptoms

Because the blood is thick and flow is sluggish, patients can get:

* headache
* dizziness
* blurry vision
* hypertension (high blood pressure)
* thrombosis (clots)
* splenomegaly (enlarged spleen)

Polycythemia vera can also cause:

* pruritus after hot showers
* erythromelalgia

Erythromelalgia means burning red pain in the hands or feet due to abnormal small-vessel blood flow.

---

## One-line intuition

Polycythemia is blood with too many RBCs, making it thick and clot-prone.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Polycythemia vera = low EPO (erythropoietin) because the marrow is autonomously overproducing cells.

Secondary polycythemia = high EPO because the kidney is responding to hypoxia (low oxygen) or inappropriate EPO production.

# Decubitus ulcer

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/01/Wound_stage.jpg)

![Image](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/mmanual-ssq1ci05/media/professional/images/s/t/a/stage_i_pressure_ulcer_buttocks_high.jpg?mw=2048&sc_lang=en)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/68/Decubitus_ulcer_stage_4.jpg)

A **decubitus ulcer** is a **pressure ulcer** AKA (also known as) a bedsore.

It is skin/tissue damage from prolonged pressure, usually over a bony area like:

* sacrum
* heel
* hip
* elbow

Decubitus = lying down.

So the name basically means:

> ulcer from lying/sitting in one position too long.

---

### Core idea

Pressure squeezes tiny blood vessels shut.

Less blood flow = **ischemia** (low oxygen delivery from low blood flow).

Ischemia causes tissue death, so the skin breaks down into an ulcer.

---

### Why it happens

Usually in patients who cannot move well:

* elderly patients
* stroke patients
* spinal cord injury patients
* bedbound patients
* malnourished patients

The big problem is:

* pressure + shear forces + moisture

Shear force = sliding force, like when a patient slowly slides down in bed.

---

### Staging intuition

* **Stage 1:** red, non-blanching skin; skin still intact  
* **Stage 2:** shallow skin loss/blister  
* **Stage 3:** deeper ulcer into subcutaneous tissue (fat layer under skin)  
* **Stage 4:** exposes muscle, tendon, or bone

Non-blanching = it does not turn white when pressed, meaning blood flow is already impaired.

---

## One-line intuition

A decubitus ulcer is tissue necrosis (cell death) from constant pressure cutting off blood supply over a bony prominence.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY (high-yield) complication: **osteomyelitis** (bone infection), especially with deep sacral ulcers that extend down to bone.

## Hemolytic uremic syndrome

![Image](https://unckidneycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/413/2018/04/fig4.png)

![Image](https://unckidneycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/413/2017/10/tma-figure-1-and-2.png)

![Image](https://imagebank.hematology.org/getimagebyid/65049?size=3)

Hemolytic uremic syndrome, or **HUS (hemolytic uremic syndrome)**, is classically:

* hemolytic anemia = red blood cells get destroyed
* thrombocytopenia = low platelets
* acute kidney injury = sudden kidney damage

Classic trigger:

* bloody diarrhea after **EHEC (enterohemorrhagic Escherichia coli)**
* especially **O157:H7**
* from undercooked beef, unpasteurized foods, or contaminated produce

---

### Core mechanism

EHEC releases **Shiga-like toxin**.

That toxin damages endothelial cells (cells lining blood vessels), especially in kidney glomeruli (kidney filtering capillaries).

Damaged endothelium causes:

* platelet activation
* small clots in tiny vessels
* red blood cells getting shredded as they squeeze through

Those shredded red blood cells are called **schistocytes (fragmented red blood cells)**.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Hemolytic anemia:** red blood cells are mechanically ripped apart by microthrombi (tiny clots)
* **Thrombocytopenia:** platelets are used up making those tiny clots
* **Kidney injury:** glomerular capillaries get clogged/damaged, so filtration drops
* **Bloody diarrhea first:** EHEC damages the colon before the toxin reaches the bloodstream

So the sequence is:

> bloody diarrhea -> Shiga toxin -> endothelial damage -> platelet microthrombi -> RBC shredding + kidney injury

---

### One-line intuition

HUS is basically **post-diarrhea kidney failure from toxin-damaged small vessels that shred red blood cells and consume platelets**.

---

### Important Step 1 association

Do **not** give antibiotics or antimotility drugs for suspected EHEC diarrhea.

Why?

They can increase toxin release/exposure and raise the risk of HUS.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HUS is a **thrombotic microangiopathy (small-vessel clotting disease)** with kidney-predominant findings; **TTP (thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura)** is more classically neuro-predominant and is due to low **ADAMTS13 (a von Willebrand factor-cleaving enzyme)**.

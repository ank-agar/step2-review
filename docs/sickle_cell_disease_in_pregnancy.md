### Core idea

Sickle cell disease in pregnancy = a pregnant patient has sickle cell disease, meaning an inherited hemoglobin disorder where RBCs (red blood cells) can become rigid, sickle-shaped, and fragile.

Pregnancy makes this higher risk because mom and fetus both depend on good oxygen delivery.

Main problems:

* **vaso-occlusion** = sickled RBCs block small blood vessels
* **hemolytic anemia** = RBCs break apart too early
* **placental insufficiency** = placenta cannot deliver enough oxygen/nutrients to fetus

---

### Why pregnancy worsens it

Pregnancy naturally causes:

* increased oxygen demand
* increased plasma volume, which can worsen anemia symptoms by dilution
* hypercoagulability, meaning blood is more likely to clot
* higher risk of dehydration/infection, which can trigger sickling

Sickling is triggered by things like:

* low oxygen
* dehydration
* acidosis, meaning too much acid in the blood
* infection
* cold/stress

So pregnancy adds extra stress to a system that already struggles with oxygen delivery.

---

### Why the symptoms/complications happen

* **Pain crises:** sickled RBCs block small vessels, causing ischemia (low blood flow/oxygen)
* **Worsening anemia:** sickled RBCs are fragile and get destroyed early
* **Acute chest syndrome:** sickling/inflammation in the lungs causes chest pain, fever, hypoxia (low oxygen), and lung infiltrates
* **Preeclampsia risk:** abnormal blood vessel function and placental injury increase hypertension (high blood pressure) risk
* **VTE risk:** VTE (venous thromboembolism) means blood clots in veins; pregnancy is already hypercoagulable, and sickle disease adds more risk
* **Infections:** functional asplenia (poor spleen function) makes encapsulated bacteria harder to clear

---

### Fetal risks

The fetus can have:

* growth restriction
* preterm birth
* low birth weight
* fetal distress
* increased risk of fetal loss

Why?

Because sickled RBCs can impair placental blood flow.

Placenta = the organ that transfers oxygen/nutrients from mom to fetus.

If maternal blood flow through the placenta is reduced:

* less oxygen reaches fetus
* fetal growth can slow
* pregnancy complications increase

---

### Management intuition

Goal:

> keep mom well-oxygenated, hydrated, pain-controlled, and infection-free so sickling is less likely.

Common principles:

* folic acid supplementation because RBC turnover is high
* close maternal-fetal monitoring
* treat infections quickly
* hydrate and give oxygen during crises if needed
* pain control when vaso-occlusive crisis happens
* transfusion for severe anemia or major complications

Hydroxyurea is usually avoided in pregnancy because of fetal safety concerns.

Hydroxyurea = a drug that increases HbF (fetal hemoglobin), which helps prevent sickling outside pregnancy.

---

### Genetics

Sickle cell disease is autosomal recessive.

Autosomal recessive means the baby needs 2 affected copies of the gene to have the disease.

So partner testing matters:

* mom has sickle cell disease
* if dad has sickle trait or disease, fetus may be at risk

Sickle trait means one sickle gene copy, usually much milder than sickle cell disease.

---

## One-line intuition

Sickle cell disease in pregnancy is dangerous because pregnancy increases oxygen demand and clotting risk, while sickled RBCs can block maternal and placental blood flow.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1: sickle cell disease causes **functional asplenia**, so pregnant patients are especially vulnerable to encapsulated bacteria like Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, and Neisseria meningitidis.

### Core idea

A **bone infarct** is death of bone and bone marrow because blood supply gets cut off.

Infarct = tissue death from ischemia.

Ischemia = not enough blood/oxygen.

So bone infarct literally means:

> part of the bone loses blood flow and dies.

---

### Where it happens

Bone infarct usually refers to osteonecrosis in the:

* metaphysis = flared part near the end of a long bone
* diaphysis = shaft of a long bone

If the same process happens near the joint surface, especially in the femoral head, people often call it:

* AVN = avascular necrosis
* osteonecrosis = bone death

Same basic mechanism, different location.

---

### Why it happens

Bone is alive, so it needs blood vessels.

If blood flow is blocked:

* osteocytes die
* marrow cells die
* the body tries to repair the edge
* the border becomes sclerotic

Sclerotic = hardened/extra dense bone.

That repair border is why bone infarcts often look like a **serpiginous** outline.

Serpiginous = snake-like/wavy.

---

### Classic imaging idea

On x-ray, a chronic bone infarct can look like:

* central lucency
* surrounded by a wavy sclerotic rim

Lucency = darker area on x-ray.

On MRI (magnetic resonance imaging), the classic finding is the:

* **double-line sign**

This means an inner bright line of granulation tissue plus an outer dark line of sclerosis.

Granulation tissue = healing/reactive tissue with new small blood vessels.

---

### Common causes

Think of anything that blocks or damages bone blood supply:

* sickle cell disease
* corticosteroid use
* alcohol use
* trauma
* Gaucher disease
* renal transplant history

In sickle cell disease, sickled RBCs (red blood cells) clog small vessels, so bone marrow can infarct.

---

## One-line intuition

A bone infarct is basically a “stroke of the bone”: blood flow stops, bone/marrow dies, and the healing rim makes a wavy sclerotic border.

---

## HY pearl

Bone infarct is classically in the **metaphysis/diaphysis**, while AVN (avascular necrosis) classically involves the **epiphysis/subchondral bone** near a joint, especially the femoral head.

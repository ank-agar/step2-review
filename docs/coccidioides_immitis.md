### Core idea

Coccidioides immitis is a dimorphic fungus (fungus that changes form depending on temperature/environment) that causes coccidioidomycosis, aka Valley fever.

Classic setting:

* desert Southwest / California San Joaquin Valley
* dust exposure
* inhalation of arthroconidia (airborne fungal spores)

---

### What form does it take?

In the soil, it grows as:

* mold
* hyphae (branching fungal filaments)
* arthroconidia (infectious spores)

In the body, especially lung tissue, it becomes:

* spherules (large round fungal structures)
* filled with endospores (smaller internal spores)

So the Step 1 image clue is:

> big spherules packed with endospores.

---

### Why it causes pneumonia

You inhale arthroconidia from dust.

They reach the lungs and transform into spherules.

The spherules rupture and release endospores, which triggers inflammation, so patients can get:

* fever
* cough
* chest pain
* fatigue
* pneumonia-like illness

This is why it is called Valley fever.

---

### Dissemination

Most infections stay in the lungs or resolve.

But it can spread, especially in high-risk patients, to:

* skin
* bones/joints
* meninges (brain and spinal cord coverings)

Coccidioides meningitis is the scary disseminated form.

---

## One-line intuition

Coccidioides immitis is a desert dust fungus: inhaled spores become big lung spherules full of endospores.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Coccidioides = spherules with endospores.

This is different from Histoplasma, which is small intracellular yeast inside macrophages (immune cells that eat microbes).

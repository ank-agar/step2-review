An aldosteronoma is an aldosterone-producing adrenal adenoma.

Break it apart:

* aldosterone = mineralocorticoid hormone that makes the kidney retain sodium
* -oma = tumor

So:

> a usually benign adrenal cortex tumor that secretes too much aldosterone.

This causes primary hyperaldosteronism, also called Conn syndrome.

Primary means the problem is inside the adrenal gland itself, not because renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system stimulation is telling it to make aldosterone.

---

### What aldosterone normally does

Aldosterone acts on the collecting duct of the kidney and causes:

* increased sodium reabsorption
* increased water retention
* increased potassium secretion
* increased hydrogen ion secretion

So too much aldosterone causes:

* hypertension (high blood pressure)
* hypokalemia (low potassium)
* metabolic alkalosis (high blood pH from losing hydrogen ions)

---

### Why renin is low

The adrenal tumor is making aldosterone on its own.

That means:

* aldosterone goes up
* sodium and water retention goes up
* blood volume and blood pressure go up

The kidney senses the high blood pressure/volume and shuts off renin.

So the classic labs are:

* high aldosterone
* low renin
* high aldosterone-to-renin ratio

---

## One-line intuition

An aldosteronoma is an adrenal tumor stuck in "save sodium, dump potassium" mode, causing hypertension with low renin.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Primary hyperaldosteronism: aldosterone high, renin low.

Secondary hyperaldosteronism: aldosterone high, renin high, because renin is driving the aldosterone increase.

## Drug-induced parkinsonism

Drug-induced parkinsonism is Parkinson-like movement symptoms caused by a medication.

Parkinsonism means symptoms that look like Parkinson disease:

* bradykinesia (slow movement)
* rigidity (stiff muscles)
* resting tremor
* shuffling gait
* masked facies (reduced facial expression)

---

### Core idea

Most commonly, the drug blocks dopamine signaling in the basal ganglia.

Dopamine is a neurotransmitter (chemical signal between neurons) that helps the basal ganglia (deep brain movement-control nuclei) promote smooth movement.

If a drug blocks dopamine D2 receptors (dopamine receptor subtype 2), the brain acts like it has too little dopamine.

So the patient looks parkinsonian.

---

### Common drugs

Classic causes:

* antipsychotics, especially haloperidol and fluphenazine
* antiemetics (anti-nausea drugs), especially metoclopramide and prochlorperazine
* dopamine-depleting drugs, like reserpine and tetrabenazine

Why?

These reduce dopamine effect in the nigrostriatal pathway (dopamine pathway from substantia nigra to striatum that controls movement).

---

### Why symptoms happen

Normally:

* dopamine helps movement start and flow smoothly

With dopamine blockade:

* direct pathway activity decreases
* indirect pathway activity increases
* thalamus gets inhibited
* motor cortex gets less stimulation

Translation:

> less dopamine signal = less movement output.

That gives bradykinesia, rigidity, and shuffling gait.

---

### Drug-induced vs Parkinson disease

Drug-induced parkinsonism is usually:

* more symmetric
* starts after beginning/increasing a dopamine-blocking drug
* often improves after stopping the drug

Parkinson disease is usually:

* asymmetric at first
* progressive
* due to degeneration of dopaminergic neurons (dopamine-producing neurons) in the substantia nigra

---

## One-line intuition

A dopamine-blocking drug makes the basal ganglia behave like dopamine is low, so the patient gets Parkinson-like stiffness and slow movement.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Antipsychotic adverse effects come from dopamine blockade in different pathways:

* nigrostriatal pathway blockade → extrapyramidal symptoms (movement side effects), including drug-induced parkinsonism
* tuberoinfundibular pathway blockade → hyperprolactinemia (high prolactin hormone)
* mesolimbic pathway blockade → antipsychotic effect

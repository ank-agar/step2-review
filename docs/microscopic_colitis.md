### Core idea

Microscopic colitis is inflammation of the colon that causes chronic watery diarrhea, but the colon often looks normal on colonoscopy.

The key is in the name:

* microscopic = you need a microscope to see it
* colitis = inflammation of the colon

So:

> normal-looking colon on scope, abnormal biopsy under microscope.

---

### Main symptom

Classic presentation:

* chronic watery, nonbloody diarrhea

Nonbloody matters because the inflammation is usually subtle and mucosal, not deep ulcerating inflammation like ulcerative colitis or Crohn disease.

---

### Two types

Microscopic colitis has 2 major subtypes:

* lymphocytic colitis
* collagenous colitis

Lymphocytic colitis:

* increased lymphocytes in the colonic epithelium
* lymphocytes = white blood cells involved in immune inflammation

Collagenous colitis:

* thickened subepithelial collagen band
* subepithelial = under the epithelial surface lining
* collagen = structural protein/scar-like support material

---

### Why diarrhea happens

The colon normally absorbs water from stool.

In microscopic colitis, the colon lining is inflamed at the microscopic level, so it does not absorb water normally.

Result:

* excess water stays in the lumen
* stool stays watery
* diarrhea is usually nonbloody

---

### Associations

Common associations include:

* older age
* female sex
* autoimmune disease, like celiac disease
* smoking
* medications, especially NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs), PPIs (proton pump inhibitors), and SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors)

---

## One-line intuition

Microscopic colitis is a "biopsy diagnosis": the colon looks normal to your eyes, but the microscope shows immune inflammation causing watery diarrhea.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On Step 1, chronic watery nonbloody diarrhea + normal colonoscopy should make you think microscopic colitis, and the diagnosis requires colon biopsy.

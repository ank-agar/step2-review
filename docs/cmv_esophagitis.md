# CMV esophagitis

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/17113.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/2a6c/9291259/1d47fad0eb55/CCR3-10-e6044-g002.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/2a6c/9291259/bfae6eac75ec/CCR3-10-e6044-g001.jpg)

CMV esophagitis means inflammation/infection of the esophagus by CMV (cytomegalovirus, a herpesvirus).

It classically happens in immunocompromised patients, especially:

* AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome)
* transplant patients
* patients on immunosuppressive drugs

---

### Core idea

CMV infects cells in the esophageal wall and causes large, painful ulcers.

So the patient gets:

* odynophagia (painful swallowing)
* dysphagia (difficulty swallowing)
* chest pain
* sometimes GI bleeding (gastrointestinal bleeding)

---

### What you see

On EGD (esophagogastroduodenoscopy, camera scope of the esophagus/stomach/duodenum), CMV tends to cause:

* large, deep, linear ulcers
* usually in the mid-to-distal esophagus

This contrasts with HSV (herpes simplex virus), which more classically causes multiple small “punched-out” ulcers.

---

### Why biopsy matters

You diagnose it with biopsy.

Histology (microscope tissue appearance) shows:

* enlarged infected cells
* intranuclear inclusions (viral material inside the nucleus)
* “owl eye” inclusions

Break that down:

* cyto- = cell
* megalo- = large

So cytomegalovirus literally hints at “big cells,” which is why infected cells look enlarged.

---

### Treatment

Treat with antivirals:

* ganciclovir
* valganciclovir
* foscarnet if resistant or severe intolerance

These drugs inhibit viral DNA replication, so the virus cannot efficiently copy itself.

---

## One-line intuition

CMV esophagitis is an opportunistic viral infection that makes big infected cells and big esophageal ulcers.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For infectious esophagitis on Step 1:

* Candida = white plaques
* HSV (herpes simplex virus) = small punched-out ulcers, multinucleated cells
* CMV (cytomegalovirus) = large linear ulcers, owl-eye inclusions

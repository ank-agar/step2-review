# Renal infarction

### Core idea

Renal infarction = death of kidney tissue because its blood supply is suddenly blocked.

Break it down:

* renal = kidney
* infarction = tissue death from ischemia (low blood flow/oxygen)

So it is basically a “kidney heart attack.”

---

### What blocks the blood?

Usually a clot blocks the renal artery (artery supplying the kidney) or one of its branches.

Common causes:

* embolus (clot that travels from somewhere else), especially from atrial fibrillation (irregular atrial rhythm that forms clots)
* thrombosis (clot forming in place)
* renal artery dissection (tear in the artery wall)
* hypercoagulable state (blood clots too easily)

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Classic presentation:

* sudden flank pain
* nausea/vomiting
* fever sometimes
* hematuria (blood in urine)

Reasoning:

* kidney tissue loses oxygen suddenly, so it hurts
* damaged kidney tissue leaks blood into urine, causing hematuria
* necrosis (cell death) releases intracellular enzymes, so LDH (lactate dehydrogenase, a cell-injury enzyme) can be very high

---

### Imaging clue

On CT (computed tomography), renal infarction often shows a wedge-shaped area that does not enhance.

Why wedge-shaped?

Because renal arteries branch like a tree. If one branch gets blocked, the downstream territory dies in a triangular/wedge pattern.

---

## One-line intuition

A clot blocks blood flow to part of the kidney, so that kidney segment becomes ischemic and dies in a wedge-shaped pattern.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Renal infarction causes coagulative necrosis, because the kidney is a solid organ. Brain infarcts are the big exception because they cause liquefactive necrosis.

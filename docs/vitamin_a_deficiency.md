# Vitamin A deficiency

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/79/Typical_location_of_Bitot%27s_spots.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4d/Bitot.jpg)

### Core idea

Vitamin A deficiency = low vitamin A, which mainly affects:

* vision
* epithelial cells (surface-lining cells)
* immune function

Vitamin A is needed to make retinal, which is part of rhodopsin (light-sensing pigment in rod cells).

So low vitamin A first causes:

* night blindness

because rod cells are especially important for seeing in dim light.

---

### Why the eye findings happen

Vitamin A helps epithelial cells stay moist and normal.

Without it, epithelial surfaces become:

* dry
* keratinized
* damaged

In the eye, this causes:

* xerophthalmia (dry eye from vitamin A deficiency)
* Bitot spots (foamy keratin plaques on the conjunctiva, which is the white-eye covering)
* keratomalacia (softening/damage of the cornea)
* blindness if severe

Break it down:

* xero- = dry
* ophthalmia = eye condition

So xerophthalmia literally means dry eye disease.

---

### Why infections increase

Vitamin A helps maintain mucosal barriers (wet protective linings like respiratory and GI tract lining).

Low vitamin A = weaker barriers, so pathogens enter more easily.

That is why deficiency increases risk of infections, especially in children.

GI tract = gastrointestinal tract, meaning stomach/intestines.

---

## One-line intuition

Vitamin A keeps rods seeing in the dark and keeps epithelial surfaces moist; without it, you get night blindness + dry, keratinized eyes.

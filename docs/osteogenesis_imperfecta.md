### Core idea

Osteogenesis imperfecta means imperfect bone formation.

It is usually a genetic collagen disorder causing fragile bones that fracture easily.

Most Step 1 version:

* mutation in COL1A1 or COL1A2
* defective type I collagen
* brittle bones
* blue sclera
* hearing loss
* dental problems called dentinogenesis imperfecta (abnormal tooth dentin formation)

---

### Why type I collagen causes this

Type I collagen is a major structural protein in:

* bone
* skin
* tendon
* dentin (hard inner layer of teeth)
* sclera (white outer layer of the eye)

So if type I collagen is weak, the tissues that depend on it become weak too.

Bone needs type I collagen as the scaffold that minerals get deposited on.

Think:

> collagen = rebar  
> calcium/mineral = concrete

If the rebar is bad, the bone can look mineralized but still breaks easily.

---

### Why the classic findings happen

* **Fragility fractures:** weak type I collagen makes poor bone scaffold
* **Blue sclera:** sclera is thinner, so underlying dark choroid shows through
* **Hearing loss:** abnormal small bones/connective tissue in the ear
* **Dentinogenesis imperfecta:** dentin depends on type I collagen
* **Short stature/bowing:** repeated fractures and poor remodeling deform long bones

---

### Genetics

Most cases are autosomal dominant (one mutated copy is enough).

Key genes:

* COL1A1
* COL1A2

These encode type I collagen chains.

---

## One-line intuition

Osteogenesis imperfecta is basically bad type I collagen, so bones lose their flexible scaffold and snap easily.

---

## Pearl 🔥

High-yield association:

* osteogenesis imperfecta = type I collagen defect
* Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (connective tissue disorder with hyperextensible skin/joint hypermobility) = often type III or type V collagen problems depending on subtype
* scurvy = impaired hydroxylation of collagen due to vitamin C deficiency

So if you see brittle bones + blue sclera, think **type I collagen**.

# Stillbirth

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Placenta_system.svg/500px-Placenta_system.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/77/Blausen_0737_PlacentalAbruption.png)

### Core idea

Stillbirth = fetal death before or during delivery, usually defined in the US as death at **≥20 weeks gestation**.

Do not confuse it with:

* **spontaneous abortion** = pregnancy loss before 20 weeks
* **neonatal death** = baby is born alive, then dies after birth

---

### Intuition

The fetus depends on the placenta for oxygen/nutrients.

So many causes of stillbirth boil down to:

> the fetus cannot get enough oxygen, blood flow, or normal development support.

Big categories:

* **placental problems**: placental insufficiency, placental abruption
* **umbilical cord problems**: cord compression, cord accident
* **maternal disease**: diabetes mellitus, hypertension, antiphospholipid syndrome
* **fetal problems**: congenital anomalies, chromosomal abnormalities
* **infection**: syphilis, parvovirus B19, listeria, etc.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Decreased fetal movement:** fetus is no longer moving normally
* **No fetal heart tones:** fetal cardiac activity has stopped
* **Ultrasound confirmation:** shows no fetal cardiac activity
* **Placental abruption:** placenta separates from uterus early, cutting off oxygen delivery

Break down **intrauterine fetal demise**:

* intrauterine = inside the uterus
* fetal = involving the fetus
* demise = death

So **IUFD (intrauterine fetal demise)** basically means fetal death inside the uterus.

---

## One-line intuition

Stillbirth is fetal death after viability threshold timing, most often from disrupted fetal oxygen delivery through placenta/cord/maternal-fetal disease.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, think **placenta = fetal lung**.

So placental abruption or placental insufficiency can cause fetal hypoxia (low oxygen) → stillbirth.

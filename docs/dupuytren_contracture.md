### Core idea

Dupuytren contracture is thickening and shortening of the palmar fascia (connective tissue sheet under the skin of the palm).

This pulls the fingers into flexion, meaning the patient cannot fully straighten them.

Most commonly affects:

* ring finger
* pinky finger

---

### Why the finger bends

The problem is not primarily the tendon.

The problem is the palmar fascia becoming fibrotic.

Fibrosis means excess scar-like connective tissue deposition.

Step-by-step:

* fibroblasts/myofibroblasts (collagen-producing contractile cells) proliferate
* collagen builds up in the palm
* firm nodules form first
* nodules become cords
* cords shorten and pull the finger into flexion

So the tendon may still be intact, but the shortened fascia acts like a tight rope pulling the finger down.

---

### Classic findings

* painless palm nodule
* thick cord in the palm
* puckering or dimpling of palmar skin
* progressive flexion contracture of MCP (metacarpophalangeal) and PIP (proximal interphalangeal) joints

MCP joint = knuckle joint.

PIP joint = middle finger joint.

---

### Associations

Classically associated with:

* older men
* Northern European ancestry
* diabetes mellitus
* alcohol use
* smoking
* family history
* Peyronie disease (fibrosis of penile connective tissue)
* Ledderhose disease (plantar fibromatosis, fibrosis of the sole fascia)

---

## One-line intuition

The palm fascia turns into a shrinking scar cord, so it drags the ring/pinky fingers into a bent position.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Dupuytren contracture is a fibroproliferative disorder of the palmar fascia, not a primary flexor tendon disease.

Scorpions technically **sting**, not bite.

They inject venom through the **stinger** at the end of the tail.

The key anatomy:

* **telson** = tail tip containing venom gland + stinger
* **aculeus** = actual sharp stinger
* venom = toxin mixture injected into skin

---

### Core idea

Most scorpion stings cause:

* immediate severe burning pain
* numbness/tingling
* mild swelling or redness

But dangerous species, especially the **Arizona bark scorpion**, can cause systemic symptoms.

**Systemic** means affecting the whole body, not just the sting site.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Scorpion venom often acts as a **neurotoxin**.

**Neurotoxin** = toxin that affects nerves.

It messes with nerve ion channels, especially **sodium channels**.

Sodium channels are like tiny gates that help nerves fire electrical signals.

So if venom makes nerves fire abnormally, you get:

* severe pain
* tingling/numbness
* muscle twitching
* roving eye movements
* drooling
* sweating
* vomiting
* high blood pressure
* trouble breathing in severe cases

---

### Who is highest risk?

Severe disease is more likely in:

* children
* older adults
* people stung by a dangerous species
* people with allergic reaction

Healthy adults usually have pain but do okay.

---

### Treatment idea

For mild stings:

* clean the area
* ice pack
* pain control
* watch symptoms

Go to the emergency department for breathing trouble, abnormal eye movements, severe muscle twitching, drooling, vomiting, confusion, or symptoms in a child.

---

## One-line intuition

A scorpion sting is basically venom irritating nerves, so the big theme is **pain + nerve/autonomic overactivity**.

**Autonomic** means involuntary body functions like sweating, salivation, blood pressure, heart rate, and breathing.

---

## High-yield pearl

The **Arizona bark scorpion** is the major medically important scorpion in the United States, and its venom can cause prominent neurologic symptoms with surprisingly little swelling at the sting site.

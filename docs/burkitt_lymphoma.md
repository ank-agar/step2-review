### Core idea

Burkitt lymphoma is a very aggressive mature B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL; lymphoma that is not Hodgkin lymphoma).

It is driven by overactive MYC (a gene that promotes cell growth and division).

Classic translocation:

* t(8;14)
* MYC on chromosome 8 moves near the immunoglobulin heavy chain gene on chromosome 14
* immunoglobulin means antibody-related

So the antibody gene enhancer keeps pushing MYC on.

Result:

* B cells divide extremely fast
* tumor grows rapidly
* high risk of tumor lysis syndrome (TLS; dangerous release of potassium, phosphate, and uric acid after tumor cells break apart)

---

### Why the findings happen

**Starry-sky histology:**

The dark background is packed malignant B cells.

The pale “stars” are macrophages (cleanup cells) eating dead tumor cells.

Why so much dead tumor?

Because Burkitt grows so fast that lots of cells are also rapidly undergoing apoptosis (programmed cell death).

---

### Associations

Common associations:

* Epstein-Barr virus (EBV; herpesvirus associated with mono and some cancers)
* endemic African type: jaw or facial mass
* sporadic type: abdominal mass, often near the ileocecal region (junction of small intestine and colon)
* immunodeficiency-associated type: seen with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV; virus that causes AIDS)

---

### High-yield markers

Usually:

* CD20 positive (B-cell marker)
* CD10 positive (germinal center B-cell marker)
* BCL6 positive (germinal center B-cell marker)
* Ki-67 nearly 100% (proliferation marker)

That Ki-67 near 100% is the big clue: almost every tumor cell is actively cycling.

---

## One-line intuition

Burkitt lymphoma is MYC stuck in “growth mode,” making B cells divide explosively.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Burkitt lymphoma = t(8;14) + MYC + starry-sky histology + very high Ki-67.

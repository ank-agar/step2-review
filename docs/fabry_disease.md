# Fabry disease

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK11608/bin/ch4f1.gif)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK11605/bin/ch24f1.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK11599/bin/ch26f2.jpg)

Fabry disease is an **X-linked lysosomal storage disease**.

Break that down:

* **X-linked** = mutation is on the X chromosome
* **lysosome** = the cell's recycling center
* **storage disease** = something cannot be broken down, so it builds up

The enzyme deficiency is:

* **alpha-galactosidase A**

The thing that accumulates is:

* **globotriaosylceramide** aka **Gb3** (a fatty glycosphingolipid molecule)

---

### Core idea

Fabry = **alpha-galactosidase A deficiency → Gb3 builds up in lysosomes → blood vessels, nerves, kidneys, heart, and eyes get damaged.**

Think:

> clogged cellular recycling bins, especially in vascular endothelial cells.

**Endothelial cells** = cells lining blood vessels.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Angiokeratomas** = small red-purple skin papules from Gb3 buildup in superficial blood vessels
* **Burning pain in hands/feet** = small nerve fiber damage; this is called **acroparesthesia** (pain/tingling in extremities)
* **Hypohidrosis** = reduced sweating because autonomic nerves/sweat glands are affected
* **Cornea verticillata** = whorl-like corneal deposits; usually does not severely impair vision
* **Proteinuria/renal failure** = Gb3 damages kidney cells, especially podocytes and vascular cells
* **Cardiomyopathy** = heart muscle thickens from storage and chronic cellular injury
* **Stroke risk** = vascular dysfunction and small-vessel disease

---

### Inheritance

Fabry is classically **X-linked recessive**.

So males are usually more severely affected because they only have one X chromosome.

Females can still have symptoms because of **lyonization** (random X chromosome inactivation).

---

## One-line intuition

Fabry disease is when a missing lysosomal enzyme causes fatty material to build up in blood vessels and organs, giving pain crises, angiokeratomas, corneal whorls, kidney failure, and heart disease.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember:

**Fabry = alpha-galactosidase A deficiency + X-linked + angiokeratomas + acroparesthesia + renal failure.**

This is different from **Tay-Sachs disease**, which is **hexosaminidase A deficiency** with **no hepatosplenomegaly**.

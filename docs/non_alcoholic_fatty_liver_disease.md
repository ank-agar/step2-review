## Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/51/Non-alcoholic_fatty_liver_disease_%28NAFLD%29_spectrum.png)

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17508/N00058-H_thumbnail.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441992/bin/liver04.jpg)

NAFLD (non-alcoholic fatty liver disease) = fat buildup in the liver not explained by heavy alcohol use.

The key word is:

* hepatic steatosis = fat accumulation inside hepatocytes (liver cells)

Usually associated with metabolic syndrome:

* obesity
* insulin resistance
* type 2 diabetes mellitus
* hyperlipidemia (high blood lipids)

---

### Core idea

The liver is getting too much fatty acid input and making/storing too much triglyceride (fat), so hepatocytes become packed with fat droplets.

Simple progression:

* normal liver
* steatosis = fatty liver
* NASH (non-alcoholic steatohepatitis) = fat + inflammation
* fibrosis = scar tissue
* cirrhosis = severe scarring with distorted liver architecture

---

### Why it happens

Insulin resistance means insulin does not suppress fat breakdown well in adipose tissue (body fat).

So more free fatty acids go to the liver.

The liver then:

* takes up more fat
* makes more triglycerides
* stores fat in hepatocytes

If fat causes oxidative stress and inflammation, it can become NASH (non-alcoholic steatohepatitis).

---

### Labs

Often:

* ALT (alanine aminotransferase) > AST (aspartate aminotransferase)

This is the opposite pattern of classic alcoholic liver disease, where AST (aspartate aminotransferase) is usually greater than ALT (alanine aminotransferase).

---

## One-line intuition

NAFLD (non-alcoholic fatty liver disease) is basically metabolic syndrome showing up in the liver as fat-filled hepatocytes.

---

## Pearl 🔥

NAFLD (non-alcoholic fatty liver disease) is the most common cause of chronic liver disease in developed countries, and NASH (non-alcoholic steatohepatitis) can progress to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (liver cancer).

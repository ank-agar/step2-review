## Rhabdomyolysis

### Core idea

Rhabdomyolysis = skeletal muscle breakdown.

Broken muscle cells spill their contents into the blood:

* myoglobin (oxygen-binding muscle protein)
* CK (creatine kinase; muscle enzyme)
* potassium
* phosphate

The big danger is:

* myoglobin damages renal tubules
* potassium can cause dangerous arrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms)

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* muscle pain
* weakness
* dark tea/cola-colored urine

The urine gets dark because myoglobin enters the urine.

Important nuance:

* urine dipstick may be positive for "blood"
* but microscopy shows few/no RBCs (red blood cells)

Why?

The dipstick detects heme pigment, and myoglobin has heme.

---

### Why kidney injury happens

Damaged muscle releases myoglobin into blood.

Then:

* myoglobin gets filtered by the kidney
* it precipitates in renal tubules
* it causes oxidative injury
* tubular obstruction + toxicity leads to ATN (acute tubular necrosis)

So the kidney is basically trying to clear too much muscle pigment and gets injured in the process.

---

### Common causes

Anything that crushes, overworks, poisons, or ischemically injures muscle:

* crush injury
* extreme exercise
* seizures
* prolonged immobilization
* statins
* cocaine/amphetamines
* heat stroke

---

## One-line intuition

Rhabdomyolysis is when muscle cells burst open, dump myoglobin into the blood, and the kidneys get clogged/toxically injured trying to filter it.

---

## Pearl

Rhabdomyolysis can cause hyperkalemia (high potassium) because skeletal muscle cells contain lots of intracellular potassium, and when they lyse (break open), potassium spills into the blood.

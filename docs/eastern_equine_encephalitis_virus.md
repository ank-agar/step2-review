### Core idea

Eastern equine encephalitis virus (EEEV) is a mosquito-borne RNA (ribonucleic acid) virus that can cause severe encephalitis (brain inflammation).

Break the name apart:

* eastern = classically eastern United States/Gulf Coast areas
* equine = horses
* encephalitis = inflammation of the brain

So the name basically means:

> a virus that can inflame the brain, especially known for severe disease in horses and humans.

---

### What kind of virus is it?

EEEV is an:

* arbovirus (arthropod-borne virus, meaning spread by insects like mosquitoes)
* Alphavirus
* Togavirus
* enveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus

Positive-sense RNA (RNA that can act like messenger RNA) means the viral genome can be translated by the host cell relatively quickly.

---

### Transmission

The main cycle is:

* bird gets infected
* mosquito bites bird
* mosquito carries virus to another bird

Humans and horses are usually infected when a bridge vector mosquito (mosquito that bites both birds and mammals) bites an infected bird, then bites a human or horse.

Humans and horses are dead-end hosts (hosts that do not pass infection onward) because they do not develop enough virus in the blood to infect the next mosquito.

---

### Why the disease is so bad

The dangerous part is when the virus invades the CNS (central nervous system, meaning brain and spinal cord).

Once in the brain, it causes:

* inflammation
* neuronal injury
* cerebral edema (brain swelling)

That is why severe cases can cause:

* fever
* headache
* vomiting
* confusion
* seizures
* coma
* death

---

### Key clinical idea

Most infected people have no symptoms, but if EEEV becomes neuroinvasive (enters the nervous system), it is very severe.

There is no specific antiviral treatment and no human vaccine, so management is supportive care.

---

## One-line intuition

EEEV is a mosquito-bird virus that accidentally infects humans/horses, but when it reaches the brain it can cause devastating encephalitis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Humans and horses are dead-end hosts in Eastern equine encephalitis because their viremia (virus level in blood) is too low to keep the mosquito transmission cycle going.

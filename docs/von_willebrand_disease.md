# von Willebrand disease

von Willebrand disease is a bleeding disorder caused by decreased or defective vWF (von Willebrand factor).

vWF normally does 2 big things:

* helps platelets stick to damaged vessel wall
* carries factor VIII (clotting factor 8) in the blood

So if vWF is low/bad:

* platelets cannot adhere well
* factor VIII can drop
* bleeding lasts longer

---

### Core idea

Think of vWF as platelet glue.

When a blood vessel is injured:

* collagen is exposed
* vWF binds collagen
* platelets bind vWF through GpIb (glycoprotein Ib)

So vWF forms the bridge:

> collagen in vessel wall ↔ vWF ↔ platelet

Without that bridge, the platelet plug forms poorly.

---

### Classic symptoms

Mostly mucosal bleeding:

* epistaxis (nosebleeds)
* easy bruising
* heavy menstrual bleeding
* bleeding after dental work/surgery

This is because platelet plug problems usually cause superficial/mucosal bleeding.

Deep joint bleeding is more classic for hemophilia (factor VIII or IX deficiency).

---

### Labs

Typical pattern:

* normal platelet count
* increased bleeding time
* normal PT (prothrombin time)
* normal or increased PTT (partial thromboplastin time)
* abnormal ristocetin test

Why PTT can increase:

vWF protects factor VIII from degradation, so low vWF can cause low factor VIII.

Why ristocetin is abnormal:

Ristocetin is a lab drug that makes vWF bind platelet GpIb. If vWF is missing/defective, platelets do not clump properly.

---

### Inheritance

Most common type is:

* type 1
* autosomal dominant
* partial quantitative deficiency

Quantitative deficiency = not enough vWF.

Qualitative defect = vWF is present but does not work properly.

---

### Treatment

Desmopressin, also called DDAVP (1-deamino-8-D-arginine vasopressin), can be used in many patients.

It increases release of stored vWF from endothelial cells (cells lining blood vessels).

---

## One-line intuition

von Willebrand disease = bad platelet adhesion because the platelet-to-collagen glue is missing or defective.

---

## HY pearl

vWD (von Willebrand disease) looks like a platelet problem clinically, but can also increase PTT (partial thromboplastin time) because vWF carries and stabilizes factor VIII.

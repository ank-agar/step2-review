# Polyhydramnios

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/60/Ultrasound_Scan_ND_145330_1455290_cr.png)

### Core idea

Polyhydramnios means **too much amniotic fluid** (fluid surrounding the fetus in the amniotic sac).

Think:

* poly = many/much
* hydramnios = amniotic fluid

So literally:

> too much fluid around the baby.

---

### Why it happens

Amniotic fluid balance is mainly:

* fetus pees fluid out
* fetus swallows fluid back in

So polyhydramnios happens when there is:

* **too much fluid production**, or
* **not enough fetal swallowing/reabsorption**

Classic causes:

* maternal diabetes mellitus (high maternal glucose causes fetal hyperglycemia, which causes fetal polyuria, meaning lots of fetal urination)
* esophageal atresia (esophagus does not connect normally, so fetus cannot swallow fluid well)
* duodenal atresia (blocked duodenum, so swallowed fluid cannot pass normally)
* anencephaly (major absence of brain/skull development, causing poor fetal swallowing)
* twin-twin transfusion syndrome (abnormal blood sharing between monochorionic twins, meaning twins sharing one placenta)

---

### Diagnosis idea

On ultrasound, you measure the amount of fluid.

Common cutoffs:

* amniotic fluid index (AFI) > 24 cm
* single deepest pocket (SDP) > 8 cm

AFI (amniotic fluid index) = add up the deepest fluid pocket in 4 uterine quadrants.

SDP (single deepest pocket) = measure the single deepest pocket of fluid.

---

### Why Step 1 cares

The biggest association to remember is:

* **GI obstruction = polyhydramnios**

GI (gastrointestinal) obstruction prevents normal fetal swallowing/absorption of amniotic fluid.

Example:

* duodenal atresia → "double bubble" sign + polyhydramnios

Why?

The fetus can still make fluid, but cannot move swallowed fluid through the GI tract normally, so fluid builds up outside the fetus.

---

## One-line intuition

Polyhydramnios is when the fetus is surrounded by excess amniotic fluid because fluid is being made too much or swallowed/absorbed too little.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Polyhydramnios points toward **impaired fetal swallowing**, especially from **esophageal atresia** or **duodenal atresia**; oligohydramnios (too little amniotic fluid) points more toward **poor fetal urine production**, like bilateral renal agenesis.

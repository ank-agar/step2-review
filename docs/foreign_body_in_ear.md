## Foreign body in ear

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19592.jpg)

![Image](https://dgnvxbcc3-res.cloudinary.com/image/upload/v1773106471/Journals/Automation/AFP/2007/1015/p1185-f1-gif.gif)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Anatomy_of_the_Human_Ear_1_Intl.svg/960px-Anatomy_of_the_Human_Ear_1_Intl.svg.png)

A **foreign body in the ear** means an object is stuck in the **external auditory canal** (ear canal).

Common examples:

* beads
* small toys
* popcorn kernels
* insects
* hearing aid pieces
* cotton swab tips

Most common in kids because they put small objects into body openings.

---

### Core idea

The ear canal is a narrow tube ending at the **tympanic membrane** (eardrum).

So the danger is:

* the object blocks sound going in → hearing loss/fullness
* it irritates canal skin → pain, bleeding, infection
* pushing it deeper can injure the eardrum

---

### Symptoms

Can be asymptomatic, especially in kids.

If symptomatic:

* ear pain
* decreased hearing
* fullness/pressure
* drainage
* bleeding
* buzzing/crawling sensation if insect

---

### Why removal can be tricky

The inner part of the external auditory canal is very sensitive because the skin lies close to bone.

Also, round smooth objects like beads are hard to grab.

So repeated attempts can:

* push the object deeper
* cause canal laceration
* perforate the tympanic membrane

---

### Important management idea

Do **not** blindly dig around in the ear.

Removal depends on what the object is:

* visible/graspable object → forceps may work
* insect → kill it first with mineral oil or lidocaine (local anesthetic)
* button battery → urgent removal
* sharp object, deep object, failed attempts, or eardrum trauma → ENT (ear, nose, throat specialist)

Avoid irrigation if:

* button battery
* suspected tympanic membrane perforation
* object that swells with water, like vegetable matter

---

## One-line intuition

A foreign body in the ear is basically “something stuck in a narrow tube next to the eardrum,” so the big risk is damage from the object or from aggressive removal.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Button batteries in the ear are an emergency because they can cause **liquefaction necrosis** (chemical tissue destruction that turns tissue soft/liquefied), so they need urgent removal.

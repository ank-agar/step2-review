### Core idea

Thymoma is a tumor of thymic epithelial cells.

The thymus is in the anterior mediastinum, meaning the front part of the central chest cavity.

So thymoma = thymus tumor sitting behind the sternum.

---

### Classic association

Thymoma is strongly associated with myasthenia gravis.

Myasthenia gravis is an autoimmune disease where antibodies attack acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction.

Neuromuscular junction = where a nerve talks to a muscle.

This causes:

* muscle weakness
* worse with repeated use
* improves with rest
* ptosis, meaning droopy eyelids
* diplopia, meaning double vision

---

### Why does thymoma cause autoimmunity?

The thymus normally teaches T cells not to attack self.

T cells are immune cells that help coordinate and kill infected or abnormal cells.

If the thymus becomes abnormal, that "self-tolerance training" can fail.

So autoreactive T cells may survive and help trigger autoimmune disease.

That is why thymoma can be linked with:

* myasthenia gravis
* pure red cell aplasia, meaning failure to make red blood cells
* hypogammaglobulinemia, meaning low antibody levels

---

### Imaging intuition

If you see an anterior mediastinal mass in an adult, think of the classic 4 T's:

* thymoma
* teratoma
* thyroid mass
* terrible lymphoma

Thymoma is the one that screams myasthenia gravis association.

---

## One-line intuition

Thymoma is a thymus epithelial tumor in the anterior mediastinum that matters for Step 1 because it can break immune self-tolerance and cause myasthenia gravis.

## Pearl

Thymoma + ptosis + muscle weakness worse with use = think myasthenia gravis from anti-acetylcholine receptor antibodies.

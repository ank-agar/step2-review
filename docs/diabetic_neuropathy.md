## Diabetic neuropathy

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/04/Blausen_0311_DiabeticNeuropathy.png)

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17457/N00007-H.jpg)

Diabetic neuropathy = nerve damage caused by chronic diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar disease).

Most common type:

* distal symmetric polyneuropathy

Break it down:

* distal = far from the center of the body
* symmetric = both sides
* polyneuropathy = many nerves are damaged

So it usually starts in the:

* feet first
* then moves upward
* later can affect hands

This is the classic stocking-glove pattern.

---

### Why diabetes damages nerves

Chronically high glucose injures nerves in 2 big ways:

* direct metabolic damage to nerve cells
* damage to small blood vessels that feed nerves

So the nerve is both:

* chemically stressed
* poorly supplied with blood/oxygen

That causes nerve dysfunction.

---

### What patients feel

Sensory symptoms:

* numbness
* tingling
* burning pain
* loss of pain/temperature sensation

Motor symptoms can include:

* weakness
* foot deformities over time

Autonomic symptoms can include:

* gastroparesis (delayed stomach emptying)
* erectile dysfunction
* urinary retention
* orthostatic hypotension (blood pressure drops when standing)

---

### Why foot ulcers happen

If the patient cannot feel the foot well:

* small trauma goes unnoticed
* pressure keeps happening in the same spot
* wound does not heal well because diabetes also hurts blood vessels

So neuropathy + poor blood flow = diabetic foot ulcer risk.

---

## One-line intuition

High sugar slowly poisons and starves peripheral nerves, so the longest nerves to the feet get damaged first.

---

## Pearl

Diabetes is the most common cause of peripheral neuropathy in the United States, and the Step 1 buzzword is stocking-glove sensory loss.

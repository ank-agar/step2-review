## Crohn disease

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Patterns_of_Crohn%27s_Disease.svg/330px-Patterns_of_Crohn%27s_Disease.svg.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19293.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19291.jpg)

Crohn disease is a type of IBD (inflammatory bowel disease) where the immune system causes chronic inflammation anywhere in the GI tract (gastrointestinal tract), from mouth to anus.

Classic location:

* terminal ileum
* colon
* especially the ileocecal region, where the ileum meets the cecum

---

### Core idea

Crohn = patchy, deep bowel inflammation.

Patchy means:

* diseased bowel
* normal bowel
* diseased bowel again

These are called **skip lesions**.

Deep means the inflammation is **transmural**.

Transmural = through the full thickness of the bowel wall.

That is why Crohn can cause:

* fistulas
* strictures
* abscesses

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Diarrhea:** inflamed bowel cannot absorb fluid normally
* **Abdominal pain:** inflamed, narrowed bowel spasms and stretches
* **Weight loss:** chronic inflammation increases energy use and decreases nutrient absorption
* **Blood in stool sometimes:** ulcers can erode into small blood vessels

---

### Why fistulas happen

Because Crohn inflammation goes through the whole bowel wall.

So instead of staying on the surface, the ulcer can tunnel outward and connect to another structure.

A fistula = abnormal connection between two body parts.

Example:

* bowel to bowel
* bowel to bladder
* bowel to skin
* rectum to vagina

---

### Key findings

Crohn is associated with:

* skip lesions
* transmural inflammation
* cobblestone mucosa
* noncaseating granulomas
* creeping fat
* fistulas and strictures

Granuloma = organized clump of macrophages (immune cells that eat debris/pathogens).

Noncaseating = no cheesy necrotic center.

---

## One-line intuition

Crohn disease is an immune-driven, patchy, full-thickness inflammation of the gut, so it can skip around and tunnel through the bowel wall.

---

## Pearl

Crohn vs UC (ulcerative colitis):

* Crohn = mouth to anus, skip lesions, transmural inflammation
* UC = colon only, continuous inflammation, mucosa/submucosa only

So fistulas point more toward Crohn because fistulas require deep transmural damage.

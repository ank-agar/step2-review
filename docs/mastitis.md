# Mastitis

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/24468.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Breast_abscess_by_hariadhi.svg/250px-Breast_abscess_by_hariadhi.svg.png)

### Core idea

Mastitis = inflammation of breast tissue.

Most commonly it happens during breastfeeding because milk is not draining well.

Think:

* milk stasis = milk sits there
* bacteria can enter through cracked nipple skin
* inflammation builds up in the breast

---

### Classic presentation

Usually a breastfeeding patient with:

* painful, swollen, warm breast
* redness
* fever/chills
* flu-like symptoms

Why fever?

Inflamed/infected breast tissue releases cytokines (immune signaling proteins), which tell the hypothalamus (brain temperature-control center) to raise body temperature.

---

### Most common bug

Usually:

* Staphylococcus aureus

This makes sense because Staph aureus commonly lives on skin, so it can enter through small nipple cracks.

---

### Complication

If infection gets walled off, it can form a breast abscess (pocket of pus).

That is why persistent focal swelling or a fluctuant mass should make you think:

* abscess
* ultrasound
* drainage may be needed

---

## One-line intuition

Milk gets backed up, bacteria can sneak in through nipple cracks, and the breast becomes hot, red, painful, and inflamed.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1: lactational mastitis is classically due to Staph aureus, and treatment usually includes continuing milk drainage plus antibiotics like dicloxacillin or cephalexin; think MRSA (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus) coverage if risk factors or severe/recurrent infection.

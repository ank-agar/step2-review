# Otosclerosis

![Image](https://med.uth.edu/orl/wp-content/uploads/sites/68/2017/10/Ch15diag5.jpg)

![Image](https://media.springernature.com/lw685/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs13244-014-0313-9/MediaObjects/13244_2014_313_Fig1_HTML.gif)

Otosclerosis is abnormal bone remodeling in the otic capsule (bony shell around the inner ear).

Classically, it fixes the stapes (tiny stirrup-shaped middle ear bone) at the oval window (opening where sound vibrations enter the inner ear).

So the stapes cannot vibrate well.

That causes:

* progressive conductive hearing loss (hearing loss because sound cannot mechanically conduct through the middle ear)
* often bilateral hearing loss
* tinnitus (ringing in the ears)
* normal tympanic membrane (eardrum), because the problem is behind it

---

### Core idea

Sound normally travels:

ear canal -> tympanic membrane (eardrum) -> ossicles (middle ear bones) -> stapes -> oval window -> cochlea (hearing organ)

In otosclerosis:

* abnormal bone grows around the stapes footplate
* stapes gets stuck
* sound cannot efficiently enter the cochlear fluid

So this is basically:

> a mechanical transmission problem.

---

### Why it is conductive hearing loss

The cochlea may still work, but the vibration never reaches it well.

Think:

* sensorineural hearing loss (inner ear/nerve problem) = bad sensor
* conductive hearing loss = sound cannot get to the sensor

Otosclerosis usually starts as conductive hearing loss because the stapes is immobilized.

If disease spreads into the cochlea, it can cause mixed hearing loss (conductive + sensorineural hearing loss).

---

### Classic associations

Often seen in:

* young to middle-aged adults
* women
* family history
* may worsen during pregnancy

Can be associated with osteogenesis imperfecta (genetic collagen disorder causing brittle bones and blue sclerae).

---

### Exam findings

Because it is conductive hearing loss:

* Weber test lateralizes to the affected ear
* Rinne test shows bone conduction > air conduction on the affected side

Audiogram can show a Carhart notch (dip in bone conduction around 2 kHz).

Sometimes there is a Schwartze sign (reddish-pink hue behind the tympanic membrane from vascular otosclerotic bone).

---

## One-line intuition

Otosclerosis = the stapes gets cemented in place, so sound vibrations cannot pass efficiently into the inner ear.

---

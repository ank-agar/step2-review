### Core idea

A corneal abrasion is a scratch or loss of the corneal epithelium (outer surface layer of the clear front part of the eye).

Think:

* cornea = clear window of the eye
* abrasion = scraped-off surface cells

So it is basically a “scratched clear window.”

---

### Why it hurts so much

The cornea has tons of sensory nerves from CN V1 (ophthalmic branch of cranial nerve V, the trigeminal nerve).

So even a tiny epithelial defect causes:

* severe eye pain
* foreign body sensation
* tearing
* photophobia (light sensitivity)
* red eye

---

### How you see it

Use fluorescein dye (green-yellow dye used to highlight epithelial defects).

Normal intact epithelium does not hold much dye.

But when epithelium is scraped off:

* dye collects in the defect
* it glows green under blue light

That is why corneal abrasions look bright green with fluorescein staining.

---

### Common causes

* fingernail scratch
* contact lens trauma
* foreign body under the eyelid
* rubbing the eye after dust/sand exposure
* paper or makeup brush injury

---

## One-line intuition

The cornea is a super-innervated clear windshield, so a tiny surface scratch feels dramatic and lights up with fluorescein dye.

---

## Pearl

Contact lens-associated corneal abrasions are higher risk because of Pseudomonas (gram-negative rod that can infect the cornea), so they need antipseudomonal antibiotic coverage and closer follow-up.

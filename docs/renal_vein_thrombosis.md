Renal vein thrombosis is a blood clot inside the renal vein.

Break it down:

* renal = kidney
* vein = vessel draining blood away from an organ
* thrombosis = clot formation inside a blood vessel

So renal vein thrombosis means:

> blood can enter the kidney, but it has trouble draining out.

That causes venous congestion, meaning blood backs up in the kidney.

---

### Core idea

The renal vein normally drains blood from the kidney into the IVC (inferior vena cava, the big vein returning blood to the heart).

If a clot blocks that vein:

* blood backs up in the kidney
* kidney pressure rises
* kidney becomes swollen/congested
* blood may leak into urine

That is why patients can get:

* flank pain
* hematuria (blood in urine)
* proteinuria (protein in urine)
* decreased kidney function

---

### Biggest Step 1 association

Classic cause:

* nephrotic syndrome

Why?

In nephrotic syndrome, the kidney leaks proteins into urine.

One important protein lost is antithrombin III, which normally helps prevent clotting.

So:

* lose antithrombin III
* less natural anticoagulation
* hypercoagulable state
* increased risk of renal vein thrombosis

Hypercoagulable means the blood is more likely to clot than normal.

---

### Why this can cause pulmonary embolism

The renal vein drains into the IVC.

So a clot can break off and travel:

renal vein → IVC (inferior vena cava) → right heart → pulmonary arteries

That causes PE (pulmonary embolism, clot in the lung arteries).

---

## One-line intuition

Renal vein thrombosis is kidney outflow obstruction by a clot, classically from nephrotic syndrome because urinary loss of antithrombin III makes the patient hypercoagulable.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Membranous nephropathy is the nephrotic syndrome classically associated with renal vein thrombosis.

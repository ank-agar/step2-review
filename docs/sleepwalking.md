# Sleepwalking

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Fases_del_sue%C3%B1o.svg/1280px-Fases_del_sue%C3%B1o.svg.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/9903.jpg)

Sleepwalking = a **parasomnia** (abnormal behavior during sleep) where someone gets up/walks/does activities while not fully awake.

Medical name:

* somnambulism

---

### Core idea

Sleepwalking happens during **NREM sleep** (non-rapid eye movement sleep), especially **N3 slow-wave sleep** (deep sleep).

So the brain is kind of split:

* motor system = active enough to move
* consciousness/memory = still asleep

That is why the person may walk around but later have little/no memory of it.

---

### Why it happens

During deep NREM sleep, the brain is hard to fully wake up.

If there is a partial arousal:

* body can move
* eyes may be open
* person may look confused
* behavior may be automatic
* memory formation is poor

So it is not usually "acting out a dream." That would make you think more of **REM sleep behavior disorder** (rapid eye movement sleep behavior disorder).

---

### Common triggers

More likely with anything that disrupts deep sleep:

* sleep deprivation
* stress
* fever
* alcohol/sedatives
* irregular sleep schedule

Common in kids and often goes away with age.

---

### Safety point

Do not aggressively shake/startle them awake.

Best move:

* gently guide them back to bed
* remove hazards
* lock windows/doors if needed

---

## One-line intuition

Sleepwalking is a **partial arousal from deep NREM sleep**: the body wakes up enough to move, but the thinking/memory part of the brain stays asleep.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Step 1 loves the contrast:

* **Sleepwalking/night terrors:** NREM sleep, early night, poor recall
* **Nightmares:** REM sleep, later night, vivid recall

## Placenta previa

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/2906_Placenta_Previa-02.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9d/Placental_previa.jpg)

Placenta previa = placenta implants too low in the uterus and covers or comes close to the cervical os (opening of the cervix).

Core idea:

* normal placenta = attached higher up, away from the exit
* placenta previa = placenta is sitting over the exit

So during pregnancy/labor, the cervix starts changing/opening, and the placenta can tear/bleed because it is in the way.

Classic presentation:

* painless vaginal bleeding in the 2nd or 3rd trimester
* bright red blood
* uterus is usually soft/nontender

Why painless?

Because the placenta is not ripping off from a painful uterine wall injury like placental abruption. It is bleeding because the low placental attachment is disrupted as the lower uterine segment stretches/cervix changes.

Important contrast:

* placenta previa = painless bleeding
* placental abruption = painful bleeding + tender uterus

Management idea:

* diagnose with ultrasound
* avoid digital vaginal exam until previa is ruled out, because touching the cervix/placenta can trigger major bleeding
* persistent complete previa usually needs C-section (cesarean delivery)

One-line intuition:

The placenta is blocking the baby’s exit, so cervical opening can shear placental vessels and cause painless bleeding.

HY pearl 🔥

Big risk factors are prior C-section (cesarean delivery), prior uterine surgery, multiparity (multiple prior pregnancies), and smoking.

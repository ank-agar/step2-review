### Core idea

Neutropenia means low neutrophils.

Neutrophils are white blood cells that act like the body's rapid-response bacterial and fungal infection fighters.

Clinically, we usually measure this with ANC (absolute neutrophil count).

* mild: ANC (absolute neutrophil count) 1000-1500 cells/microliter
* moderate: ANC (absolute neutrophil count) 500-1000 cells/microliter
* severe: ANC (absolute neutrophil count) less than 500 cells/microliter

---

### Why infections happen

Neutrophils are especially important for killing bacteria and fungi early.

So if neutrophils are low:

* bacteria/fungi are harder to contain
* infections can spread faster
* pus may be minimal because pus is mostly dead neutrophils
* fever may be the only obvious sign

That is why febrile neutropenia (fever plus low neutrophils) is treated as an emergency.

---

### Common causes

Think:

* decreased production in bone marrow
* increased destruction in blood/periphery
* medication effect

High-yield causes:

* chemotherapy
* leukemia or bone marrow infiltration
* aplastic anemia
* vitamin B12 or folate deficiency
* autoimmune destruction
* drugs like clozapine, methimazole, propylthiouracil, sulfonamides, carbamazepine

---

## One-line intuition

Neutropenia = too few neutrophil "first responders," so bacterial and fungal infections become more dangerous and may present mainly with fever.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Fever in severe neutropenia is an oncologic emergency: give broad-spectrum anti-pseudomonal antibiotics because Pseudomonas is a dangerous gram-negative rod in immunocompromised patients.

# Inguinal hernia

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17943/N01023-H.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/17075.jpg)

An inguinal hernia is when abdominal contents, usually bowel or fat, protrude through the inguinal canal.

The inguinal canal is a passage in the lower abdominal wall that carries the spermatic cord in males and the round ligament in females.

---

### Core idea

Think:

* weak spot in lower abdominal wall
* abdominal pressure pushes bowel/fat outward
* causes a groin bulge

The bulge often gets worse with:

* coughing
* straining
* lifting
* standing

because all of these increase intra-abdominal pressure (pressure inside the abdomen).

---

### Direct vs indirect

The key Step 1 anatomy landmark is the inferior epigastric vessels (blood vessels on the inner anterior abdominal wall).

* **Indirect inguinal hernia:** lateral to inferior epigastric vessels
* **Direct inguinal hernia:** medial to inferior epigastric vessels

Mnemonic:

* **MDs don’t LIe**
* **M**edial = **D**irect
* **L**ateral = **I**ndirect

---

### Why indirect can enter the scrotum

Indirect hernias follow the normal path of testicular descent:

* deep inguinal ring
* inguinal canal
* superficial inguinal ring
* sometimes into scrotum

So it uses an already-made tunnel.

Direct hernias push straight through a weak abdominal wall area called Hesselbach triangle (region bordered by rectus abdominis muscle, inguinal ligament, and inferior epigastric vessels), so they usually do not travel as far into the scrotum.

---

## One-line intuition

An inguinal hernia is bowel/fat being pushed through the groin canal because pressure finds a weak spot.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Indirect inguinal hernias are usually congenital due to a patent processus vaginalis (persistent embryologic outpouching of peritoneum), while direct inguinal hernias are usually acquired from weakness in Hesselbach triangle.

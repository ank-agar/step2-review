### Core idea

Paraplegia = paralysis of both legs, usually due to damage to the spinal cord below the cervical region.

Cervical region = neck spinal cord.

So the arms are usually spared because the injury is lower than the nerves that control the arms.

---

### Why the legs are affected

Motor signals normally go:

* brain
* down the spinal cord
* out to leg muscles

If the spinal cord is damaged in the thoracic, lumbar, or sacral region:

* the brain may still make the command
* but the signal cannot pass normally to the legs
* so voluntary leg movement is lost or weakened

Thoracic = chest/back level.

Lumbar = lower back level.

Sacral = pelvis/lower spine level.

---

### What else can be affected

Depending on the level and severity, paraplegia can also cause problems with:

* sensation below the lesion
* bladder control
* bowel control
* sexual function
* spasticity

Spasticity = increased muscle tone/stiffness from loss of normal brain control over spinal reflexes.

---

### Complete vs incomplete

* Complete spinal cord injury: no motor or sensory function below the lesion
* Incomplete spinal cord injury: some motor or sensory function remains below the lesion

Lesion = damaged area.

---

## One-line intuition

Paraplegia means the "wire" from the brain to the legs is damaged, so the legs cannot receive normal movement commands.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Paraplegia usually comes from thoracic/lumbar/sacral spinal cord injury, while quadriplegia means all 4 limbs are affected and usually localizes to a cervical spinal cord injury.

Quadriplegia = paralysis of both arms and both legs.

## Barotrauma

![Image](https://dan.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/internal_ear_diagram.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/46/Alveolus_diagram.svg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/b4e3/7276275/2b4efd7f2d13/DHMJ-50-66-g01.jpg)

Barotrauma = tissue injury caused by a pressure difference.

Break it down:

* baro- = pressure
* trauma = injury

So it literally means:

> pressure injury.

---

### Core idea

Air-filled spaces in the body need to equalize pressure with the outside environment.

Common air spaces:

* middle ear
* sinuses
* lungs
* GI tract (gastrointestinal tract)

If pressure changes too fast, trapped gas expands or compresses and damages tissue.

---

### Why the injury happens

The big rule is Boyle law:

* Boyle law = gas volume increases when pressure decreases, and gas volume decreases when pressure increases

Example:

* going deeper underwater = outside pressure increases
* coming up from underwater = outside pressure decreases

So if gas is trapped:

* descent can squeeze an air space
* ascent can make trapped air expand

That stretch/squeeze is what injures tissue.

---

### Ear barotrauma

Most common type.

The middle ear is an air-filled space behind the tympanic membrane (eardrum).

It normally equalizes pressure through the Eustachian tube (tube connecting middle ear to nasopharynx, the upper throat behind the nose).

If the Eustachian tube is blocked:

* pressure cannot equalize
* tympanic membrane gets pulled inward or pushed outward
* pain, hearing loss, bleeding, or rupture can happen

Intuition:

The eardrum is like a flexible wall. If pressure is unequal on the two sides, the wall bends and gets injured.

---

### Lung barotrauma

Pulmonary barotrauma = pressure injury to lung tissue.

This can happen with:

* mechanical ventilation
* scuba ascent while holding breath
* air trapping in obstructive lung disease

If alveoli (tiny air sacs where gas exchange occurs) overexpand, they can rupture.

Air can then leak into:

* pleural space → pneumothorax (air in pleural space causing lung collapse)
* mediastinum → pneumomediastinum (air in central chest around heart/great vessels)
* subcutaneous tissue → subcutaneous emphysema (air under the skin)
* blood vessels → arterial gas embolism (air bubbles entering arteries)

---

## One-line intuition

Barotrauma is what happens when trapped air cannot equalize pressure, so it squeezes or expands until tissue gets damaged.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: pulmonary barotrauma from positive-pressure ventilation can cause pneumothorax, especially when high airway pressures overdistend alveoli.

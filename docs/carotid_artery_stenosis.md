# Carotid artery stenosis

Carotid artery stenosis = narrowing of the carotid artery, usually from atherosclerotic plaque.

Break it down:

* carotid artery = major neck artery that supplies blood to the brain
* stenosis = narrowing
* atherosclerosis = cholesterol plaque buildup in arteries

So the core idea is:

> plaque narrows the neck artery that feeds the brain.

---

### Why it matters

The narrowed carotid artery can cause brain ischemia (low blood flow/oxygen to brain tissue) by:

* reducing blood flow directly
* forming a thrombus (clot) on the plaque
* sending an embolus (traveling clot/plaque fragment) to the brain

That can cause:

* TIA (transient ischemic attack; temporary stroke-like symptoms)
* ischemic stroke (brain injury from blocked blood flow)
* amaurosis fugax (temporary painless monocular vision loss)

Monocular means one eye.

---

### Why symptoms happen

If plaque breaks off or a clot forms, it can travel into cerebral arteries (brain arteries).

Then the downstream brain area loses blood flow, so you get focal neurologic deficits, like:

* weakness on one side
* numbness
* aphasia (language impairment)
* vision loss

The symptoms are focal because each brain artery supplies a specific brain region.

---

### Diagnosis idea

Usually screened with carotid duplex ultrasound.

Duplex ultrasound = ultrasound anatomy image + Doppler flow measurement.

Why Doppler helps:

* narrow tube = faster flow through the narrowed area
* faster velocity suggests more severe stenosis

---

## One-line intuition

Carotid stenosis is basically a cholesterol-clogged neck artery that can throw clots to the brain and cause TIA/stroke.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Amaurosis fugax is a classic carotid artery stenosis clue: transient painless vision loss in one eye from retinal ischemia (low blood flow to the retina).

### Core idea

Chronic kidney disease, or **CKD** (chronic kidney disease), means the kidneys have had **long-term damage** and cannot filter blood normally.

Usually this is defined as kidney damage or low **GFR** (glomerular filtration rate) for **at least 3 months**.

GFR (glomerular filtration rate) = how much blood the kidneys filter per minute.

---

### What is actually getting damaged?

The key unit is the **nephron** (the microscopic filtering unit of the kidney).

Each nephron has:

* **glomerulus** (capillary filter)
* **tubule** (reabsorbs useful stuff and secretes waste)

In CKD, nephrons are slowly lost or scarred.

So fewer working nephrons = less filtering capacity.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **High creatinine:** damaged kidneys cannot clear creatinine well
* **Uremia** (buildup of nitrogenous waste in blood): waste products accumulate
* **Proteinuria** (protein in urine): glomerular filter gets leaky
* **Hypertension** (high blood pressure): kidneys regulate salt/water and RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system)
* **Edema** (swelling): salt and water retention
* **Anemia** (low red blood cell count): damaged kidneys make less EPO (erythropoietin), which normally stimulates red blood cell production
* **Bone disease:** kidneys cannot activate vitamin D well and cannot excrete phosphate well

---

### Big causes

Most common causes:

* diabetes mellitus
* hypertension (high blood pressure)

Diabetes damages small vessels/glomeruli by glycation and hyperfiltration stress.

Hypertension damages renal arterioles, causing nephrosclerosis (hardening/scarring of kidney vessels).

Both eventually lead to nephron loss and scarring.

---

## One-line intuition

CKD is like slowly losing kidney filters over months to years, so wastes, salt, water, acid, phosphate, and potassium become harder to control.

---

## Pearl

CKD causes **secondary hyperparathyroidism**: phosphate is retained and vitamin D activation falls, so calcium drops, which stimulates PTH (parathyroid hormone).

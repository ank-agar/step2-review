# Hypomagnesemia

Hypomagnesemia = low magnesium in the blood.

Magnesium is important for:

* stabilizing nerves/muscles
* helping potassium stay inside cells
* normal PTH (parathyroid hormone) release
* normal cardiac electrical activity

So when magnesium is low, cells become too excitable.

---

### Classic findings

Low magnesium can cause:

* tremor
* cramps
* tetany (involuntary muscle contraction from nerve hyperexcitability)
* seizures
* arrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms)
* torsades de pointes (polymorphic ventricular tachycardia where QRS complexes twist around the baseline)

---

### Why low magnesium causes low potassium

Magnesium normally helps prevent potassium wasting in the kidney.

If magnesium is low:

* kidney loses more potassium
* hypokalemia (low potassium in blood) happens
* potassium replacement may not work well until magnesium is fixed

Intuition:

> If magnesium is low, potassium keeps leaking out through the kidney.

---

### Why low magnesium causes low calcium

Severe hypomagnesemia decreases PTH (parathyroid hormone) release.

Low PTH means:

* less calcium release from bone
* less calcium reabsorption in kidney
* less vitamin D activation

So magnesium deficiency can cause hypocalcemia (low calcium in blood).

---

### Common causes

Think:

* alcohol use disorder
* diarrhea/malabsorption (poor intestinal absorption)
* diuretics (drugs that increase urination)
* PPIs (proton pump inhibitors, acid-reducing drugs)
* aminoglycosides (antibiotics that can injure kidney tubules)
* cisplatin (chemotherapy drug that can waste magnesium in urine)

---

## One-line intuition

Low magnesium makes nerves, muscles, and the heart electrically unstable, and it drags potassium and calcium down with it.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 clue: refractory hypokalemia (low potassium that will not correct) = check magnesium, because potassium often will not normalize until hypomagnesemia is treated.

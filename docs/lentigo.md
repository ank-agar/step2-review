### Core idea

A **lentigo** is a flat brown macule (small, flat skin spot) caused by increased melanin (skin pigment) and often increased melanocytes (melanin-producing cells) along the basal layer of the epidermis (bottom layer of the outer skin).

Plural = **lentigines**.

---

### What it looks like

Usually:

* flat
* brown/tan/black
* sharply bordered
* surrounded by normal-looking skin

The most common type is **solar lentigo**:

* “sun spot”
* “age spot”
* caused by chronic UV (ultraviolet) exposure
* common on face, hands, shoulders, and arms

---

### Why it happens

UV light stimulates melanocytes to make more melanin.

So the skin gets a localized pigment deposit:

* more melanin in basal keratinocytes (main epidermal skin cells)
* melanocytes may be increased
* but they usually spread in a line along the basal layer

Key contrast:

* **lentigo:** melanocytes line up along the basal layer
* **nevus/mole:** melanocytes often form nests/clusters

---

### One-line intuition

A lentigo is basically a localized “extra pigment spot,” often from years of sun exposure.

---

### Important distinction

Most lentigines are benign (not cancer), but a changing or irregular pigmented lesion can mimic melanoma (malignant tumor of melanocytes).

Concerning features:

* asymmetry
* irregular border
* multiple colors
* rapid change
* bleeding or ulceration

---

## Pearl 🔥

**Lentigo maligna** is melanoma in situ (melanoma confined to the epidermis) on chronically sun-damaged skin, classically on the face of older adults.

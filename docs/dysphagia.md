## Dysphagia

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK541071/bin/Swallowing-01.jpg)

![Image](https://live.staticflickr.com/4/7731493_e142b12381_o.jpg)

### Core idea

Dysphagia = difficulty swallowing.

It means food/liquid has trouble moving from:

* mouth
* pharynx (throat)
* esophagus (food tube)
* stomach

So the problem can be **high up** in the throat or **lower down** in the esophagus.

---

### Two big types

**Oropharyngeal dysphagia** = trouble starting a swallow.

Think:

* coughing/choking
* nasal regurgitation
* aspiration (food/liquid entering airway)
* stroke or neuromuscular disease

Why?

Swallowing needs coordinated nerves/muscles to close the airway and push food down. If that coordination fails, food can go toward the trachea (windpipe) instead of the esophagus.

---

**Esophageal dysphagia** = food feels stuck after swallowing.

Think:

* “food stuck in chest”
* obstruction
* motility problem (movement problem)

Key pattern:

* solids only → mechanical obstruction, like stricture (narrowing) or cancer
* solids + liquids from the start → motility disorder, like achalasia

---

### Why solids vs liquids matters

If only solids get stuck, the esophagus is physically narrowed.

Liquids can still pass through a small opening, but solid food cannot.

If both solids and liquids get stuck, the issue is usually movement.

The esophagus is not pushing properly, so even liquid has trouble going down.

---

### Important related terms

* odynophagia = painful swallowing
* aspiration = food/liquid entering the airway
* achalasia = lower esophageal sphincter (LES, muscle ring at bottom of esophagus) fails to relax
* GERD = gastroesophageal reflux disease, acid reflux from stomach into esophagus

---

## One-line intuition

Dysphagia is a “transport problem” where food either cannot start safely or cannot move smoothly down the esophagus.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Progressive dysphagia to **solids first, then liquids** is concerning for esophageal cancer because a growing mass gradually narrows the lumen (inside opening).

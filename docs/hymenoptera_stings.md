Hymenoptera stings = stings from:

* bees
* wasps
* yellow jackets
* hornets
* fire ants

The big Step 1 idea is:

> venom can cause local inflammation, but dangerous reactions are usually IgE-mediated type I hypersensitivity.

IgE = immunoglobulin E, the antibody involved in allergies.

Type I hypersensitivity = immediate allergic reaction caused by mast cell degranulation.

---

### What happens locally

The insect injects venom into skin.

Venom triggers:

* pain
* redness
* swelling
* itching

Why?

Venom and immune cells release mediators like histamine.

Histamine causes:

* vasodilation = blood vessels widen, causing redness/warmth
* increased vascular permeability = vessels leak fluid, causing swelling
* nerve irritation = pain and itching

So the local bump is basically:

> venom + histamine making vessels leaky and nerves angry.

---

### Why anaphylaxis can happen

If the person is sensitized, their immune system has IgE already stuck on mast cells.

On the next sting:

* venom allergen binds IgE
* IgE cross-links on mast cells
* mast cells degranulate
* histamine/leukotrienes/prostaglandins are released

That causes systemic effects:

* urticaria = hives
* angioedema = deeper swelling, often lips/face/airway
* bronchospasm = airway smooth muscle constriction
* hypotension = low blood pressure from vasodilation/fluid leakage

This is anaphylaxis.

---

### Management intuition

For a simple local sting:

* remove stinger if present
* cold compress
* antihistamines
* pain control

For anaphylaxis:

* epinephrine immediately

Epinephrine helps because it:

* constricts blood vessels, raising blood pressure
* relaxes bronchial smooth muscle, improving breathing
* decreases further mast cell mediator release

---

### Bee vs wasp clue

Honeybees often leave a barbed stinger in the skin.

Wasps/yellow jackets/hornets usually do not leave the stinger, so they can sting repeatedly.

---

## One-line intuition

Hymenoptera stings are usually local venom inflammation, but the Step 1 danger is IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation causing anaphylaxis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Venom immunotherapy can prevent future severe reactions in patients with true Hymenoptera sting allergy.

Think:

> recurrent sting anaphylaxis risk goes down with controlled exposure/desensitization.

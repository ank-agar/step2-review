## Dyspareunia

![Image](https://www.nva.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/vulvar_anatomy.jpg)

![Image](https://www.physio-pedia.com/images/thumb/0/02/Pelvic_Floor_Muscles.jpg/400px-Pelvic_Floor_Muscles.jpg)

### Core idea

Dyspareunia means pain with sexual intercourse.

Think of it by location:

* superficial pain = vulva/vagina/entry problem
* deep pain = pelvic organ problem

---

### Why it happens

Superficial causes:

* vulvovaginitis (inflammation/infection of vulva and vagina)
* atrophic vaginitis (thin dry vaginal mucosa from low estrogen)
* vaginismus (involuntary pelvic floor muscle spasm)
* vestibulodynia (pain at the vaginal vestibule)

Deep causes:

* endometriosis (endometrial-like tissue outside uterus)
* pelvic inflammatory disease
* ovarian cysts
* pelvic adhesions (scar tissue)

The reasoning is simple:

* dry/inflamed tissue hurts with friction
* pelvic floor spasm makes penetration painful
* deep pelvic disease hurts when organs are moved

---

## One-line intuition

Dyspareunia = pain with sex; superficial pain points to vulvovaginal/pelvic floor causes, deep pain points to pelvic organ disease.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Postmenopausal dyspareunia is commonly from genitourinary syndrome of menopause (low-estrogen thinning/dryness of vulvovaginal tissue).

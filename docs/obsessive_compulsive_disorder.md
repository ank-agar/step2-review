Obsessive-compulsive disorder, or OCD (obsessive-compulsive disorder), is a disorder with:

* obsessions = intrusive, unwanted thoughts/images/urges
* compulsions = repetitive behaviors or mental acts done to reduce anxiety

Classic examples:

* obsession: "My hands are contaminated."
* compulsion: washing hands repeatedly
* obsession: "I left the stove on."
* compulsion: checking the stove over and over

---

### Core idea

OCD is not just liking things clean or organized.

It is a loop:

* intrusive thought appears
* anxiety rises
* compulsion temporarily lowers anxiety
* brain learns, "that ritual saved me"
* the obsession comes back stronger

So the compulsion gives short-term relief but reinforces the cycle long-term.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Think of OCD as the brain getting stuck on a false alarm.

The thought feels threatening even if the person knows it is irrational.

That is why OCD is usually ego-dystonic, meaning the thought feels unwanted and inconsistent with the person's real values.

The main circuit involved is the cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical loop:

* cortex = thought/appraisal
* striatum/basal ganglia = habit and action gating
* thalamus = relay back to cortex

If this loop is overactive or poorly gated, the brain has trouble saying:

> "This is checked. Move on."

---

### Treatment intuition

First-line treatment:

* CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy), especially ERP (exposure and response prevention)
* SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors)

ERP means:

* expose the person to the trigger
* prevent the compulsion
* let anxiety fall on its own

This teaches the brain that the feared outcome does not happen even without the ritual.

SSRIs help because serotonin signaling modulates anxiety and OCD-related circuits.

Clomipramine is also effective; it is a TCA (tricyclic antidepressant) with strong serotonin reuptake inhibition.

---

## One-line intuition

OCD is a false-alarm brain loop where compulsions briefly relieve anxiety but train the obsession to keep returning.

---

## HY pearl

OCD is ego-dystonic, while OCPD (obsessive-compulsive personality disorder) is ego-syntonic, meaning the person sees their perfectionism/control as correct or part of who they are.

Also, in kids, sudden OCD symptoms after group A strep infection can suggest PANDAS (pediatric autoimmune neuropsychiatric disorders associated with streptococcal infections).

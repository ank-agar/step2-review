Small cell lung cancer, or **SCLC (small cell lung cancer)**, is an aggressive lung cancer strongly associated with smoking.

It is called "small cell" because under the microscope the tumor cells look:

* small
* dark blue
* tightly packed
* very little cytoplasm (cell fluid outside the nucleus)
* nuclei molding into each other

Think:

> tiny crowded blue cells that grow and spread fast.

---

### Core idea

SCLC is a **neuroendocrine tumor**.

Neuroendocrine means the cancer cells can behave partly like nerve/hormone-secreting cells.

That is why SCLC can cause **paraneoplastic syndromes**.

Paraneoplastic syndrome = symptoms caused by tumor-secreted substances, not just by the tumor mass itself.

---

### Classic associations

* **Smoking:** strongest association
* **Central/hilar lung mass:** near the bronchi (large airways)
* **Early metastasis:** spreads early, so surgery is usually not the main treatment
* **Chemotherapy/radiation sensitive:** responds initially, but prognosis is still poor

---

### Why symptoms happen

* **Cough/hemoptysis (coughing blood):** tumor grows near bronchi and irritates/bleeds into airways
* **Shortness of breath:** tumor blocks airflow or lung function
* **Weight loss/fatigue:** cancer creates systemic inflammation and high energy use
* **SVC syndrome (superior vena cava syndrome):** central tumor compresses the superior vena cava, causing face/neck swelling

---

### High-yield paraneoplastic syndromes

SCLC can secrete hormone-like substances:

* **ADH (antidiuretic hormone)** → **SIADH (syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion)** → hyponatremia (low blood sodium)
* **ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone)** → Cushing syndrome (high cortisol state)
* Antibodies against presynaptic calcium channels → Lambert-Eaton syndrome (proximal muscle weakness that improves with use)

---

## One-line intuition

Small cell lung cancer is the smoking-related, central, neuroendocrine lung cancer that spreads early and causes paraneoplastic syndromes.

---

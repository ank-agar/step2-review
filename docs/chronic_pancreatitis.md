## Chronic pancreatitis

Chronic pancreatitis is long-term inflammation of the pancreas that causes irreversible damage.

The pancreas slowly becomes:

* fibrotic, meaning scarred
* calcified, meaning calcium deposits/stones form
* atrophic, meaning shrunken
* poorly functional

---

### Core idea

Repeated pancreatic injury turns the pancreas into a scarred, calcified gland.

So it cannot do its two big jobs well:

* exocrine function = making digestive enzymes
* endocrine function = making hormones like insulin

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Epigastric pain radiating to the back:** the pancreas is retroperitoneal, meaning behind the abdominal cavity, so pain often goes to the back
* **Pain worse after meals:** eating stimulates pancreatic enzyme secretion, which increases pressure/pain in a scarred pancreatic duct
* **Steatorrhea:** fatty, foul-smelling stool because low pancreatic lipase means fat is not digested
* **Weight loss:** malabsorption, meaning poor nutrient absorption
* **Diabetes mellitus:** loss of islet cells, meaning insulin-producing endocrine cells

---

### Classic imaging findings

Think:

* pancreatic calcifications
* dilated pancreatic duct
* atrophic pancreas

On CT (computed tomography), calcifications look like bright white spots in the pancreas.

---

### Causes to know

Common causes/risk factors:

* chronic alcohol use
* smoking
* recurrent acute pancreatitis
* cystic fibrosis
* obstruction of the pancreatic duct
* genetic mutations like PRSS1 (cationic trypsinogen gene), SPINK1 (serine protease inhibitor Kazal type 1 gene), and CFTR (cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator gene)

Reasoning:

If trypsin gets activated too early, it digests the pancreas itself. Repeated autodigestion causes inflammation, then fibrosis, then loss of pancreatic function.

---

## One-line intuition

Chronic pancreatitis = the pancreas repeatedly injures itself until it becomes scarred, calcified, and unable to make enough enzymes or insulin.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In chronic pancreatitis, amylase and lipase can be normal because the pancreas is so burned out that it may not have enough functioning cells left to leak enzymes.

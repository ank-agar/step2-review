### Core idea

Anoxic brain injury = brain damage from **no oxygen** reaching brain tissue.

Break it down:

* **anoxic** = no oxygen
* **hypoxic** = low oxygen
* **ischemic** = low blood flow

So anoxic brain injury usually happens after things like:

* cardiac arrest
* drowning
* choking/asphyxiation
* severe respiratory failure
* prolonged hypotension (low blood pressure)

---

### Why neurons die

Neurons are very oxygen-hungry.

No oxygen means:

* oxidative phosphorylation stops
* ATP (adenosine triphosphate, the cell's energy currency) drops
* Na+/K+ ATPase (sodium-potassium pump) fails
* Na+ (sodium) enters cells
* water follows sodium
* cells swell and get injured

Then calcium enters too, which causes:

* glutamate release
* excitotoxicity (overstimulation that damages neurons)
* free radical injury
* apoptosis/necrosis (programmed cell death/cell rupture)

---

### Why the brain is especially vulnerable

The brain uses a lot of oxygen but stores very little energy.

So if oxygen delivery stops, neurons run out of ATP fast.

Most vulnerable areas:

* hippocampus
* cerebral cortex
* basal ganglia
* cerebellar Purkinje cells

These areas have high metabolic demand, so they are hit early when oxygen is low.

---

### Clinical picture

Can cause:

* coma
* seizures
* memory problems
* cognitive impairment
* movement problems
* brain edema (swelling)

MRI (magnetic resonance imaging), especially DWI (diffusion-weighted imaging), can show restricted diffusion from injured neurons.

---

## One-line intuition

Anoxic brain injury is basically **global neuronal energy failure**: no oxygen -> no ATP -> ion pumps fail -> neurons swell, overfire, and die.

---

## Pearl 🔥

After global hypoxia/ischemia, the **hippocampal CA1 neurons** are classically very vulnerable, so memory problems are common.

### Core idea

Multifocal atrial tachycardia, or **MAT (multifocal atrial tachycardia)**, is an irregular fast rhythm where multiple different spots in the atria fire as pacemakers.

Break it down:

* multifocal = many foci, or many firing spots
* atrial = from the atria, the upper heart chambers
* tachycardia = fast heart rate

So MAT means:

> many atrial pacemaker spots are competing, causing a fast irregular rhythm.

---

### What you see on ECG

On **ECG (electrocardiogram)**:

* heart rate > 100/min
* irregularly irregular rhythm
* at least 3 different P-wave shapes
* variable PR intervals
* narrow QRS complexes usually

P wave = atrial depolarization, meaning electrical activation of the atria.

Why different P-wave shapes?

Each atrial focus sends electricity through the atria in a different direction, so each one creates a different-looking P wave.

---

### Classic association

MAT is classically associated with:

* **COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease)**
* hypoxemia (low blood oxygen)
* theophylline toxicity
* electrolyte problems, especially low potassium or low magnesium

Why COPD causes it:

* low oxygen irritates atrial cells
* lung disease can strain/stretch the right atrium
* beta-agonists/theophylline can increase automaticity

Automaticity = heart cells firing on their own.

---

### MAT vs atrial fibrillation

Both can be irregularly irregular.

Key difference:

* MAT: visible P waves with at least 3 different shapes
* **AFib (atrial fibrillation):** no organized P waves

So if you see irregularly irregular rhythm + multiple weird P waves, think MAT.

---

## One-line intuition

MAT is like the atria have multiple backup pacemakers all firing chaotically, but still making visible P waves.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, MAT is the arrhythmia to associate with severe COPD, and treatment is mainly correcting the underlying problem, especially oxygenation and electrolytes, not cardioversion.

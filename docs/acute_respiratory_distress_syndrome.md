# Acute respiratory distress syndrome

Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) = severe inflammatory lung injury causing **diffuse alveolar damage**.

Alveoli (tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens) become:

* leaky
* flooded with protein-rich fluid
* lined by hyaline membranes (dead cell debris + protein)
* stiff and hard to inflate

So the patient gets **severe hypoxemia** (low blood oxygen) that does not fully correct with oxygen.

---

### Core idea

ARDS is basically:

> damaged alveolar-capillary barriers causing “wet, stiff lungs.”

Alveolar-capillary barrier = the thin wall between alveolar air and capillary blood.

When that barrier breaks:

* fluid leaks into alveoli
* oxygen cannot diffuse well
* alveoli collapse
* lungs become less compliant

Compliance = how easily the lung stretches.

Low compliance = stiff lungs.

---

### Common triggers

Big Step 1 causes:

* sepsis
* pneumonia
* aspiration
* pancreatitis
* trauma
* massive transfusion
* burns
* inhalational injury

Intuition:

The trigger causes a huge inflammatory response, neutrophils (acute inflammatory white blood cells) rush in, and they damage the lung barrier.

---

### Why oxygen is low

Normally:

* air enters alveoli
* blood passes next to alveoli
* oxygen moves from air into blood

In ARDS:

* alveoli are filled with fluid/debris
* blood still flows past them
* but oxygen cannot enter that blood well

This creates a right-to-left shunt.

Right-to-left shunt = blood goes from right heart to left heart without getting properly oxygenated.

That is why oxygen can be hard to fix even if you give extra oxygen.

---

### Classic findings

* acute dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* tachypnea (fast breathing)
* hypoxemia (low blood oxygen)
* bilateral pulmonary infiltrates on chest x-ray
* normal pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (suggests not left heart failure)

Pulmonary infiltrates = white/fluffy areas on x-ray from fluid/inflammation in the lung.

---

### ARDS vs cardiogenic pulmonary edema

Both can look like “wet lungs.”

But:

* ARDS = leaky capillaries from inflammation
* cardiogenic pulmonary edema = backup pressure from left heart failure

So in ARDS, the pulmonary capillary wedge pressure is normal because the problem is permeability, not high hydrostatic pressure.

Hydrostatic pressure = pressure pushing fluid out of vessels.

---

### Treatment intuition

Use:

* low tidal volume ventilation
* PEEP (positive end-expiratory pressure)

Tidal volume = amount of air delivered per breath.

PEEP (positive end-expiratory pressure) = pressure left in the lungs at the end of exhalation to keep alveoli open.

Why low tidal volume?

Because ARDS lungs are stiff and fragile, so big breaths can over-stretch alveoli and worsen injury.

Why PEEP?

Because it helps keep flooded/collapsing alveoli open so more surface area can participate in gas exchange.

---

## One-line intuition

ARDS = inflammatory damage makes alveoli leaky and stiff, so oxygen cannot cross into blood.

---

## HY pearl

ARDS causes **diffuse alveolar damage with hyaline membranes**, and the key distinction from cardiogenic pulmonary edema is **normal pulmonary capillary wedge pressure**.

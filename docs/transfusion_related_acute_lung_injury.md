### Core idea

Transfusion-related acute lung injury (TRALI) is acute lung injury that happens during or within 6 hours after a blood transfusion.

It causes sudden:

* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* hypoxemia (low blood oxygen)
* bilateral pulmonary infiltrates (fluid-like opacities in both lungs on chest X-ray)
* noncardiogenic pulmonary edema (fluid in lungs not caused by heart failure)

---

### Why it happens

Donor blood can contain antibodies against the recipient's white blood cells.

The big ones are:

* anti-HLA antibodies (antibodies against human leukocyte antigen, a cell-identification protein)
* anti-HNA antibodies (antibodies against human neutrophil antigen, proteins on neutrophils)

These antibodies activate neutrophils (acute inflammation white blood cells) in the lung capillaries.

Then neutrophils damage the pulmonary endothelium (blood vessel lining in the lungs).

That makes the capillaries leaky, so fluid enters the alveoli (air sacs).

So the patient cannot oxygenate well.

---

### Intuition

Think:

> transfusion triggers neutrophils in lung vessels -> capillary leak -> alveoli fill with fluid -> sudden hypoxia.

It is basically an ARDS-like reaction.

ARDS (acute respiratory distress syndrome) means diffuse inflammatory lung injury with leaky alveolar-capillary membranes.

---

### How to recognize it

Classic test stem:

* patient gets transfusion
* within 6 hours: respiratory distress
* chest X-ray shows bilateral infiltrates
* no signs of volume overload

This last part matters because it separates TRALI from TACO.

TACO (transfusion-associated circulatory overload) is pulmonary edema from too much volume/heart strain.

TRALI = immune lung injury.

TACO = volume overload.

---

### Why no heart failure signs?

Because TRALI is not from increased hydrostatic pressure (fluid pushed out because pressure is too high).

It is from increased vascular permeability (vessels become leaky because they are inflamed/damaged).

So wedge pressure is usually normal.

Pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) estimates left atrial pressure and helps assess cardiogenic pulmonary edema.

---

## One-line intuition

TRALI is a transfusion-triggered neutrophil attack in the lungs that makes capillaries leaky and floods alveoli with fluid.

---

## HY pearl

TRALI is classically associated with plasma-rich blood products, especially from multiparous female donors, because pregnancy can expose the donor to fetal antigens and lead to anti-HLA antibody formation.

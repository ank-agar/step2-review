# Carotid artery dissection

### Core idea

Carotid artery dissection = a tear in the inner lining of the carotid artery.

The carotid artery is a major neck artery that supplies blood to the brain.

When the inner layer tears, blood enters the wall of the artery and splits the wall layers apart.

That creates:

* a narrowed true lumen (normal blood channel)
* a false lumen (new abnormal channel inside the artery wall)
* possible clot formation

---

### Why it causes stroke

The tear exposes injured vessel wall, so platelets and clotting factors stick there.

That can cause stroke in 2 main ways:

* the artery narrows, so less blood reaches the brain
* clot breaks off and embolizes (travels downstream) to brain arteries

So the big danger is ischemic stroke, meaning brain injury from reduced blood flow.

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

* unilateral neck pain or headache
* transient ischemic attack, or TIA (temporary stroke-like neurologic symptoms)
* ischemic stroke
* partial Horner syndrome

Horner syndrome = ptosis (droopy eyelid), miosis (small pupil), and anhidrosis (decreased sweating).

In carotid dissection, the sympathetic fibers running along the internal carotid artery can get irritated, causing Horner syndrome.

---

### Common causes

Can be:

* traumatic, especially neck hyperextension/rotation
* spontaneous, sometimes associated with connective tissue disease

Connective tissue disease means the vessel wall is weaker than usual, so it is easier for the artery layers to split.

Examples:

* Ehlers-Danlos syndrome
* Marfan syndrome
* fibromuscular dysplasia

---

### Imaging

CTA (computed tomography angiography) or MRA (magnetic resonance angiography) can show:

* tapered narrowing
* intimal flap, meaning the torn inner lining
* false lumen
* occlusion, meaning the vessel is blocked

---

## One-line intuition

The carotid artery wall splits open, blood tracks into the wall, the lumen narrows, and clot can shoot into the brain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Carotid artery dissection is an important cause of stroke in young or middle-aged patients, especially when stroke symptoms come with unilateral neck pain/headache or Horner syndrome.

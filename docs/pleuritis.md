# Pleuritis

### Core idea

Pleuritis, also called pleurisy, is inflammation of the pleura.

The pleura is the thin lining around the lungs:

* visceral pleura = layer stuck to the lung
* parietal pleura = layer lining the chest wall
* pleural space = tiny lubricated space between them

---

### Why it hurts with breathing

Normally, the pleural layers slide smoothly when you breathe.

In pleuritis, inflammation makes them irritated/rough, so breathing causes the layers to rub against each other.

That causes:

* sharp, stabbing chest pain
* worse pain with inspiration (breathing in)
* worse pain with coughing or sneezing
* sometimes a pleural friction rub, meaning a scratchy sound from inflamed pleura rubbing

---

### Important causes

Common causes include:

* viral infection
* pneumonia
* pulmonary embolism (PE; blood clot in the lung arteries)
* autoimmune disease, like systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE; autoimmune disease that can inflame serosal linings) or rheumatoid arthritis (RA; autoimmune joint disease)
* malignancy (cancer)
* pneumothorax (air in the pleural space causing lung collapse)

---

## One-line intuition

Pleuritis is basically "sandpaper pleura": inflamed lung lining rubs during breathing, causing sharp pleuritic chest pain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

The parietal pleura is pain-sensitive, but the visceral pleura is not.

So pleuritic chest pain mainly comes from inflammation irritating the parietal pleura.

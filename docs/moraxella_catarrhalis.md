Moraxella catarrhalis is a gram-negative diplococcus.

Break that down:

* gram-negative = stains pink/red on Gram stain because it has a thin peptidoglycan cell wall
* diplo- = pairs
* coccus = round bacteria

So Moraxella catarrhalis is basically:

> a pink/red pair-forming round bacterium that likes the upper respiratory tract.

---

### What it causes

Classic infections:

* otitis media (middle ear infection), especially in children
* sinusitis (sinus infection)
* bronchitis or pneumonia, especially in patients with COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease)

The big Step 1 vibe:

> kid with ear infection, or older COPD patient with respiratory infection.

---

### Why it causes ear infections

Moraxella normally hangs out in the nasopharynx (the upper throat behind the nose).

The eustachian tube connects the nasopharynx to the middle ear.

So if bacteria move upward through that tube:

* bacteria enter the middle ear
* inflammation builds up
* fluid/pus accumulates behind the tympanic membrane (eardrum)
* patient gets ear pain, fever, and sometimes decreased hearing

That is why upper respiratory bacteria commonly cause otitis media.

---

### How to recognize it in micro

Important lab clues:

* gram-negative diplococci
* oxidase positive
* catalase positive
* grows on blood/chocolate agar
* “hockey puck” colonies

Hockey puck colonies means the colony can be pushed across the agar intact, instead of smearing apart.

---

### Treatment idea

Many Moraxella catarrhalis strains make beta-lactamase.

Beta-lactamase = enzyme that breaks beta-lactam antibiotics like penicillin/amoxicillin.

So plain amoxicillin may fail.

That is why amoxicillin-clavulanate can work:

* amoxicillin kills bacteria
* clavulanate inhibits beta-lactamase

---

## One-line intuition

Moraxella catarrhalis is a gram-negative paired coccus from the upper airway that climbs into the ear/sinuses or worsens COPD lungs.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Moraxella catarrhalis is one of the classic causes of otitis media, along with Streptococcus pneumoniae and Haemophilus influenzae.

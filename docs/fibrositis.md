### Core idea

Fibrositis is an older term for fibromyalgia.

Break it down:

* fibro- = fibrous tissue/connective tissue
* -itis = inflammation

So the old idea was:

> inflammation of fibrous tissue causing muscle/body pain.

But that name is misleading because fibromyalgia usually does **not** have true inflammation.

Modern idea:

* widespread musculoskeletal pain
* fatigue
* poor sleep
* tenderness
* normal inflammatory labs

Musculoskeletal means involving muscles, bones, joints, tendons, and ligaments.

---

### Why the pain happens

The main problem is thought to be abnormal pain processing in the CNS (central nervous system = brain and spinal cord).

Think:

> the pain volume knob is turned up.

So normal signals from muscles/tendons can feel painful even without tissue damage.

This is called central sensitization (increased sensitivity of pain pathways in the brain/spinal cord).

That explains why patients can have:

* diffuse pain
* tender points
* fatigue
* sleep problems
* normal ESR/CRP

ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate) and CRP (C-reactive protein) are inflammation markers.

---

### Important distinction

Fibrositis/fibromyalgia is **not** like polymyositis or rheumatoid arthritis.

Those are inflammatory/autoimmune diseases.

Fibromyalgia has pain and tenderness, but no muscle destruction.

So you usually see:

* normal CK (creatine kinase = muscle breakdown enzyme)
* normal strength on exam
* normal inflammatory markers

Weakness is usually from pain/fatigue, not true muscle fiber damage.

---

## One-line intuition

Fibrositis is the old name for fibromyalgia: widespread pain from an over-sensitive pain-processing system, not actual inflamed fibrous tissue.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 clue: fibromyalgia = chronic widespread pain + fatigue/sleep disturbance + normal labs; treat with graded exercise, CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy), and meds like SNRIs (serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors) or pregabalin.

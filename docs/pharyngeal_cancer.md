Pharyngeal cancer = cancer of the pharynx (throat tube behind the nose/mouth that carries air and food).

The pharynx has 3 parts:

* nasopharynx = behind the nose
* oropharynx = behind the mouth, includes tonsils/base of tongue
* hypopharynx = lower pharynx, near larynx (voice box) and esophagus (food tube)

Most pharyngeal cancers are SCC (squamous cell carcinoma), meaning cancer from squamous cells (flat lining cells).

---

### Core idea

Think:

> lining cells of the throat become malignant and form a mass.

That mass can cause:

* trouble swallowing because it narrows the food/air passage
* sore throat or ear pain because nearby nerves refer pain to the ear
* hoarseness if it affects the larynx (voice box) or nearby structures
* neck mass because head/neck cancers often spread to cervical lymph nodes (neck lymph nodes)

---

### Biggest associations

Depends on location:

* nasopharyngeal cancer: EBV (Epstein-Barr virus)
* oropharyngeal cancer: HPV (human papillomavirus), especially HPV-16
* hypopharyngeal cancer: smoking + alcohol

Why?

* viruses like EBV/HPV push cells toward uncontrolled growth
* smoking/alcohol chronically damage mucosa (lining tissue), so mutations accumulate

---

## One-line intuition

Pharyngeal cancer is basically a malignant throat-lining mass; its risk factor depends on which pharynx region it starts in.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association:

* nasopharyngeal carcinoma = EBV (Epstein-Barr virus)
* oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma = HPV-16 (human papillomavirus type 16), often p16-positive (p16 is a tumor suppressor marker used as a clue for HPV-driven cancer)

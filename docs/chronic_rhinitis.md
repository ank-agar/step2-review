# Chronic rhinitis

![Image](https://www.getbodysmart.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Nasal-Mucosa-Expanded-View-1024x732.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/55/Blausen_0015_AllergicRhinitis.png)

### Core idea

Chronic rhinitis = long-term inflammation/irritation of the nasal mucosa (mucous membrane lining the nose).

It causes:

* runny nose
* nasal congestion
* sneezing
* post-nasal drip (mucus dripping down the throat)
* itchy nose, especially if allergic

---

### Break it down

* rhino- = nose
* -itis = inflammation
* chronic = persistent or recurrent over time

So chronic rhinitis means:

> the nose lining stays irritated/inflamed for a long time.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The nasal mucosa normally warms, humidifies, and filters air using:

* mucus
* cilia (tiny hair-like structures that move mucus)
* blood vessels
* immune cells

When it is chronically irritated:

* **Congestion:** blood vessels dilate and mucosa swells, narrowing the nasal airway
* **Runny nose:** glands make extra mucus
* **Sneezing/itching:** sensory nerves in the nose get stimulated
* **Post-nasal drip:** excess mucus drains backward into the throat

---

### Main types

**Allergic rhinitis** = IgE-mediated (immunoglobulin E-mediated) reaction to allergens.

Common triggers:

* pollen
* dust mites
* animal dander
* mold

**Nonallergic rhinitis** = chronic rhinitis without an allergic immune trigger.

Common triggers:

* cold air
* smoke
* strong smells
* spicy foods
* medications
* hormonal changes

---

## One-line intuition

Chronic rhinitis is basically an overreactive, swollen, mucus-producing nose lining.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Allergic rhinitis is a type I hypersensitivity reaction, meaning allergen cross-links IgE (immunoglobulin E) on mast cells, causing histamine release.

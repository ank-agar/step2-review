### Core idea

Takotsubo cardiomyopathy is a temporary stress-induced weakening of the heart muscle.

It is also called:

* stress cardiomyopathy
* broken heart syndrome
* apical ballooning syndrome

The key finding is that the left ventricle (main pumping chamber of the heart) balloons out at the apex (tip of the heart).

---

### Why it happens

Usually after a big emotional or physical stressor, the body releases a surge of catecholamines (epinephrine/norepinephrine; sympathetic "fight-or-flight" hormones).

Those catecholamines stun the myocardium (heart muscle), especially the apical region.

So the heart muscle is not dead like in a myocardial infarction (MI; heart attack from blocked coronary blood flow), but it temporarily cannot contract well.

---

### Classic presentation

It can look exactly like acute coronary syndrome (ACS; sudden reduced blood flow to the heart):

* chest pain
* shortness of breath
* ST-segment elevation or T-wave inversion on ECG (electrocardiogram; heart electrical tracing)
* mild troponin elevation (troponin = blood marker of heart muscle injury)

But coronary angiography (imaging of coronary arteries) often shows no major obstructive coronary artery blockage.

---

### Why the ventricle looks ballooned

The basal heart segments (upper part of the left ventricle) still squeeze strongly.

The apical segments (tip/lower part of the left ventricle) are stunned and squeeze poorly.

So during systole (heart contraction), the base squeezes while the apex bulges outward, making the left ventricle look like a Japanese octopus trap called a takotsubo.

---

## One-line intuition

Takotsubo is basically a catecholamine surge stunning the heart, causing reversible apical ballooning that mimics an MI but usually lacks a blocked coronary artery.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Classic patient: postmenopausal woman after intense emotional stress, with MI-like symptoms but clean coronary arteries and reversible left ventricular dysfunction.

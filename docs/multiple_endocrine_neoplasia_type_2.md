### Core idea

Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (MEN2) is an inherited cancer syndrome where patients form tumors in multiple endocrine organs.

The big Step 1 association:

* MEN2 = RET mutation
* RET (REarranged during Transfection) = proto-oncogene
* inheritance = autosomal dominant

So the issue is:

> RET is stuck "on," so endocrine/neural crest-derived cells get too much growth signaling.

---

### Main tumors

MEN2 classically involves:

* medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), from parafollicular C cells
* pheochromocytoma, from adrenal medulla chromaffin cells
* parathyroid hyperplasia/adenoma, mainly in MEN2A

Medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) secretes calcitonin, because parafollicular C cells normally make calcitonin.

Pheochromocytoma secretes catecholamines (epinephrine/norepinephrine), causing episodic:

* hypertension
* headache
* sweating
* palpitations

---

### MEN2A vs MEN2B

MEN2A:

* medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)
* pheochromocytoma
* parathyroid hyperplasia/adenoma

MEN2B:

* medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)
* pheochromocytoma
* mucosal neuromas
* marfanoid habitus

Marfanoid habitus means a tall, thin body type with long limbs, like Marfan syndrome.

Mucosal neuromas are benign nerve growths on mucosal surfaces, especially lips/tongue.

---

### Why these tumors group together

RET is important in development and growth of neural crest-derived tissues.

Neural crest cells contribute to:

* thyroid parafollicular C cells
* adrenal medulla chromaffin cells
* parts of the peripheral/enteric nervous system

So a germline activating RET mutation can hit multiple related tissues at once.

That is why MEN2 gives:

* C-cell tumor = medullary thyroid carcinoma
* adrenal medulla tumor = pheochromocytoma
* nerve tumors = mucosal neuromas in MEN2B

---

## One-line intuition

MEN2 is a RET gain-of-function syndrome where neural crest/endocrine tissues overgrow, especially thyroid C cells and adrenal medulla.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Always rule out pheochromocytoma before thyroid surgery in MEN2, because manipulating a catecholamine-secreting tumor can trigger a hypertensive crisis.

### Core idea

**Chlamydophila pneumoniae** is now usually called **Chlamydia pneumoniae**.

It is an **obligate intracellular bacterium** (a bacterium that must live inside host cells) that causes:

* atypical pneumonia
* bronchitis
* pharyngitis/sinusitis

Think:

> respiratory Chlamydia, not sexually transmitted Chlamydia.

---

### Why it is "atypical"

It causes **atypical pneumonia** (pneumonia with milder symptoms and less obvious lobar consolidation than classic bacterial pneumonia).

Symptoms are often:

* gradual onset
* dry cough
* low-grade fever
* sore throat/hoarseness

This is similar to **Mycoplasma pneumoniae**, so it can look like "walking pneumonia."

---

### Life cycle

Chlamydia has 2 important forms:

* **EB (elementary body):** infectious form
* **RB (reticulate body):** replicating form

Intuition:

* **EB enters the cell**
* EB turns into **RB**
* RB multiplies inside the cell
* RB turns back into EB
* EBs leave and infect more cells

So:

> EB = enters/begins infection  
> RB = replicates

---

### Why treatment is different

Because it lives **inside cells**, you need antibiotics that enter cells well:

* macrolides
* tetracyclines

**Beta-lactams** (cell wall synthesis inhibitors) are not useful because Chlamydia has a weird cell wall structure and is intracellular.

---

## One-line intuition

**Chlamydophila pneumoniae is an intracellular respiratory Chlamydia that causes atypical "walking" pneumonia using EB infectious bodies and RB replicating bodies.**

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember the atypical pneumonia bugs:

* **Mycoplasma pneumoniae**
* **Chlamydophila pneumoniae**
* **Legionella pneumophila**

These are treated with **macrolides** because they are intracellular/atypical organisms, not classic lobar pneumonia bugs.

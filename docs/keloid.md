A **keloid** is an overgrown scar where the body makes too much collagen after skin injury.

The key feature:

* it grows **beyond the original wound borders**

That is what separates it from a **hypertrophic scar**:

* hypertrophic scar = raised scar that stays inside the original wound
* keloid = raised scar that spreads outside the original wound

---

### Core idea

Normally, after a cut or acne lesion, fibroblasts make collagen to patch the wound.

Fibroblasts = cells that make connective tissue/collagen.

In a keloid, that wound-healing response does not shut off normally, so collagen keeps piling up.

So the scar becomes:

* raised
* firm/rubbery
* shiny
* itchy or painful sometimes
* larger than the original injury

---

### Why it happens

Think:

> too much repair tissue after skin damage.

Skin injury triggers inflammation and fibroblast activation.

Fibroblasts then deposit collagen.

If collagen production is excessive and disorganized, the scar grows outward and becomes a keloid.

Common triggers:

* acne
* piercings
* surgery
* burns
* cuts
* vaccinations

---

### Common locations

Keloids classically occur on:

* earlobes
* chest/sternum
* shoulders
* upper back
* jawline/neck

These are areas where skin tension or repeated irritation can make abnormal scarring more likely.

---

### Treatment intuition

Treatment tries to reduce fibroblast activity and flatten the excess collagen.

Common options include:

* intralesional corticosteroid injections
* silicone gel/sheets
* pressure therapy
* cryotherapy
* laser therapy
* surgical removal plus additional therapy

Intralesional means injected directly into the lesion.

Important: surgery alone can make keloids recur, sometimes even larger, because cutting the skin is another injury.

---

## One-line intuition

A keloid is a scar where the body over-heals and keeps laying down collagen past the original wound edge.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 distinction:

* **Hypertrophic scar:** excess collagen, but stays within wound borders
* **Keloid:** excess collagen that extends beyond wound borders and tends not to regress

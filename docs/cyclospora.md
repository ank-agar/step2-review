Cyclospora is a protozoan parasite (single-celled eukaryotic parasite) that causes cyclosporiasis, a diarrheal illness.

The important species is:

* Cyclospora cayetanensis

It infects the small intestine and classically causes:

* watery diarrhea
* bloating/cramps
* nausea
* fatigue
* weight loss

---

### Core idea

Cyclospora is a food/water-borne parasite that causes prolonged watery diarrhea after eating contaminated produce or drinking contaminated water.

Classic contaminated foods include:

* raspberries
* basil
* cilantro
* lettuce/other fresh produce

---

### Why direct fecal-oral spread is not typical

Cyclospora is passed in stool as an unsporulated oocyst.

Oocyst = hardy egg-like parasite form.

Unsporulated = not mature/infectious yet.

It needs time in the environment to sporulate, meaning mature into the infectious form.

So:

* freshly passed stool oocyst = not infectious yet
* environmental maturation = becomes infectious
* contaminated food/water = transmission

This is different from Cryptosporidium, which can be infectious right away.

---

### Diagnosis

Diagnose with stool testing.

Important lab clues:

* oocysts are acid-fast variable
* oocysts autofluoresce under UV (ultraviolet) light
* PCR (polymerase chain reaction) stool testing can detect it

Acid-fast variable means some oocysts stain pink/red, while others may stain weakly or not at all.

---

### Treatment

Treatment is:

* TMP-SMX (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole)

Intuition:

Cyclospora is a coccidian parasite (group of protozoa that form oocysts), and TMP-SMX blocks folate metabolism, which helps stop parasite replication.

---

## One-line intuition

Cyclospora = contaminated produce/water parasite that must mature outside the body before causing prolonged watery diarrhea.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Cyclospora causes watery diarrhea like Cryptosporidium, but Step 1 loves the distinction that Cyclospora oocysts are not immediately infectious when passed in stool.

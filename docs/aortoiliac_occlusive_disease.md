## Aortoiliac occlusive disease

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/51e3/11962307/8fe082550d62/gr2.jpg)

Aortoiliac occlusive disease means blockage/narrowing of the:

* distal abdominal aorta (main artery in the belly)
* iliac arteries (big pelvic arteries that send blood to the legs)

Usually due to atherosclerosis (cholesterol plaque buildup in arteries).

---

### Core idea

Blood cannot flow well from the aorta into the legs.

So patients get lower-body ischemia (low oxygen delivery from poor blood flow).

Classic symptoms:

* buttock/thigh claudication (crampy exercise pain from poor blood flow)
* weak or absent femoral pulses (groin pulses)
* erectile dysfunction

This classic triad is called Leriche syndrome.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Buttock/thigh claudication:** the blockage is high up, before blood reaches the pelvis and legs
* **Absent femoral pulses:** little blood reaches the femoral arteries (main thigh arteries)
* **Erectile dysfunction:** internal iliac artery flow (pelvic blood flow) is reduced
* **Cold legs / ulcers / gangrene in severe cases:** tissue is not getting enough oxygenated blood

Think:

> high blockage = both legs and pelvis lose inflow.

---

### Diagnosis

Often diagnosed with:

* ABI (ankle-brachial index; ankle blood pressure compared with arm blood pressure)
* Doppler ultrasound (sound-wave blood flow test)
* CTA (computed tomography angiography; CT scan with contrast to see arteries)
* angiography (contrast dye study of blood vessels)

ABI is low because ankle pressure drops when leg blood flow is blocked.

---

### Treatment idea

Treatment is about restoring flow:

* risk-factor control: stop smoking, statins, antiplatelet therapy
* endovascular stenting (opening the artery from inside with a metal scaffold)
* bypass surgery if severe/complex

---

## One-line intuition

Aortoiliac occlusive disease is basically atherosclerosis clogging the aortic fork, so the legs and pelvis do not get enough blood.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Leriche syndrome = claudication + impotence + decreased femoral pulses due to aortoiliac occlusion.

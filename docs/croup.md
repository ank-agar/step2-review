## Croup

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK431070/bin/CroupNew.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/12/Croup_steeple_sign.jpg)

![Image](https://laryngopedia.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/ln51020204-2.jpg)

Croup is viral laryngotracheobronchitis (inflammation of the larynx, trachea, and bronchi).

Classic presentation:

* barking cough
* inspiratory stridor (high-pitched sound during breathing in from upper airway narrowing)
* hoarseness
* low-grade fever
* usually in young kids

Most common cause:

* parainfluenza virus

---

### Core idea

Croup causes swelling below the vocal cords.

That area is called the subglottic region (the airway just below the vocal cords).

In kids, this region is already narrow, so even a little swelling makes airflow turbulent.

That turbulence gives:

* stridor
* harsh barking cough

---

### Why the cough sounds “barky”

The swollen upper airway acts like a tight tube.

Air gets forced through a narrowed larynx/trachea, creating a loud, seal-like cough instead of a normal cough.

---

### X-ray finding

AP (anteroposterior, front-to-back) neck x-ray can show:

* steeple sign

Steeple sign = narrowing of the upper trachea that looks like a church steeple.

But diagnosis is usually clinical, meaning based on symptoms/exam rather than needing imaging.

---

### Treatment idea

Main treatment:

* corticosteroids (anti-inflammatory drugs), like dexamethasone

Severe cases:

* nebulized epinephrine (inhaled epinephrine), which quickly shrinks airway swelling

---

## One-line intuition

Croup is a viral infection that swells the narrow airway just below the vocal cords, causing barking cough + inspiratory stridor.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Croup = steeple sign.

Epiglottitis (infection/inflammation of the epiglottis, the flap over the airway) = thumbprint sign, drooling, toxic appearance, and tripod positioning.

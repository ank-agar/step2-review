# Horner syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Oculosympathetic_pathway.jpg/960px-Oculosympathetic_pathway.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/37/Miosis.jpg/1280px-Miosis.jpg)

Horner syndrome = damage to the sympathetic pathway going to the eye/face.

Sympathetic = "fight or flight" nervous system.

Classic triad:

* ptosis (droopy eyelid)
* miosis (small/constricted pupil)
* anhidrosis (decreased sweating)

All are usually on the same side as the lesion.

---

### Why the findings happen

Sympathetics normally help open the eye, dilate the pupil, and cause sweating.

So if you lose sympathetics:

* **Ptosis:** loss of superior tarsal muscle/Muller muscle tone, so the eyelid droops a little
* **Miosis:** loss of iris dilator muscle, so the parasympathetic system wins and the pupil constricts
* **Anhidrosis:** loss of sympathetic input to sweat glands, so sweating decreases

The key intuition:

> Horner syndrome = the eye loses its "fight-or-flight" look.

No sympathetic drive means no wide-open eyelid, no dilated pupil, and less sweating.

---

### Important causes

The sympathetic pathway is long, so lesions can happen in many places:

* brainstem stroke, especially lateral medullary syndrome
* spinal cord lesion
* Pancoast tumor (apical lung tumor at the top of the lung)
* carotid artery dissection (tear in the carotid artery wall)
* neck trauma/surgery

Pancoast tumor is high yield because it can compress the sympathetic chain near the lung apex.

---

## One-line intuition

Horner syndrome is ipsilateral sympathetic loss to the face/eye, causing ptosis + miosis + anhidrosis.

---

## Pearl

Painful acute Horner syndrome should make you think **carotid artery dissection**, especially with neck pain or headache.

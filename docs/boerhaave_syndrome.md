### Core idea

Boerhaave syndrome is a **full-thickness esophageal rupture** after forceful vomiting or retching.

Full-thickness means the tear goes through the whole wall of the esophagus, not just the inner lining.

---

### Why it happens

During violent vomiting:

* pressure inside the esophagus suddenly shoots up
* the upper esophageal sphincter may not relax
* the trapped pressure rips the esophageal wall

Most commonly, the tear is in the **left posterolateral distal esophagus** near the stomach.

Posterolateral means back-and-side. Distal esophagus means the lower part of the esophagus close to the stomach.

---

### What it causes

The esophagus normally carries swallowed material safely into the stomach.

If it ruptures, saliva, food, acid, and bacteria can leak into the mediastinum.

Mediastinum means the central chest space between the lungs.

This can cause:

* severe chest pain after vomiting
* pneumomediastinum, meaning air in the mediastinum
* pleural effusion, meaning fluid around the lung
* subcutaneous emphysema, meaning air under the skin
* mediastinitis, meaning inflammation/infection of the mediastinum
* sepsis/shock if untreated

---

### Classic clue

Mackler triad:

* vomiting
* severe chest pain
* subcutaneous emphysema

But the full triad is not always present, so the biggest clue is:

> severe chest pain after forceful vomiting.

---

### Boerhaave vs Mallory-Weiss

Boerhaave syndrome:

* full-thickness tear
* causes mediastinitis/pneumomediastinum
* surgical emergency

Mallory-Weiss syndrome:

* mucosal tear only
* causes hematemesis, meaning vomiting blood
* usually after retching

So:

> Mallory-Weiss = bleeding tear. Boerhaave = whole-wall rupture.

---

## One-line intuition

Boerhaave syndrome is the esophagus “bursting” from pressure after violent vomiting, letting GI contents leak into the chest.

---

## Pearl 🔥

If a vignette says **vomiting followed by severe chest pain + air in the mediastinum**, think **Boerhaave syndrome**, not myocardial infarction (MI, heart attack).

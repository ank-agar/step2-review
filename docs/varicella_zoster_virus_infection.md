Varicella-zoster virus (VZV) is a herpesvirus that causes two main diseases:

* primary infection = varicella, aka chickenpox
* reactivation = herpes zoster, aka shingles

Think:

> first infection spreads everywhere, later reactivation follows one nerve.

---

### Core idea

VZV first infects through the respiratory tract, spreads through the blood, and causes a diffuse itchy vesicular rash.

After that, the virus does not fully leave.

It hides in sensory nerve ganglia (clusters of sensory neuron cell bodies), especially dorsal root ganglia (sensory ganglia near the spinal cord).

Years later, if cell-mediated immunity (T-cell immune control) drops, the virus can reactivate and travel down a sensory nerve to the skin.

That causes shingles: a painful vesicular rash in a dermatomal distribution (skin area supplied by one sensory nerve root).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Diffuse chickenpox rash:** primary VZV enters blood, so lesions appear all over the body
* **Lesions in different stages:** new crops keep appearing, so you see macules, papules, vesicles, and crusts together
* **Shingles pain:** reactivated virus inflames the sensory nerve
* **One-sided band-like rash:** one sensory ganglion/reactivated nerve root is involved, so the rash follows one dermatome
* **Postherpetic neuralgia:** nerve damage/inflammation can leave persistent pain after the rash resolves

---

### Classic Step 1 associations

VZV is:

* enveloped
* double-stranded DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid)
* herpesvirus family
* latent in dorsal root ganglia

Treatment is usually acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir.

These drugs inhibit viral DNA polymerase (enzyme that copies viral DNA).

---

## One-line intuition

VZV is the virus that gives you chickenpox first, then hides in nerves and can come back later as shingles.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Shingles usually does **not cross the midline** because one dorsal root ganglion/reactivated sensory nerve supplies one unilateral dermatome.

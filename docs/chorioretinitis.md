Chorioretinitis = inflammation of the:

* choroid
* retina

Break the word apart:

* chorio- = choroid, the vascular layer that feeds the retina
* retin- = retina, the light-sensing layer
* -itis = inflammation

So chorioretinitis means:

> inflammation of the blood-supply layer + the seeing layer in the back of the eye.

---

### Core idea

The retina sits right next to the choroid, so inflammation often spreads between them.

That is why you may see the related terms:

* choroiditis = mainly choroid inflammation
* retinitis = mainly retina inflammation
* chorioretinitis = both are involved

---

### Why vision gets affected

The retina is basically the camera sensor of the eye.

If it gets inflamed:

* photoreceptors cannot transmit light signals normally
* the macula may swell or scar
* inflammatory cells can cloud the vitreous, the gel inside the eye

So symptoms can include:

* blurry vision
* floaters
* decreased vision
* scotoma, meaning a blind spot
* sometimes eye pain or redness if broader uveitis is present

---

### Common causes

High-yield infectious causes:

* Toxoplasma gondii, especially classic for focal chorioretinitis
* CMV (cytomegalovirus), especially in immunocompromised patients
* syphilis
* tuberculosis

Noninfectious autoimmune/inflammatory causes can also do it.

---

## One-line intuition

Chorioretinitis is when inflammation hits the back-wall layers of the eye, damaging the retina directly and/or its blood supply from the choroid.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Toxoplasma chorioretinitis classically causes a fluffy white retinal lesion with overlying vitreous inflammation, described as a “headlight in the fog.”

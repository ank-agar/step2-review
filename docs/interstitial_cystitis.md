# Interstitial cystitis

Interstitial cystitis is also called **IC/BPS (interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome)**.

It is chronic bladder pain/pressure with urinary symptoms, but **no obvious infection**.

Break the words apart:

* interstitial = involving the tissue spaces/wall
* cystitis = bladder inflammation

So the idea is:

> irritated, painful bladder wall causing urgency and frequency even when there is no UTI (urinary tract infection).

---

### Classic symptoms

* suprapubic pain (pain above the pubic bone, where the bladder sits)
* urinary frequency
* urinary urgency
* nocturia (waking up at night to urinate)
* pain that often worsens as the bladder fills and improves after urination

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Think of the bladder lining as a protective waterproof barrier.

In IC/BPS, that barrier may be dysfunctional, so urine irritates the bladder wall.

That causes:

* **pain:** irritated bladder sensory nerves fire more easily
* **urgency:** the bladder feels “full/angry” even with small volumes
* **frequency:** the patient voids often to avoid painful filling
* **sterile urine:** symptoms look like UTI, but cultures are usually negative

---

### Important finding

Some patients have **Hunner lesions**.

Hunner lesions are inflammatory ulcer-like patches in the bladder mucosa (inner lining).

They are not present in everyone, but when present they strongly support IC/BPS.

---

## One-line intuition

Interstitial cystitis is basically a chronically hypersensitive, irritated bladder that acts infected even when there is no infection.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, think:

**chronic bladder pain + urgency/frequency + negative urine culture = interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome.**

### Core idea

A **duodenal tear** is a rip in the wall of the duodenum, which is the first part of the small intestine right after the stomach.

If the tear goes all the way through the wall, it becomes a **perforation** (full-thickness hole), so intestinal contents can leak out.

---

### Why it matters

The duodenum carries:

* acidic stomach contents
* bile
* pancreatic enzymes

So if it tears, those irritating digestive fluids can leak into surrounding spaces and cause inflammation/infection.

That can lead to:

* severe abdominal pain
* peritonitis (inflammation of the abdominal lining)
* sepsis (dangerous whole-body inflammatory response to infection)
* retroperitoneal air/fluid if the tear is in the retroperitoneal part of the duodenum

Retroperitoneal means behind the peritoneum, which is the lining of the abdominal cavity.

---

### Common causes

* blunt abdominal trauma, like handlebar injury or car crash
* penetrating trauma, like stabbing or gunshot wound
* peptic ulcer disease, especially duodenal ulcer
* endoscopy/ERCP injury

ERCP (endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography) is a scope procedure used to access the bile and pancreatic ducts.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

If digestive contents leak:

* acid irritates tissue → sudden severe pain
* bacteria leak out → infection risk
* air escapes bowel → free air on imaging
* pancreatic enzymes/bile irritate nearby tissues → inflammation around pancreas/duodenum

Intuition:

The duodenum is like a high-pressure chemical pipe right after the stomach. If it rips, caustic digestive fluid leaks where it should not be.

---

### Imaging clue

CT (computed tomography) may show:

* free air
* retroperitoneal air
* duodenal wall thickening
* fluid around the duodenum
* contrast leak if oral contrast was used

The key Step-style idea is:

* intraperitoneal perforation → peritonitis/free air under diaphragm
* retroperitoneal duodenal tear → air/fluid behind the peritoneum, symptoms can be more subtle

---

## One-line intuition

A duodenal tear is a rip in the first part of the small intestine that can leak acid, bile, air, and enzymes into the abdomen or retroperitoneum.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Most of the duodenum is **retroperitoneal**, so a duodenal tear can cause retroperitoneal air rather than obvious free intraperitoneal air.

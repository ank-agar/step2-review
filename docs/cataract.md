### Core idea

A cataract is clouding/opacification of the lens of the eye.

The lens normally should be clear so light can pass through and focus on the retina (light-sensitive layer in the back of the eye).

If the lens gets cloudy:

* less light reaches the retina
* light scatters in random directions
* vision becomes blurry, hazy, or glare-sensitive

---

### Why it happens

The lens is made of organized transparent proteins.

With aging, UV (ultraviolet) light, diabetes, steroids, trauma, or congenital infections, those proteins can denature/clump.

So the intuition is:

> clear organized lens proteins become clumped/disorganized, so the lens turns cloudy.

That cloudy lens acts like a dirty windshield.

---

### Common symptoms

Patients can have:

* painless blurry vision
* glare/halos around lights
* worse night vision
* faded colors
* frequent eyeglass prescription changes

Important: cataracts are usually painless.

Painful red eye makes you think of other problems, like acute angle-closure glaucoma (sudden increased intraocular pressure, meaning pressure inside the eye).

---

### Treatment

Definitive treatment is cataract surgery:

* remove the cloudy natural lens
* replace it with an artificial intraocular lens (IOL, lens implanted inside the eye)

---

## One-line intuition

A cataract is a foggy lens, so light gets scattered before it can form a sharp image on the retina.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Congenital cataracts can be caused by congenital rubella infection, and rubella is classically associated with cataracts + sensorineural deafness + PDA (patent ductus arteriosus, persistent fetal connection between the aorta and pulmonary artery).

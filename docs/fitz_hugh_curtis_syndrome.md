## Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome

Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome is **perihepatitis** (inflammation around the liver capsule) due to **PID** (pelvic inflammatory disease).

Key idea:

* infection starts in the genital tract
* spreads upward through the pelvis
* reaches the liver capsule/peritoneum
* causes thin scar-like adhesions between the liver and abdominal wall/diaphragm

These adhesions are called **"violin-string" adhesions** because they look like stretched strings over the liver.

---

### Why RUQ pain happens

RUQ (right upper quadrant) pain happens because the **liver capsule** gets inflamed.

Important: the liver tissue itself is usually not the main problem.

So LFTs (liver function tests) can be normal or only mildly abnormal because the inflammation is mostly on the outside covering of the liver, not deep liver parenchyma.

---

### Classic causes

Most commonly from sexually transmitted PID due to:

* **Chlamydia trachomatis**
* **Neisseria gonorrhoeae**

Think:

> PID + RUQ pain = Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome

---

### Symptoms

Can present with:

* sharp RUQ (right upper quadrant) abdominal pain
* pleuritic pain, meaning worse with breathing/coughing
* fever
* pelvic pain
* cervical motion tenderness
* vaginal discharge

It can mimic gallbladder disease, especially cholecystitis (inflammation of the gallbladder).

---

## One-line intuition

Fitz-Hugh-Curtis is basically **PID that climbed high enough to irritate the liver capsule**, making RUQ pain and violin-string adhesions.

---

## Pearl 🔥

The buzzword is **violin-string adhesions** between the liver capsule and abdominal wall/diaphragm.

If a vignette gives a young sexually active patient with PID symptoms plus RUQ pain, think **Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome**.

### Core idea

Cataplexy = sudden loss of muscle tone while awake, usually triggered by strong emotions.

Classic triggers:

* laughing
* excitement
* anger
* surprise

The key point:

* the person is conscious
* but the muscles briefly "turn off"

So it can look like:

* jaw dropping
* head nodding
* knees buckling
* slurred speech
* full collapse

---

### Why this happens

Cataplexy is basically REM sleep paralysis happening at the wrong time.

REM sleep = rapid eye movement sleep, the dream stage where the body normally becomes paralyzed so you do not act out dreams.

Normally:

* orexin keeps you awake and stabilizes muscle tone
* orexin is also called hypocretin
* orexin comes from the lateral hypothalamus

In narcolepsy type 1:

* orexin neurons are lost
* wake/REM boundaries become unstable
* emotion can trigger REM-like atonia while the person is awake

Atonia = loss of muscle tone.

So the intuition is:

> emotion flips on the REM paralysis circuit, but consciousness stays awake.

---

### Important distinction

Cataplexy is not the same as fainting or a seizure.

* Cataplexy: conscious, sudden muscle tone loss
* Syncope: fainting from transient low brain perfusion
* Seizure: abnormal electrical brain activity, often with altered awareness

That preserved awareness is the big clue.

---

## One-line intuition

Cataplexy is emotion-triggered REM sleep paralysis leaking into wakefulness.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Cataplexy is highly associated with narcolepsy type 1 due to loss of orexin/hypocretin neurons in the lateral hypothalamus.

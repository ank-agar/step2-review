A **subhepatic abscess** is a pocket of pus **under the liver**.

Break it down:

* **sub-** = under
* **hepatic** = liver
* **abscess** = walled-off collection of pus

So:

> pus trapped in the space below the liver.

---

### Where exactly?

Usually in the **subhepatic space**, especially near **Morison pouch**.

**Morison pouch** = the potential space between the liver and right kidney.

It matters because fluid/pus likes to collect in dependent spaces, especially when a patient is lying down.

---

### Common causes

Think infection or leakage from nearby abdominal organs:

* perforated gallbladder
* acute cholecystitis (gallbladder inflammation)
* perforated duodenal ulcer
* appendicitis with a high/subhepatic appendix
* post-op complication after abdominal surgery

Basically, bacteria spill into the peritoneal cavity (abdominal lining space), then the body walls it off into an abscess.

---

### What symptoms?

Often nonspecific:

* fever
* right upper quadrant pain
* nausea/vomiting
* leukocytosis (high white blood cell count)
* sometimes right shoulder pain

Right shoulder pain can happen because irritation near the diaphragm hits the **phrenic nerve** (nerve from C3-C5 that senses the diaphragm), which refers pain to the shoulder.

---

### Imaging idea

On CT (computed tomography), an abscess often looks like a:

* fluid collection
* rim-enhancing wall
* gas bubbles or air-fluid level sometimes
* surrounding fat stranding, meaning inflamed fat around it

---

## One-line intuition

A subhepatic abscess is an infected pus pocket hiding under the liver, often after gallbladder/duodenal/appendix infection or surgery.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, connect **RUQ (right upper quadrant) infection + fever + referred right shoulder pain** with irritation near the diaphragm via the **phrenic nerve C3-C5**.

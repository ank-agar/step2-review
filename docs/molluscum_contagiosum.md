### Core idea

Molluscum contagiosum is a contagious viral skin infection caused by MCV (molluscum contagiosum virus), which is a poxvirus.

It causes small:

* flesh-colored or pink bumps
* dome-shaped papules (small raised skin lesions)
* central umbilication (a little dimple in the middle)

So the classic buzzword is:

> pearly, umbilicated papules.

---

### Why the dimple happens

The virus infects epidermal cells (skin surface cells).

Those infected cells fill up with viral material and form a central waxy plug.

That plug pulls/opens the center of the bump, making it look like a tiny belly button.

So:

* dome = piled-up infected epidermal cells
* central dimple = central plug of viral material
* pearly look = smooth, shiny surface

---

### Transmission

It spreads by:

* direct skin-to-skin contact
* fomites (objects that carry infection), like towels
* scratching/shaving, which can spread it to nearby skin
* sexual contact in adults if lesions are genital

---

### Important associations

More common or more extensive in:

* children
* atopic dermatitis (eczema-prone skin)
* immunosuppression, especially HIV (human immunodeficiency virus)

In immunosuppressed patients, lesions can be larger, more numerous, and harder to clear.

---

## One-line intuition

Molluscum contagiosum is a poxvirus that makes smooth little skin bumps with a central viral plug, giving the classic umbilicated look.

---

## Pearl

Poxviruses are DNA viruses, but they replicate in the cytoplasm, not the nucleus.

That is high yield because most DNA viruses replicate in the nucleus.

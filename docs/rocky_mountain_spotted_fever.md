## Rocky Mountain spotted fever

Rocky Mountain spotted fever is a tick-borne infection caused by **Rickettsia rickettsii**.

Break it down:

* **Rickettsia rickettsii** = small obligate intracellular bacterium (must live inside host cells)
* **tick-borne** = transmitted by a tick bite
* **spotted fever** = fever + spotted rash

Classic Step 1 idea:

* fever
* severe headache
* myalgias (muscle aches)
* rash that starts on wrists/ankles and spreads inward
* can involve palms and soles

---

### Core mechanism

Rickettsia rickettsii infects **endothelial cells** (cells lining blood vessels).

That causes **vasculitis** (inflammation of blood vessels).

So the symptoms come from damaged, leaky vessels:

* rash = small vessel injury in skin
* petechiae (tiny red-purple spots from bleeding) = worse vascular damage
* edema (swelling) = leaky vessels
* severe disease = poor blood flow and organ damage

---

### Why the rash pattern is HY

The rash often starts at the:

* wrists
* ankles

Then spreads to:

* trunk
* palms
* soles

This palm/sole involvement is a big clue because many rashes spare palms and soles.

---

### Treatment

Treat with **doxycycline**.

Important: do not wait for the rash or lab confirmation if suspicion is high.

Why?

Because early disease may look nonspecific, but delayed treatment can become fatal quickly.

---

## One-line intuition

A tick injects Rickettsia rickettsii, which attacks blood vessel lining, causing fever plus a wrist/ankle-to-palm/sole rash from vasculitis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Rocky Mountain spotted fever is treated with **doxycycline even in children**, because the risk of untreated RMSF (Rocky Mountain spotted fever) is much worse than the tooth-staining concern.

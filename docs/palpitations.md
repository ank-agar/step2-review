# Palpitations

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9e/SinusRhythmLabels.svg)

Palpitations = feeling like your heart is:

* racing
* pounding
* fluttering
* skipping beats

Key idea:

> Palpitations are a symptom, not a diagnosis.

They just mean the patient is aware of their heartbeat.

---

### Why they happen

Normally, the sinoatrial node (SA node = sinoatrial node, the heart's normal pacemaker) fires regular electrical signals.

If the rhythm becomes too fast, irregular, or has extra early beats, the patient may feel it.

Common causes:

* anxiety/panic
* caffeine or stimulants
* hyperthyroidism (too much thyroid hormone)
* anemia (low red blood cells/hemoglobin)
* premature atrial contractions (PACs = early atrial beats)
* premature ventricular contractions (PVCs = early ventricular beats)
* atrial fibrillation (AFib = irregular atrial rhythm)

---

### Intuition

Think of the heart like a metronome.

If it suddenly speeds up, pauses, or throws in an early beat, the brain notices:

* "my heart skipped"
* "my heart is fluttering"
* "my heart is racing"

That feeling = palpitations.

---

### Why PVCs can feel like a skipped beat

A PVC (premature ventricular contraction) happens early.

Then the heart often has a short compensatory pause before the next normal beat.

So the patient may feel:

* early weak beat
* pause
* then a stronger beat

That stronger beat happens because the ventricle had extra time to fill with blood during the pause.

---

### Red flags

Palpitations are more concerning if associated with:

* syncope (fainting)
* chest pain
* shortness of breath
* known structural heart disease
* family history of sudden cardiac death

Those suggest a more dangerous arrhythmia (abnormal heart rhythm).

---

## One-line intuition

Palpitations are when the patient feels the heart's electrical rhythm doing something noticeable: too fast, irregular, or extra-beat-y.

---

## Pearl

Hyperthyroidism can cause palpitations because thyroid hormone increases beta-adrenergic sensitivity (stronger response to epinephrine/norepinephrine), so the heart becomes more "revved up."

### Core idea

Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency is an inherited lack of functional **AAT (alpha-1 antitrypsin)**.

AAT is a **protease inhibitor** (a protein that blocks tissue-digesting enzymes).

Its main job:

* protect the lungs from **neutrophil elastase** (an enzyme from neutrophils that breaks down elastin)

So if AAT is low:

* elastase is unchecked
* alveolar walls get destroyed
* emphysema develops early

---

### Why lungs get damaged

Think:

> AAT is the brake. Elastase is the scissors.

Normally:

* neutrophils release elastase during inflammation
* AAT blocks elastase
* alveoli stay intact

In AAT deficiency:

* not enough AAT reaches the lung
* elastase keeps cutting elastin
* alveoli lose support and merge into larger air spaces

That causes **emphysema** (destruction/enlargement of air spaces distal to terminal bronchioles).

Classic Step 1 pattern:

* early-onset emphysema
* especially in lower lobes
* worse with smoking

---

### Why liver gets damaged

The liver makes AAT.

In the common severe mutation, the AAT protein misfolds and gets stuck inside hepatocytes (liver cells).

So there are 2 problems:

* lungs: too little AAT in blood -> elastase damages alveoli
* liver: misfolded AAT accumulates -> hepatocyte injury -> cirrhosis

This is why the same disease can cause:

* emphysema
* neonatal hepatitis/jaundice
* cirrhosis
* hepatocellular carcinoma (primary liver cancer)

---

### Histology

Liver biopsy classically shows:

* **PAS-positive, diastase-resistant globules**

PAS (periodic acid-Schiff) stains carbohydrates/glycoproteins.

Diastase is an enzyme that digests glycogen.

So if the globules are still there after diastase:

> they are not just glycogen; they are retained abnormal AAT protein.

---

## One-line intuition

AAT deficiency = no protection from elastase in the lung, plus misfolded protein buildup in the liver.

---

## Pearl

AAT deficiency causes **panacinar emphysema** (whole acinus affected), classically worse in the **lower lobes**, unlike smoking-related centriacinar emphysema, which is classically worse in the upper lobes.

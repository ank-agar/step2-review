### Core idea

Malocclusion means a bad bite: the upper and lower teeth do not line up normally when the jaw closes.

Break it down:

* mal- = bad/abnormal
* occlusion = how the teeth come together

So:

> abnormal relationship between the upper and lower dental arches.

---

### Why it matters

If the teeth do not meet correctly, force gets distributed abnormally.

That can cause:

* trouble chewing
* abnormal tooth wear
* speech issues
* jaw pain/TMJ problems (temporomandibular joint problems)
* harder cleaning, increasing caries risk (tooth decay risk)

---

### Common patterns

* **Overjet:** upper front teeth project too far forward horizontally
* **Deep bite/overbite:** upper teeth overlap lower teeth too much vertically
* **Open bite:** upper and lower teeth do not touch in front or back
* **Underbite/Class III:** lower teeth/jaw sit too far forward
* **Crossbite:** upper teeth bite inside the lower teeth
* **Crowding:** not enough arch space, so teeth overlap/rotate

---

### Angle classification

This is the classic orthodontic classification based on molar relationship:

* **Class I:** normal molar relationship, but teeth may still be crowded/rotated
* **Class II:** mandible is relatively posterior, often with overjet
* **Class III:** mandible is relatively anterior, often with underbite

Mandible = lower jaw.

---

## One-line intuition

Malocclusion is when the bite is mechanically misaligned, so teeth meet in the wrong position and transmit force the wrong way.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Class II malocclusion is associated with mandibular retrognathia (lower jaw set too far back), while Class III is associated with mandibular prognathism (lower jaw set too far forward).

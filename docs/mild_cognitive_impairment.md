### Core idea

Mild cognitive impairment, or MCI (mild cognitive impairment), is:

* noticeable decline in memory or thinking
* worse than normal aging
* but not severe enough to impair independent daily function

So the key distinction is:

* **MCI:** cognition is worse, but ADLs (activities of daily living) are mostly preserved
* **dementia:** cognition is worse and daily function is impaired

---

### Why it happens

The brain areas used for memory and thinking may start working less efficiently.

Common causes include:

* early Alzheimer disease
* vascular disease, meaning small vessel damage in the brain
* depression
* sleep problems
* medications
* vitamin B12 deficiency
* hypothyroidism, meaning low thyroid hormone

That is why MCI is not automatically Alzheimer disease.

---

### Types

* **Amnestic MCI:** mainly memory problems
* **Nonamnestic MCI:** mainly non-memory problems, like language, attention, judgment, or visuospatial skills (understanding where objects are in space)

Amnestic means memory-related.

---

### Intuition

Think of MCI as the middle zone:

normal aging → mild cognitive impairment → dementia

The person is clearly more forgetful or cognitively slower than expected, but they can still handle most daily life independently.

---

### Important distinction

Forgetting where you put keys can be normal aging.

Forgetting appointments, conversations, or recent events repeatedly is more concerning for MCI.

Dementia is when the cognitive decline starts disrupting function, like managing finances, medications, cooking safely, or getting lost in familiar places.

---

## One-line intuition

MCI is abnormal cognitive decline with preserved independence.

---

## HY pearl

On USMLE-style questions, the biggest clue separating MCI from dementia is preserved ADLs (activities of daily living).

# Chordae tendineae rupture

![Image](https://anatomytool.org/sites/default/files/16795-375.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/4261/3801145/d005a3fd9ff4/gr7.jpg)

Chordae tendineae rupture = tearing of the chordae tendineae (tough fibrous "heart strings" that connect papillary muscles to atrioventricular valves).

Most Step 1 questions care about the **mitral valve**, so rupture usually causes:

* mitral valve leaflet prolapse/flail leaflet
* acute MR (mitral regurgitation)
* holosystolic murmur
* blood leaking from LV (left ventricle) back into LA (left atrium)

---

### Core idea

Chordae tendineae act like support strings that stop the AV (atrioventricular) valves from flipping backward during ventricular contraction.

If they rupture, the valve leaflet loses its anchor.

So during systole (ventricular contraction), the leaflet gets pushed backward into the atrium, causing regurgitation (backward blood flow).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Holosystolic murmur:** blood leaks backward during all of systole
* **Pulmonary edema:** blood backs up into LA, then pulmonary veins, then lungs
* **Acute shortness of breath:** sudden MR does not give the heart time to compensate
* **Flail leaflet on echo (echocardiogram/heart ultrasound):** the torn chordae no longer hold the leaflet down

---

### Common causes

* myxomatous degeneration (weak, floppy valve tissue)
* infective endocarditis (infection damaging valve/chordae)
* trauma
* ischemia/MI (myocardial infarction/heart attack), especially if papillary muscle support is affected

---

## One-line intuition

The valve's support string snaps, so the mitral leaflet flies backward and blood shoots back into the left atrium.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Only AV (atrioventricular) valves have chordae tendineae:

* mitral valve
* tricuspid valve

Semilunar valves (aortic and pulmonic valves) do **not** have chordae tendineae.

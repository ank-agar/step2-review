### Core idea

Vaginitis = inflammation/infection of the vagina.

Think:

* itching/burning
* vaginal discharge
* odor
* pain with urination or sex

The big Step 1 causes are:

* bacterial vaginosis
* Candida vulvovaginitis
* Trichomonas vaginitis

---

### How to separate them

**Bacterial vaginosis**

* thin gray-white discharge
* fishy odor
* vaginal pH (acidity level) > 4.5
* clue cells on microscopy

Why?

Normal Lactobacillus bacteria make acid and keep the vagina acidic. In bacterial vaginosis, Lactobacillus decreases and anaerobes (bacteria that grow without oxygen), especially Gardnerella vaginalis, overgrow.

So:

> less acid protection → higher pH → fishy amine odor.

---

**Candida vulvovaginitis**

* thick white "cottage cheese" discharge
* intense itching
* normal vaginal pH, usually < 4.5
* budding yeast/pseudohyphae (chain-like yeast forms) on KOH (potassium hydroxide) prep

Why?

Candida is a fungus/yeast that can overgrow when normal flora is disrupted, like after antibiotics, diabetes, pregnancy, or immunosuppression.

---

**Trichomonas vaginitis**

* frothy yellow-green discharge
* vaginal pH > 4.5
* motile flagellated protozoa (moving parasite with whip-like tails)
* strawberry cervix can happen

Why?

Trichomonas vaginalis is an STI (sexually transmitted infection), so it causes a more inflammatory vaginitis.

---

## One-line intuition

Vaginitis is vaginal irritation/inflammation, and Step 1 wants you to identify the cause by discharge + pH + microscopy.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Candida has **normal vaginal pH**, while bacterial vaginosis and Trichomonas usually have **high vaginal pH > 4.5**.

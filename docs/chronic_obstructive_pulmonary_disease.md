## Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease

![Image](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/sites/default/files/inline-images/Normal%20Lungs%20and%20Lungs%20With%20COPD.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0b/COPD.JPG)

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a chronic lung disease where air gets trapped because airflow out of the lungs is blocked.

Main forms:

* chronic bronchitis = chronic productive cough from inflamed mucus-filled airways
* emphysema = destruction of alveolar walls, causing enlarged air spaces and poor elastic recoil

Alveoli (tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens) normally stretch open and then recoil back down.

In COPD:

* airways narrow
* mucus plugs the bronchioles (small airways)
* alveolar walls are destroyed
* lungs lose elastic recoil

So the patient can breathe in, but has trouble breathing out.

That causes:

* air trapping
* hyperinflated lungs
* flattened diaphragm
* increased work of breathing
* decreased FEV1/FVC ratio

FEV1 (forced expiratory volume in 1 second) falls because the patient cannot blow air out fast.

FVC (forced vital capacity) may also fall, but FEV1 falls more, so:

> obstructive disease = low FEV1/FVC ratio

Classic cause:

* smoking

Classic symptoms:

* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* chronic cough
* sputum production
* wheezing

Why smoking causes it:

* smoke irritates airways -> inflammation + mucus -> chronic bronchitis
* smoke recruits neutrophils/macrophages -> protease release -> alveolar wall destruction -> emphysema

## One-line intuition

COPD is like old damaged lungs that cannot spring back, so air gets stuck inside.

## Pearl 🔥

Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency can cause early emphysema, especially in the lower lobes, because alpha-1 antitrypsin normally blocks elastase (an enzyme that breaks down elastin).

### Core idea

Hereditary elliptocytosis is an inherited RBC (red blood cell) membrane disorder where RBCs become:

* oval
* elliptical
* cigar-shaped

Instead of normal flexible round discs, the cells look stretched out.

---

### Why the cells become elliptical

The problem is usually in RBC cytoskeleton proteins:

* spectrin
* protein 4.1
* glycophorin C

The cytoskeleton is the internal protein scaffold that helps the RBC keep its shape.

In hereditary elliptocytosis, the horizontal connections in that scaffold are weak.

So when RBCs squeeze through tiny vessels and the spleen, they get mechanically stretched and cannot fully snap back.

Result:

* elliptocytes on peripheral smear
* sometimes hemolysis (RBC destruction)

---

### Clinical picture

Most patients are asymptomatic.

If severe, it can cause:

* hemolytic anemia (low RBC count from RBC destruction)
* jaundice from increased bilirubin (heme breakdown product)
* splenomegaly (enlarged spleen)
* pigment gallstones from chronic hemolysis

---

### Inheritance

Usually AD (autosomal dominant), meaning one mutated copy can be enough to cause disease.

---

## One-line intuition

The RBC membrane skeleton is too weak side-to-side, so red cells get stretched into ellipses as they circulate.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 contrast:

* hereditary elliptocytosis = horizontal cytoskeleton defect → elliptical RBCs
* hereditary spherocytosis = vertical membrane attachment defect → spherical RBCs

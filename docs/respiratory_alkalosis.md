## Respiratory alkalosis

### Core idea

Respiratory alkalosis = blood becomes too basic because you are breathing off too much CO2 (carbon dioxide).

Classic ABG (arterial blood gas) pattern:

* pH high
* PaCO2 (arterial carbon dioxide pressure) low
* HCO3- (bicarbonate) normal or low if chronic

So the key is:

> too much ventilation -> CO2 goes down -> pH goes up

---

### Why CO2 changes pH

CO2 acts like acid in the blood because of this equation:

CO2 + H2O <-> H2CO3 <-> H+ + HCO3-

Definitions:

* H2O = water
* H2CO3 = carbonic acid
* H+ = hydrogen ion, the acidic part
* HCO3- = bicarbonate, the basic buffer

If you hyperventilate, you blow off CO2.

Less CO2 pulls the equation left, so H+ decreases.

Less H+ means the blood is less acidic, so pH rises.

---

### Common causes

Think: anything that makes you breathe too much.

* anxiety or panic attack
* pain
* fever
* pregnancy
* high altitude
* pulmonary embolism (blood clot in lung vessels)
* sepsis (life-threatening infection with organ dysfunction)
* early salicylate toxicity (aspirin toxicity)
* overventilation on a ventilator

---

### Symptoms

Low CO2 can cause:

* lightheadedness
* tingling around mouth/fingers
* cramps or tetany (involuntary muscle contraction)
* palpitations

Why tetany happens:

Alkalosis makes albumin (blood protein) bind more calcium, so free ionized calcium decreases.

Low ionized calcium makes nerves/muscles more excitable.

---

### Compensation

The kidneys compensate by dumping HCO3- (bicarbonate).

That brings pH back down toward normal.

But kidneys are slow, so:

* acute respiratory alkalosis: high pH, low PaCO2, HCO3- not very low yet
* chronic respiratory alkalosis: HCO3- drops more because kidneys had time to compensate

---

## One-line intuition

Respiratory alkalosis is "overbreathing": you blow off acidic CO2, so the blood pH goes up.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Salicylate toxicity (aspirin toxicity) classically causes early respiratory alkalosis by directly stimulating the respiratory center, then later causes an anion gap metabolic acidosis (acid buildup with increased unmeasured anions).

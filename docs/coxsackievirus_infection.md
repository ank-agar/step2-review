### Core idea

Coxsackievirus is an **enterovirus** (virus that mainly enters through the GI tract, meaning gastrointestinal tract).

It is usually spread by:

* fecal-oral route
* respiratory droplets
* contaminated hands/surfaces

Classic association:

* hand, foot, and mouth disease
* herpangina
* myocarditis/pericarditis
* pleurodynia
* aseptic meningitis

---

### Hand, foot, and mouth disease

HFMD (hand, foot, and mouth disease) = fever + painful mouth sores + vesicular rash on hands/feet.

Vesicular means blister-like.

The name is very literal:

* hand lesions
* foot lesions
* mouth ulcers

---

### Why the findings happen

Coxsackievirus infects epithelial cells (surface-lining cells), especially in the mouth, hands, and feet.

That local cell injury + inflammation creates:

* mouth ulcers
* small blisters/vesicles
* sore throat
* fever from immune activation

So the intuition is:

> virus enters through GI/respiratory tract, spreads briefly in blood, then causes blister-like lesions in skin/mucosa.

---

### Coxsackie A vs B

**Coxsackie A** tends to cause:

* hand, foot, and mouth disease
* herpangina

Herpangina = painful vesicles/ulcers in the posterior mouth/pharynx (back of the throat area).

**Coxsackie B** tends to cause:

* myocarditis (heart muscle inflammation)
* pericarditis (inflammation of the sac around the heart)
* pleurodynia (sharp chest/abdominal muscle pain)
* aseptic meningitis (meningitis with no bacteria on routine culture)

---

## One-line intuition

Coxsackievirus is a fecal-oral enterovirus that loves mucosa/skin and sometimes muscle/heart, so it can give mouth blisters, hand/foot rash, meningitis, or myocarditis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association: Coxsackie B can cause viral myocarditis and is also associated with type 1 diabetes mellitus via pancreatic beta-cell injury.

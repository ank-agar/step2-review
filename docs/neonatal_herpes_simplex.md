### Core idea

Neonatal herpes simplex = herpes simplex virus infection in a newborn.

HSV (herpes simplex virus) can be:

* HSV-1 (herpes simplex virus type 1; usually oral herpes/cold sores)
* HSV-2 (herpes simplex virus type 2; usually genital herpes)

Most cases happen when the baby contacts infected genital secretions during delivery.

---

### Why it is dangerous

Newborns have weak cell-mediated immunity.

Cell-mediated immunity = T-cell immune defense, which is especially important for fighting viruses.

So HSV can spread fast and cause:

* vesicles on skin, eyes, or mouth
* encephalitis
* disseminated disease

Encephalitis = brain inflammation.

Disseminated disease = infection spread throughout the body, often involving liver, lungs, and brain.

---

### Classic presentations

Think 3 buckets:

* **SEM disease:** skin, eye, mouth disease
* **CNS disease:** central nervous system disease, meaning brain/spinal cord involvement
* **Disseminated disease:** widespread organ involvement

The skin finding is classically:

* grouped vesicles on an erythematous base

Vesicles = small fluid-filled blisters.

Erythematous = red.

---

### Why maternal primary infection is highest risk

Primary infection = first-time HSV infection.

If mom gets a new genital HSV infection near delivery, she has:

* high viral shedding
* not enough time to make protective IgG

IgG (immunoglobulin G) is the antibody that crosses the placenta to protect the baby.

So:

> new maternal HSV near delivery = high virus exposure + low transferred antibody.

---

### Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnose with HSV PCR (polymerase chain reaction), usually from lesions, blood, and CSF.

CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) = fluid around brain/spinal cord.

Treat immediately with IV acyclovir.

IV (intravenous) = through a vein.

Do not wait if neonatal HSV is suspected, because delay can be deadly.

---

## One-line intuition

Neonatal herpes is HSV hitting a baby before the baby has enough T-cell and maternal antibody protection, so a “skin blister virus” can become brain or whole-body disease.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember: neonatal HSV is one of the TORCH infections.

TORCH = toxoplasmosis, other infections, rubella, cytomegalovirus, herpes simplex virus.

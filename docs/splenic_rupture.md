# Splenic rupture

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Diagram_showing_the_position_of_the_spleen_CRUK_417.svg/960px-Diagram_showing_the_position_of_the_spleen_CRUK_417.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e7/CT_Spleen_Rupture.jpg)

Splenic rupture means the spleen tears open and bleeds into the abdomen.

The spleen is a very vascular organ, meaning it has a lot of blood flow. So if it ruptures, the big danger is:

* internal bleeding
* hypovolemic shock (shock from low blood volume)
* death if not treated quickly

---

### Core idea

The spleen sits in the left upper quadrant (LUQ; upper left belly), under the left ribs.

So trauma to the left ribs/left abdomen can tear it.

Classic causes:

* blunt abdominal trauma, like car crash or sports injury
* rib fracture
* infectious mononucleosis (Epstein-Barr virus infection) because it can enlarge the spleen

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **LUQ pain:** the injured spleen is in the upper left abdomen
* **Left shoulder pain:** blood irritates the diaphragm, causing referred pain through the phrenic nerve; this is called Kehr sign
* **Low blood pressure/tachycardia:** bleeding lowers circulating blood volume, so the heart compensates by beating faster
* **Peritonitis (inflamed abdominal lining):** blood in the abdomen irritates the peritoneum

---

### Diagnosis

In trauma:

* unstable patient: FAST ultrasound (Focused Assessment with Sonography in Trauma ultrasound) to look for free fluid/blood
* stable patient: CT scan (computed tomography scan) to see the splenic injury better

---

## One-line intuition

Splenic rupture = a blood-filled organ tears, so think **LUQ trauma + internal bleeding + possible left shoulder pain**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Avoid contact sports in infectious mononucleosis because Epstein-Barr virus can cause splenomegaly (enlarged spleen), which increases risk of splenic rupture.

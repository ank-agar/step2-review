Ascites = abnormal fluid buildup in the peritoneal cavity (potential space between the abdominal wall lining and abdominal organs).

Most classic cause for Step 1:

* cirrhosis (chronic liver scarring)
* causing portal hypertension (high pressure in the portal venous system draining blood from the gut to the liver)

---

### Why cirrhosis causes ascites

Cirrhosis makes blood flow through the liver difficult.

So pressure backs up in the portal venous system:

* increased hydrostatic pressure (fluid-pushing pressure) pushes fluid out into the abdomen
* low albumin (main blood protein that holds fluid inside vessels) decreases oncotic pressure (fluid-pulling pressure)
* kidney senses low effective circulating volume and activates RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system), causing sodium/water retention

More retained fluid + leaky high-pressure vessels = ascites.

---

### What it looks like clinically

Think:

* distended abdomen
* shifting dullness
* fluid wave
* shortness of breath if severe because the abdomen pushes up on the diaphragm (main breathing muscle)

---

### Important complication

Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis = infection of ascitic fluid without an obvious surgical source.

Peritonitis means inflammation/infection of the peritoneum (abdominal lining).

Classic clue:

* cirrhotic patient with ascites + fever + abdominal pain + altered mental status

---

## One-line intuition

Ascites is basically “third-spacing” of fluid into the belly because liver disease causes high portal pressure, low albumin, and kidney water retention.

---

## Pearl 🔥

SAAG (serum-ascites albumin gradient) helps identify portal hypertension:

* SAAG ≥ 1.1 g/dL suggests portal hypertension, classically cirrhosis
* SAAG < 1.1 g/dL suggests non-portal causes like malignancy (cancer) or infection

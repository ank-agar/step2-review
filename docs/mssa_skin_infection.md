## MSSA skin infection

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil///PHIL_Images/14586/14586_lores.jpg)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/media/images/Impetigosores.png)

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/staphylococcus-aureus/media/images/staphylococcus-aureus.jpg)

MSSA skin infection means a skin infection caused by **methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus**.

Break it down:

* **MSSA** = methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus
* **Staphylococcus aureus** = gram-positive cocci (round bacteria) in clusters
* **methicillin-sensitive** = treatable with normal anti-staph beta-lactam antibiotics (cell-wall antibiotics), unlike MRSA (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus)

---

### Core idea

Staph aureus likes to infect skin and soft tissue, especially when there is a break in the skin.

It commonly causes:

* folliculitis = infection of hair follicles
* furuncles/boils = deeper infected hair follicles
* carbuncles = connected group of boils
* abscess = pus-filled pocket
* impetigo = superficial crusty skin infection
* cellulitis = deeper spreading skin infection

---

### Why pus happens

Staph aureus is **pyogenic**, meaning pus-forming.

It attracts lots of neutrophils (acute bacterial infection white blood cells), so you get:

* redness
* warmth
* tenderness
* swelling
* pus/drainage

Think:

> Staph makes localized pus pockets.

---

### MSSA vs MRSA

The organism is still Staphylococcus aureus in both.

The difference is antibiotic resistance:

* **MSSA** = sensitive to methicillin-like drugs
* **MRSA** = resistant due to altered penicillin-binding protein (PBP, the bacterial cell-wall enzyme targeted by beta-lactam antibiotics)

So MSSA is generally easier to treat than MRSA.

---

### Treatment intuition

If there is an abscess, antibiotics alone may not work well because pus is walled off.

So the key treatment is often:

* incision and drainage = open it and let pus out
* plus antibiotics if severe, spreading, systemic, or high-risk

Why:

> antibiotics have trouble penetrating a closed pus pocket.

---

## One-line intuition

MSSA skin infection is Staph aureus causing a pus-forming skin infection, but it is still sensitive to standard anti-staph beta-lactam antibiotics.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Staphylococcus aureus is **coagulase-positive** and classically forms abscesses; Streptococcus pyogenes more classically causes diffuse cellulitis/erysipelas without a focal pus pocket.

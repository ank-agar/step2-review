Vitamin A toxicity = too much vitamin A in the body.

Also called:

* hypervitaminosis A

Hypervitaminosis = too much of a vitamin.

---

### Core idea

Vitamin A is fat-soluble, meaning it dissolves in fat and gets stored instead of quickly peed out.

So if you take too much preformed vitamin A over time, it builds up mainly in the liver.

Preformed vitamin A = active vitamin A forms like:

* retinol
* retinyl esters
* retinoid medications

---

### Why toxicity happens

Think:

> fat-soluble vitamin = storage problem

Water-soluble vitamins are easier to excrete in urine.

Fat-soluble vitamins like A, D, E, and K can accumulate in tissues.

Vitamin A especially builds up in hepatic stellate cells.

Hepatic = liver-related.  
Stellate cells = liver cells that normally store vitamin A.

When those storage cells get overloaded, vitamin A starts causing tissue injury.

---

### Classic symptoms

Chronic vitamin A toxicity can cause:

* headache
* nausea/vomiting
* dry, rough skin
* hair loss
* cracked lips
* bone pain
* liver injury
* hepatosplenomegaly

Hepatosplenomegaly = enlarged liver and spleen.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Headache:** vitamin A can increase intracranial pressure
* **Dry skin/cracked lips/hair loss:** retinoids strongly affect epithelial cell turnover
* **Bone pain:** excess vitamin A increases bone remodeling and can weaken bone
* **Liver injury:** vitamin A is stored in the liver, so overdose directly stresses hepatic stellate cells

Intracranial pressure = pressure inside the skull.  
Epithelial cells = cells lining surfaces like skin and mucosa.

---

### Acute vs chronic

**Acute toxicity** = huge dose over a short time.

Usually:

* nausea/vomiting
* dizziness
* blurry vision
* severe headache

**Chronic toxicity** = too much over weeks to months.

Usually:

* dry skin
* alopecia
* bone pain
* fatigue
* liver dysfunction

Alopecia = hair loss.

---

### Step 1 trap

Beta-carotene does **not** usually cause vitamin A toxicity.

Beta-carotene = plant precursor to vitamin A, like from carrots.

The body regulates conversion of beta-carotene into active vitamin A.

Too much beta-carotene can cause orange skin, called carotenemia, but not classic vitamin A toxicity.

Carotenemia = orange/yellow skin from excess carotene.

---

## One-line intuition

Vitamin A toxicity is basically: too much fat-soluble retinol gets stored in the liver, overflows the storage system, and injures skin, bone, brain pressure regulation, and liver.

---

## Pearl

Vitamin A is teratogenic.

Teratogenic = can cause birth defects.

That is why isotretinoin, a vitamin A-derived acne medication, is absolutely contraindicated in pregnancy.

Contraindicated = should not be used because the risk is too high.

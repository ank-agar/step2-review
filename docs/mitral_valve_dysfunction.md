## Mitral valve dysfunction

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4e/Blausen_0645_MitralValve_RegurgitationvsStenosis.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18148.jpg)

![Image](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/sites/default/files/inline-images/NHLBI_Fig05a_final01%20English.jpg)

Mitral valve dysfunction means the mitral valve is not doing its job correctly.

The mitral valve sits between the:

* left atrium
* left ventricle

Its normal job:

* opens during diastole (heart relaxation/filling) so blood flows left atrium -> left ventricle
* closes during systole (heart contraction/pumping) so blood does **not** go backward

---

### Main ways it can be dysfunctional

* **Mitral regurgitation:** valve does not close tightly, so blood leaks backward into the left atrium
* **Mitral stenosis:** valve is narrowed/stiff, so blood has trouble moving from left atrium to left ventricle
* **Mitral valve prolapse:** floppy valve leaflets bulge backward into the left atrium and can cause regurgitation

---

### Why symptoms happen

If the valve is **leaky**:

* blood goes backward into the left atrium
* left atrial pressure rises
* pressure backs up into pulmonary veins (lung veins)
* causes dyspnea (shortness of breath), pulmonary edema (fluid in lungs), and fatigue

If the valve is **narrow**:

* blood cannot empty easily from left atrium to left ventricle
* left atrial pressure rises
* pressure backs up into lungs
* can also cause dyspnea and pulmonary hypertension (high blood pressure in lung vessels)

---

### Murmur intuition

Mitral regurgitation:

* blood leaks backward during systole
* so it causes a **holosystolic murmur** at the apex

Mitral stenosis:

* blood struggles to pass through during diastole
* so it causes a **diastolic rumbling murmur**

---

## One-line intuition

The mitral valve is the door between the left atrium and left ventricle: dysfunction means the door is either leaky, too tight, or floppy.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Rheumatic fever classically causes **mitral stenosis** years later because immune-mediated inflammation scars and fuses the valve leaflets.

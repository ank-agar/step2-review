## SIADH

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Kidney_nephron_molar_transport_diagram.svg/960px-Kidney_nephron_molar_transport_diagram.svg.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK279055/bin/endocrin-test-siadh-Image003.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537591/bin/diagn-diabet-insip-Image003.jpg)

SIADH (syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion) = too much ADH (antidiuretic hormone, aka vasopressin) when the body should not be releasing it.

ADH normally tells the kidney:

* reabsorb free water
* concentrate the urine
* dilute the blood

So in SIADH:

* ADH is high/inappropriately active
* collecting ducts reabsorb too much water
* serum sodium gets diluted
* urine stays concentrated

---

### Core lab pattern

SIADH causes euvolemic hypotonic hyponatremia.

Break that down:

* euvolemic = no obvious edema or dehydration
* hypotonic = low serum osmolality (blood is too diluted)
* hyponatremia = low sodium in blood

Classic labs:

* low serum sodium
* low serum osmolality
* high urine osmolality
* high urine sodium

The key weird part:

> blood is diluted, but urine is still concentrated.

Normally, diluted blood should shut off ADH and make dilute urine.

In SIADH, ADH keeps acting anyway.

---

### Why sodium gets low

The body is not mainly losing sodium.

It is retaining too much water.

Extra water dilutes sodium in the blood, so measured serum Na+ falls.

Think:

> sodium amount may be near normal, but the water bucket got bigger.

---

### Why patients look euvolemic

At first, ADH causes mild volume expansion because water is retained.

But then the body compensates by increasing sodium and water excretion through natriuretic mechanisms (natriuresis = sodium excretion in urine).

So the patient ends up looking euvolemic, but still has diluted serum sodium.

That is why SIADH usually does not cause big edema.

---

### Common causes

HY causes include:

* small cell lung carcinoma (a neuroendocrine lung cancer)
* CNS (central nervous system) disease, like stroke, trauma, infection
* pulmonary disease, like pneumonia
* drugs, especially SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors), carbamazepine, cyclophosphamide

Small cell lung carcinoma is classic because it can make ectopic ADH (hormone made from the wrong tissue).

---

## One-line intuition

SIADH = ADH is acting when it should be off, so the kidney keeps water, dilutes the blood, and makes concentrated urine.

---

## Pearl 🔥

SIADH is classically associated with small cell lung carcinoma and causes euvolemic hyponatremia with inappropriately concentrated urine.

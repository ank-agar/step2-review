# Pheochromocytoma

![Image](https://nci-media.cancer.gov/pdq/media/images/739009.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/72/Adrenal_paraganglioma_clinical_Pheochromocytoma.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4f/Pheochromocytoma_high_mag.jpg)

Pheochromocytoma is a tumor of the adrenal medulla.

The adrenal medulla is the inner part of the adrenal gland that makes catecholamines (epinephrine and norepinephrine, which are “fight-or-flight” hormones).

So the core idea is:

> adrenal medulla tumor → too many catecholamines → episodic sympathetic surges

Classic symptoms:

* episodic hypertension
* headache
* sweating
* palpitations/tachycardia
* tremor or anxiety-like feeling

Why this happens:

* catecholamines stimulate alpha-1 receptors → vasoconstriction → high blood pressure
* catecholamines stimulate beta-1 receptors → increased heart rate/contractility → palpitations
* sympathetic activation → sweating, tremor, panic-like episodes

Think of it like the body randomly hitting the “fight-or-flight” button even when there is no danger.

Diagnosis is usually with:

* plasma free metanephrines
* or 24-hour urine metanephrines

Metanephrines are breakdown products of catecholamines, so they stay elevated more reliably than the short catecholamine bursts.

Histology can show Zellballen nests, meaning tumor cells arranged in small nests surrounded by supportive cells.

Important associations:

* MEN2A/MEN2B (multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A or 2B)
* VHL (von Hippel-Lindau disease)
* NF1 (neurofibromatosis type 1)

## One-line intuition

A pheochromocytoma is an adrenal medulla tumor that dumps fight-or-flight hormones, causing episodic hypertension + headache + sweating + palpitations.

## HY pearl

Always give alpha blockade before beta blockade.

Why: if you give a beta blocker first, alpha-1 vasoconstriction is left unopposed, which can cause a dangerous hypertensive crisis.

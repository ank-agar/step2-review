## Hypofibrinogenemia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c0/Coagulation_Cascade_Diagram.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e3/Blood_clot_formation.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8b/Human_fibrinogen_structural_scheme.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d7/Blood_clot_in_scanning_electron_microscopy.jpg)

Hypofibrinogenemia means **low fibrinogen**.

Fibrinogen is **factor I (clotting factor 1)**, a liver-made protein that gets converted into **fibrin (the insoluble mesh that stabilizes a clot)**.

So the core problem is:

* not enough fibrinogen
* less fibrin mesh
* weak clot
* bleeding tendency

---

### Intuition

Platelets make the first temporary plug.

Fibrin is like the “net” or “glue” that reinforces that plug.

So in hypofibrinogenemia, the platelet plug may start forming, but the final clot is unstable because there is not enough fibrinogen to turn into fibrin.

---

### Why bleeding happens

Thrombin converts:

> fibrinogen → fibrin

If fibrinogen is low, thrombin has less substrate to work on.

That means less fibrin gets made, so clots are weaker and bleeding can continue or recur.

Common bleeding patterns:

* mucosal bleeding
* easy bruising
* heavy menstrual bleeding
* bleeding after surgery/trauma
* umbilical stump bleeding in severe congenital cases

---

### Causes

Can be:

* congenital, meaning inherited
* acquired, meaning developed later

Acquired causes include:

* liver disease, because fibrinogen is made in the liver
* DIC (disseminated intravascular coagulation), where clotting factors get consumed
* massive bleeding/transfusion dilution

---

### Labs

Because fibrinogen is part of the common final clot-forming step, low fibrinogen can cause:

* increased PT (prothrombin time)
* increased PTT (partial thromboplastin time)
* increased thrombin time
* low fibrinogen level

Thrombin time is especially intuitive because it directly tests the conversion of fibrinogen to fibrin.

---

## One-line intuition

Hypofibrinogenemia = not enough factor I, so the body cannot build a strong fibrin mesh to stabilize clots.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember: fibrinogen is **factor I**, and fibrinogen problems affect the **final common pathway** of coagulation.

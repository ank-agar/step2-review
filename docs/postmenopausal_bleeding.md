Postmenopausal bleeding = any vaginal bleeding after menopause.

Menopause means no menstrual periods for 12 months because ovarian estrogen production has dropped.

So bleeding after that is abnormal until proven otherwise.

---

### Core idea

In a postmenopausal patient, the endometrium (inner lining of the uterus) should be thin and quiet.

If bleeding happens, think:

* most commonly: endometrial/vaginal atrophy
* most important to rule out: endometrial cancer

Atrophy means thinning. Low estrogen makes the vaginal and endometrial tissue thin, fragile, and easy to bleed.

---

### Why bleeding can happen

Common causes:

* **Atrophy:** low estrogen makes tissue fragile
* **Endometrial polyp:** a benign mucosal growth that can bleed
* **Endometrial hyperplasia:** excess growth of the endometrium
* **Endometrial carcinoma:** cancer of the endometrial lining
* **Hormone therapy/tamoxifen:** can stimulate or alter the endometrium
* **Cervical/vaginal lesions:** bleeding may look uterine but come from lower genital tract

---

### Why we take it seriously

Most postmenopausal bleeding is not cancer.

But endometrial cancer often presents with postmenopausal bleeding, so the symptom is a red flag.

The big question is:

> Is the endometrium thin and atrophic, or is it abnormally thick/proliferating?

Proliferating means cells are growing/dividing.

---

### Evaluation idea

Usually start with:

* transvaginal ultrasound, or
* endometrial biopsy

TVUS (transvaginal ultrasound) measures the endometrial stripe, meaning the thickness of the uterine lining.

Key cutoff:

* **less than or equal to 4 mm:** very low risk of endometrial cancer
* **greater than 4 mm:** needs endometrial sampling/biopsy

Biopsy means taking tissue to look under a microscope.

---

## One-line intuition

After menopause, the uterine lining should be thin and inactive; if it bleeds, rule out abnormal endometrial growth or cancer.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Unopposed estrogen increases risk of endometrial hyperplasia and endometrial cancer.

Unopposed estrogen means estrogen without progesterone balancing it.

Progesterone normally stabilizes and sheds the endometrium, so without it, estrogen keeps pushing the lining to grow.

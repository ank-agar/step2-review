### Core idea

C1r deficiency is a rare complement deficiency where the classical complement pathway does not start properly.

Complement = immune proteins in blood that help:

* opsonize bacteria (coat bacteria so phagocytes can eat them)
* clear immune complexes (antibody-antigen clumps)
* form MAC (membrane attack complex, a pore that lyses microbes)

---

### Where C1r fits

The C1 complex is:

* C1q
* C1r
* C1s

C1q recognizes antibody-antigen complexes.

Then C1r activates C1s.

Then C1s cleaves C4 and C2, forming C3 convertase (enzyme that activates C3).

So if C1r is missing:

> C1q can recognize the target, but the signal cannot move forward well.

---

### Why it causes disease

You get poor classical pathway activation, so:

* immune complexes are not cleared well
* apoptotic debris (dead-cell fragments) is not cleaned up well
* the immune system may start reacting against self

That is why early classical complement deficiencies can cause SLE-like disease.

SLE = systemic lupus erythematosus, an autoimmune disease with immune complex deposition.

---

### Clinical associations

Think:

* recurrent infections, especially encapsulated bacteria
* lupus-like autoimmune disease
* low classical complement function on CH50 testing

CH50 = total hemolytic complement assay, a test of the classical complement pathway.

---

## One-line intuition

C1r is an early “relay enzyme” in the classical complement pathway, so deficiency blocks immune-complex cleanup and predisposes to lupus-like disease.

---

## Pearl

Early classical pathway deficiencies, especially C1q, C1r, C1s, C2, and C4, are classically associated with SLE-like autoimmune disease because they impair immune complex clearance.

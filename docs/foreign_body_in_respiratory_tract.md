# Foreign body in respiratory tract

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5e/Respiratory_system_complete_en.svg)

![Image](https://med.libretexts.org/@api/deki/files/27713/1.png?revision=1)

![Image](https://med.libretexts.org/@api/deki/files/27711/2.png?revision=1)

A foreign body in the respiratory tract means an object got into the airway instead of the esophagus (food tube).

Common examples:

* peanut
* popcorn
* toy part
* coin
* tooth/dental object

---

### Core idea

The object blocks airflow somewhere in the:

* larynx (voice box)
* trachea (windpipe)
* bronchi (large airways going into the lungs)

If it blocks high up, it can cause choking/stridor (high-pitched upper airway sound).

If it goes deeper, it often lodges in a bronchus.

---

### Why it often goes to the right side

The right main bronchus is:

* wider
* shorter
* more vertical

So inhaled objects tend to fall into the right bronchial tree more easily.

---

### Why X-ray can look weird

Many aspirated objects are radiolucent (do not show up clearly on X-ray), like peanuts or carrots.

So you may not see the object itself.

Instead, you look for indirect signs:

* unilateral wheezing
* decreased breath sounds on one side
* hyperinflation (too much air trapped in lung)
* atelectasis (lung collapse)
* recurrent pneumonia in the same area

---

### Why hyperinflation happens

The foreign body can act like a check valve:

* air gets in during inspiration (breathing in)
* air cannot get out well during expiration (breathing out)

So air gets trapped distal to the obstruction, making that lung/lobe look overinflated.

---

## One-line intuition

An aspirated object blocks part of the airway, often the right main bronchus, causing choking first and then unilateral wheeze/air trapping.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Foreign body aspiration in a child with sudden choking + unilateral wheezing = rigid bronchoscopy for diagnosis and removal.

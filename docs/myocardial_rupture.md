Myocardial rupture means the heart muscle physically tears.

Break it down:

* myocardium = heart muscle
* rupture = tear/break

So this is literally:

> the wall or support structures of the heart tearing open.

Most Step 1 context:

* happens after MI (myocardial infarction = heart attack)
* classically around 3-7 days after MI
* due to macrophages (immune cells that clean up dead tissue) digesting the dead myocardium

---

### Why it happens after MI

After an MI, the heart muscle dies from lack of blood flow.

Then macrophages come in to clean up the dead tissue.

That sounds helpful, but it temporarily makes the wall weak because the macrophages are basically chewing through the damaged myocardium.

So:

* dead heart muscle = weak wall
* macrophage cleanup = even weaker wall
* heart keeps pumping under pressure
* weak area can tear

---

### Main types

#### 1. Free wall rupture

The outside wall of the ventricle tears.

Blood leaks into the pericardial sac (sac around the heart), causing cardiac tamponade (compression of the heart by fluid/blood).

Classic findings:

* sudden hypotension
* jugular venous distention
* muffled heart sounds
* often sudden death

Intuition:

> the heart bursts outward, so blood fills the sac around the heart and squeezes the heart shut.

---

#### 2. Ventricular septal rupture

The interventricular septum (wall between the left and right ventricles) tears.

This creates an acute VSD (ventricular septal defect = hole between ventricles).

Classic findings:

* new harsh holosystolic murmur
* left-to-right shunt
* acute heart failure

Intuition:

> blood gets forced from the high-pressure left ventricle into the lower-pressure right ventricle.

---

#### 3. Papillary muscle rupture

The papillary muscle (muscle that anchors the mitral valve through chordae tendineae) tears.

This causes acute mitral regurgitation (backflow from left ventricle to left atrium).

Classic findings:

* new systolic murmur at the apex
* pulmonary edema
* severe shortness of breath

Most commonly affected:

* posteromedial papillary muscle

Why?

* it usually has only one blood supply, often from the RCA (right coronary artery)
* the anterolateral papillary muscle has dual supply, so it is better protected

---

## One-line intuition

After an MI, macrophages soften the dead heart wall, so the pumping pressure can tear the weakened myocardium.

---

## Pearl

Post-MI rupture is classically a 3-7 day complication because that is when macrophages are actively digesting necrotic (dead) myocardium.

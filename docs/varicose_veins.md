### Core idea

Varicose veins are enlarged, twisted superficial veins.

Superficial veins = veins close to the skin.

They usually happen in the legs because leg veins have to push blood upward against gravity.

---

### Why they happen

Leg veins have one-way valves that keep blood moving:

* up the leg
* back toward the heart

If the valves become weak or damaged, blood falls backward.

This backward flow is called venous reflux.

Reflux causes blood to pool in the vein, which increases pressure and stretches the vein wall.

That stretched, tortuous vein becomes a varicose vein.

---

### What you see clinically

Varicose veins can look like:

* blue/purple bulging veins
* rope-like twisted veins under the skin
* leg heaviness or aching
* swelling
* itching or burning around the vein

Symptoms often get worse with prolonged standing and improve with leg elevation.

Why?

Standing increases hydrostatic pressure, meaning the column of blood in the leg vein pushes downward more.

---

## One-line intuition

Varicose veins are basically stretched-out superficial veins caused by failed one-way valves and blood pooling.

---

## Pearl

Varicose veins are a form of chronic venous insufficiency, and severe long-standing venous hypertension can cause stasis dermatitis and venous ulcers, classically near the medial malleolus.

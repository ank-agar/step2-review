### Core idea

Treponema pallidum is a thin, spiral-shaped spirochete (corkscrew-shaped bacterium) that causes syphilis.

Think:

* spiral shape
* corkscrew motility
* sexually transmitted infection
* can spread systemically

---

### Why the shape matters

It is very thin and corkscrew-shaped, so it can:

* move through mucus membranes
* enter tiny breaks in skin
* spread through blood and lymphatics (fluid drainage vessels)

That is why syphilis can start as one painless lesion, then later cause body-wide findings.

---

### Classic disease stages

Primary syphilis:

* painless chancre (ulcer) at the infection site
* regional lymphadenopathy (swollen nearby lymph nodes)

Secondary syphilis:

* diffuse rash
* classically involves palms and soles
* condyloma lata (broad, moist wart-like lesions)

Tertiary syphilis:

* gummas (granulomatous destructive lesions)
* aortitis (inflammation of the aorta)
* neurosyphilis (infection involving the nervous system)

---

### Diagnosis intuition

Treponema pallidum is hard to see on regular light microscopy because it is too thin.

So you use:

* dark-field microscopy to directly see spirochetes
* nontreponemal tests like RPR (rapid plasma reagin) or VDRL (Venereal Disease Research Laboratory)
* treponemal tests like FTA-ABS (fluorescent treponemal antibody absorption)

---

### Treatment

Penicillin G is the classic treatment.

Why?

It is a bacterium with a cell wall, so beta-lactam antibiotics (antibiotics that block bacterial cell wall synthesis) work well.

---

## One-line intuition

Treponema pallidum is a corkscrew-shaped spirochete that burrows in, spreads systemically, and causes syphilis in stages.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Secondary syphilis rash involving the palms and soles is extremely high-yield for Step 1.

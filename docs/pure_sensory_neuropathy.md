## Pure sensory neuropathy

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK532291/bin/DRG.jpg)

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17457/N00007-H.jpg)

Pure sensory neuropathy = damage to **sensory nerves only**.

So the patient has problems with feeling, but **motor function is spared**.

Meaning:

* numbness
* tingling/paresthesias (abnormal sensations like pins and needles)
* burning pain
* loss of vibration/proprioception (body position sense)
* sensory ataxia (unsteady gait because the brain is not getting position information)

But usually:

* no true weakness
* no major muscle atrophy

---

### Core idea

Sensory nerves are the “incoming wires.”

They carry information from the body to the CNS (central nervous system), like:

* touch
* pain
* temperature
* vibration
* joint position

So if only these nerves are damaged, the body can still move, but it cannot feel normally.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Numbness:** sensory input is not reaching the brain
* **Tingling/burning pain:** damaged sensory nerves fire abnormally
* **Loss of vibration/proprioception:** large sensory fibers are affected
* **Sensory ataxia:** the patient cannot tell where their feet are in space
* **Preserved strength:** motor nerves are not the main problem

---

### Important Step 1 association

Pure sensory neuropathy can be seen with damage to the dorsal root ganglia.

DRG (dorsal root ganglion) = cluster of sensory neuron cell bodies near the spinal cord.

Break it down:

* dorsal root = sensory input entering spinal cord
* ganglion = collection of neuron cell bodies

So DRG damage mainly causes sensory problems, not weakness.

---

## One-line intuition

Pure sensory neuropathy means the “feeling wires” are damaged, but the “movement wires” are mostly okay.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Sensory ataxia gets worse when the eyes close because vision was helping compensate for lost proprioception (position sense). This is a positive Romberg sign.

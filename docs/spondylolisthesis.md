## Spondylolisthesis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/SpondyloL5S1CTSagMark.png/120px-SpondyloL5S1CTSagMark.png)

Spondylolisthesis = one vertebra slips forward over the vertebra below it.

Most classic location:

* L5 slipping forward over S1

L5 = fifth lumbar vertebra.

S1 = first sacral vertebra.

---

### Core idea

Think:

> the spine is a stack of blocks, and one block slides forward.

That forward slip can narrow the space where nerves exit, so it can cause:

* low back pain
* pain shooting down the leg
* numbness/tingling
* hamstring tightness

But mild cases can also be asymptomatic.

---

### Why it happens

Common causes:

* **Pars defect:** stress fracture in the pars interarticularis (thin bridge of bone between parts of a vertebra)
* **Degeneration:** age-related disc/facet joint wear that makes the spine less stable
* **Congenital abnormality:** abnormal spine formation from birth
* **Trauma/tumor/infection:** less common causes that weaken support

The big Step 1 association is:

* spondylolysis → spondylolisthesis

Spondylolysis = defect/fracture of the pars interarticularis.

Why that causes slipping:

* the pars normally helps anchor the posterior vertebra
* if it fractures, the vertebral body is less restrained
* then the vertebra can slide forward

---

### Grading

Spondylolisthesis is graded by how much of the vertebral body has slipped forward:

* Grade I: 0-25%
* Grade II: 25-50%
* Grade III: 50-75%
* Grade IV: 75-100%
* Grade V: >100%

So higher grade = more forward slip.

---

## One-line intuition

Spondylolisthesis is basically a vertebral "block" sliding forward, often because the bony bridge holding it in place is cracked or the joints/discs have degenerated.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Isthmic spondylolisthesis is classically due to a pars interarticularis defect, and on oblique lumbar X-ray this can give the **Scottie dog with a collar** sign.

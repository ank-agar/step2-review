### Core idea

Granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA) is an autoimmune small-vessel vasculitis (inflammation of blood vessels) that classically hits:

* upper airway: sinusitis, nasal ulcers, nosebleeds, saddle nose
* lungs: cough, hemoptysis (coughing blood), nodules/cavitary lesions
* kidneys: rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis (fast kidney filter inflammation)

Break the name apart:

* granulomatosis = granulomas (organized immune cell clumps)
* polyangiitis = inflammation of many blood vessels

So GPA is basically:

> granulomas + many inflamed small vessels, especially in the ENT (ear, nose, throat), lungs, and kidneys.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Sinusitis/nasal damage:** granulomatous inflammation destroys upper airway tissue
* **Hemoptysis:** inflamed lung vessels leak blood into airspaces
* **Cavitary lung nodules:** immune inflammation causes tissue necrosis (cell death), leaving holes/cavities
* **Hematuria/proteinuria:** kidney glomeruli (blood-filtering tufts) get inflamed and leaky
* **Red blood cell casts:** red blood cells leak into kidney tubules and clump into cast-shaped molds

---

### Key lab association

Usually associated with:

* c-ANCA (cytoplasmic anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody)
* anti-PR3 (anti-proteinase 3 antibody)

Intuition:

ANCA (anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody) activates neutrophils (acute inflammatory white blood cells), and those neutrophils attack vessel walls.

---

## One-line intuition

GPA is an autoimmune disease where neutrophils chew up small vessels, causing destructive sinus/lung disease plus nephritic kidney disease.

---

## Pearl 🔥

GPA = **c-ANCA/PR3 + upper airway + lungs + kidneys**.

Microscopic polyangiitis (MPA) is also ANCA-associated, but it usually lacks granulomas and upper airway destructive disease.

## Frostbite

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/9339.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7a/Third-degree-frostbite.png/960px-Third-degree-frostbite.png)

Frostbite is **freezing injury to skin and deeper tissue**.

Most common sites:

* fingers
* toes
* ears
* nose
* cheeks

because these are exposed and have relatively less warm blood flow.

---

### Core idea

Cold does 2 main things:

* **freezes tissue water** → ice crystals damage cell membranes
* **vasoconstricts blood vessels** (narrows blood vessels) → less blood flow → ischemia (low oxygen delivery)

So frostbite is basically:

> cold injury + poor blood flow → tissue death if severe.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Numbness:** cold slows nerve signaling
* **Pale/white skin:** vasoconstriction reduces blood flow
* **Hard/waxy skin:** tissue water is freezing
* **Blisters after rewarming:** damaged vessels leak fluid
* **Black tissue:** necrosis (tissue death) from severe ischemia

---

### Stages

* **Frostnip:** cold/numb skin, no permanent tissue damage
* **Superficial frostbite:** skin injury, swelling/blisters can happen
* **Deep frostbite:** deeper tissue injury, black eschar (dead scab-like tissue), possible amputation

---

### Treatment intuition

Rewarm with **warm water**, not dry heat.

Do **not** rub/massage it because ice crystals and damaged tissue can get mechanically shredded more.

Also do **not** rewarm if the tissue might freeze again, because freeze-thaw-refreeze causes worse vascular injury.

---

## One-line intuition

Frostbite is when tissue literally freezes, damaging cells directly and cutting off blood flow until the tissue becomes ischemic.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Hemorrhagic blisters (blood-filled blisters) suggest **deeper frostbite**, while clear blisters are more consistent with **superficial frostbite**.

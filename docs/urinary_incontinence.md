### Core idea

Urinary incontinence = involuntary urine leakage.

Basically:

* bladder is trying to store urine
* urethra/sphincter is supposed to keep it in
* leakage happens when storage pressure beats outlet resistance

Sphincter = circular muscle that closes an opening.

---

### Main types

**Stress incontinence**

Urine leaks when abdominal pressure rises:

* coughing
* sneezing
* laughing
* exercise

Why?

The pelvic floor or urethral sphincter is weak, so pressure pushes urine out.

Think:

> weak outlet problem

---

**Urge incontinence**

Sudden intense urge to pee, then leakage before reaching the bathroom.

Why?

The detrusor muscle contracts too early.

Detrusor = smooth muscle of the bladder wall that squeezes urine out.

Think:

> overactive bladder problem

---

**Overflow incontinence**

Constant dribbling because the bladder is too full and cannot empty well.

Why?

Usually obstruction or weak bladder contraction.

Examples:

* benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH, enlarged prostate)
* diabetic autonomic neuropathy (nerve damage from diabetes affecting automatic body functions)
* spinal cord injury

Think:

> overfilled tank spilling over

---

### Why leakage happens

Normal urination needs coordination:

* bladder relaxes while storing urine
* sphincter stays closed
* when peeing, bladder contracts
* sphincter relaxes

Incontinence happens when that coordination fails.

So the issue can be:

* weak sphincter/pelvic floor
* overactive bladder contraction
* poor bladder emptying
* neurologic disease affecting bladder control

---

## One-line intuition

Urinary incontinence is urine leaking because the bladder squeezes too much, the outlet is too weak, or the bladder gets too full.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Stress incontinence = leakage with cough/sneeze/exercise due to weak pelvic floor or urethral support.

Urge incontinence = detrusor overactivity, classically treated with antimuscarinics like oxybutynin.

# Renal laceration

![Image](https://images2.urology-textbook.com/nierentrauma_aast.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/03/Renal_hematoma_diagram.png)

![Image](https://images2.urology-textbook.com/Nierentrauma_CT.jpg)

A renal laceration is a tear/cut in the kidney tissue, usually after trauma.

Renal = kidney  
Laceration = tear

So the simple idea:

> the kidney gets physically split or cut, causing bleeding and sometimes urine leakage.

---

### Core idea

The kidney has:

* cortex = outer kidney tissue
* medulla = inner kidney tissue
* collecting system = urine-draining part of the kidney
* renal vessels = kidney blood vessels

A laceration can be mild if it only cuts superficial kidney tissue, or severe if it reaches the collecting system or major vessels.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Flank pain:** the kidney sits in the back/side area, so trauma causes side/back pain
* **Hematuria (blood in urine):** torn kidney tissue lets blood enter urine
* **Perinephric hematoma:** blood collects around the kidney; hematoma means a collection of blood
* **Urinary extravasation:** urine leaks out if the collecting system is torn; extravasation means fluid leaking outside its normal space
* **Shock:** severe bleeding can drop blood pressure

---

### Grading intuition

Think:

* small shallow tear = lower grade
* deeper tear = higher grade
* tear into collecting system = worse
* shattered kidney or torn renal hilum = worst

Renal hilum means the area where the renal artery, renal vein, and ureter enter/exit the kidney.

---

### Diagnosis

Best test is usually CT (computed tomography) abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast if the patient is stable.

Why contrast?

* shows active bleeding
* shows kidney perfusion
* delayed images can show urine leak from the collecting system

---

## One-line intuition

A renal laceration is trauma splitting kidney tissue, so the big things to watch for are bleeding, hematuria, and urine leak.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Renal trauma is usually managed nonoperatively if the patient is hemodynamically stable; hemodynamically stable means blood pressure and circulation are holding up.

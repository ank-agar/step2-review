Trauma in pregnancy = any physical injury to a pregnant patient.

Most common causes:

* motor vehicle crashes
* falls
* assault/intimate partner violence

The key Step 1 idea:

> stabilize the mother first, because fetal survival depends on maternal oxygenation and blood flow.

So even though there are 2 patients, the priority is still maternal ABCs:

* airway
* breathing
* circulation

ABCs (airway, breathing, circulation) means making sure the patient can breathe and perfuse organs.

---

### Why pregnancy makes trauma different

In later pregnancy, the uterus is large and can compress major vessels when the patient lies flat.

The big one is the IVC (inferior vena cava), which returns blood from the lower body to the heart.

If the uterus compresses the IVC:

* less venous return to the heart
* lower cardiac output
* less uteroplacental blood flow
* fetal distress

So in major trauma/resuscitation, use:

* left lateral tilt
* or manual left uterine displacement

This moves the uterus off the IVC.

---

### Biggest fetal danger

The classic feared complication is placental abruption.

Placental abruption = premature separation of the placenta from the uterus before delivery.

Why trauma causes it:

* uterus = muscular and elastic
* placenta = relatively inelastic
* sudden acceleration/deceleration shears the placenta off the uterine wall

So the placenta can partially detach, causing:

* vaginal bleeding
* abdominal pain
* uterine tenderness
* contractions
* fetal distress

Important: abruption can happen even without obvious vaginal bleeding because bleeding may stay hidden behind the placenta.

---

### Monitoring idea

After trauma, if pregnancy is beyond about 20 weeks, monitor for contractions and fetal heart rate after the mother is stable.

Tocodynamometry (uterine contraction monitoring) is used because contractions can be an early sign of abruption or preterm labor.

Ultrasound can help, but a normal ultrasound does not fully rule out abruption.

---

### Rh issue

If the mother is Rh-negative, trauma can cause fetomaternal hemorrhage.

Fetomaternal hemorrhage = fetal blood entering maternal circulation.

That can expose the mother to fetal Rh-positive RBCs (red blood cells), causing anti-D antibodies.

So Rh-negative pregnant trauma patients generally need Rh immune globulin.

Rh immune globulin = anti-D antibody that prevents the mother from becoming sensitized to Rh-positive fetal blood.

---

## One-line intuition

Trauma in pregnancy is managed by saving the mother first, then watching closely for placental abruption because the elastic uterus can shear away from the inelastic placenta.

---

## Pearl

Placental abruption is the most common cause of fetal death after blunt trauma, other than maternal death itself.

## Dwarfism

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK559263/bin/Achondroplasia.jpg)

![Image](https://media.springernature.com/full/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs11604-021-01206-5/MediaObjects/11604_2021_1206_Fig1_HTML.jpg)

Dwarfism means pathologically short stature, usually adult height less than about 4 ft 10 in.

The most common cause is:

* achondroplasia

Achondroplasia = a skeletal dysplasia (abnormal bone/cartilage development) caused by gain-of-function mutation in:

* FGFR3 (fibroblast growth factor receptor 3)

Think of FGFR3 as the brake on cartilage growth.

So in achondroplasia:

* FGFR3 is overactive
* cartilage cells in growth plates do not proliferate enough
* long bones do not lengthen normally
* result = short limbs, especially proximal limbs

Proximal limb shortening is called:

* rhizomelic shortening

Rhizomelic = shortening of the upper arms and thighs.

---

### Why the body proportions look like this

Long bones grow mainly by endochondral ossification (bone forms from a cartilage model).

Achondroplasia messes up endochondral ossification.

So structures that depend heavily on cartilage growth are most affected:

* femur/humerus
* tibia/fibula
* growth plates

But membranous bones are relatively spared.

Intramembranous ossification (bone forms directly from mesenchyme, not cartilage) helps form much of the skull vault.

That is why achondroplasia classically has:

* short limbs
* relatively normal trunk
* large head
* frontal bossing
* midface hypoplasia

---

### Classic findings

* short stature
* short proximal limbs
* macrocephaly (large head)
* frontal bossing (prominent forehead)
* midface hypoplasia (underdeveloped middle face)
* trident hand
* bowed legs
* spinal stenosis sometimes

Trident hand = extra separation between the middle and ring fingers, making the hand look like it has 3 prongs.

---

### Genetics

Achondroplasia is usually autosomal dominant.

Autosomal dominant = one mutated copy is enough to cause disease.

Most cases are new mutations, and risk increases with advanced paternal age.

---

## One-line intuition

Dwarfism from achondroplasia is basically: overactive FGFR3 puts too much brake on cartilage growth plates, so long bones cannot lengthen normally.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

Achondroplasia affects endochondral ossification, but intramembranous ossification is relatively normal.

That explains the Step 1 pattern:

* short limbs
* normal-ish trunk
* large skull/frontal bossing

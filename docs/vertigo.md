Vertigo = false sensation of movement, classically “the room is spinning.”

It is not just vague dizziness.

Core idea:

* dizziness = broad nonspecific lightheaded/unsteady feeling
* vertigo = specific illusion of motion

---

### Why vertigo happens

Your brain compares balance input from:

* inner ear vestibular system (balance organs in the inner ear)
* eyes
* proprioception (body position sense from joints/muscles)

If one vestibular system sends the wrong signal, the brain thinks:

> “The head is moving.”

But the eyes/body say:

> “No, we are still.”

That mismatch creates:

* spinning sensation
* nausea/vomiting
* nystagmus (involuntary rhythmic eye movement)
* imbalance

---

### Common causes

Peripheral vertigo = problem in the inner ear or CN VIII (cranial nerve 8, vestibulocochlear nerve).

Examples:

* BPPV (benign paroxysmal positional vertigo)
* vestibular neuritis (inflammation of vestibular nerve)
* labyrinthitis (inflammation of inner ear labyrinth)
* Meniere disease

Central vertigo = problem in the brainstem/cerebellum.

Examples:

* stroke
* multiple sclerosis
* tumor

---

### BPPV intuition

BPPV (benign paroxysmal positional vertigo) is the classic Step 1 vertigo cause.

Tiny calcium carbonate crystals called otoconia normally sit in the utricle.

If they break loose and fall into a semicircular canal, especially the posterior canal:

* head movement makes the crystals move
* crystals drag fluid in the canal
* canal falsely signals rotation
* patient feels sudden brief spinning

So BPPV is basically:

> loose ear crystals tricking the brain into thinking the head is rotating.

---

### Peripheral vs central clue

Peripheral vertigo usually has:

* intense spinning
* nausea/vomiting
* horizontal or torsional nystagmus
* symptoms triggered by head movement

Central vertigo is more concerning when there are neurologic signs like:

* diplopia (double vision)
* dysarthria (slurred speech)
* limb ataxia (poor coordination)
* weakness/numbness

---

## One-line intuition

Vertigo happens when the vestibular system sends a false motion signal, so the brain perceives spinning even when the body is still.

---

## Pearl 🔥

BPPV causes brief positional vertigo without hearing loss; Meniere disease causes episodic vertigo with hearing loss, tinnitus (ringing in ears), and ear fullness.

Proteinuria means excess protein in the urine.

Normally, the glomerulus (kidney filtering tuft) keeps big important proteins, especially albumin (main blood protein), in the blood.

So proteinuria usually means:

* the kidney filter is leaky
* protein is spilling from blood into urine

---

### Core idea

Think:

> damaged kidney sieve = protein leaks into pee

Protein is not supposed to freely cross the glomerular filtration barrier (the kidney's filter between blood and urine).

That barrier has:

* fenestrated endothelium (capillary lining with holes)
* glomerular basement membrane
* podocytes (cells with foot processes that form filtration slits)

If that barrier is injured, albumin can pass through.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Foamy urine:** protein lowers surface tension, so urine can make persistent foam
* **Edema:** losing albumin lowers plasma oncotic pressure (protein pulling water into blood vessels), so fluid leaves vessels into tissue
* **Hypoalbuminemia:** albumin is being lost in urine
* **Hyperlipidemia:** liver tries to compensate by making more proteins, and also increases lipoprotein production

---

### Big Step 1 connection

Proteinuria is a key feature of nephrotic syndrome.

Nephrotic syndrome classically has:

* heavy proteinuria
* hypoalbuminemia
* edema
* hyperlipidemia
* lipiduria (fat in urine)

Nephritic syndrome is more about inflammation causing:

* hematuria (blood in urine)
* RBC casts (red blood cell casts)
* hypertension
* decreased GFR (glomerular filtration rate)

---

## One-line intuition

Proteinuria means the kidney filter is damaged enough that proteins that should stay in blood are leaking into urine.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Nephrotic-range proteinuria is >3.5 g/day and points you toward podocyte/glomerular barrier damage, like minimal change disease, FSGS (focal segmental glomerulosclerosis), membranous nephropathy, or diabetic nephropathy.

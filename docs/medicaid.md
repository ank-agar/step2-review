Medicaid is a government health insurance program for people with low income or certain qualifying needs.

It is run by:

* the federal government
* each state

So the key idea is:

> Medicaid = health coverage for low-income patients, funded jointly by federal + state government.

---

### Who commonly gets Medicaid?

Classically, Medicaid covers groups like:

* children
* pregnant women
* low-income adults
* people with disabilities
* some elderly patients needing long-term care

Eligibility varies by state because each state runs its own Medicaid program within federal rules.

---

### Medicaid vs Medicare

This is the big confusion.

* **Medicaid:** based mostly on low income / need
* **Medicare:** based mostly on age 65+ or certain disabilities

Think:

* Medic**aid** = financial **aid**
* Medi**care** = older adults need medical **care**

Some patients can have both. These are called **dual eligible** patients, meaning they qualify for both Medicare and Medicaid.

---

### Why states matter

Medicaid is not one identical national plan.

Federal rules set the baseline, but states decide many details, like:

* exact eligibility cutoffs
* covered optional benefits
* how care is delivered
* whether certain expansion groups are covered

That is why Medicaid can look different in California vs Texas vs New York.

---

### Important related terms

* **CHIP (Children's Health Insurance Program):** coverage for children whose families make too much for Medicaid but still need help affording insurance
* **SSI (Supplemental Security Income):** federal income support program for people who are elderly, blind, or disabled with limited income/resources; SSI often connects to Medicaid eligibility
* **FPL (federal poverty level):** income cutoff used to decide eligibility for many assistance programs
* **ACA (Affordable Care Act):** law that allowed states to expand Medicaid to more low-income adults

---

## One-line intuition

Medicaid is the safety-net health insurance program: it helps pay for medical care when someone qualifies because of low income, disability, pregnancy, childhood, or long-term care needs.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For exams, do not mix up Medicare and Medicaid:

* **Medicare = elderly**
* **Medicaid = low income**

Medicaid is also the major payer for long-term nursing home care in the United States (U.S.).

Snake bites are wounds from snake teeth/fangs.

The big question is:

* **dry bite** = snake bites but injects little/no venom
* **envenomation** = venom is injected into tissue

Venom = toxic snake fluid that can damage tissue, blood, nerves, and organs.

---

### Core idea

Most snake bites cause local injury:

* puncture marks
* pain
* bleeding
* swelling
* bruising

Venomous bites can also cause systemic symptoms, meaning symptoms throughout the body:

* nausea/vomiting
* weakness
* low blood pressure
* trouble breathing
* bleeding problems
* muscle weakness/paralysis

---

### Why the findings happen

Venom is basically a toxin mixture.

Different snakes have different toxin patterns:

* **hemotoxins** damage blood vessels/RBCs (red blood cells) and impair clotting
* **neurotoxins** damage nerve signaling
* **cytotoxins** directly damage local cells/tissue

So:

* swelling/bruising = local vessel + tissue injury
* bleeding = coagulation (clotting) disruption
* necrosis = tissue death from cytotoxic venom
* weakness/paralysis = neurotoxin blocking nerve-muscle signaling
* hypotension (low blood pressure) = vascular leakage + systemic shock

---

### First aid idea

Treat any possible venomous snake bite as an emergency.

Do:

* call emergency services
* keep the person still
* remove rings/watches before swelling worsens
* mark the edge of swelling with the time
* take a photo of the snake only if safe

Do **not**:

* cut the wound
* suck out venom
* apply ice
* apply a tourniquet
* try to catch the snake

These do not remove venom well and can worsen tissue injury.

---

### Antivenom

Antivenom = antibodies that bind venom toxins.

It works because it neutralizes venom circulating in the blood/tissues before it causes more damage.

Think:

> venom is the toxin, antivenom is the antibody-based sponge for that toxin.

---

## One-line intuition

A snake bite is dangerous when venom gets injected, because venom can locally destroy tissue and systemically disrupt blood clotting, nerves, breathing, and blood pressure.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember:

**viper bites** classically cause local pain/swelling + bleeding/coagulopathy (clotting disorder), while **coral snake bites** are more neurotoxic and can cause weakness/paralysis with less obvious local tissue damage.

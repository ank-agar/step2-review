### Core idea

Lymphocytic colitis is a type of **microscopic colitis** (colon inflammation that looks normal on colonoscopy but abnormal under the microscope).

The key finding is:

* lots of **lymphocytes** (immune white blood cells) in the colon lining

So the colon may look grossly normal, but biopsy shows inflammation.

---

### Classic presentation

* chronic watery diarrhea
* non-bloody diarrhea
* abdominal cramping
* usually older adults
* colonoscopy often looks normal

That “normal colonoscopy + watery diarrhea” combo is the clue.

---

### Why diarrhea happens

Inflammation damages the colon lining.

Normally, the colon absorbs water from stool.

But in lymphocytic colitis:

* lymphocytes irritate the epithelium (surface lining)
* water absorption decreases
* inflammatory signals increase secretion

So water stays in the lumen (inside of the gut tube) → watery diarrhea.

---

### Diagnosis

Diagnosis is by colon biopsy.

You need the biopsy because the disease is **microscopic**:

* colonoscopy can look normal
* histology (microscope study of tissue) shows increased intraepithelial lymphocytes

**Intraepithelial** means “inside the epithelial layer.”

---

### Lymphocytic vs collagenous colitis

Both are microscopic colitis.

* **Lymphocytic colitis:** increased lymphocytes
* **Collagenous colitis:** thick collagen band under the epithelium

**Collagen** is structural connective-tissue protein.

---

## One-line intuition

Lymphocytic colitis = immune cells sneak into the colon lining, making the colon leak/retain water even though it looks normal to the naked eye.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Microscopic colitis causes chronic watery **non-bloody** diarrhea with a usually normal colonoscopy, so biopsy is required.

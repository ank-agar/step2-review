Chest wall injury means trauma to the structures that make up the chest wall:

* ribs
* sternum
* intercostal muscles
* soft tissue
* pleura, meaning the lining around the lung

Basically:

> the protective cage around the lungs gets damaged.

---

### Core idea

The chest wall normally does 2 big jobs:

* protects the lungs, heart, and great vessels
* expands outward so the lungs can fill with air

So if the chest wall is injured, the problem is usually:

* pain with breathing
* poor ventilation, meaning poor air movement
* possible lung injury underneath

---

### Common types

**Rib fracture** = broken rib.

This causes sharp pain, especially with deep inspiration, meaning breathing in.

Why?

The ribs move every time you breathe, so a fracture keeps getting irritated.

**Flail chest** = multiple adjacent ribs broken in multiple places, creating a free-floating chest wall segment.

Classic finding:

* paradoxical breathing

Paradoxical breathing means the injured segment moves opposite the rest of the chest:

* inspiration: normal chest wall moves out, flail segment gets sucked in
* expiration: normal chest wall moves in, flail segment bulges out

Why?

During inspiration, intrathoracic pressure, meaning pressure inside the chest, becomes negative. A stable rib cage resists that pull, but a loose flail segment gets pulled inward.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Chest pain:** fractured ribs and torn muscle/pleura activate pain fibers
* **Shallow breathing:** patient avoids deep breaths because it hurts
* **Atelectasis:** shallow breaths let alveoli, meaning tiny air sacs, collapse
* **Hypoxemia:** low blood oxygen can happen if ventilation is poor
* **Pneumothorax:** broken rib can puncture lung, letting air enter the pleural space
* **Hemothorax:** trauma can cause blood to collect in the pleural space
* **Pulmonary contusion:** bruised lung tissue causes edema and impaired gas exchange

---

### Big danger

The scary part is not just the broken rib.

The scary part is what it can do to breathing:

* pain prevents deep inspiration
* flail segment makes ventilation inefficient
* lung contusion worsens oxygenation
* pneumothorax can collapse the lung

So chest wall injury can become respiratory failure if severe.

---

## One-line intuition

Chest wall injury is damage to the rib cage/soft tissues that makes breathing painful or mechanically ineffective, and it can injure the lung underneath.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Flail chest is classically associated with **pulmonary contusion**, and the contusion often causes more hypoxemia than the rib motion itself.

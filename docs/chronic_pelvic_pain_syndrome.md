### Core idea

Chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CPPS) = chronic pain in the pelvis/perineum (area between genitals and anus) for **>3 months**, usually without a clear infection or structural cause.

In men, it is often called chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome, but the key idea is:

* not necessarily bacterial prostatitis
* often pelvic floor muscle + nerve pain
* symptoms wax and wane

---

### What patients feel

Common symptoms:

* pelvic or perineal pain
* pain with urination
* urinary frequency/urgency
* pain after ejaculation
* testicular, penile, suprapubic, rectal, or low back discomfort

Suprapubic means above the pubic bone.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Think of the pelvis as a crowded room:

* bladder
* prostate/uterus
* rectum
* pelvic floor muscles
* pudendal nerve and autonomic nerves

If pelvic floor muscles become chronically tense/spasmed, they can irritate nearby nerves and organs.

So you can get:

* **pain:** irritated pelvic nerves
* **urinary symptoms:** bladder/urethra nerves get sensitized
* **pain after ejaculation:** prostate/pelvic floor contraction flares the irritated system
* **negative cultures:** because this is often not an active bacterial infection

---

### Big distinction

Acute bacterial prostatitis:

* fever
* chills
* very tender prostate
* positive urine culture
* treat with antibiotics

CPPS (chronic pelvic pain syndrome):

* chronic pelvic pain
* usually no fever
* usually negative cultures
* treatment is multimodal: pelvic floor physical therapy, alpha-blockers sometimes, anti-inflammatory meds sometimes, stress/pain modulation

Multimodal means using multiple treatment approaches together.

---

## One-line intuition

CPPS is like the pelvic floor + pelvic nerves getting stuck in a chronic pain loop, even when there is no ongoing infection.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1-style thinking: chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome is **NIH Category III prostatitis**, meaning chronic pelvic pain symptoms **without proven bacterial infection**.

### Core idea

Hyperprolactinemia = too much prolactin (milk-production hormone from the anterior pituitary gland) in the blood.

Prolactin normally:

* stimulates breast milk production
* suppresses GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone)

So high prolactin mainly causes:

* galactorrhea (milk production not related to normal breastfeeding)
* infertility
* amenorrhea (absence of periods)
* low libido

---

### Why high prolactin causes infertility/amenorrhea

High prolactin inhibits GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) from the hypothalamus.

Less GnRH means less:

* LH (luteinizing hormone)
* FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone)

Less LH/FSH means the ovaries/testes are not stimulated properly.

So:

* women: anovulation (no ovulation) + amenorrhea
* men: low testosterone symptoms, low libido, erectile dysfunction

---

### Why dopamine matters

Dopamine is the main prolactin-inhibiting hormone.

Think:

> dopamine puts the brake on prolactin.

So if dopamine signaling goes down, prolactin goes up.

Common Step 1 causes:

* prolactinoma (prolactin-secreting pituitary adenoma)
* antipsychotics, especially D2 receptor blockers
* hypothyroidism
* pituitary stalk compression

---

### Why hypothyroidism can cause it

In primary hypothyroidism, thyroid hormone is low.

The body responds by increasing TRH (thyrotropin-releasing hormone).

TRH stimulates:

* TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone)
* prolactin

So hypothyroidism can cause high prolactin.

---

### Pituitary tumor symptoms

If the cause is a prolactinoma, symptoms come from:

* prolactin excess
* mass effect

Mass effect can cause:

* headache
* bitemporal hemianopia (loss of outer visual fields in both eyes)

Why bitemporal hemianopia?

The pituitary sits under the optic chiasm, so a growing pituitary tumor can compress crossing optic nerve fibers.

---

## One-line intuition

Hyperprolactinemia is high prolactin, which makes milk and shuts down the reproductive axis by suppressing GnRH.

---

## Pearl

Treat prolactinoma with dopamine agonists like cabergoline or bromocriptine, because dopamine inhibits prolactin release.

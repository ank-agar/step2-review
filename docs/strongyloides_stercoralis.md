### Core idea

**Strongyloides stercoralis** is a parasitic nematode (roundworm) that causes **strongyloidiasis** (infection with Strongyloides).

The big Step 1 idea:

* it enters through skin
* migrates to lungs
* gets swallowed
* lives in the small intestine
* can reinfect the same person again and again

That last part is the key: **autoinfection** (the parasite completes its life cycle inside the same host).

---

### Why it matters

Most helminths (parasitic worms) eventually leave the body unless you get re-exposed.

But Strongyloides can keep cycling inside you:

* larvae in the gut become infective
* they penetrate intestinal mucosa or perianal skin
* they re-enter circulation
* they come back to the intestine

So infection can persist for years.

---

### Classic presentation

Think:

* abdominal pain/diarrhea
* eosinophilia (high eosinophils, a type of white blood cell)
* itchy rash
* **larva currens** (fast-moving itchy serpiginous rash)

Serpiginous means snake-like or wavy.

---

### Dangerous scenario

If the patient gets immunosuppressed, especially with corticosteroids (steroid anti-inflammatory drugs), Strongyloides can explode into:

* **hyperinfection syndrome** (massive increase in worm burden)
* disseminated infection (spread throughout the body)
* gram-negative sepsis (bloodstream infection from gut bacteria)

Why gram-negative sepsis?

The larvae can drag gut bacteria across the intestinal wall into the blood.

---

## One-line intuition

Strongyloides is the worm that can **reinfect you from inside your own body**, so steroids can turn a quiet infection into life-threatening hyperinfection.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Before giving steroids to someone from an endemic area with eosinophilia, think about Strongyloides, because corticosteroids can trigger fatal hyperinfection.

## Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic coma

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Hyperosmolar_hyperglycemic_state_by_hariadhi.svg/960px-Hyperosmolar_hyperglycemic_state_by_hariadhi.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/Hyperglycemia.png/500px-Hyperglycemia.png)

Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic coma is the severe end of **HHS (hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state)**:

* very high glucose
* very high serum osmolality (blood is super concentrated)
* severe dehydration
* altered mental status, sometimes coma
* little/no ketoacidosis

This usually happens in **type 2 diabetes mellitus (insulin resistance with relative insulin deficiency)**.

---

### Core idea

There is enough insulin to stop fat breakdown, but not enough insulin to control glucose.

So:

* glucose gets extremely high
* glucose spills into urine
* water follows glucose into urine
* patient becomes massively dehydrated
* blood becomes hyperosmolar
* brain cells lose water
* mental status worsens → coma

---

### Why no major ketoacidosis?

Ketones come from fat breakdown.

In HHS (hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state), the patient still has some insulin.

That small amount of insulin is enough to inhibit **hormone-sensitive lipase (enzyme that breaks down triglycerides in fat cells)**, so there is less free fatty acid release and less ketone production.

But that insulin is not enough to drive glucose into tissues well, so glucose keeps rising.

---

### Classic labs

Think:

* glucose >600 mg/dL
* serum osmolality >320 mOsm/kg
* pH usually >7.30
* bicarbonate usually >18 mEq/L
* absent or mild ketones

Compare to **DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis)**:

* DKA = ketones + acidosis
* HHS = extreme dehydration + hyperosmolality

---

## One-line intuition

HHS is basically “blood sugar so high that it drags water out through the kidneys,” causing severe dehydration and brain cell shrinkage.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Mental status changes in HHS (hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state) correlate best with serum osmolality, not glucose itself.

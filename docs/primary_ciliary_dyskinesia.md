# Primary ciliary dyskinesia

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK1122/bin/pcd-Image001.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK448201/bin/PCDx36944x.jpg)

![Image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/41e1/4541762/c611c7172255/1806-3713-jbpneu-41-03-00251-gf06.jpg)

Primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) is a genetic problem where motile cilia (tiny hair-like structures that move fluid/mucus) do not beat properly.

Main idea:

* cilia are supposed to sweep mucus out of the airways
* in PCD, cilia are defective
* mucus sits there
* bacteria sit there
* repeated infections happen

So PCD causes:

* chronic sinusitis (sinus inflammation/infection)
* recurrent otitis media (middle ear infections)
* chronic cough
* recurrent pneumonia
* bronchiectasis (permanent airway dilation from chronic infection/inflammation)

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Normally, airway cilia act like a moving escalator:

> mucus + trapped bacteria get pushed upward and cleared.

In PCD, the escalator is broken.

So mucus cannot clear well, leading to:

* plugged airways
* chronic infections
* airway wall damage
* bronchiectasis

---

### Why situs inversus can happen

Situs inversus (organs reversed left-to-right) happens because embryonic nodal cilia (cilia in the early embryo that help set left-right body patterning) also do not work properly.

If those cilia cannot create the normal left-right signal, organ placement can become random.

That is why some patients get:

* dextrocardia (heart on the right side)
* situs inversus totalis (mirror-image reversal of organs)

---

### Kartagener syndrome

Kartagener syndrome is PCD with the classic triad of:

* chronic sinusitis
* bronchiectasis
* situs inversus

Basically:

> broken cilia in airways + broken cilia during embryologic left-right patterning.

---

## One-line intuition

PCD = cilia cannot sweep, so mucus/infections build up; embryonic cilia also may fail to orient organs correctly.

---

## Pearl 🔥

PCD is usually autosomal recessive (both gene copies must be abnormal), and the classic ciliary defect is abnormal dynein arms (motor proteins that help cilia beat).

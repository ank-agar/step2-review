## Patent foramen ovale

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK538432/bin/BB8A73AA-268B-4175-8791-02C28337707F.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0a/Patent-foramen-ovale-en.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537314/bin/PFO.jpg)

Patent foramen ovale (PFO) means the fetal opening between the right atrium and left atrium stays open after birth.

Break it down:

* patent = open
* foramen = hole/opening
* ovale = oval-shaped

So:

> PFO = an open oval hole between the atria.

---

### Core idea

In the fetus, the foramen ovale is normal because fetal lungs are not doing oxygen exchange yet.

It lets blood go:

* right atrium
* through foramen ovale
* left atrium
* systemic circulation

This bypasses the lungs.

After birth, the baby starts breathing, pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR; resistance in lung blood vessels) falls, left atrial pressure rises, and the flap should close.

In PFO, the flap does not fully seal.

---

### Why it matters

Most PFOs are asymptomatic.

The danger is a paradoxical embolus (a clot that starts in the venous system but crosses to the arterial system).

Normally, a venous clot goes to the lungs and causes pulmonary embolism (PE; clot in pulmonary arteries).

But with PFO, if right-sided pressure temporarily rises, like during Valsalva maneuver (bearing down against a closed airway), the clot can cross:

* right atrium
* PFO
* left atrium
* brain

That can cause an ischemic stroke (brain infarct from blocked blood flow).

---

### PFO vs ASD

PFO is a flap that failed to seal.

Atrial septal defect (ASD; true hole in the atrial septum) is missing septal tissue.

Intuition:

* PFO = door is present but not fully shut
* ASD = part of the wall is missing

---

## One-line intuition

PFO is a leftover fetal shortcut between the atria that can let venous clots sneak into arterial circulation.

---

## Pearl 🔥

PFO is classically associated with cryptogenic stroke (stroke with no obvious cause), especially if a venous clot crosses right-to-left through the PFO during transient increased right atrial pressure.

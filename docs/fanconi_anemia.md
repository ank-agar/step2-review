### Core idea

Fanconi anemia is an inherited DNA repair disorder.

The problem is defective repair of DNA interstrand cross-links (ICLs), which are abnormal bonds that tie the two DNA strands together.

If the DNA strands are stuck together, the cell cannot copy DNA properly.

So the cells that divide a lot get hurt most:

* bone marrow stem cells
* developing fetus cells
* mucosal cells

That gives:

* bone marrow failure
* congenital abnormalities
* increased cancer risk

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* short stature
* cafe-au-lait spots
* thumb/radius abnormalities
* microcephaly (small head size)
* renal abnormalities
* pancytopenia (low red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets)
* increased risk of acute myeloid leukemia (AML, cancer of myeloid blood-forming cells)

---

### Why pancytopenia happens

Bone marrow stem cells need to divide constantly to make blood cells.

But in Fanconi anemia:

* DNA damage builds up
* replication stalls
* damaged stem cells die

So the marrow cannot make enough:

* red blood cells → anemia/fatigue
* white blood cells → infections
* platelets → bleeding/bruising

This is why Fanconi anemia causes aplastic anemia (bone marrow failure with low blood cell production).

---

### Why cancer risk goes up

If DNA damage is not repaired correctly, mutations accumulate.

That makes cells more likely to become malignant, especially:

* acute myeloid leukemia (AML)
* squamous cell carcinoma (cancer from squamous epithelial cells), especially head/neck and anogenital regions

---

## One-line intuition

Fanconi anemia = broken DNA cross-link repair, so rapidly dividing cells fail or become cancerous.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Fanconi anemia is usually autosomal recessive (need 2 mutated copies) and is associated with hypersensitivity to DNA cross-linking agents like cisplatin and mitomycin C.

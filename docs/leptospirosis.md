### Core idea

Leptospirosis is an infection caused by **Leptospira**, a thin spiral-shaped bacterium called a **spirochete** (spiral-shaped bacteria).

Classic exposure:

* rat urine
* contaminated freshwater
* flooding/sewage water
* animals like dogs, cattle, pigs, or rodents

The bacteria enter through:

* cuts in skin
* eyes
* nose/mouth mucosa

So think:

> animal urine contaminates water → human skin/mucosa exposure → systemic infection.

---

### Classic findings

Leptospirosis can look like a flu-like illness:

* fever
* headache
* severe muscle aches, especially calves
* conjunctival suffusion (red eyes without pus)

Severe disease is called **Weil disease**:

* jaundice (yellow skin/eyes from bilirubin buildup)
* kidney failure
* hemorrhage (bleeding)
* liver dysfunction

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Calf pain:** Leptospira likes to invade muscle, causing inflammation and tenderness
* **Red eyes:** conjunctival blood vessels dilate, causing conjunctival suffusion (redness without discharge)
* **Jaundice:** liver injury impairs bilirubin handling
* **Kidney failure:** bacteria damage renal tubules (kidney tubes that process filtrate)
* **Bleeding/ARDS:** severe endothelial injury can cause hemorrhage and ARDS (acute respiratory distress syndrome, severe inflammatory lung injury)

---

## One-line intuition

Leptospirosis is a **rat-urine freshwater spirochete infection** that starts flu-like but can progress to **jaundice + kidney failure**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 clue:

* **freshwater exposure + calf tenderness + conjunctival suffusion** = think **Leptospira**.

Treat with **doxycycline** for mild disease; severe disease can use IV penicillin G or ceftriaxone (IV = intravenous, into a vein).

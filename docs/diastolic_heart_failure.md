### Core idea

Diastolic heart failure = the heart cannot **fill well** during diastole (relaxation phase).

Usually this is called **HFpEF (heart failure with preserved ejection fraction)**.

The ventricle is often:

* stiff
* thickened
* poorly compliant

So it can still squeeze okay, but it starts with less blood inside.

---

### Why ejection fraction is preserved

**EF (ejection fraction)** means:

> percentage of blood in the ventricle that gets pumped out.

In diastolic heart failure:

* filling is decreased
* EDV (end-diastolic volume; blood in ventricle after filling) is decreased or not enough for the body's needs
* systolic squeeze is relatively normal

So the heart may pump out a normal **percentage** of the blood it received, but the **total amount** pumped out can still be low.

Simple numbers:

* normal: fills with 100 mL, pumps 60 mL -> EF = 60%
* diastolic failure: fills with 60 mL, pumps 36 mL -> EF = 60%

Same percentage, less total blood.

---

### Why symptoms happen

The stiff LV (left ventricle) resists filling.

That causes:

* increased LVEDP (left ventricular end-diastolic pressure; pressure in the LV after filling)
* backup into LA (left atrium)
* backup into pulmonary veins
* pulmonary congestion/edema

So patients get:

* dyspnea (shortness of breath)
* orthopnea (shortness of breath when lying flat)
* paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (waking up short of breath at night)
* pulmonary edema
* fatigue from low forward output

---

### Common causes

Think: anything that makes the ventricle stiff.

* chronic hypertension -> LVH (left ventricular hypertrophy)
* aortic stenosis
* restrictive cardiomyopathy
* hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
* aging-related stiffness

---

## One-line intuition

Diastolic heart failure is a **filling problem**, not mainly a squeezing problem.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1: chronic hypertension causes **concentric LVH (left ventricular hypertrophy)**, which makes the LV stiff -> decreased compliance -> diastolic dysfunction/HFpEF.

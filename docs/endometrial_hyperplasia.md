# Endometrial hyperplasia

### Core idea

Endometrial hyperplasia = too much growth of the endometrium (inner lining of the uterus).

Think:

* estrogen makes the endometrium grow
* progesterone tells it to mature and stabilize

So if there is **unopposed estrogen** (estrogen without enough progesterone), the lining keeps proliferating.

---

### Why it happens

Estrogen is the "build the lining" signal.

Progesterone is the "stop growing and organize the lining" signal.

So with too much estrogen effect:

* glands multiply
* the endometrium thickens
* bleeding becomes irregular because the thick lining is unstable

Common settings:

* obesity, because adipose tissue makes estrogen
* PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome), because chronic anovulation (not ovulating) means low progesterone
* estrogen therapy without progesterone
* granulosa cell tumor, because it can produce estrogen

---

### Symptoms

Usually presents with abnormal uterine bleeding (irregular, heavy, or postmenopausal bleeding).

Why bleeding?

The lining grows too much, becomes fragile/disorganized, and sheds unpredictably.

---

### Why we care

The big issue is whether there is **atypia** (abnormal-looking cells).

* hyperplasia without atypia = lower cancer risk
* atypical hyperplasia = much higher risk of endometrial carcinoma (cancer of the endometrial lining)

One-line intuition:

The endometrium is being overstimulated to grow, and if the cells start looking atypical, it becomes a premalignant lesion.

---

## HY pearl

Unopposed estrogen increases risk of both **endometrial hyperplasia** and **endometrial carcinoma**.

Classic Step 1 risk factors:

* obesity
* PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome)
* nulliparity (never having given birth)
* early menarche (early first period)
* late menopause
* estrogen-only hormone therapy

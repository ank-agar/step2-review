## Antiphospholipid syndrome

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK430980/bin/APS.jpg)

![Image](https://www.hss.edu/globalassets/images/conditions/livedo-reticularis-arm.jpg)

![Image](https://www.hss.edu/globalassets/images/conditions/livedo-reticularis-legs.jpg)

Antiphospholipid syndrome, or **APS (antiphospholipid syndrome)**, is an autoimmune hypercoagulable disorder.

Meaning:

* autoimmune = the immune system attacks self
* hypercoagulable = blood clots too easily
* antiphospholipid antibodies = antibodies against phospholipid-binding proteins in the body

The classic problem is:

> antibodies make the blood more likely to clot.

So APS can cause:

* DVT (deep vein thrombosis)
* PE (pulmonary embolism)
* stroke
* recurrent pregnancy loss
* livedo reticularis, which is a lacy/purple netlike skin pattern

---

### Why clotting happens

Even though the name sounds like the antibodies attack "phospholipids," the key target is often **beta-2 glycoprotein I**, a phospholipid-binding protein.

These antibodies activate:

* endothelial cells, which are blood vessel lining cells
* platelets, which help form clots
* coagulation pathways, which make fibrin clot

So the body shifts toward thrombosis, meaning pathologic clot formation.

---

### Pregnancy connection

APS can cause recurrent miscarriages because placental blood vessels thrombose.

Intuition:

* placenta needs steady blood flow
* APS makes tiny placental clots
* fetus gets poor perfusion
* pregnancy loss can happen

---

### The weird lab clue

APS can cause a prolonged PTT (partial thromboplastin time), even though the patient clots too much.

Why?

In the test tube, the antibody interferes with phospholipid-dependent clotting tests, so PTT looks prolonged.

But in the body, it causes endothelial/platelet activation, so the patient is hypercoagulable.

So:

> prolonged PTT in lab, thrombosis in real life.

---

## One-line intuition

APS is when autoimmune antibodies make vessels and platelets act pro-clotting, causing thrombosis and pregnancy loss.

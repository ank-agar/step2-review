### Core idea

Serotonin syndrome is **too much serotonin activity**, usually from medications.

Serotonin = **5-HT (5-hydroxytryptamine)**, a neurotransmitter involved in mood, GI motility, temperature regulation, and muscle tone.

So the body becomes overactivated in 3 big ways:

* mental status changes
* autonomic instability
* neuromuscular hyperactivity

---

### Classic triad

Think:

* **AMS (altered mental status):** agitation, confusion, anxiety
* **Autonomic hyperactivity:** fever, sweating, hypertension, tachycardia, diarrhea
* **Neuromuscular hyperactivity:** tremor, hyperreflexia, clonus, rigidity

Clonus = rhythmic involuntary muscle contractions.

Hyperreflexia = exaggerated reflexes.

---

### Why it happens

Too much serotonin overstimulates serotonin receptors, especially in the CNS (central nervous system).

This can happen from:

* SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors)
* SNRIs (serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors)
* MAOIs (monoamine oxidase inhibitors)
* linezolid
* tramadol
* MDMA (3,4-methylenedioxymethamphetamine)
* triptans
* St. John's wort

The highest-risk setup is usually **combining serotonergic drugs**, especially:

* SSRI + MAOI

because one drug increases serotonin in the synapse, and the other prevents serotonin breakdown.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Agitation/confusion:** excess CNS serotonin overstimulates brain circuits
* **Diarrhea/increased bowel sounds:** serotonin increases GI (gastrointestinal) motility
* **Sweating/tachycardia/hypertension:** autonomic nervous system gets overactivated
* **Fever:** mainly from intense muscle activity, not a true hypothalamic fever
* **Hyperreflexia/clonus:** spinal motor circuits become overexcitable

Important: hyperreflexia and clonus are often worse in the **lower extremities**.

---

### Serotonin syndrome vs NMS

NMS (neuroleptic malignant syndrome) is from dopamine blockade, classically antipsychotics.

Serotonin syndrome:

* rapid onset, often within hours
* hyperreflexia
* clonus
* diarrhea

NMS:

* slower onset, usually days
* lead-pipe rigidity
* bradyreflexia or normal reflexes
* usually no diarrhea

---

## One-line intuition

Serotonin syndrome is a medication-induced serotonin overload where the patient is **agitated, sweaty, tachycardic, diarrheal, hyperreflexic, and clonus-y**.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, the biggest diagnostic clue is:

* **serotonergic drug exposure + clonus/hyperreflexia**

Also, treatment starts with stopping the offending drug and supportive care; **cyproheptadine** is a serotonin antagonist sometimes used for more significant cases.

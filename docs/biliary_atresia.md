## Biliary atresia

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17493/N00043-H.jpg)

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17822/N00857-H.jpg)

![Image](https://www.mayoclinic.org/-/media/kcms/gbs/patient-consumer/images/2026/02/12/20/18/newborn-stools-mc15quln-632px.jpg)

Biliary atresia is a newborn disease where the bile ducts are absent, blocked, or scarred closed.

Break it down:

* biliary = bile ducts
* atresia = absence or closure of a normal opening/tube

So biliary atresia = bile cannot drain from the liver into the intestine.

---

### Core idea

Bile is made in the liver and normally flows:

**liver -> bile ducts -> gallbladder/intestine**

In biliary atresia, that drainage path is blocked.

So bile gets trapped in the liver, causing:

* cholestasis (blocked bile flow)
* direct hyperbilirubinemia (high conjugated bilirubin)
* progressive liver injury
* cirrhosis (scarring of the liver)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Jaundice:** bilirubin builds up because bile cannot exit the liver
* **Dark urine:** conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble, so it spills into urine
* **Pale/clay-colored stools:** bile normally makes stool brown; no bile reaching gut = pale stool
* **Hepatomegaly:** liver enlarges from inflammation and bile backup
* **Fat-soluble vitamin deficiency:** no bile in intestine means poor absorption of vitamins A, D, E, and K

---

### Classic presentation

Newborn with:

* persistent jaundice after 2 weeks
* pale stools
* dark urine
* elevated direct bilirubin

This is not normal physiologic newborn jaundice.

Physiologic newborn jaundice is usually unconjugated bilirubin and improves.

Biliary atresia causes conjugated/direct bilirubin and gets worse.

---

### Treatment

Main early treatment is the Kasai procedure, also called hepatoportoenterostomy.

Break it down:

* hepato- = liver
* porto- = porta hepatis, the liver hilum area where bile should drain
* entero- = intestine
* -stomy = surgically created opening

Intuition:

The surgeon connects intestine directly to the liver drainage area to bypass the destroyed bile ducts.

Many patients still eventually need liver transplant.

---

## One-line intuition

Biliary atresia is a newborn bile duct blockage, so bile backs up into the liver instead of coloring stool brown.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Persistent jaundice **plus pale stools** in a newborn = think biliary atresia until proven otherwise, because early Kasai surgery works best when done early.

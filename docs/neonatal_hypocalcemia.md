# Neonatal hypocalcemia

![Image](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/mmanual-ssq1ci05/media/home/images/e/n/d/end_parathyroid_glands.gif?sc_lang=en)

![Image](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/mmanual-ssq1ci05/media/veterinary/images/p/t/h/pth-regulation-function-figure-ham-sized.jpg?sc_lang=en&mw=1920)

Neonatal hypocalcemia = low calcium in a newborn.

Hypocalcemia means low blood calcium. Neonatal means newborn.

Usually defined as:

* term baby: total calcium < 8 mg/dL
* preterm baby: total calcium < 7 mg/dL

Ionized calcium is the more important form because it is the free, active calcium that nerves and muscles actually “feel.”

---

### Core idea

Calcium normally stabilizes nerves and muscles.

So if calcium is low:

* nerves fire too easily
* muscles twitch/spasm
* severe cases can cause seizures

One-line intuition:

> Low calcium removes the “brake” on nerve firing, so the baby can become jittery, tetanic, or seize.

---

### Why newborns get it

There are 2 big timing patterns:

### Early-onset neonatal hypocalcemia

Usually in the first 1-3 days of life.

Common associations:

* prematurity
* infant of a diabetic mother
* perinatal stress/asphyxia

Why?

Before birth, the placenta supplies calcium from mom to baby.

After birth, that placental calcium supply suddenly stops, and the newborn’s parathyroid hormone system has to “wake up.”

Parathyroid hormone (PTH) is the hormone that raises blood calcium.

If the baby is premature or stressed, that PTH response can be weak or delayed.

So calcium drops.

---

### Late-onset neonatal hypocalcemia

Usually around days 3-7 of life.

Classically due to high phosphate intake, especially cow milk/formula issues.

Why phosphate matters:

* phosphate binds calcium
* high phosphate lowers free calcium
* low free calcium causes symptoms

So:

> High phosphate can “trap” calcium and make the active calcium level fall.

---

### Symptoms

Can be asymptomatic, but if symptomatic:

* jitteriness
* poor feeding
* vomiting
* apnea (pauses in breathing)
* hypotonia (low muscle tone)
* tetany (muscle spasm from nerve hyperexcitability)
* seizures

ECG (electrocardiogram, heart electrical tracing) can show prolonged QT interval, meaning delayed ventricular repolarization.

---

### Important mechanism

PTH (parathyroid hormone) normally raises calcium by:

* increasing calcium release from bone
* increasing calcium reabsorption in kidney
* activating vitamin D

Vitamin D helps the gut absorb calcium.

So the calcium rescue system is basically:

> PTH + vitamin D = raise calcium.

If that system is immature, suppressed, or overwhelmed by phosphate, calcium falls.

---

## One-line intuition

Neonatal hypocalcemia is when a newborn’s calcium drops because the baby loses placental calcium support and cannot yet regulate calcium well.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Infant of a diabetic mother is high-yield for early neonatal hypocalcemia because maternal diabetes can suppress the newborn’s parathyroid hormone response after birth.

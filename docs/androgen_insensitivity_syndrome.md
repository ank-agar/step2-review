### Core idea

Androgen insensitivity syndrome (AIS) = a 46,XY person has testes, but the body cannot respond properly to androgens (male sex hormones like testosterone and dihydrotestosterone).

So the testes make testosterone, but the tissues are basically "deaf" to it.

Result:

* genetic sex: 46,XY
* gonads: testes
* external phenotype: often female in complete AIS (androgen insensitivity syndrome)

---

### Why the findings happen

Normally, testes make:

* testosterone, which supports Wolffian duct development (male internal reproductive tract)
* anti-Mullerian hormone (AMH), which makes Mullerian structures regress

In complete AIS:

* AMH is still present, so no uterus, fallopian tubes, or upper vagina
* androgen receptors do not work, so Wolffian structures do not develop well
* external genitalia develop along female lines because androgen signaling is absent

That is why you can see:

* primary amenorrhea (no first period)
* normal breast development
* sparse or absent pubic/axillary hair
* blind-ending vagina
* absent uterus
* testes often in abdomen, inguinal canal, or labia

---

### Why breasts develop

Testosterone can be converted into estrogen by aromatase (enzyme that converts androgens into estrogens).

So even though androgen signaling is blocked, estrogen signaling still works.

That gives breast development.

---

### Key lab/intuitive pattern

Think:

* 46,XY
* high testosterone
* high luteinizing hormone (LH) because the body senses poor androgen effect
* female external phenotype
* no uterus

One-line intuition:

> Testes are present and making hormones, but the androgen receptor is broken, so testosterone cannot masculinize the body.

---

## Pearl 🔥

AIS is X-linked recessive and classically presents as a phenotypic female with primary amenorrhea, absent uterus, sparse pubic hair, and 46,XY karyotype.

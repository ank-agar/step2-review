### Core idea

Growth hormone deficiency means the anterior pituitary does not make enough GH (growth hormone).

GH normally helps kids grow by telling the liver and tissues to make IGF-1 (insulin-like growth factor 1), which stimulates bone and tissue growth.

So low GH means:

* low IGF-1
* poor linear growth
* short stature with normal body proportions

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Short stature:** low GH means low IGF-1, so long bones do not lengthen normally
* **Normal proportions:** the skeleton is small overall, not malformed
* **Delayed bone age:** growth plates mature more slowly
* **Increased fat, especially trunk fat:** GH normally promotes lipolysis (fat breakdown)
* **Neonatal hypoglycemia:** GH normally helps maintain blood glucose, especially during fasting
* **Micropenis in males:** can happen if there are broader pituitary hormone problems

---

### Common causes

Can be:

* congenital, meaning present from birth
* idiopathic, meaning no clear cause is found
* acquired from pituitary damage, tumor, surgery, radiation, or trauma

The key organ is the anterior pituitary.

---

### Diagnosis idea

You usually do not diagnose it from one random GH level, because GH is released in pulses.

Instead, think:

* poor growth velocity
* low IGF-1
* delayed bone age
* GH stimulation testing
* sometimes pituitary MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) to look for structural problems

---

## One-line intuition

The pituitary is not sending enough "grow" signal, so the liver makes less IGF-1 and the child grows slowly but proportionately.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 pearl: GH deficiency causes **proportionate short stature**, while achondroplasia causes **disproportionate short stature** because the problem is abnormal cartilage/bone growth, not low hormone signal.

Joint pain = **arthralgia** (pain in a joint).

If the joint is painful **and inflamed**, that is **arthritis** (joint inflammation).

So:

* arthralgia = joint pain
* arthritis = inflamed joint

---

### Core idea

A joint hurts when something irritates pain-sensitive structures around the joint, especially:

* synovium (joint lining)
* capsule (fibrous covering around the joint)
* ligaments
* periosteum (pain-sensitive covering of bone)

Cartilage itself has poor pain sensation, but when cartilage breaks down, the bone and synovium get stressed, so the joint hurts.

---

### Why joint pain happens

Common mechanisms:

* **Wear-and-tear:** cartilage breaks down, like in OA (osteoarthritis)
* **Inflammation:** synovium gets inflamed, like in RA (rheumatoid arthritis)
* **Crystal deposition:** crystals irritate the joint, like gout
* **Infection:** bacteria enter the joint, causing septic arthritis
* **Trauma:** sprain, fracture, or ligament/tendon injury near the joint

The pain comes from swelling, stretch, inflammatory mediators, and mechanical stress.

---

### Big Step 1 split

**Noninflammatory joint pain:**

* worse with use
* better with rest
* brief morning stiffness

Classic example: OA (osteoarthritis).

**Inflammatory joint pain:**

* worse in the morning
* improves with use
* warmth/swelling
* prolonged morning stiffness

Classic example: RA (rheumatoid arthritis).

---

## One-line intuition

Joint pain is the joint saying: “my lining, capsule, bone surface, or surrounding support tissue is being irritated.”

---

## Pearl 🔥

RA (rheumatoid arthritis) usually affects **MCP** (metacarpophalangeal joints = big knuckles) and **PIP** (proximal interphalangeal joints = middle finger joints), but usually **spares DIP** (distal interphalangeal joints = joints closest to the fingernails).

DIP involvement points more toward OA (osteoarthritis) or psoriatic arthritis.

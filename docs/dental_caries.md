### Core idea

Dental caries = tooth decay/cavities.

It happens when bacteria in plaque (sticky bacterial biofilm on teeth) metabolize sugar and make acid.

That acid dissolves tooth minerals, especially in:

* enamel (hard outer tooth layer)
* dentin (softer layer under enamel)
* eventually pulp (inner nerves/blood vessels)

---

### Why the cavity forms

Think:

> sugar + bacteria = acid → enamel demineralization → hole in tooth

Early on, the enamel just loses minerals, so you can see a white spot.

If acid attacks keep happening, the enamel breaks down and forms a cavity.

Once decay reaches dentin, it spreads faster because dentin is softer than enamel.

Once it reaches pulp, you can get:

* tooth pain
* sensitivity to hot/cold/sweets
* pulpitis (inflammation of the tooth pulp)
* abscess (pocket of pus from infection)

---

### Why fluoride helps

Fluoride helps remineralize enamel (put minerals back into enamel) and makes enamel more resistant to acid.

So early caries can be reversible.

But once there is an actual hole/cavity, the lost tooth structure usually needs a filling.

---

## One-line intuition

Dental caries is bacteria eating sugar and “peeing out” acid that dissolves the tooth from the outside inward.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1: **Streptococcus mutans** is the classic dental caries organism because it makes acid and uses sucrose to make sticky glucans (sugar polymers) that help it adhere to teeth.

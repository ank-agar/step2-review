# Breath-holding spells

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Nervous_system_diagram_numbered.svg/960px-Nervous_system_diagram_numbered.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Sinus_bradycardia_lead2.svg/960px-Sinus_bradycardia_lead2.svg.png)

Breath-holding spells are benign episodes in young children where an emotional trigger causes the child to briefly stop breathing and sometimes pass out.

Benign = not dangerous long-term.

Usually happens in toddlers after:

* anger
* frustration
* crying
* pain
* fear

Important: the kid is not doing it on purpose.

---

### Two types

### Cyanotic breath-holding spell

Cyanotic = blue color from low oxygen.

Sequence:

* child gets angry/frustrated
* cries hard
* exhales
* stops breathing
* turns blue
* may briefly pass out

Why blue?

Because they are not inhaling well, so blood oxygen drops briefly.

---

### Pallid breath-holding spell

Pallid = pale.

Usually after sudden pain or fright.

Sequence:

* child gets scared/hurt
* vagus nerve reflex gets triggered
* heart rate slows
* less blood reaches brain
* child turns pale and faints

Vagus nerve = parasympathetic nerve that can slow the heart.

So pallid spells are more like a brief reflex syncope (fainting episode).

---

### Why it can look like a seizure

If the brain briefly gets less oxygen or blood flow, the child can:

* stiffen
* go limp
* have a few jerky movements
* briefly lose consciousness

But the key is:

* clear trigger
* short episode
* rapid recovery

That makes breath-holding spell more likely than epilepsy (recurrent unprovoked seizures).

---

## One-line intuition

Breath-holding spells are toddler reflex fainting episodes: blue type is from crying/holding breath, pale type is from vagal slowing of the heart.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY association: breath-holding spells can be associated with iron deficiency anemia, so recurrent spells may improve with iron treatment if the child is iron deficient.

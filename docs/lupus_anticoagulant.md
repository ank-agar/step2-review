## Lupus anticoagulant

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK430980/bin/APS.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c0/Coagulation_Cascade_Diagram.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3e/Signs_and_symptoms_of_Antiphospholipid_syndrome.jpg)

Lupus anticoagulant is an **antiphospholipid antibody** (autoantibody against phospholipid-binding proteins).

The name is confusing:

* **in vitro** (in a test tube): it acts like an anticoagulant
* **in vivo** (inside the body): it causes clotting

So the key paradox is:

> lupus anticoagulant prolongs clotting tests, but the patient is hypercoagulable.

Hypercoagulable means more likely to form blood clots.

---

### Why does PTT go up?

PTT (partial thromboplastin time) is a lab test of the intrinsic/common coagulation pathway.

The PTT test needs phospholipid surfaces to run normally.

Lupus anticoagulant binds phospholipid-protein complexes, so it interferes with the lab reaction.

That gives:

* increased PTT (partial thromboplastin time)
* usually normal PT (prothrombin time)
* no classic bleeding tendency from lupus anticoagulant itself

So do **not** think:

> prolonged PTT always means bleeding.

Here, the prolonged PTT is mostly a lab-test artifact.

---

### Why does it cause clots in the body?

In real blood vessels, antiphospholipid antibodies can activate:

* endothelial cells (cells lining blood vessels)
* platelets
* complement (immune proteins that amplify inflammation)
* coagulation pathways

That pushes the patient toward thrombosis.

Thrombosis means pathologic clot formation inside a blood vessel.

---

### Mixing study

A mixing study means you mix patient plasma with normal plasma.

If the PTT corrects:

* think clotting factor deficiency

If the PTT does **not** correct:

* think inhibitor

Lupus anticoagulant is an inhibitor, so:

* prolonged PTT
* mixing study does not correct

Why?

Because adding normal plasma gives more clotting factors, but the antibody is still there interfering with the phospholipid-dependent test.

---

### Association

Lupus anticoagulant is part of APS (antiphospholipid syndrome).

APS can cause:

* venous thrombosis
* arterial thrombosis
* recurrent pregnancy loss
* livedo reticularis
* thrombocytopenia

Livedo reticularis means a net-like purplish skin pattern.

Thrombocytopenia means low platelets.

---

## One-line intuition

Lupus anticoagulant makes the lab look anticoagulated, but the patient is actually prone to clots.

---

## Pearl

Recurrent miscarriages + thrombosis + prolonged PTT that does **not** correct with mixing = think lupus anticoagulant / APS (antiphospholipid syndrome).

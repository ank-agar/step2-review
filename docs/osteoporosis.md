### Core idea

Osteoporosis = low bone mass + weak bone microarchitecture.

Basically:

* bone becomes more porous
* trabeculae (thin internal bone struts) become thinner
* bones fracture more easily

The key is that the bone is **less dense**, but the mineralization is usually normal.

So osteoporosis is:

> normal bone quality mineral-wise, but not enough bone.

---

### Why it happens

Bone is constantly remodeled by:

* osteoclasts (cells that resorb/break down bone)
* osteoblasts (cells that build bone)

Osteoporosis happens when:

> bone resorption > bone formation

So the skeleton slowly loses structural support.

---

### Classic risk factors

Think:

* older age
* postmenopausal state
* low estrogen
* chronic glucocorticoid use
* smoking
* low calcium/vitamin D
* immobilization

Estrogen normally inhibits osteoclasts, so after menopause, osteoclast activity increases.

That means:

> less estrogen = more bone breakdown.

---

### Classic fractures

Most tested fracture sites:

* vertebrae
* femoral neck/hip
* distal radius/wrist

Vertebral compression fractures can cause:

* back pain
* loss of height
* kyphosis (excessive forward curvature of the upper spine)

---

### Diagnosis

Diagnosed with DEXA scan (dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry, a test that measures bone mineral density).

T-score (comparison to young healthy adults):

* osteopenia (low bone mass): -1 to -2.5
* osteoporosis: less than or equal to -2.5

---

## One-line intuition

Osteoporosis is when the bone scaffolding gets too thin, so normal stress can cause fractures.

---

## HY pearl

Osteoporosis has normal serum calcium, phosphate, and alkaline phosphatase in uncomplicated disease.

That helps distinguish it from osteomalacia (defective bone mineralization), where vitamin D problems can cause abnormal calcium/phosphate labs.

### Core idea

Osteochondritis dissecans is when a small piece of bone under joint cartilage loses blood supply and starts to weaken/separate.

Break the word apart:

* osteo = bone
* chondro = cartilage
* -itis = inflammation
* dissecans = separating/splitting off

So basically:

> bone + cartilage at a joint start separating from the main bone.

---

### What is actually happening

Usually in an adolescent athlete:

* repetitive stress injures the subchondral bone
* subchondral bone = bone just under cartilage
* blood supply decreases
* that bone weakens or dies
* the overlying cartilage can crack
* the fragment may stay attached or break loose into the joint

If it breaks loose, it becomes a loose body, meaning a floating piece of bone/cartilage inside the joint.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Pain with activity:** damaged subchondral bone is stressed during running/jumping
* **Swelling:** joint irritation causes effusion, meaning extra fluid in the joint
* **Clicking/catching/locking:** loose cartilage/bone fragment mechanically gets in the way
* **Giving way:** the joint surface becomes irregular, so motion feels unstable

---

### Common locations

Most commonly:

* knee, especially medial femoral condyle
* elbow, especially capitellum in throwing athletes/gymnasts
* ankle, especially talar dome

Medial femoral condyle = inner rounded end of the femur (thigh bone) at the knee.

---

## One-line intuition

A tiny area of joint-supporting bone loses blood supply, so the cartilage-bone piece becomes weak and can split off like a pothole in the joint surface.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 pearl: osteochondritis dissecans classically affects adolescent athletes and can cause joint locking if the osteochondral fragment becomes a loose body.

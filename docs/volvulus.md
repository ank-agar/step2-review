# Volvulus

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/17462/N00012-H_thumbnail.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/10031.jpg)

![Image](https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/images/4/46/Midgut_volvulus.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441962/bin/Midgut__volvulus.jpg)

Volvulus = twisting of bowel around itself and its mesentery (the tissue that carries blood vessels to the intestine).

Core idea:

* bowel twists
* lumen gets blocked
* blood supply can get squeezed
* obstruction + ischemia can happen

Ischemia means poor blood flow causing tissue injury.

---

### Why it is dangerous

Think of twisting a garden hose:

* first, contents cannot pass through -> bowel obstruction
* then, veins get compressed -> swelling
* then, arteries can get compressed -> ischemia
* if severe, bowel can become necrotic (dead tissue) and perforate

That is why volvulus can become a surgical emergency.

---

### Common Step 1 associations

In infants:

* midgut volvulus can happen because of intestinal malrotation

Malrotation = abnormal rotation/fixation of the intestines during development.

In older adults:

* sigmoid volvulus is classic
* often associated with chronic constipation and a redundant sigmoid colon

Redundant sigmoid colon = extra-long/mobile sigmoid colon that can twist more easily.

---

### Classic imaging clue

Sigmoid volvulus can show a:

* coffee bean sign on abdominal X-ray

Coffee bean sign = massively dilated twisted sigmoid colon that looks like a giant coffee bean.

Midgut volvulus can show:

* corkscrew appearance on upper GI contrast study

Upper GI (upper gastrointestinal) contrast study = imaging where swallowed contrast outlines the stomach/small bowel.

---

## One-line intuition

Volvulus is bowel twisting on its own blood supply, so it causes obstruction first and can cause ischemic bowel next.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Bilious vomiting in a newborn is midgut volvulus until proven otherwise, especially if due to malrotation.

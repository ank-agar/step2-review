# Infantile spinal muscular atrophy

![Image](https://www.togetherinsma.com/content/dam/commercial/specialty/sma/caregiver/en_us/images/pages/smn1-gene/smn1-gene.png.web.1280.1280.png)

Infantile spinal muscular atrophy is the severe infant form of SMA (spinal muscular atrophy).

Also called:

* SMA type 1 (spinal muscular atrophy type 1)
* Werdnig-Hoffmann disease

### Core idea

The anterior horn cells (lower motor neurons in the spinal cord) degenerate.

So the muscles are structurally okay at first, but they stop getting nerve input.

No nerve signal to muscle means:

* weakness
* hypotonia (low muscle tone)
* atrophy (muscle wasting)
* decreased reflexes

---

### Cause

Most cases are due to deletion or mutation of SMN1 (survival motor neuron 1 gene) on chromosome 5.

Inheritance:

* autosomal recessive

Autosomal recessive means both copies of the gene need to be affected.

---

### Why the baby looks "floppy"

SMN protein (survival motor neuron protein) helps motor neurons survive.

If SMN1 is defective:

* low SMN protein
* anterior horn motor neurons die
* muscles lose innervation
* muscles become weak and small

That gives the classic infant findings:

* floppy baby
* symmetric proximal weakness
* poor head control
* difficulty feeding
* respiratory weakness
* tongue fasciculations (small involuntary muscle twitches)
* decreased deep tendon reflexes

The weakness is usually proximal, meaning closer to the trunk, like shoulders/hips more than hands/feet.

---

### Why sensation is normal

This is a motor neuron disease.

It mainly affects LMNs (lower motor neurons), not sensory neurons.

So:

* motor function is bad
* sensation is relatively preserved

---

## One-line intuition

Infantile SMA is a genetic loss of spinal lower motor neurons, so the baby cannot properly activate muscles and becomes profoundly weak/floppy.

---

## Pearl 🔥

SMN2 (survival motor neuron 2 gene) is the backup gene:

* more SMN2 copies = more SMN protein
* more SMN protein = milder disease

So SMN2 copy number modifies severity in SMA.

### Core idea

Posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES) is brain swelling, usually in the posterior brain, that causes acute neurologic symptoms and is often reversible if the trigger is fixed.

Posterior = back of brain  
Reversible = can improve  
Encephalopathy = brain dysfunction

So PRES basically means:

> sudden, potentially reversible brain dysfunction from swelling in the back of the brain.

---

### What causes it

Classic triggers:

* severe hypertension (high blood pressure)
* eclampsia/preeclampsia (pregnancy-related hypertension with end-organ injury)
* renal failure
* immunosuppressants like cyclosporine or tacrolimus
* chemotherapy
* autoimmune disease/sepsis

---

### Why the findings happen

Normally, brain vessels autoregulate, meaning they tighten or relax to keep blood flow stable.

In PRES:

* blood pressure rises too fast or endothelium (blood vessel lining) gets injured
* cerebral autoregulation fails
* the blood-brain barrier (tight vessel barrier protecting brain tissue) leaks
* fluid enters the brain tissue
* this causes vasogenic edema (fluid leakage into extracellular brain space)

The posterior circulation is more vulnerable because it has less sympathetic tone (less protective vessel constriction).

That is why PRES loves the parieto-occipital lobes (posterior parietal and occipital brain regions).

---

### Symptoms

Think: swollen posterior brain = irritated cortex + visual cortex involvement.

Common findings:

* headache
* seizures
* confusion/altered mental status
* visual symptoms, including cortical blindness (vision loss from occipital cortex dysfunction)

---

### Imaging

MRI (magnetic resonance imaging), especially T2/FLAIR (fluid-attenuated inversion recovery), shows bright vasogenic edema in the posterior white matter, classically bilateral parieto-occipital edema.

Key distinction:

* vasogenic edema = blood-brain barrier leak; often reversible
* cytotoxic edema = cell injury from ischemia; more like infarction/stroke

---

## One-line intuition

PRES is what happens when high pressure or endothelial injury makes posterior brain vessels leak fluid, swelling the occipital/parietal brain and causing seizures, headache, confusion, and visual symptoms.

---

## Pearl 🔥

PRES is strongly associated with eclampsia and severe hypertension, and the classic imaging pattern is bilateral parieto-occipital vasogenic edema.

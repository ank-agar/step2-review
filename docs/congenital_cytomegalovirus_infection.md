### Core idea

Congenital cytomegalovirus infection means a fetus gets infected with CMV (cytomegalovirus) during pregnancy.

CMV (cytomegalovirus) is a herpesvirus, and congenital means present at birth.

So:

> maternal CMV infection crosses the placenta and infects the fetus.

---

### Classic findings

Think: CMV loves the fetal brain, liver/spleen, blood cells, and inner ear.

Common findings:

* sensorineural hearing loss (hearing loss from inner ear or auditory nerve damage)
* periventricular calcifications (calcium deposits around the brain ventricles)
* microcephaly (small head)
* seizures
* hepatosplenomegaly (large liver and spleen)
* jaundice (yellow skin/eyes from bilirubin buildup)
* petechial or blueberry muffin rash (small purple-red spots from bleeding or extramedullary hematopoiesis)
* chorioretinitis (inflammation of the retina and choroid in the eye)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Periventricular calcifications:** CMV infects developing brain tissue near the ventricles, causing injury that later calcifies
* **Sensorineural hearing loss:** CMV damages the inner ear, especially the cochlea (hearing organ)
* **Jaundice/hepatosplenomegaly:** the liver and spleen get inflamed and overworked
* **Petechiae:** low platelets and abnormal blood formation cause tiny skin bleeds
* **Microcephaly/seizures:** fetal brain development is disrupted

---

### Key association

CMV is part of the TORCH infections:

* T = toxoplasmosis
* O = other
* R = rubella
* C = cytomegalovirus
* H = herpes simplex virus

These are infections that can cross the placenta and harm the fetus.

---

## One-line intuition

Congenital CMV is a placental viral infection that injures the fetal brain and inner ear, so think periventricular calcifications + hearing loss.

---

## Pearl 🔥

CMV classically causes **periventricular calcifications**, while toxoplasmosis classically causes **diffuse intracranial calcifications**.

# PCP intoxication

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/35/NMDA_receptor_activation_and_antagonists.PNG)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/97/Phencyclidine_structure.svg)

PCP intoxication means intoxication from **phencyclidine (PCP)**, a **dissociative anesthetic**.

Dissociative = the person feels disconnected from:

* body
* environment
* reality

---

### Core idea

PCP blocks **NMDA receptors (N-methyl-D-aspartate receptors)**, which are glutamate receptors in the brain.

Glutamate is usually an excitatory neurotransmitter, meaning it helps neurons fire.

So PCP is basically:

> NMDA receptor blockade -> weird perception + dissociation + agitation/psychosis

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* **nystagmus** = involuntary rhythmic eye movements
* agitation/violent behavior
* hallucinations
* paranoia
* analgesia = decreased pain sensation
* hypertension = high blood pressure
* tachycardia = fast heart rate
* hyperthermia = high body temperature

The weird combo is:

> psychosis + superhuman-looking agitation + not feeling pain

That happens because PCP disrupts cortical perception while also causing sympathetic activation.

---

### Why nystagmus happens

PCP affects brain circuits involved in eye movement control.

So on exams, **nystagmus is a big clue** because many other agitated intoxications do not have prominent nystagmus.

---

### Why it can be dangerous

Because the patient may be:

* very agitated
* hyperthermic
* hypertensive
* insensitive to pain

This can lead to:

* trauma
* seizures
* rhabdomyolysis (muscle breakdown releasing myoglobin)
* acute kidney injury from myoglobin damaging renal tubules

---

### Treatment

Main treatment is supportive:

* quiet room with low stimulation
* benzodiazepines (sedating anti-anxiety drugs that increase GABA-A receptor activity)
* cooling if hyperthermic
* IV fluids (intravenous fluids) if rhabdomyolysis risk

Avoid escalating stimulation because that can worsen agitation.

---

## One-line intuition

PCP blocks NMDA receptors, so the brain gets disconnected from normal reality processing while the body becomes agitated, hypertensive, and pain-insensitive.

---

## Pearl

For Step 1, **PCP intoxication = nystagmus + violent/agitated behavior + analgesia**.

Differentiate from cocaine/amphetamine intoxication: those also cause hypertension and agitation, but **PCP classically has nystagmus and analgesia**.

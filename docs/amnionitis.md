### Core idea

Amnionitis = inflammation/infection of the **amnion** (the inner fetal membrane around the amniotic fluid).

Usually, when people say **amnionitis**, they basically mean **chorioamnionitis**:

* chorion = outer fetal membrane
* amnion = inner fetal membrane
* -itis = inflammation

So chorioamnionitis = inflammation/infection of the fetal membranes surrounding the baby.

---

### How it happens

Most commonly, bacteria ascend upward:

* vagina/cervix
* through the fetal membranes
* into the amniotic cavity
* causing inflammation of the amnion/chorion

Classic organisms include:

* group B strep
* E. coli
* anaerobes
* genital tract flora

---

### Why the findings happen

The bacteria trigger an acute inflammatory response, so neutrophils rush into the membranes.

That causes:

* **maternal fever:** cytokines (inflammatory signaling proteins) reset the hypothalamic temperature set point
* **uterine tenderness:** inflamed uterus/membranes become painful
* **fetal tachycardia:** fetal heart rate rises from stress/inflammation/fever
* **foul-smelling fluid:** bacterial infection in amniotic fluid
* **preterm labor or rupture of membranes:** inflammation weakens membranes and increases prostaglandins (labor-promoting molecules)

---

### One-line intuition

Amnionitis is basically an ascending bacterial infection that turns the amniotic sac into an inflamed, neutrophil-filled environment.

---

### Big complication

Baby can develop:

* neonatal sepsis (bloodstream infection in a newborn)
* pneumonia
* meningitis (infection/inflammation of the brain and spinal cord coverings)

Mother can develop:

* postpartum endometritis (infection of the uterine lining after delivery)
* sepsis

---

## Pearl 🔥

Histology shows **neutrophils in the chorion/amnion**.

If inflammation extends into the umbilical cord, that is **funisitis**, which suggests a fetal inflammatory response.

# Arterial embolism

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/18076.jpg)

![Image](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/sites/default/files/inline-images/image.jpg)

![Image](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/sites/default/files/inline-images/Atherosclerosis%20diagram.gif)

### Core idea

An arterial embolism is when an embolus (a traveling blockage, usually a blood clot) gets stuck in an artery and suddenly blocks blood flow.

Think:

* thrombus = clot sitting where it formed
* embolus = clot/material that broke off and traveled
* arterial embolism = traveling clot blocks an artery

---

### Why it is dangerous

Arteries carry oxygenated blood to tissues.

So if an artery is blocked:

* less blood gets downstream
* less oxygen reaches tissue
* tissue becomes ischemic (low blood supply/oxygen)
* prolonged ischemia can become infarction (tissue death from lack of blood)

This is why arterial embolism can cause:

* stroke if it blocks a brain artery
* myocardial infarction (heart attack) if it blocks a coronary artery
* acute limb ischemia (sudden low blood flow to an arm/leg) if it blocks a limb artery
* renal infarct if it blocks a kidney artery
* mesenteric ischemia if it blocks intestinal arteries

---

### Common source

Classic Step 1 source:

* atrial fibrillation, or AFib (irregular atrial rhythm)

Why?

In AFib, the atria (upper heart chambers) do not contract smoothly, so blood can stagnate, especially in the left atrial appendage (small pouch of the left atrium).

Stagnant blood is more likely to clot.

Then that clot can travel:

left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → systemic arteries → brain/limb/kidney/etc.

---

### How it presents

Because emboli block suddenly, symptoms are often sudden too.

For limb embolism, think the 6 Ps of acute limb ischemia:

* pain
* pallor (pale skin)
* pulselessness
* paresthesia (tingling/numbness)
* paralysis
* poikilothermia (cold limb)

The key intuition:

> A sudden arterial blockage makes the downstream tissue cold, painful, weak, numb, and pulseless.

---

### Thrombus vs embolus

Thrombus:

* forms in the vessel
* stays attached there

Embolus:

* breaks loose
* travels through blood
* lodges somewhere narrower

So an embolus is basically a traveling thrombus until it gets stuck.

---

## One-line intuition

Arterial embolism is a traveling clot that plugs an artery, suddenly starving downstream tissue of oxygen.

---

## Pearl 🔥

AFib (atrial fibrillation) is a high-yield cause of embolic stroke because clots form in the left atrium and then shoot into the systemic arterial circulation.

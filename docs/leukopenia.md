### Core idea

Leukopenia means **low white blood cell count**.

Break the word apart:

* leuko- = white
* -penia = deficiency/low amount

So:

> leukopenia = too few leukocytes (white blood cells)

White blood cells are immune defense cells, so when they are low, the big concern is:

* increased infection risk

---

### What cells are low?

Leukopenia can mean low counts of any white blood cell type, but Step 1 usually cares most about:

* neutrophils

Low neutrophils specifically is called **neutropenia**.

Neutrophils are the main fast responders against bacteria and fungi, so neutropenia especially increases risk for:

* bacterial infections
* fungal infections

---

### Why it happens

Think of white blood cells as coming from the bone marrow factory.

Leukopenia can happen because:

* the marrow is not making enough white blood cells
* white blood cells are being destroyed
* white blood cells are being trapped/sequestered in places like the spleen

Common causes:

* chemotherapy or radiation suppressing bone marrow
* viral infections
* autoimmune destruction
* aplastic anemia, which is bone marrow failure
* leukemia or marrow infiltration crowding out normal marrow
* drugs, like clozapine or some antibiotics
* severe infection/sepsis using up immune cells

---

### Key lab idea

You usually notice leukopenia on a CBC (complete blood count).

Then you look at the differential, which breaks white blood cells into types:

* neutrophils
* lymphocytes
* monocytes
* eosinophils
* basophils

For infection risk, the most important number is often the ANC (absolute neutrophil count), which tells you the true neutrophil count.

---

## One-line intuition

Leukopenia = low white blood cells, so the immune system has fewer “soldiers” available to fight infection.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Neutropenia is especially high-yield: fever + neutropenia after chemotherapy is a medical emergency because the patient may not have enough neutrophils to contain bacteria or fungi.

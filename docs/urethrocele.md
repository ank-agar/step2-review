## Urethrocele

![Image](https://content.healthwise.net/resources/14.8/en-us/media/medical/hw/h9991322_004.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/9064.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4f/Urethral_Sling.jpg)

### Core idea

A **urethrocele** is when the **urethra** (tube that carries urine from the bladder to outside the body) drops from its normal position and bulges into the front wall of the vagina.

Think:

* **urethra loses support**
* it sags downward
* it pushes into the anterior vaginal wall

Anterior means front.

---

### Why it happens

Normally, the urethra is held up by:

* pelvic floor muscles
* connective tissue
* ligaments

If that support gets weak, the urethra can prolapse.

Common reasons:

* pregnancy/vaginal delivery
* aging
* low estrogen after menopause
* chronic increased abdominal pressure, like coughing, constipation, or heavy lifting

Low estrogen matters because estrogen helps keep urogenital tissue strong and elastic, so low estrogen makes the support tissue thinner/weaker.

---

### Symptoms

Can cause:

* vaginal pressure/fullness
* feeling of a bulge
* urinary incontinence (unintentional urine leakage)
* urinary retention (trouble emptying the bladder)
* irritation or discomfort

Mild cases can be asymptomatic (no symptoms).

---

### Important association

Urethrocele often occurs with **cystocele** (bladder prolapse into the anterior vaginal wall).

Together this is called **cystourethrocele**:

* cysto- = bladder
* urethro- = urethra
* -cele = herniation/bulging

So cystourethrocele = bladder + urethra both sagging forward into the vagina.

---

## One-line intuition

A urethrocele is basically the urethra losing its pelvic support and bulging into the front wall of the vagina.

---

## HY pearl

Pelvic organ prolapse terms are named by **what structure is bulging**:

* cystocele = bladder
* rectocele = rectum
* enterocele = small bowel
* urethrocele = urethra

### Core idea

Renal tuberculosis is tuberculosis infection of the kidney, usually from **Mycobacterium tuberculosis** spreading through the blood from a prior lung infection.

Think:

> TB (tuberculosis) hides in the body, then later reactivates and seeds the kidney.

It often affects:

* renal cortex (outer kidney tissue)
* papillae (tips of the medullary pyramids that drain urine)
* calyces (urine-collecting cups)
* renal pelvis and ureter later

---

### Why the findings happen

TB causes **granulomas** (organized immune cell clusters trying to wall off infection).

Those granulomas can undergo **caseous necrosis** (cheese-like tissue death), so the kidney gets:

* scarring
* cavitary lesions
* papillary destruction
* ureteral strictures
* calcification in late disease

As the collecting system gets inflamed and scarred:

* urine flow becomes abnormal
* calyces can dilate
* the kidney can become poorly functional

---

### Classic urine finding

Classic clue:

* **sterile pyuria**

Meaning:

* pyuria = white blood cells in urine
* sterile = routine bacterial culture is negative

So:

> WBCs (white blood cells) in urine, but no regular bacteria grow.

Why? Because TB needs special acid-fast testing/culture, so it may not show up on normal urine culture.

---

### Symptoms

Can look like a chronic UTI (urinary tract infection):

* dysuria (painful urination)
* frequency
* hematuria (blood in urine)
* flank pain

But unlike a typical UTI, routine cultures may be negative.

---

## One-line intuition

Renal TB is a slow, destructive TB infection of the kidney that causes granulomas, caseous necrosis, scarring, and sterile pyuria.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, think **renal TB = sterile pyuria + acid-fast bacilli**.

Acid-fast bacilli are bacteria that retain special stain because of their waxy mycolic acid cell wall, classic for Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

### Core idea

A tympanic membrane perforation is a hole or tear in the tympanic membrane (eardrum).

The tympanic membrane normally separates:

* external auditory canal (outer ear canal)
* middle ear

So if it gets perforated, the middle ear is more exposed to:

* water
* bacteria
* trauma

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Conductive hearing loss:** the eardrum cannot vibrate normally, so sound conduction to the ossicles (middle ear bones) is reduced
* **Ear drainage:** middle ear infection fluid can drain out through the hole
* **Pain that suddenly improves:** pressure from acute otitis media (middle ear infection) can rupture the eardrum, releasing pressure
* **Vertigo/tinnitus sometimes:** inner ear irritation can cause vertigo (spinning sensation) or tinnitus (ringing sound)

---

### Common causes

* acute otitis media (middle ear infection)
* trauma, like cotton swabs or slap/blast injury
* barotrauma (pressure injury), like diving or flying
* tympanostomy tubes (ear tubes)

---

### One-line intuition

The eardrum is supposed to be a vibrating barrier; a perforation makes it a broken barrier, so hearing drops and infections/drainage become easier.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Tympanic membrane perforation causes **conductive hearing loss**, because the problem is sound transmission through the outer/middle ear, not the cochlea (inner ear hearing organ).

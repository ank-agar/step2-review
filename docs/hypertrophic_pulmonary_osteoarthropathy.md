Hypertrophic pulmonary osteoarthropathy, or HPOA (hypertrophic pulmonary osteoarthropathy), is a paraneoplastic syndrome (symptoms caused by cancer effects, not direct tumor invasion) classically associated with lung cancer.

It causes a triad:

* digital clubbing (bulbous enlargement of fingertips/toes with nail angle changes)
* periostitis/periostosis (inflammation/new bone formation along the periosteum, which is the outer lining of bone)
* arthralgia (joint pain)

---

### Core idea

A lung tumor makes growth signals that affect the distal extremities, especially fingers, wrists, ankles, tibia, and fibula.

So the patient gets:

* clubbed fingers
* painful swollen joints
* long-bone pain
* X-ray showing periosteal new bone formation

---

### Why lung cancer causes bone/joint findings

Think:

> lung tumor → increased growth factors → fibroblast/vascular/bone growth in distal limbs

Key mediators include:

* VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor, which promotes blood vessel growth)
* PDGF (platelet-derived growth factor, which promotes connective tissue growth)
* PGE2 (prostaglandin E2, an inflammatory signaling molecule)

These signals cause:

* increased blood flow and soft tissue growth at fingertips → clubbing
* osteoblast activation (bone-forming cell activation) under the periosteum → periostosis
* joint irritation/effusions → arthralgia

---

### Classic Step 1 association

Most important association:

* bronchogenic carcinoma (lung cancer)

Especially think of HPOA when you see:

* smoker
* lung mass
* clubbing
* painful wrists/ankles/knees
* periosteal reaction on X-ray

---

## One-line intuition

HPOA is lung cancer sending growth signals to the extremities, causing clubbed fingers plus painful periosteal new bone formation.

---

## HY pearl

If a patient has clubbing + joint pain + periostosis, do not just call it arthritis. On Step 1, think secondary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy from lung cancer until proven otherwise.

### Core idea

Clostridium perfringens is a:

* gram-positive rod (purple rod-shaped bacterium on Gram stain)
* anaerobe (grows without oxygen)
* spore-former (makes tough dormant survival forms)

It classically causes:

* gas gangrene
* food poisoning with watery diarrhea

---

### Why it causes gas gangrene

Its major toxin is alpha toxin, which is lecithinase/phospholipase C (enzyme that breaks down phospholipids in cell membranes).

So it destroys:

* muscle cell membranes → myonecrosis (muscle death)
* red blood cell membranes → hemolysis (red blood cell rupture)
* tissue blood supply → more anaerobic environment

That dead, low-oxygen tissue lets C. perfringens grow even more.

The bacteria also ferment tissue carbohydrates and make gas, causing crepitus (crackling feeling under the skin from gas).

---

### Why it causes food poisoning

Spores can survive cooking.

If cooked meat/gravy is left warm for a long time, spores germinate into active bacteria.

Then after eating it:

* enterotoxin (gut toxin) damages intestinal epithelial cells
* watery diarrhea and cramps occur
* usually no fever and no vomiting

Timing is usually about 8-16 hours after eating contaminated food.

---

## One-line intuition

C. perfringens is the anaerobic spore-forming rod that uses alpha toxin to melt tissue and gas production to cause gas gangrene.

---

## Pearl

C. perfringens shows a double zone of hemolysis on blood agar because alpha toxin causes incomplete hemolysis and theta toxin causes complete hemolysis.

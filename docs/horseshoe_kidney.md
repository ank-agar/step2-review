## Horseshoe kidney

![Image](https://icons.cdn.biorender.com/w550xh620/648c7b4dcbd10400203ae436/648c7b09cbd10400203ae431.png)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482385/bin/horseshoe.jpg)

### Core idea

A horseshoe kidney is when the two kidneys fuse together, usually at their lower poles, forming a U-shape.

The fused middle part is called the **isthmus** (bridge of tissue connecting the two kidneys).

---

### Why it happens

During embryologic development, the kidneys normally:

* start low in the pelvis
* ascend upward into the abdomen
* rotate into their normal position

In horseshoe kidney, the lower poles fuse early.

Then, as the fused kidney tries to ascend, it gets stuck under the **inferior mesenteric artery** (artery supplying the distal colon).

So the kidney stays:

* lower than normal
* abnormally rotated
* fused in the midline

---

### Why it causes problems

The abnormal shape can mess up urine drainage.

Poor drainage means urine sits around, which increases risk of:

* hydronephrosis (swelling of the kidney from backed-up urine)
* kidney stones
* urinary tract infections
* ureteropelvic junction obstruction, or UPJ obstruction (blockage where the renal pelvis drains into the ureter)

The basic idea:

> weird anatomy -> weird urine flow -> stasis -> stones/infection/obstruction

---

### Important associations

Horseshoe kidney is classically associated with:

* Turner syndrome (45,X disorder causing gonadal dysgenesis and short stature)
* trisomy 18, also called Edwards syndrome (extra chromosome 18)

---

## One-line intuition

The kidneys fused at the bottom, tried to rise, got caught under the inferior mesenteric artery, and ended up low + U-shaped.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Horseshoe kidney is the most common renal fusion anomaly, and on Step 1 the key embryology clue is **failure of normal ascent because the fused kidney gets trapped under the inferior mesenteric artery**.

## Intestinal obstruction

![Image](https://content.healthwise.net/resources/14.8/en-us/media/medical/hw/h9991434_002.jpg)

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/18144/N01500-H.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/1169.jpg)

### Core idea

Intestinal obstruction = something blocks normal movement through the intestine.

That means food, fluid, gas, and secretions cannot move forward normally.

It can happen in:

* small bowel
* large bowel

And it can be:

* **partial:** some contents still pass
* **complete:** nothing passes

---

### Intuition

Think of the bowel like a tube.

If the tube gets blocked, everything before the blockage backs up.

So proximal bowel (bowel before the blockage) becomes:

* dilated
* full of gas/fluid
* painful from stretching

Distal bowel (bowel after the blockage) becomes:

* decompressed/collapsed
* empty

---

### Classic symptoms

* crampy abdominal pain
* vomiting
* abdominal distension
* constipation
* obstipation (inability to pass stool or gas)

Why vomiting happens:

The intestine keeps secreting fluid and trying to push contents forward, but the blockage prevents passage, so material backs up.

---

### Common causes

Small bowel obstruction is commonly caused by:

* adhesions (scar tissue bands, usually after abdominal surgery)
* hernias
* tumors
* volvulus (twisting of bowel)
* intussusception (one segment of bowel telescopes into another)

Large bowel obstruction is commonly caused by:

* colorectal cancer
* volvulus
* diverticular strictures (narrowing after diverticulitis)

---

### Imaging idea

On abdominal X-ray, obstruction can show:

* dilated bowel loops
* air-fluid levels

Air-fluid levels happen because gas rises above fluid inside the blocked, dilated bowel loops.

---

### Big danger

If pressure builds too much, blood flow to the bowel wall can drop.

That can cause:

* ischemia (low blood flow)
* necrosis (tissue death)
* perforation (hole in bowel wall)
* peritonitis (inflammation/infection of the abdominal lining)

That is why severe pain, fever, tachycardia (fast heart rate), or peritoneal signs are concerning.

---

## One-line intuition

Intestinal obstruction is a blocked bowel tube, so contents pile up before the blockage and the bowel swells with gas/fluid.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Prior abdominal surgery + crampy abdominal pain + vomiting + distension = think adhesive small bowel obstruction, because adhesions are the most common cause of small bowel obstruction in adults.

## Laryngeal obstruction

Laryngeal obstruction means blockage/narrowing at the **larynx** (voice box), which is the airway area containing the vocal cords.

So air is trying to move through a tight opening near the vocal cords.

---

### Core idea

The larynx is a small, high-resistance part of the upper airway.

If it gets narrowed by:

* swelling/edema
* laryngospasm (sudden vocal cord closure)
* vocal cord paralysis
* tumor
* foreign body
* congenital narrowing

then airflow becomes turbulent and noisy.

That noisy upper-airway sound is called **stridor** (high-pitched breathing sound from upper airway obstruction).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Dyspnea (shortness of breath):** less air can pass through the larynx
* **Stridor:** air rushing through a narrowed upper airway becomes turbulent
* **Hoarseness:** the vocal cords are in the larynx, so obstruction often affects voice
* **Retractions/accessory muscle use:** the patient has to generate more negative pressure to pull air through the narrowed opening
* **Hypoxia (low oxygen) if severe:** obstruction limits ventilation

---

### Important distinction

Laryngeal obstruction is usually an **upper airway obstruction**.

That means symptoms often sound worse during **inspiration** (breathing in), because negative pressure can pull floppy or swollen upper-airway tissue inward.

---

## One-line intuition

Laryngeal obstruction = the “voice box doorway” to the trachea is too narrow, so air squeaks through it.

---

## Pearl 🔥

On spirometry, **variable extrathoracic obstruction** (obstruction outside the chest, like at the larynx) classically causes flattening of the **inspiratory limb** of the flow-volume loop.

Ulcerative colitis is a type of IBD (inflammatory bowel disease) where there is chronic inflammation and ulceration of the colon mucosa.

Key idea:

* starts in the rectum
* spreads proximally in a continuous pattern
* only involves colon/rectum
* inflammation is mainly mucosal/submucosal, not transmural

Break the name apart:

* ulcerative = forms ulcers
* colitis = inflammation of the colon

So:

> ulcers + inflammation in the colon, starting at the rectum and spreading continuously upward.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* Bloody diarrhea: inflamed mucosa ulcerates and bleeds
* Urgency/tenesmus: rectal inflammation makes the patient feel like they constantly need to poop
* Abdominal cramps: irritated colon spasms
* Anemia: chronic blood loss from the GI (gastrointestinal) tract

Tenesmus means the feeling of needing to defecate even when little or nothing comes out.

---

### Classic findings

Endoscopy can show:

* erythema (redness)
* friable mucosa (bleeds easily)
* superficial ulcers
* pseudopolyps

Histology can show:

* crypt abscesses
* mucosal inflammation
* distorted crypt architecture

A crypt abscess is neutrophils collected inside an intestinal gland/crypt.

---

### UC vs Crohn

Ulcerative colitis:

* continuous lesions
* rectum involved
* colon only
* mucosal/submucosal inflammation
* no fistulas usually

Crohn disease:

* skip lesions
* mouth-to-anus involvement
* transmural inflammation
* fistulas/strictures common

Intuition:
UC is like a continuous carpet burn on the inner lining of the colon, starting at the rectum.

---

### Important associations

Associated with:

* primary sclerosing cholangitis, PSC (inflammation/fibrosis of bile ducts)
* toxic megacolon
* colorectal cancer risk

The colorectal cancer risk increases with longer disease duration and greater extent of colon involvement.

---

## One-line intuition

Ulcerative colitis is continuous mucosal inflammation of the colon that starts at the rectum and causes bloody diarrhea.

---

## Pearl 🔥

UC is associated with p-ANCA (perinuclear anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies), while Crohn disease is more associated with ASCA (anti-Saccharomyces cerevisiae antibodies).

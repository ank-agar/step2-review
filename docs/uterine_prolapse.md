## Uterine prolapse

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/18236/N01633-H_thumbnail.jpg)

### Core idea

Uterine prolapse = the uterus (womb) drops down into the vagina because the pelvic floor/support tissues become weak.

Think:

* uterus normally sits high in pelvis
* ligaments + pelvic floor muscles hold it up
* if support weakens, gravity + pressure pull it downward

---

### Why it happens

The uterus is supported by:

* pelvic floor muscles
* uterosacral/cardinal ligaments (connective tissue supports of the uterus)
* vaginal wall support

If those weaken, the uterus descends.

Common causes/risk factors:

* childbirth trauma/stretching
* older age
* menopause, because lower estrogen (sex hormone that helps maintain vaginal/pelvic tissue) weakens support tissue
* chronic increased intra-abdominal pressure (pressure inside the abdomen), like chronic cough, constipation, obesity, or heavy lifting

---

### Symptoms

Patients may feel:

* pelvic pressure/heaviness
* a bulge coming out of the vagina
* urinary problems, like retention (trouble emptying bladder) or incontinence (leaking urine)
* constipation or difficulty with bowel movements
* low back/pelvic discomfort

Severe prolapse can protrude outside the vaginal opening.

---

### Treatment idea

Mild cases:

* pelvic floor exercises
* lifestyle changes to reduce pressure

More symptomatic cases:

* pessary (removable device placed in the vagina to support prolapsed organs)
* surgery if needed

---

## One-line intuition

The uterus is not the problem by itself - the support underneath it fails, so the uterus sinks downward like a poorly supported tent pole.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Uterine prolapse is a type of pelvic organ prolapse, and it is strongly associated with multiparity (having given birth multiple times), because vaginal deliveries stretch and weaken pelvic floor support.

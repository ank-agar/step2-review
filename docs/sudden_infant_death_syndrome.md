### Core idea

Sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS) is the sudden unexplained death of an infant less than 1 year old, usually during sleep.

Key word:

* unexplained

Meaning: after history, autopsy (exam after death), and scene investigation, no clear cause is found.

---

### Intuition

Think of SIDS as a failure of the baby to respond properly to stress during sleep.

Normally, if a baby has low oxygen or high carbon dioxide (CO2, carbon dioxide), the brainstem (part of the brain that controls breathing and heart rate) should wake the baby up or increase breathing.

In SIDS, the baby may have an underlying vulnerability in arousal/breathing control, so they do not rescue themselves.

---

### Triple-risk model

SIDS is classically explained by 3 things happening together:

* vulnerable infant
* critical developmental period
* outside stressor

Examples of outside stressors:

* prone sleeping (sleeping on the stomach)
* soft bedding
* overheating
* smoke exposure
* bed sharing

So the idea is:

> vulnerable baby + risky sleep stressor + early infancy = SIDS risk

---

### Why sleep position matters

Prone sleeping can increase rebreathing.

Rebreathing means the baby breathes in previously exhaled air, so:

* oxygen goes down
* carbon dioxide (CO2) goes up
* the baby should wake up and move

But if the arousal system is abnormal, the baby may not wake up.

That is why back sleeping lowers risk.

---

### Risk factors

Important risk factors include:

* prone sleep position
* soft bedding or loose blankets
* maternal smoking
* prematurity
* low birth weight
* overheating
* bed sharing

Protective factors include:

* supine sleeping (sleeping on the back)
* firm, flat sleep surface
* no loose bedding or soft objects
* room sharing without bed sharing
* breastfeeding
* pacifier use during sleep

---

## One-line intuition

SIDS is when a vulnerable infant cannot wake up or breathe harder in response to a sleep-related stressor.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

Most important prevention point: place infants supine (on the back) to sleep on a firm, flat surface with no loose bedding.

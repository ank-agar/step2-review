### Core idea

Hypoxia means **low oxygen at the tissue level**.

So the key word is:

* **hypoxia = tissues are not getting/using enough O2 (oxygen)**

This is slightly different from **hypoxemia (low oxygen in arterial blood)**.

You can have hypoxemia causing hypoxia, but hypoxia can also happen even if blood oxygen is okay.

---

### Main types

Think of oxygen delivery like a delivery chain:

**lungs get O2 -> blood carries O2 -> heart/blood flow delivers O2 -> cells use O2**

Hypoxia happens when any step breaks.

* **Hypoxemic hypoxia:** low O2 in blood, usually from lung disease or high altitude
* **Anemic hypoxia:** not enough Hb (hemoglobin), so blood cannot carry enough O2
* **Stagnant/ischemic hypoxia:** blood flow is too low, so O2 does not reach tissues
* **Histotoxic hypoxia:** tissues cannot use O2 even though it arrives

Classic histotoxic example:

* cyanide poisoning

Cyanide blocks the electron transport chain (mitochondrial ATP-making pathway), so cells cannot use oxygen to make ATP (adenosine triphosphate, the cell's energy molecule).

---

### Why hypoxia causes symptoms

Cells need O2 because oxygen is the final electron acceptor in the electron transport chain.

If O2 is low:

* mitochondria make less ATP
* cells switch more toward anaerobic metabolism (ATP production without enough oxygen)
* lactic acid increases
* high-energy organs fail first

Most sensitive organs:

* brain
* heart

That is why hypoxia can cause:

* confusion
* agitation
* cyanosis (bluish skin from increased deoxygenated hemoglobin)
* tachycardia (fast heart rate)
* dyspnea (shortness of breath)

---

### Easy way to remember the difference

**Hypoxemia = low O2 in blood.**

**Hypoxia = low O2 in tissues.**

Example:

* pneumonia can cause hypoxemia, which then causes hypoxia
* anemia can cause hypoxia without major hypoxemia, because PaO2 (partial pressure of oxygen in arterial blood) may be normal, but total O2 carrying capacity is low

---

## One-line intuition

Hypoxia means the tissue oxygen supply/use is not meeting tissue oxygen demand.

---

## Pearl 🔥

In carbon monoxide poisoning, PaO2 can be normal, but O2 content is low because carbon monoxide binds Hb (hemoglobin) and prevents normal O2 delivery.

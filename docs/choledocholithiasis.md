### Core idea

Choledocholithiasis = gallstone in the common bile duct.

Break it down:

* choledocho- = common bile duct
* lithiasis = stones

So it literally means:

> stones in the common bile duct.

The common bile duct carries bile from the liver/gallbladder into the duodenum (first part of the small intestine).

---

### Why it matters

If a stone blocks the common bile duct, bile cannot drain into the gut.

That causes:

* jaundice because conjugated bilirubin backs up into blood
* dark urine because conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble and gets peed out
* pale stools because less bile pigment reaches the intestine
* RUQ pain, meaning right upper quadrant pain
* increased alkaline phosphatase because bile duct cells are irritated by obstruction

---

### Big complications

If bacteria infect the obstructed bile duct:

* ascending cholangitis = infected bile duct obstruction

Classic Charcot triad:

* fever
* jaundice
* RUQ pain

If the stone blocks near the ampulla of Vater (opening where the bile duct and pancreatic duct drain):

* gallstone pancreatitis can happen

because pancreatic enzymes get trapped and start inflaming the pancreas.

---

### How to think of it

Cholelithiasis = stones in the gallbladder.

Choledocholithiasis = stones escaped into the common bile duct.

So:

* gallbladder stone sitting there = cholelithiasis
* stone stuck in drainage pipe = choledocholithiasis

---

### Treatment idea

ERCP (endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography) can diagnose and treat it.

Endoscopic = scope through the mouth into the GI tract.

Retrograde = going backward up into the bile duct.

Cholangiopancreatography = imaging the bile ducts and pancreatic duct.

Main idea: go in with a scope, open the duct area, and pull the stone out.

---

## One-line intuition

Choledocholithiasis is a gallstone clogging the bile drainage pipe, causing backup of conjugated bilirubin and possible infection/pancreatitis.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, obstructive jaundice from choledocholithiasis gives increased conjugated bilirubin + increased alkaline phosphatase, and Charcot triad points to ascending cholangitis.

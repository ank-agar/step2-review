Prothrombin G20210A mutation is an inherited thrombophilia (tendency to form blood clots).

Break it down:

* prothrombin = factor II (clotting factor 2)
* G20210A = guanine changes to adenine at DNA position 20210
* mutation is in the F2 gene (gene that makes prothrombin)

The core problem:

* mutation causes increased prothrombin
* more prothrombin means more thrombin
* thrombin converts fibrinogen to fibrin
* fibrin stabilizes clots

So this mutation makes blood more likely to clot, especially in veins.

---

### Why it causes clots

Prothrombin is the precursor to thrombin.

Thrombin is like the clot-building enzyme:

* activates fibrinogen into fibrin
* activates platelets
* amplifies the coagulation cascade

So if you have too much prothrombin, you can generate too much thrombin, making the blood hypercoagulable (too prone to clotting).

---

### Classic clinical associations

Increased risk of:

* DVT (deep vein thrombosis; clot in a deep vein, usually leg)
* PE (pulmonary embolism; clot that travels to the lungs)
* pregnancy-related venous clots

It is usually venous, not arterial.

Meaning:

* venous = DVT/PE
* arterial = MI (myocardial infarction/heart attack) or stroke

---

## One-line intuition

Prothrombin G20210A mutation = too much factor II, so the body makes clots too easily.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Prothrombin G20210A is the second most common inherited thrombophilia after factor V Leiden (mutation that makes factor V resistant to degradation by activated protein C).

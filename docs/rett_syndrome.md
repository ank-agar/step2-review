## Rett syndrome

![Image](https://media.springernature.com/m312/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1038%2Fsj.ejhg.5201580/MediaObjects/41431_2006_Article_BF5201580_Fig1_HTML.gif)

![Image](https://media.springernature.com/m312/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1038%2Fsj.ejhg.5201580/MediaObjects/41431_2006_Article_BF5201580_Fig3_HTML.jpg)

Rett syndrome is an **X-linked dominant neurodevelopmental disorder** caused by mutation in **MECP2 (methyl-CpG-binding protein 2)**.

Classic picture:

* mostly girls
* normal early development
* then regression around 6-18 months
* loss of speech
* loss of purposeful hand use
* repetitive hand-wringing or hand-washing movements
* seizures
* autistic features
* breathing abnormalities
* acquired microcephaly (head growth slows after birth)

---

### Core idea

The child starts out looking fairly normal, then the brain fails to maintain normal development because MECP2 is needed for proper gene regulation in neurons.

MECP2 helps control **transcription (copying DNA into RNA)** by binding methylated DNA.

So if MECP2 is mutated:

* neurons do not regulate gene expression normally
* synapses do not mature/function normally
* developmental skills are lost

This is why Rett syndrome is more of a **developmental regression problem**, not a primary muscle problem.

---

### Why it mostly affects girls

It is **X-linked dominant**, meaning the abnormal gene is on the X chromosome and one bad copy can cause disease.

Females have two X chromosomes, so one normal copy can partly buffer the disease.

Males usually have one X chromosome, so a severe MECP2 mutation is often lethal before birth or causes severe neonatal encephalopathy (severe newborn brain dysfunction).

---

### One-line intuition

Rett syndrome = normal baby girl at first, then MECP2 mutation causes loss of language/hand skills plus stereotyped hand-wringing.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, the big clue is:

**girl + normal early development + regression + hand-wringing = Rett syndrome.**

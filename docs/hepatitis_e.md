Hepatitis E is liver inflammation caused by HEV (hepatitis E virus).

Classic idea:

* fecal-oral spread
* contaminated water
* acute hepatitis
* usually self-limited
* dangerous in pregnancy

Break it down:

* hepatitis = inflammation of the liver
* E = enteric-style transmission, meaning it spreads through the gut route
* fecal-oral = stool contamination gets swallowed, usually through water or food

---

### What kind of virus is it?

HEV (hepatitis E virus) is a:

* positive-sense RNA virus
* nonenveloped virus in stool
* icosahedral virus

Positive-sense RNA means its RNA can act like mRNA (messenger RNA), so host ribosomes can translate it directly into viral proteins.

---

### Why does it cause jaundice?

HEV infects the liver, causing hepatocyte (liver cell) injury.

Damaged hepatocytes cannot handle bilirubin normally.

Bilirubin is the yellow heme-breakdown pigment that the liver usually processes and sends into bile.

So bilirubin backs up:

* yellow skin/eyes = jaundice
* dark urine = conjugated bilirubin spilling into urine
* pale stool = less bilirubin reaching the gut

---

### Symptoms

Looks like other acute viral hepatitides:

* fever
* fatigue
* nausea/vomiting
* RUQ pain (right upper quadrant pain)
* jaundice
* dark urine
* clay-colored stool

Many infections are mild or asymptomatic, especially in children.

---

### Why pregnancy is the scary association

Hepatitis E can cause severe acute liver failure in pregnant patients, especially in the third trimester.

The exact reason is not just one thing, but likely involves pregnancy-related immune and hormonal changes that make HEV infection harder to control.

So for Step 1:

> fecal-oral hepatitis + pregnancy = think hepatitis E.

---

### Transmission

Common routes:

* contaminated water in areas with poor sanitation
* undercooked pork, wild boar, deer/venison, or shellfish
* zoonotic spread, meaning animal-to-human transmission

This is similar to HAV (hepatitis A virus) in that both are fecal-oral and usually acute.

Big difference:

* HAV (hepatitis A virus): vaccine available in the US
* HEV (hepatitis E virus): no routine vaccine available in the US

---

## One-line intuition

Hepatitis E is basically a fecal-oral, waterborne acute hepatitis that usually resolves, but becomes high-yield dangerous in pregnancy.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

Hepatitis E is classically associated with very high mortality in pregnant women, especially in the third trimester.

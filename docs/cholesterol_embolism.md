# Cholesterol embolism

### Core idea

Cholesterol embolism = cholesterol crystals break off from an atherosclerotic plaque and travel downstream.

Atherosclerotic plaque = fatty cholesterol-rich buildup inside an artery wall.

Embolus = something that travels through blood and gets stuck somewhere else.

So the simple idea is:

> a plaque gets disturbed, little cholesterol shards shower downstream, and they plug small arteries.

---

### When does it usually happen?

Classically after a vascular procedure, especially:

* cardiac catheterization
* angiography
* vascular surgery
* stenting

Why?

Because the catheter or procedure can scrape/disrupt an atherosclerotic plaque in a large artery, like the aorta, releasing cholesterol crystals into the bloodstream.

---

### Why the findings happen

The cholesterol crystals lodge in small arteries, causing:

* ischemia = low blood flow/oxygen to tissue
* inflammation = immune reaction to the crystals
* organ damage = especially kidney and skin findings

Classic findings:

* blue/purple toes
* livedo reticularis = lace-like purple skin pattern
* kidney injury
* eosinophilia = increased eosinophils, a type of white blood cell
* low complement sometimes = complement proteins get consumed during inflammation

The toes can turn blue even if the foot pulses are still present.

Why?

Because the big arteries are still open, but the tiny downstream arteries are clogged by cholesterol crystals.

---

### Histology clue

Biopsy shows cholesterol clefts.

Clefts = empty slit-like spaces.

The cholesterol crystals dissolve during tissue processing, so you do not see the actual crystals. You see the empty spaces they left behind.

---

## One-line intuition

Cholesterol embolism is like a fragile atherosclerotic plaque “shedding debris” into the blood, causing tiny downstream artery blockages.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

After cardiac catheterization + kidney injury + blue toe/livedo reticularis + eosinophilia = think cholesterol embolism.

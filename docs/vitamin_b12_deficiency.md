## Vitamin B12 deficiency

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Vitamina_B12.png/1280px-Vitamina_B12.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/51/Hypersegmented_neutrophil.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/VitB12DefFull.jpg/500px-VitB12DefFull.jpg)

Vitamin B12 deficiency means low cobalamin (vitamin B12), which causes problems with:

* DNA synthesis
* red blood cell production
* myelin maintenance

So it affects both:

* blood
* nerves

---

### Core idea

B12 is needed to make normal DNA.

If DNA synthesis is slow, cells cannot divide normally.

But the cytoplasm (cell body contents outside the nucleus) keeps growing.

So you get big, immature-looking cells:

* megaloblasts in bone marrow
* macro-ovalocytes on smear
* hypersegmented neutrophils

That is why B12 deficiency causes megaloblastic macrocytic anemia.

Macrocytic anemia = anemia with large red blood cells.

Megaloblastic anemia = anemia from impaired DNA synthesis.

---

### Why the neuro symptoms happen

B12 is also needed for normal myelin (fatty insulation around nerves).

Low B12 can damage:

* dorsal columns: vibration/proprioception (position sense)
* lateral corticospinal tracts: motor control

This is called subacute combined degeneration.

Subacute combined degeneration = degeneration of multiple spinal cord tracts due to B12 deficiency.

Symptoms can include:

* numbness/tingling
* loss of vibration sense
* gait problems
* weakness

---

### Why B12 deficiency happens

Common causes:

* pernicious anemia: autoimmune loss of intrinsic factor
* vegan diet: low intake of animal products
* terminal ileum disease/resection: poor absorption
* gastric bypass or gastrectomy: less intrinsic factor/stomach acid
* Diphyllobothrium latum: fish tapeworm that consumes B12

Intrinsic factor = protein made by gastric parietal cells that helps absorb B12 in the terminal ileum.

Terminal ileum = last part of the small intestine.

---

### Labs

Classic findings:

* increased MCV (mean corpuscular volume)
* decreased B12
* increased homocysteine
* increased methylmalonic acid

Key distinction:

* B12 deficiency: increased homocysteine + increased methylmalonic acid
* folate deficiency: increased homocysteine only

Methylmalonic acid is the big clue that it is B12, not folate.

---

## One-line intuition

B12 deficiency makes DNA synthesis bad, so blood cells become big; it also damages myelin, so nerves malfunction.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Do not give folate alone if B12 deficiency is possible.

Folate can fix the anemia, but it does not fix the neurologic damage, so the patient can keep losing nerve function silently.

### Core idea

An **endocervical polyp** is a usually benign (not cancerous) finger-like growth from the **endocervix** (inner cervical canal).

Break it down:

* **endo-** = inside
* **cervical** = related to the cervix (lower part of the uterus)
* **polyp** = mucosa-lined growth projecting from a surface

So:

> a small soft growth coming from inside the cervical canal.

---

### Why it causes bleeding

These polyps are often:

* fragile
* vascular (blood-vessel-rich)
* hanging on a stalk

So they can bleed with contact, especially after sex or during a pelvic exam.

Classic symptoms:

* intermenstrual bleeding (bleeding between periods)
* postcoital bleeding (bleeding after sex)
* abnormal vaginal discharge
* often asymptomatic (no symptoms)

---

### What it looks like

Usually a small red/pink smooth mass protruding through the cervical os (opening of the cervix).

Important contrast:

* **endocervical polyp** = from cervical canal
* **endometrial polyp** = from endometrium (inner lining of uterus)

---

## One-line intuition

It is like a tiny benign skin tag, but coming from the mucus-producing inner cervix, so it can easily bleed.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Endocervical polyps are usually benign, but they are commonly removed and sent to pathology to rule out malignancy (cancer), especially if there is abnormal bleeding.

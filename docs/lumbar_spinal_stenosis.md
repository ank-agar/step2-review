### Core idea

Lumbar spinal stenosis = narrowing of the spinal canal in the lumbar spine (lower back).

That narrowing compresses the nerve roots (nerves exiting the spinal cord), especially the cauda equina (bundle of lower spinal nerve roots).

---

### Why symptoms happen

The lumbar spinal cord itself usually ends around L1-L2, so lumbar stenosis mostly compresses nerve roots, not the spinal cord.

Compression of nerve roots causes:

* low back pain
* buttock/leg pain
* numbness/tingling
* weakness
* neurogenic claudication

Neurogenic claudication = leg pain/heaviness from nerve compression, usually worse with walking or standing.

---

### Classic clue

Symptoms get worse with lumbar extension (bending the back backward).

Why?

Extension makes the spinal canal even narrower, so the nerves get squeezed more.

Symptoms improve with lumbar flexion (bending forward), like leaning on a shopping cart.

Why?

Flexion opens up the spinal canal and gives the nerve roots more room.

---

### Common causes

Usually degenerative, meaning age-related wear-and-tear:

* facet joint hypertrophy (enlarged arthritic spine joints)
* ligamentum flavum hypertrophy (thickening of a spinal ligament)
* disc bulging
* osteophytes (bone spurs)
* degenerative spondylolisthesis (one vertebra slipping forward from degeneration)

All of these physically crowd the canal.

---

## One-line intuition

Lumbar spinal stenosis is a crowded lower-back spinal canal that squeezes nerve roots, so walking/standing hurts and bending forward helps.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Lumbar spinal stenosis causes neurogenic claudication, which improves with sitting/flexion; peripheral artery disease causes vascular claudication, which improves mainly with rest because the issue is blood flow, not posture.

# T-cell lymphoma

T-cell lymphoma is a cancer of T lymphocytes (immune white blood cells that normally help coordinate or kill infected/abnormal cells).

It is a type of non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL = lymphoma without Reed-Sternberg cells as the defining feature).

Core idea:

* a T cell gets mutations
* it keeps dividing
* it collects in lymph nodes, skin, blood, spleen, or extranodal tissue (tissue outside lymph nodes)

So instead of normal controlled immune surveillance, you get a clonal T-cell population (many cells copied from one abnormal original cell).

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Lymphadenopathy:** malignant T cells expand inside lymph nodes
* **B symptoms:** fever, night sweats, weight loss from inflammatory cytokines (immune signaling proteins)
* **Skin rash/plaques:** cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (CTCL = T-cell lymphoma involving skin), especially mycosis fungoides
* **Abnormal blood cells:** some forms enter blood, like Sezary syndrome

---

### Important examples

Common Step 1-associated types:

* **Mycosis fungoides:** skin-homing malignant T cells
* **Sezary syndrome:** leukemic form of cutaneous T-cell lymphoma with malignant T cells in blood
* **Adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma:** associated with HTLV-1 (human T-cell leukemia virus type 1)
* **Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma:** associated with EBV (Epstein-Barr virus)

Sezary cells have cerebriform nuclei (brain-like folded nuclei).

---

## One-line intuition

T-cell lymphoma = a rogue T lymphocyte clone growing where T cells normally live or travel.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Mycosis fungoides and Sezary syndrome are usually CD4+ (cluster of differentiation 4 positive) T-cell cancers; Sezary syndrome classically has malignant T cells with cerebriform nuclei in the blood.

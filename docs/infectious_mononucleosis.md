# Infectious mononucleosis

### Core idea

Infectious mononucleosis is usually an Epstein-Barr virus infection.

Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) is a herpesvirus that infects B cells through CD21.

CD21 is a receptor on B cells that EBV uses like a doorway.

Classic symptoms:

* fever
* sore throat/pharyngitis (inflamed throat)
* posterior cervical lymphadenopathy (swollen lymph nodes in the back of the neck)
* fatigue
* splenomegaly (enlarged spleen)

---

### Why the findings happen

EBV infects B cells.

Then CD8 T cells attack those infected B cells.

CD8 T cells are cytotoxic T cells, meaning they kill infected cells.

So the big picture is:

> EBV infects B cells, but the huge immune response is mostly CD8 T cells reacting to those B cells.

That explains the blood smear:

* atypical lymphocytes = reactive CD8 T cells
* they look large and irregular because they are activated
* lymphocytosis = increased lymphocytes in the blood

---

### Why the throat and lymph nodes get big

The tonsils and lymph nodes are lymphoid tissue.

Lymphoid tissue means immune-cell-rich tissue.

So when EBV triggers a big lymphocyte response, those areas swell:

* tonsillar enlargement/exudates
* posterior cervical lymph node swelling
* spleen enlargement

The spleen enlarges because it filters blood and contains lots of immune cells.

That is why mono patients should avoid contact sports: splenic rupture can happen.

---

### Diagnosis clues

Common test:

* heterophile antibody test, also called Monospot

Heterophile antibodies are antibodies that cross-react with animal red blood cells.

More specific EBV labs:

* VCA IgM = acute infection
* VCA IgG = current or past infection
* EBNA IgG = past infection

IgM means early antibody. IgG means longer-term antibody.

---

## One-line intuition

Mono is EBV infecting B cells, causing a huge reactive CD8 T-cell response that makes the throat, lymph nodes, spleen, and blood smear look dramatic.

---

## Pearl

If a patient with EBV mono gets ampicillin or amoxicillin, they can develop a diffuse maculopapular rash.

Maculopapular means flat red spots plus small raised bumps.

### Core idea

Tumor lysis syndrome, or **TLS** (tumor lysis syndrome), is when a bunch of cancer cells die quickly and dump their intracellular contents into the blood.

Classic after chemotherapy, especially in fast-growing, chemo-sensitive tumors like:

* Burkitt lymphoma
* acute lymphoblastic leukemia, or **ALL** (acute lymphoblastic leukemia)
* acute myeloid leukemia, or **AML** (acute myeloid leukemia)

But it can also happen spontaneously if the tumor is turning over really fast.

---

### What gets released?

When tumor cells lyse, they release:

* potassium
* phosphate
* nucleic acids, meaning DNA/RNA breakdown products

So labs show:

* **hyperkalemia** = high potassium
* **hyperphosphatemia** = high phosphate
* **hypocalcemia** = low calcium
* **hyperuricemia** = high uric acid

---

### Why hypocalcemia happens

Phosphate goes up because tumor cells release lots of phosphate.

That phosphate binds calcium in the blood.

So:

> phosphate rises, calcium gets pulled down.

This can cause:

* tetany, meaning involuntary muscle contraction
* muscle cramps
* seizures
* prolonged QT interval on ECG (electrocardiogram)

---

### Why uric acid goes up

Tumor cells contain lots of nucleic acids.

Nucleic acids break down into purines.

Purines get converted into uric acid.

So rapid tumor breakdown causes lots of uric acid, which can precipitate in kidney tubules and cause **AKI** (acute kidney injury).

---

### Why it is dangerous

Main dangers:

* **hyperkalemia:** arrhythmias, meaning abnormal heart rhythms
* **hyperphosphatemia + hypocalcemia:** calcium phosphate deposits and seizures/tetany
* **hyperuricemia:** uric acid crystal injury in kidneys
* **AKI:** kidneys cannot clear the extra potassium/phosphate/uric acid, so the cycle worsens

---

### Treatment intuition

The big idea is:

> flush the kidneys, stop uric acid buildup, and correct dangerous electrolytes.

So management includes:

* aggressive IV (intravenous) hydration
* allopurinol, which prevents new uric acid formation
* rasburicase, which breaks down existing uric acid
* dialysis if severe/refractory, meaning not improving with standard treatment

Key difference:

* **allopurinol prevents more uric acid**
* **rasburicase removes uric acid already there**

---

## One-line intuition

Tumor lysis syndrome is cancer cells exploding open, dumping potassium/phosphate/DNA contents into the blood, overwhelming the kidneys and causing life-threatening electrolyte abnormalities.

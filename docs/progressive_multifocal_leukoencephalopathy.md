### Core idea

Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) is a dangerous demyelinating disease (loss of myelin, the insulation around CNS neurons) caused by reactivation of JC virus (John Cunningham virus).

It usually happens in immunocompromised patients, especially:

* AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome)
* transplant patients
* patients on natalizumab, an MS (multiple sclerosis) medication

---

### Break the name apart

* progressive = gets worse over time
* multifocal = many separate spots
* leukoencephalopathy = disease of brain white matter

So:

> a worsening disease with multiple white-matter lesions in the brain.

---

### Why it happens

JC virus infects oligodendrocytes (CNS cells that make myelin).

When oligodendrocytes die:

* myelin breaks down
* white matter gets damaged
* nerve signals slow down or fail

That causes focal neurologic deficits (specific problems based on which brain area is hit), like:

* weakness
* vision problems
* ataxia (poor coordination)
* cognitive decline
* seizures sometimes

---

### Key MRI idea

MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) shows multiple white-matter lesions, classically without much mass effect.

Mass effect means the lesion is pushing nearby brain structures. PML is more like myelin being stripped away than a big expanding tumor/abscess.

---

### Step 1 association

Think:

* AIDS patient
* low CD4 count (cluster of differentiation 4 T-cell count)
* progressive neurologic deficits
* JC virus
* demyelination of CNS (central nervous system) white matter

---

## One-line intuition

PML is JC virus waking up in an immunosuppressed patient and destroying oligodendrocytes, causing patchy white-matter demyelination.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Natalizumab increases PML risk because it blocks leukocyte (white blood cell) adhesion/migration into the CNS, so immune surveillance in the brain drops and JC virus can reactivate.

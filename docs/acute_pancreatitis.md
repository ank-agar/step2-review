# Acute pancreatitis

![Image](https://pancreapedia.org/sites/default/files/fig3_new.jpg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK482468/bin/medical1-01.jpg)

### Core idea

Acute pancreatitis is sudden inflammation of the pancreas.

The key mechanism:

* pancreatic digestive enzymes activate too early
* they start digesting the pancreas itself
* this causes inflammation, edema (swelling), fat necrosis (fat cell death), and sometimes hemorrhage (bleeding)

So the pancreas basically gets injured by its own enzymes.

---

### Why it happens

Most common causes:

* gallstones
* alcohol

Gallstones cause pancreatitis because a stone can block the ampulla of Vater (shared opening where the bile duct and pancreatic duct drain into the duodenum, which is the first part of the small intestine).

That blockage traps pancreatic enzymes inside the pancreas.

Alcohol causes pancreatitis by directly injuring acinar cells (enzyme-producing pancreatic cells) and increasing thick pancreatic secretions that can plug ducts.

---

### Symptoms

Classic presentation:

* severe epigastric pain (upper middle abdominal pain)
* pain radiates to the back
* nausea/vomiting
* worse after eating

Why back pain?

The pancreas is retroperitoneal (behind the peritoneum, which is the abdominal lining), so inflammation can irritate deeper structures and refer pain to the back.

---

### Labs

The big lab finding is:

* increased lipase

Lipase is a pancreatic enzyme that digests fat.

Amylase can also increase, but lipase is more specific for pancreatitis.

Diagnosis usually needs 2 of 3:

* typical abdominal pain
* lipase or amylase more than 3x the upper limit of normal
* imaging showing pancreatitis

---

### Complications

Important complications:

* hypocalcemia (low calcium)
* pancreatic pseudocyst
* acute respiratory distress syndrome, or ARDS (severe inflammatory lung injury)
* shock
* necrotizing pancreatitis (pancreatic tissue death)

Why hypocalcemia?

Fat necrosis releases fatty acids, which bind calcium. This is called saponification (soap-like calcium-fat complex formation), so blood calcium drops.

---

## One-line intuition

Acute pancreatitis = pancreatic enzymes turn on too early and the pancreas starts digesting itself.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Gallstone pancreatitis is suggested by acute pancreatitis plus increased ALT (alanine aminotransferase, a liver enzyme), especially ALT >150, because that points to biliary obstruction.

### Core idea

Hepatitis A is an acute viral infection of the liver caused by HAV (hepatitis A virus).

Key idea:

* fecal-oral spread
* acute infection
* does not become chronic
* preventable with vaccine

Fecal-oral means tiny amounts of infected stool get into someone else's mouth, usually through contaminated food, water, hands, or close contact.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The virus infects the liver, so the liver gets inflamed.

That causes:

* **RUQ pain:** RUQ (right upper quadrant) is where the liver sits
* **jaundice:** yellow skin/eyes from bilirubin buildup
* **dark urine:** bilirubin spills into urine
* **clay-colored stool:** less bilirubin reaches the gut
* **fatigue, fever, nausea:** immune response to viral infection

Bilirubin is the yellow breakdown product from heme (part of hemoglobin in red blood cells).

---

### Transmission intuition

Think:

> Hep A = ate something contaminated.

Classic sources:

* contaminated food or water
* poor hand hygiene
* daycare outbreaks
* travel to areas with poor sanitation
* close personal/sexual contact

---

### Step 1 contrast

Hepatitis A and hepatitis E are the fecal-oral ones.

Hepatitis B, C, and D are mainly blood/body-fluid transmitted.

Also:

* HAV (hepatitis A virus) = acute only
* HBV (hepatitis B virus) and HCV (hepatitis C virus) = can become chronic

---

## One-line intuition

Hepatitis A is a short-term liver infection you get from ingesting contaminated stool particles, so it causes acute hepatitis but no chronic carrier state.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HAV (hepatitis A virus) has **IgM anti-HAV** during acute infection and **IgG anti-HAV** after recovery or vaccination.

IgM = recent infection.

IgG = past exposure/immunity.

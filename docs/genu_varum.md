Genu varum means **bow legs**.

Break it down:

* **genu** = knee
* **varum** = angled inward

So in genu varum:

* the knees bow outward
* the lower legs angle inward
* when the ankles touch, the knees stay separated

---

### Core idea

The legs look like an **O-shape**.

Opposite condition:

* **genu valgum** = knock knees
* legs look more like an **X-shape**

---

### Why it happens

In little kids, mild genu varum can be normal because leg alignment changes as the child grows.

But pathologic genu varum can happen from:

* **rickets** = poor bone mineralization, often from vitamin D deficiency
* **Blount disease** = abnormal growth of the upper inner tibia (shin bone)
* skeletal dysplasias = inherited bone growth disorders
* trauma or asymmetric growth plate injury

---

### Why rickets causes bowing

Vitamin D helps absorb calcium and phosphate.

Low vitamin D -> poor mineralization of growing bone -> bones become soft.

Then body weight pushes on soft leg bones, so they bend outward over time.

That bending gives the classic bow-legged appearance.

---

### How to recognize it

On exam:

* ankles can touch
* knees stay apart
* legs curve outward around the knees

On X-ray:

* the mechanical axis shifts medially
* the medial knee gets more load

That is why chronic genu varum can increase risk of medial knee osteoarthritis.

---

## One-line intuition

Genu varum = the knees bow away from each other, making the legs look like an O.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Normal kids go through this rough alignment sequence:

* infant/toddler: genu varum
* around age 2: legs straighten
* around age 3-4: genu valgum
* by around age 7: adult-like alignment

So persistent, worsening, asymmetric, or severe bowing is what makes you worry about pathology like rickets or Blount disease.

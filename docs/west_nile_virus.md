West Nile virus is a **Flavivirus** (enveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus) spread mainly by **Culex mosquitoes**.

Core cycle:

* bird gets infected
* mosquito bites bird and picks up virus
* mosquito bites human
* human gets infected

Humans and horses are **dead-end hosts** (hosts that do not make enough virus in the blood to pass it back to mosquitoes).

---

### What it causes

Most infections are asymptomatic.

If symptomatic:

* fever
* headache
* body aches
* sometimes rash

Severe disease can cause **neuroinvasive disease** (virus entering the nervous system), especially:

* meningitis (inflammation of meninges, the brain/spinal cord coverings)
* encephalitis (inflammation of brain tissue)
* acute flaccid paralysis (sudden weak/floppy paralysis)

---

### Why neuro symptoms happen

After the mosquito bite, the virus replicates locally, enters blood, and in some patients crosses into the **CNS** (central nervous system).

Once in the CNS, it inflames neurons and supporting tissue, causing:

* altered mental status
* seizures
* tremor/movement problems
* weakness or paralysis

The paralysis can look like poliomyelitis because it can damage anterior horn cells (lower motor neurons in the spinal cord).

---

### Diagnosis

Best test:

* West Nile virus IgM in serum or CSF

IgM (immunoglobulin M, early antibody) means recent infection.

CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) may show:

* lymphocytic pleocytosis (increased lymphocytes in CSF)
* elevated protein
* normal glucose

That pattern is typical for viral CNS infection.

---

### Treatment

Treatment is supportive:

* fluids
* pain/fever control
* respiratory support if severe

No routine antiviral cure and no routine human vaccine.

---

## One-line intuition

West Nile virus is a mosquito-borne Flavivirus that cycles between birds and mosquitoes, but occasionally spills into humans and can inflame the brain/spinal cord.

---

## Pearl 🔥

West Nile virus is a **Flavivirus**, like yellow fever, dengue, Zika, and St. Louis encephalitis virus.

# Mittelschmerz

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f1/Order_of_changes_in_ovary.svg)

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539704/bin/Anatomy_of_the_ovaries.jpg)

Mittelschmerz = midcycle ovulation pain.

Break the word apart:

* mittel = middle
* schmerz = pain

So it literally means:

> middle pain

Meaning pain in the middle of the menstrual cycle, around ovulation.

---

### Core idea

Around day 14 of a typical 28-day cycle, a mature ovarian follicle ruptures and releases an egg.

That rupture can cause:

* one-sided lower abdominal/pelvic pain
* mild cramping or sharp twinge
* sometimes light spotting

Usually it is benign and short-lived.

---

### Why the pain happens

During ovulation:

* the follicle swells
* the follicle ruptures
* a little follicular fluid or blood can leak out
* that fluid irritates the peritoneum (abdominal lining)

That irritation causes the pain.

So the pain is not from the uterus contracting like period cramps. It is more from the ovary/peritoneum getting irritated at the moment of egg release.

---

### Classic clue

Pain is often:

* unilateral (one-sided)
* midcycle
* brief
* alternating sides between cycles sometimes

Why alternating sides?

Because either ovary can ovulate in a given cycle.

---

### Important differential

If the pain is severe, persistent, or associated with fever, heavy bleeding, vomiting, or pregnancy, think beyond mittelschmerz:

* ectopic pregnancy
* appendicitis
* ovarian torsion
* ruptured ovarian cyst
* pelvic inflammatory disease, aka PID (infection of the upper female reproductive tract)

---

## One-line intuition

Mittelschmerz is the "pop" of ovulation causing brief one-sided pelvic pain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Ovulation is triggered by the LH surge, where LH (luteinizing hormone) causes follicle rupture and release of the oocyte (egg cell).

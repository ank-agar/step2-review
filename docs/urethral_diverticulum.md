### Core idea

A urethral diverticulum is a pouch/outpouching from the urethra.

Urethra = tube that carries urine from the bladder to outside the body.

Diverticulum = abnormal sac-like outpouching.

So the intuition is:

> urine can get trapped in a little side-pocket off the urethra.

---

### Why it happens

Most often, especially in females, it comes from infected/blocked periurethral glands.

Periurethral glands = small glands around the urethra.

Process:

* gland gets blocked/infected
* abscess forms
* abscess opens into the urethra
* leftover cavity becomes a urine-collecting pouch

That pouch can keep filling with urine, causing stasis.

Stasis = fluid sitting still instead of draining.

---

### Classic symptoms

Think of the "3 Ds":

* dysuria = painful urination
* dyspareunia = painful sex
* postvoid dribbling = leaking urine after peeing

Can also cause:

* recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection)
* urinary frequency/urgency
* anterior vaginal wall mass
* pus or urine expressed from the urethra when the mass is compressed

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Recurrent UTI:** urine sits in the pouch, so bacteria can grow
* **Dribbling:** trapped urine leaks out after voiding
* **Pain/dysuria:** inflammation and infection irritate the urethra
* **Mass:** the pouch bulges into the anterior vaginal wall

---

### Diagnosis

Best imaging is usually MRI (magnetic resonance imaging), because it shows the soft tissue anatomy and the connection to the urethra.

---

## One-line intuition

A urethral diverticulum is a urine-trapping side pouch off the urethra, classically causing dysuria, dyspareunia, and postvoid dribbling.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Urethral diverticulum can mimic recurrent uncomplicated UTI, so suspect it when someone has repeated urinary symptoms plus postvoid dribbling or an anterior vaginal wall mass.

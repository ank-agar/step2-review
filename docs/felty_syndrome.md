# Felty syndrome

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Blausen_0676_Neutrophil_%28crop%29.png/500px-Blausen_0676_Neutrophil_%28crop%29.png)

Felty syndrome is a complication of long-standing rheumatoid arthritis.

Classic triad:

* rheumatoid arthritis (autoimmune inflammatory joint disease)
* splenomegaly (enlarged spleen)
* neutropenia (low neutrophil count; neutrophils are bacteria-fighting white blood cells)

---

### Core idea

Think:

> Bad rheumatoid arthritis + big spleen + low neutrophils.

The low neutrophils are the scary part because neutrophils protect against bacterial infections.

So patients can get:

* recurrent infections
* mouth ulcers
* skin infections
* fever

---

### Why does this happen?

In rheumatoid arthritis, the immune system is already overactive.

In Felty syndrome, that immune dysregulation can lead to neutrophils being:

* destroyed by autoimmune mechanisms
* trapped/sequestered in the enlarged spleen
* underproduced or poorly maintained by bone marrow immune signaling

So even though the patient has inflammation, the circulating neutrophil count is low.

---

### Why splenomegaly matters

The spleen filters blood and removes blood cells.

If the spleen is enlarged/overactive, it can hold onto or destroy more blood cells than usual.

That contributes to:

* neutropenia (low neutrophils)
* sometimes anemia (low red blood cells)
* sometimes thrombocytopenia (low platelets)

---

## One-line intuition

Felty syndrome is severe rheumatoid arthritis where the immune system plus enlarged spleen drops neutrophils, making infection risk high.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HY Step 1 association:

* rheumatoid arthritis + splenomegaly + neutropenia = Felty syndrome
* rheumatoid arthritis + neutropenia can also resemble T-cell large granular lymphocytic leukemia (T-cell LGL leukemia; a clonal expansion of cytotoxic T cells)

## Melena

![Image](https://s3.amazonaws.com/static.wd7.us/e/ed/Upper_GI_bleeding._Adapted_from_ACG_Clinical_Guideline-_Upper_Gastrointestinal_and_Ulcer_Bleeding.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/12/Melena_Bleeding_DU.jpg)

Melena = black, tarry, sticky, foul-smelling stool from digested blood in the GI tract (gastrointestinal tract).

Classic source:

* upper GI bleed (bleeding above the ligament of Treitz, usually esophagus/stomach/duodenum)

Most common Step 1 cause:

* peptic ulcer disease (ulcer in the stomach or duodenum)

---

### Why it turns black

Blood starts red.

But if bleeding happens high up in the GI tract, the blood has time to travel through stomach acid + digestive enzymes.

That digestion changes hemoglobin (oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells) into darker breakdown products.

So:

* fresh blood = red
* digested blood = black/tarry

That is why melena usually means the bleeding source is higher up.

---

### Melena vs hematochezia

Melena:

* black, tarry stool
* usually upper GI bleed
* blood is digested

Hematochezia (bright red blood per rectum):

* red blood in stool
* usually lower GI bleed
* blood is fresh

But a massive upper GI bleed can move so fast that it can also cause hematochezia.

---

### Common causes

Think: things that bleed in the upper GI tract.

* peptic ulcer disease
* gastritis (stomach inflammation)
* esophagitis (esophagus inflammation)
* esophageal varices (dilated esophageal veins, usually from portal hypertension)
* Mallory-Weiss tear (mucosal tear after forceful vomiting)
* gastric cancer

---

## One-line intuition

Melena is what happens when blood gets “cooked/digested” as it travels through the GI tract, turning it black and tar-like.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Melena classically points to an upper GI bleed, but black stool is not always melena: iron supplements and bismuth subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol) can also make stool black without bleeding.

## Epididymal cyst

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19692.jpg)

![Image](https://www.frontiersin.org/files/Articles/1245842/xml-images/fped-11-1245842-g002.webp)

An epididymal cyst is a benign (not cancerous) fluid-filled sac in the epididymis.

The epididymis is the coiled tube sitting on the back/top of the testicle where sperm mature and are stored.

So the basic idea is:

> a little fluid bubble forms next to the testicle, not inside the testicle.

---

### What it feels like

Usually:

* smooth
* soft or slightly firm
* painless
* separate from the testicle
* often near the top/back of the testicle

That “separate from the testicle” part is important because testicular cancer usually arises within the testicle itself.

---

### Why it happens

The epididymis is made of tiny ducts (small tubes).

If one of those ducts gets blocked or dilated (stretched open), fluid can collect and form a cyst.

If the cyst contains sperm, it is often called a spermatocele.

So:

* epididymal cyst = fluid-filled sac in the epididymis
* spermatocele = similar cyst, but contains sperm

Clinically, they are often discussed together because both are usually benign scrotal lumps.

---

### How it is diagnosed

Ultrasound is the key test.

Ultrasound usually shows a well-defined fluid-filled area near the epididymis, with a normal testicle.

This helps distinguish it from:

* testicular tumor
* hydrocele (fluid around the testicle)
* varicocele (dilated veins, classically like a “bag of worms”)
* epididymitis (inflammation of the epididymis)

---

## One-line intuition

An epididymal cyst is basically a harmless fluid bubble in the sperm-storage tube next to the testicle.

---

## HY pearl 🔥

A scrotal mass that is extratesticular (outside the testicle) is much more likely benign, while an intratesticular (inside the testicle) solid mass is concerning for testicular cancer until proven otherwise.

### Core idea

Opioid use disorder is addiction/dependence on opioids, meaning compulsive opioid use despite harm.

Opioid withdrawal is what happens when the body has adapted to opioids, then the opioid is suddenly removed.

Opioids include:

* morphine
* heroin
* oxycodone
* fentanyl
* methadone

---

### Why opioids are addictive

Opioids bind mu opioid receptors (main opioid receptor for pain relief, euphoria, respiratory depression, and dependence).

In the brain reward pathway:

* opioids inhibit GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid, an inhibitory neurotransmitter)
* less GABA means less inhibition of dopamine neurons
* dopamine increases in the nucleus accumbens

So the simplified chain is:

> opioid -> less GABA brake -> more dopamine reward

That dopamine surge teaches the brain:

> "Do this again."

---

### Why withdrawal happens

With chronic opioid use, the body compensates by increasing its baseline "wake-up/stress" signaling.

A big player is the locus coeruleus, which releases norepinephrine (sympathetic fight-or-flight neurotransmitter).

While opioids are present, they suppress this system.

When opioids are removed:

* the opioid brake disappears
* norepinephrine output rebounds
* the patient gets sympathetic overactivity

So withdrawal is basically:

> body adapted to being sedated, then suddenly overshoots in the opposite direction.

---

### Classic opioid withdrawal findings

Think "everything is turned up and leaking":

* anxiety/agitation
* mydriasis (dilated pupils)
* lacrimation (tearing)
* rhinorrhea (runny nose)
* sweating
* piloerection (goosebumps)
* yawning
* abdominal cramps
* diarrhea
* nausea/vomiting
* muscle aches

Important contrast:

* opioid intoxication -> miosis (pinpoint pupils), respiratory depression, sedation
* opioid withdrawal -> mydriasis, diarrhea, sweating, agitation

---

### Why diarrhea happens

Opioids normally slow the gut.

That is why opioid use causes constipation.

When the opioid is removed, the gut rebounds:

* increased motility
* cramping
* diarrhea

So:

> opioid use = constipation  
> opioid withdrawal = diarrhea

---

### Treatment idea

Medications for opioid use disorder:

* methadone: full mu opioid receptor agonist
* buprenorphine: partial mu opioid receptor agonist
* naltrexone: opioid receptor antagonist

For withdrawal symptoms:

* clonidine or lofexidine can decrease norepinephrine release and reduce sympathetic symptoms

Key idea:

> agonist therapy prevents withdrawal/craving without the rapid high of short-acting opioids.

---

## One-line intuition

Opioids push the brain's reward system and suppress stress/gut activity; withdrawal is the rebound when the opioid brake is suddenly removed.

---

## Pearl

Opioid withdrawal is very uncomfortable but usually not life-threatening by itself, unlike alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal, which can cause seizures and death.

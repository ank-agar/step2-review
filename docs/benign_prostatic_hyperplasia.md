## Benign prostatic hyperplasia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Benign_prostate_hyperplasia.jpg/250px-Benign_prostate_hyperplasia.jpg)

Benign prostatic hyperplasia, or BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia), is non-cancerous enlargement of the prostate gland.

Break it down:

* benign = not malignant/not cancer
* prostatic = involving the prostate
* hyperplasia = increased number of cells

So BPH means:

> more prostate cells than normal, causing the prostate to enlarge.

---

### Core idea

The prostate surrounds the urethra (tube that carries urine out of the bladder).

When the prostate enlarges, it squeezes the urethra.

That causes lower urinary tract symptoms, or LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms):

* weak stream
* hesitancy, meaning trouble starting urination
* dribbling
* incomplete emptying
* urinary frequency
* urgency
* nocturia, meaning waking up at night to urinate

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Think of the prostate like a donut around the urethra.

If the donut gets bigger inward, the hole gets smaller.

So urine has a harder time leaving the bladder:

* weak stream: narrowed urethra limits flow
* hesitancy: bladder must generate more pressure before urine starts
* incomplete emptying: bladder cannot fully overcome the obstruction
* frequency/urgency/nocturia: retained urine irritates and stretches the bladder

Over time, bladder outlet obstruction, or BOO (bladder outlet obstruction), can cause:

* urinary retention
* recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection)
* bladder stones
* hydronephrosis (kidney swelling from backed-up urine) in severe cases

---

### Pathology

BPH mainly involves the transition zone of the prostate, which is the periurethral area around the urethra.

This is why it causes urinary obstruction.

Microscopically, there is proliferation of:

* glands
* stroma, meaning supportive connective tissue/smooth muscle

Grossly, BPH often forms nodules that compress the urethra.

---

### Hormone link

BPH is driven by DHT (dihydrotestosterone), which is made from testosterone by 5-alpha reductase (enzyme that converts testosterone into DHT).

That is why:

* finasteride and dutasteride inhibit 5-alpha reductase
* less DHT means the prostate can shrink over time

Alpha-1 blockers, like tamsulosin, relax smooth muscle in the prostate/bladder neck, so urine flows more easily.

---

## One-line intuition

BPH is a benign, DHT-driven enlargement of the prostate that squeezes the urethra and causes obstructive urinary symptoms.

---

## Pearl 🔥

BPH usually arises in the transition zone, while prostate cancer usually arises in the peripheral zone.

That is why BPH causes urinary obstruction early, but prostate cancer is often detected as a posterior hard nodule on DRE (digital rectal exam).

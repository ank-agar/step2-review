## Prolonged pregnancy

Prolonged pregnancy means pregnancy that goes too long past the expected delivery date.

Usually:

* **late-term** = 41 weeks to 41 weeks + 6 days
* **postterm** = 42 weeks or more

For Step 1, think:

> baby stays in the uterus too long → placenta starts underperforming → fetal stress.

---

### Core idea

The placenta is supposed to deliver:

* oxygen
* nutrients
* waste removal

But after a pregnancy goes too long, the placenta can become less efficient.

That causes:

* uteroplacental insufficiency (poor blood/oxygen delivery between uterus and placenta)
* oligohydramnios (low amniotic fluid)
* fetal hypoxia (low fetal oxygen)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Oligohydramnios:** less fetal urine output because placental insufficiency lowers fetal perfusion
* **Meconium passage:** fetal stress can make the fetus pass meconium (first stool) into amniotic fluid
* **Meconium aspiration:** the baby can inhale meconium-stained fluid, causing respiratory distress after birth
* **Macrosomia:** longer growth time can make the fetus large
* **Shoulder dystocia:** large baby gets stuck at the shoulders during delivery
* **Stillbirth risk:** chronic placental insufficiency can eventually cause severe fetal hypoxia

---

### What the baby may look like

A postmature baby can have:

* dry, peeling skin
* long nails
* less subcutaneous fat
* meconium-stained skin

Intuition:

> the baby is not just "extra developed" — the intrauterine environment is becoming less supportive.

---

## One-line intuition

Prolonged pregnancy = pregnancy past 41-42 weeks where the placenta may start failing, causing low amniotic fluid, fetal stress, meconium, macrosomia, and stillbirth risk.

---

## HY pearl

Postterm pregnancy is classically associated with **oligohydramnios + meconium aspiration + macrosomia/shoulder dystocia**.

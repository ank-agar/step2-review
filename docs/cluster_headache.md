### Core idea

Cluster headache is a severe primary headache disorder with repeated attacks of:

* one-sided pain
* usually around/behind one eye
* plus autonomic symptoms on the same side

Autonomic symptoms means involuntary nervous system symptoms, like:

* tearing
* red eye
* runny/stuffy nose
* sweating/flushing
* ptosis (droopy eyelid)
* miosis (small pupil)

---

### Why it is called "cluster"

The attacks come in clusters:

* daily attacks for weeks to months
* then remission for months or years

So the name is about timing, not because the pain is mild or random.

---

### Why the eye/nose symptoms happen

Cluster headache is a TAC (trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia).

Break that down:

* trigeminal = CN V (cranial nerve 5), sensation from the face
* autonomic = involuntary symptoms like tearing/nasal congestion
* cephalalgia = headache

The trigeminal pain pathway gets activated, especially around V1 (ophthalmic division of the trigeminal nerve), which refers pain to the eye/forehead area.

Then it triggers parasympathetic outflow, causing:

* lacrimation (tearing)
* conjunctival injection (red eye)
* rhinorrhea (runny nose)
* nasal congestion

So:

> trigeminal pain pathway fires + autonomic reflex turns on = one-sided eye pain with tearing/runny nose.

---

### Classic pattern

* severe unilateral orbital/supraorbital/temporal pain
* lasts 15 to 180 minutes
* can happen up to 8 times per day
* often occurs at the same time each day
* patient is often restless/pacing, unlike migraine where patients often want to lie still

---

## One-line intuition

Cluster headache is like the trigeminal nerve screaming around one eye while the autonomic system makes that same side tear, redden, droop, and run.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Acute treatment: 100% oxygen or a triptan, especially sumatriptan.

Prevention: verapamil is the classic first-line preventive drug.

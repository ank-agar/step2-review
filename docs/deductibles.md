### Core idea

A deductible is the amount of money the patient must pay first before insurance starts paying for many covered services.

Insurance = a plan that helps pay medical costs.

Covered services = things the insurance plan agrees are eligible for payment.

---

### Simple example

If your deductible is **$1,000**:

* you pay the first $1,000 of covered medical bills
* after that, insurance starts helping pay
* then you may still owe a copay or coinsurance

Copay = fixed amount you pay, like $30 for a visit.

Coinsurance = percentage you pay, like 20% of the bill.

---

### Intuition

Think of the deductible as the "pay-this-first" threshold.

Before the deductible:

* patient pays most/all of the allowed cost

After the deductible:

* insurance starts sharing the cost

Allowed cost = the price the insurance plan recognizes for that service.

---

### Why deductibles matter clinically

High deductibles can make patients delay care because they know they must pay more upfront.

So if a patient says:

> "I cannot afford the test until my deductible is met"

that means they may still be responsible for a large amount before insurance helps much.

---

### Deductible vs premium vs out-of-pocket max

Premium = monthly payment to keep insurance active.

Deductible = amount paid before insurance starts sharing many costs.

Out-of-pocket maximum = the most the patient has to pay in a year for covered in-network care; after that, insurance usually pays 100%.

In-network = doctors/hospitals contracted with the insurance plan.

---

## One-line intuition

A deductible is the amount you pay first before insurance really starts helping with many medical bills.

---

## Pearl

Preventive care often does **not** require meeting the deductible first, meaning things like screening/vaccines may be covered earlier to encourage prevention.

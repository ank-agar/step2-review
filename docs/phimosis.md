# Phimosis

![Image](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil///PHIL_Images/17347/17347_lores.jpg)

Phimosis = the foreskin cannot be pulled back over the glans penis (head of the penis).

Break it down:

* foreskin = skin covering the glans
* glans = head/tip of the penis
* retract = pull back

So phimosis is basically:

> tight foreskin opening → glans cannot be exposed easily

---

### Core idea

The opening of the foreskin is too narrow or scarred, so it acts like a tight ring around the tip of the penis.

That can cause:

* trouble retracting the foreskin
* pain with erections
* ballooning of the foreskin during urination
* recurrent balanitis (inflammation of the glans)
* hygiene problems

---

### Physiologic vs pathologic

Physiologic phimosis = normal tight/nonretractable foreskin in young boys.

This is common because the foreskin is naturally attached to the glans early in life and usually loosens over time.

Pathologic phimosis = abnormal tightness due to scarring/inflammation.

Common causes:

* repeated balanitis (inflammation of the glans)
* poor hygiene
* diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar disease)
* lichen sclerosus, also called balanitis xerotica obliterans (scarring inflammatory skin disease)

---

### Why diabetes matters

Diabetes increases glucose (sugar) in urine and tissues, which helps microbes grow and increases infections/inflammation.

Repeated inflammation → scarring of foreskin → tighter opening → phimosis.

---

### Big complication to know

Phimosis can predispose to paraphimosis.

Paraphimosis = foreskin gets stuck behind the glans and cannot return forward.

That is an emergency because the tight foreskin ring can block venous/lymphatic drainage, causing swelling and possible ischemia (poor blood flow).

---

## One-line intuition

Phimosis is a tight foreskin opening that cannot slide back over the glans.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Phimosis = foreskin cannot be retracted over the glans.

Paraphimosis = retracted foreskin cannot be put back over the glans, and this is a urologic emergency.

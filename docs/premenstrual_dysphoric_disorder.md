## Premenstrual dysphoric disorder

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/MenstrualCycle2_en.svg/1280px-MenstrualCycle2_en.svg.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/24368.jpg)

### Core idea

Premenstrual dysphoric disorder, or **PMDD (premenstrual dysphoric disorder)**, is basically **severe PMS (premenstrual syndrome)** with major mood symptoms.

It happens in the **luteal phase** of the menstrual cycle, meaning the phase **after ovulation and before menstruation**.

Symptoms usually:

* start in the week or two before the period
* improve a few days after bleeding starts
* are minimal/absent after the period

---

### Main symptoms

PMDD can cause:

* severe irritability/anger
* depression or hopelessness
* anxiety/tension
* mood swings
* difficulty concentrating
* fatigue
* sleep/appetite changes
* breast tenderness, bloating, headache, cramps

If suicidal thoughts happen, that is urgent. In the United States, call or text **988** for immediate crisis help.

---

### Why it happens

The key idea is **not usually abnormal hormone levels**.

It is more like:

> the brain is overly sensitive to normal estrogen/progesterone changes.

After ovulation:

* progesterone rises
* estrogen/progesterone later fall before the period
* serotonin signaling can be affected

**Serotonin** is a neurotransmitter, meaning a brain chemical, involved in mood, sleep, and appetite.

So the hormone shift can trigger mood symptoms in susceptible people.

---

### PMS vs PMDD

**PMS (premenstrual syndrome):**

* milder
* more physical symptoms
* annoying but usually less disabling

**PMDD (premenstrual dysphoric disorder):**

* severe mood symptoms
* disrupts school/work/relationships
* can look like major depression, but it cycles with the menstrual cycle

---

## One-line intuition

PMDD is a cyclic mood disorder where normal pre-period hormone changes trigger severe emotional symptoms.

---

## Pearl 🔥

PMDD symptoms occur in the **luteal phase** and improve after menstruation starts; treatment can include **SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors)** and certain combined **OCPs (oral contraceptive pills)**.

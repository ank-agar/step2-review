Hirsutism = excess **terminal hair** growth in women in a **male-pattern distribution**.

Terminal hair means thick, dark, coarse hair, not fine “peach fuzz” hair.

Common areas:

* upper lip/chin
* chest
* abdomen
* back
* inner thighs

---

### Core idea

Androgens (male-type sex hormones, like testosterone) stimulate hair follicles in certain body areas.

So if androgen levels are high, or the hair follicles are extra sensitive to androgens:

* fine vellus hair becomes thick terminal hair
* hair grows in androgen-sensitive areas
* the pattern looks more “male-type”

---

### Most common cause

Think:

* **PCOS** = polycystic ovarian syndrome

PCOS causes increased androgen production from the ovaries, leading to:

* hirsutism
* acne
* irregular periods
* infertility/anovulation

---

### Other important causes

* adrenal tumor
* ovarian tumor
* congenital adrenal hyperplasia
* Cushing syndrome
* androgenic medications

Rapid-onset hirsutism or virilization is more concerning for a tumor.

Virilization means masculinization, such as:

* deep voice
* clitoromegaly
* increased muscle mass
* male-pattern balding

---

### Diagnosis clue

Hirsutism is graded with the **Ferriman-Gallwey score**, which checks hair growth in androgen-sensitive body areas.

Higher score = more severe hirsutism.

---

## One-line intuition

Hirsutism is basically androgen-driven conversion of soft peach-fuzz hair into thick dark hair in male-pattern areas.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, hirsutism + irregular menses strongly points to **PCOS** (polycystic ovarian syndrome), which is associated with increased LH (luteinizing hormone), insulin resistance, and increased androgens.

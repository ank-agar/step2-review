# Schizophrenia

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Dopaminergic_pathways.svg/500px-Dopaminergic_pathways.svg.png)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/17239.jpg)

Schizophrenia is a chronic psychotic disorder where a person loses touch with reality and has problems with:

* perception
* thinking
* emotion
* behavior

Psychosis means impaired reality testing, like hallucinations or delusions.

---

### Core idea

Think:

> too much dopamine in the wrong brain pathway causes false salience.

False salience means the brain assigns too much meaning to random things.

So normal stimuli can feel deeply meaningful, threatening, or connected.

That helps explain:

* **delusions:** fixed false beliefs
* **hallucinations:** sensory perceptions without a real external stimulus
* **disorganized speech/behavior:** thoughts are not being organized normally

---

### Positive vs negative symptoms

**Positive symptoms** = extra things added:

* hallucinations
* delusions
* disorganized speech
* disorganized or catatonic behavior

**Negative symptoms** = normal things taken away:

* flat affect (reduced emotional expression)
* alogia (reduced speech)
* avolition (reduced motivation)
* anhedonia (reduced pleasure)
* social withdrawal

Intuition:

* positive = brain is generating extra abnormal experiences
* negative = normal motivation/emotion/speech circuits are underactive

---

### Why dopamine matters

High dopamine activity in the **mesolimbic pathway** causes many positive symptoms.

Break it down:

* meso- = midbrain
* limbic = emotion/reward system

So the mesolimbic dopamine pathway is basically a reward/meaning pathway.

If it is overactive, the brain may treat random thoughts or sounds as important, producing delusions and hallucinations.

---

### Treatment idea

Antipsychotics usually block **D2 receptors** (dopamine type 2 receptors).

Why that helps:

* less dopamine signaling
* less false salience
* fewer positive psychotic symptoms

But blocking dopamine elsewhere can cause side effects, especially movement problems, because dopamine is also needed in motor pathways.

---

### Diagnosis timing

For schizophrenia, symptoms must last at least **6 months**, with at least **1 month** of active-phase symptoms like delusions, hallucinations, or disorganized speech.

---

## One-line intuition

Schizophrenia is a chronic psychotic disorder where abnormal dopamine signaling makes the brain misinterpret reality and assign false meaning to thoughts, sounds, or events.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

Schizophrenia is associated with **increased dopamine** in the mesolimbic pathway, while **NMDA receptors** (N-methyl-D-aspartate glutamate receptors) may be underactive.

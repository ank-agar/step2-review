### Core idea

Cleft lip is a congenital defect (present at birth) where the upper lip does not fully fuse during development.

So instead of a continuous upper lip, there is a gap/split.

It can be:

* unilateral = one side
* bilateral = both sides
* complete = extends up into the nostril
* incomplete = only part of the lip is involved

---

### Why it happens

During embryology (development of the embryo), the upper lip forms when facial prominences fuse.

Key failure:

* maxillary prominences fail to fuse with medial nasal prominences

That failed fusion leaves an opening in the upper lip.

So the simple intuition:

> the face is built by pieces merging together, and cleft lip happens when the lip pieces do not fully merge.

---

### Cleft lip vs cleft palate

* Cleft lip = defect in the upper lip
* Cleft palate = defect in the roof of the mouth

They can happen together, but they are not the exact same defect.

Why feeding can be hard:

* cleft lip can weaken the seal around the nipple
* cleft palate can prevent strong suction because the mouth and nose are connected

---

### Associations

Cleft lip can be isolated, but risk increases with:

* family history
* maternal smoking
* folate deficiency
* some teratogens (substances that disrupt fetal development), like anticonvulsants

---

## One-line intuition

Cleft lip = failure of embryologic facial fusion, leaving a split in the upper lip.

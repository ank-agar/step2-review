# Acute bronchitis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3c/Acute-bronchitis.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19364.jpg)

### Core idea

Acute bronchitis = short-term inflammation of the bronchi (large airways that carry air into the lungs).

Most commonly caused by a virus after a cold or flu-like illness.

So think:

> viral upper respiratory infection moves down into the bronchi → irritated airways → cough.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Cough:** inflamed bronchi become irritated and trigger cough receptors
* **Mucus/phlegm:** inflamed airway lining makes extra mucus
* **Wheezing:** swollen bronchi narrow the airway, so air whistles through
* **Chest soreness:** repeated coughing strains chest wall muscles
* **Low-grade fever/sore throat/runny nose:** usually from the viral infection

Important: yellow or green sputum (mucus coughed up from the airways) does **not automatically mean bacteria**.

---

### Acute vs chronic bronchitis

**Acute bronchitis**

* sudden onset
* usually viral
* lasts days to weeks
* cough is the main symptom

**Chronic bronchitis**

* productive cough for at least 3 months per year for 2 consecutive years
* classically due to smoking
* type of COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease)

---

### Antibiotics?

Usually no.

Why?

Because most acute bronchitis is viral, and antibiotics kill bacteria, not viruses.

Treatment is usually supportive:

* fluids
* rest
* NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) like ibuprofen for pain/fever
* cough control if needed

---

## One-line intuition

Acute bronchitis is basically a temporary “angry, mucus-making bronchial tube” after a viral cold, causing cough more than anything else.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Acute bronchitis usually has a **normal chest X-ray**; if you see focal lung consolidation (solid-looking infected lung area), high fever, or hypoxia (low blood oxygen), think **pneumonia** instead.

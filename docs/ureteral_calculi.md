## Ureteral calculi

### Core idea

Ureteral calculi = stones in the ureter.

* ureter = tube that carries urine from kidney to bladder
* calculi = stones

So this is usually a kidney stone that formed in the kidney, then moved into the ureter and got stuck.

---

### Why it hurts so much

The ureter is a narrow smooth muscle tube.

When a stone gets stuck:

* urine cannot drain well
* pressure builds up behind the blockage
* the ureter spasms trying to push the stone forward

That causes renal colic, meaning severe waves of flank pain from urinary tract obstruction.

Pain often radiates:

* flank to groin
* testicle/labia region

because ureter pain follows the T11-L2 nerve levels.

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* severe colicky flank pain
* hematuria (blood in urine)
* nausea/vomiting
* patient cannot get comfortable

If the stone obstructs urine flow, it can cause hydronephrosis, meaning dilation of the kidney collecting system from backed-up urine.

---

### High-yield stone types

Most kidney/ureter stones are calcium oxalate stones.

Other important types:

* uric acid stones: radiolucent on x-ray, associated with gout or acidic urine
* struvite stones: from urease-positive bacteria, can form staghorn calculi
* cystine stones: due to cystinuria, often hexagonal crystals

Radiolucent means hard to see on plain x-ray.

---

## One-line intuition

A ureteral calculus is basically a small rock stuck in the urine drainage tube, causing pressure buildup and painful ureter spasms.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Ureteral stones commonly lodge at narrow points, especially the ureterovesical junction, meaning where the ureter enters the bladder.

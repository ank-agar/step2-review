## Labyrinthitis

![Image](https://teachmeanatomy.info/wp-content/uploads/The-Membranous-Labyrinth.jpg)

![Image](https://teachmeanatomy.info/wp-content/uploads/Three-Parts-of-the-Bony-Labryinth.jpg)

![Image](https://teachmeanatomy.info/wp-content/uploads/Overview-of-the-Ear-External-Middle-and-Internal.jpg)

![Image](https://teachmeanatomy.info/wp-content/uploads/The-Labyrinthine-Artery.jpg)

Labyrinthitis is inflammation of the inner ear labyrinth.

The labyrinth is the fluid-filled inner ear system that helps with:

* hearing
* balance

It includes:

* cochlea = hearing organ
* vestibule = balance/position organ
* semicircular canals = rotation/head movement sensors

So if the labyrinth gets inflamed, the brain gets bad signals about motion and sound.

---

### Core idea

Labyrinthitis = inner ear inflammation causing:

* vertigo
* nausea/vomiting
* hearing loss
* tinnitus

Tinnitus means ringing/buzzing sound perception when no outside sound is present.

Vertigo means a false spinning sensation.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Vertigo:** inflamed semicircular canals send incorrect movement signals to the brain
* **Nausea/vomiting:** the vestibular system strongly connects to brainstem vomiting centers
* **Hearing loss:** inflammation can involve the cochlea
* **Tinnitus:** irritated cochlear/auditory signaling creates abnormal sound perception
* **Nystagmus:** mismatched vestibular input makes the eyes drift and jerk

Nystagmus means rhythmic involuntary eye movement.

---

### Labyrinthitis vs vestibular neuritis

This is the big testable distinction:

* **Labyrinthitis:** vertigo + hearing symptoms
* **Vestibular neuritis:** vertigo without hearing loss

Why?

Vestibular neuritis mainly inflames the vestibular nerve, which handles balance.

Labyrinthitis inflames the labyrinth itself, which contains both balance and hearing structures.

---

## One-line intuition

The inner ear is sending chaotic balance signals, and if the cochlea is involved too, hearing gets affected.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Acute vertigo with hearing loss points more toward labyrinthitis; acute vertigo without hearing loss points more toward vestibular neuritis.

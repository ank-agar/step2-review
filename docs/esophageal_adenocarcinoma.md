### Core idea

Esophageal adenocarcinoma is a malignant gland-forming cancer of the esophagus, usually in the distal esophagus near the stomach.

Break the word apart:

* esophageal = related to the esophagus
* adeno- = gland
* carcinoma = malignant cancer from epithelium (lining cells)

So:

> gland-forming cancer from the lining of the lower esophagus.

---

### Why it happens

Most Step 1 cases come from chronic GERD (gastroesophageal reflux disease), meaning acid reflux damages the lower esophagus over time.

The sequence is:

* chronic acid injury
* Barrett esophagus
* dysplasia
* adenocarcinoma

Barrett esophagus means the normal squamous epithelium (flat protective lining) is replaced by intestinal-type columnar epithelium (taller gland-like lining).

Why?

The esophagus is trying to adapt to acid exposure, so it switches to a more acid-resistant glandular/intestinal-type lining. But that repeated injury + repair increases mutation risk, which can progress to cancer.

---

### Classic clues

Think:

* older male
* obesity
* long history of GERD (gastroesophageal reflux disease)
* Barrett esophagus
* progressive dysphagia (difficulty swallowing)
* weight loss
* distal esophageal mass

Progressive dysphagia means solids first, then liquids later, because the tumor gradually narrows the esophageal lumen (inside tube space).

---

## One-line intuition

Chronic acid reflux turns the lower esophagus into Barrett mucosa, and Barrett mucosa can mutate into gland-forming cancer.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Esophageal adenocarcinoma is classically linked to GERD (gastroesophageal reflux disease), Barrett esophagus, obesity, and the distal esophagus.

Squamous cell carcinoma of the esophagus is more linked to smoking, alcohol, caustic injury, achalasia, and the upper/middle esophagus.

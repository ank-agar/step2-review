### Core idea

Peyronie disease is scar tissue formation in the tunica albuginea (tough fibrous covering around the erectile tissue of the penis).

That scar tissue is called a plaque (localized fibrous scar, not artery plaque).

---

### Why it causes curvature

During an erection, normal erectile tissue expands.

But the scarred plaque is stiff and does not stretch well.

So one side expands normally, while the scarred side is tethered.

That pulls the penis toward the plaque and causes:

* curvature
* indentation/hourglass narrowing
* painful erections
* sometimes ED (erectile dysfunction, difficulty getting or keeping an erection)

---

### Why the scar forms

Most likely:

* repeated small trauma
* abnormal wound healing
* excess collagen deposition

Collagen (structural scar protein) makes the plaque firm and inelastic.

So the intuition is:

> Peyronie disease = a stiff internal scar that prevents one side from stretching during erection.

---

### Important associations

It is associated with other fibrotic conditions, especially:

* Dupuytren contracture (fibrosis of the palmar fascia causing finger flexion contractures)

That makes sense because both are fibroblast/collagen overgrowth problems.

---

## One-line intuition

Peyronie disease is like a tight scar patch on one side of the tunica albuginea, so the penis bends when it fills with blood.

---

## Pearl

HY Step 1 pearl: Peyronie disease is a localized fibromatosis, classically associated with Dupuytren contracture.

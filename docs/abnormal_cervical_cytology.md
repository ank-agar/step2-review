## Abnormal cervical cytology

Abnormal cervical cytology means abnormal-looking cells were found on a Pap smear/cervical cytology test (test that scrapes cells from the cervix and looks at them under a microscope).

Key idea:

* cytology = looking at cells
* cervical = from the cervix (lower part of the uterus that opens into the vagina)
* abnormal = cells look atypical, often from HPV-related changes

Most important cause is HPV (human papillomavirus), especially high-risk HPV types 16 and 18.

---

### What the Pap smear is detecting

It is looking for dysplasia (disordered cell growth that can become precancerous).

Common result language:

* ASC-US (atypical squamous cells of undetermined significance) = mildly weird cells, unclear significance
* LSIL (low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion) = usually HPV effect/mild dysplasia
* HSIL (high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion) = more concerning precancerous dysplasia
* AGC (atypical glandular cells) = abnormal gland cells, needs more workup

Squamous cells are flat epithelial cells on the outer cervix. Glandular cells are mucus-producing cells from the endocervical canal.

---

### Why HPV causes abnormal cells

High-risk HPV makes viral proteins:

* E6 inhibits p53 (tumor suppressor that normally stops damaged cells)
* E7 inhibits Rb (retinoblastoma protein, a tumor suppressor that normally controls the G1 to S cell cycle checkpoint)

So the cell loses brakes on division.

That leads to:

* larger/darker nuclei
* irregular nuclear shapes
* increased nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio
* dysplasia

---

## One-line intuition

Abnormal cervical cytology means the Pap smear saw cervical cells that look dysplastic, usually because HPV is pushing the cells to divide abnormally.

---

## Pearl 🔥

HPV E6 knocks out p53 and HPV E7 knocks out Rb, which is why high-risk HPV can drive cervical dysplasia and cervical cancer.

# Oral cancer

![Image](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/media-assets/18332/N01752-H_thumbnail.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/45/NIH_DOC_17_Leukoplakia.jpg)

### Core idea

Oral cancer means malignant growth in the mouth/oral cavity.

Most oral cancers are:

* squamous cell carcinoma
* meaning cancer of squamous epithelial cells, which are the flat lining cells of the mouth

Common sites:

* lateral tongue
* floor of mouth
* lower lip
* gums
* buccal mucosa, meaning inner cheek lining

---

### What it looks like clinically

Think:

* nonhealing mouth ulcer
* red patch called erythroplakia
* white patch called leukoplakia
* mouth pain
* bleeding
* loose teeth
* trouble swallowing
* neck lymph node enlargement

Key intuition:

> A mouth sore that does not heal should make you think biopsy, not just “canker sore.”

---

### Why the risk factors matter

Major risk factors:

* tobacco
* alcohol
* betel nut/areca nut chewing
* HPV (human papillomavirus), especially HPV-16

Tobacco and alcohol damage DNA (genetic material) in the squamous lining cells.

Alcohol also makes tobacco carcinogens, meaning cancer-causing chemicals, penetrate tissue more easily.

So together:

* tobacco = DNA mutation source
* alcohol = helps carcinogens enter tissue
* combined use = much higher risk

---

### Step 1 intuition

The mouth is lined by squamous epithelium.

Repeated irritation/mutation causes:

normal squamous cells → dysplasia (disordered precancerous growth) → carcinoma in situ (cancer cells still above the basement membrane) → invasive squamous cell carcinoma

Once it invades below the basement membrane, it can spread to cervical lymph nodes, meaning lymph nodes in the neck.

---

## One-line intuition

Oral cancer is usually squamous cell carcinoma from chronically damaged mouth lining, classically presenting as a nonhealing ulcer or red/white patch.

---

## Pearl

Erythroplakia, meaning a red oral patch, is more concerning for dysplasia/cancer than leukoplakia, meaning a white oral patch.

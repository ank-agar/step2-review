### Core idea

Volume overload means there is too much fluid in the vascular system/body.

Usually this is excess:

* sodium
* water

The body becomes "overfilled," so pressure rises inside blood vessels and fluid leaks into tissues.

---

### Why the findings happen

Think:

> too much fluid in the pipes -> pressure builds up -> fluid gets pushed out

That causes:

* **edema (swelling from fluid in tissues):** fluid leaves capillaries and enters the interstitial space (space between cells)
* **pulmonary edema (fluid in the lungs):** excess fluid backs up into lung vessels and leaks into alveoli (air sacs)
* **dyspnea (shortness of breath):** wet alveoli cannot exchange oxygen well
* **JVD (jugular venous distention; bulging neck veins):** high venous pressure backs up into the neck veins
* **weight gain:** extra salt/water increases total body fluid
* **hypertension sometimes:** more intravascular volume can increase pressure

---

### Common causes

Big ones:

* **CHF (congestive heart failure):** heart cannot pump fluid forward well, so fluid backs up
* **renal failure:** kidneys cannot excrete sodium/water
* **cirrhosis:** low effective arterial volume activates RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system), so body retains sodium/water
* too much IV (intravenous) fluid or blood transfusion

---

### One-line intuition

Volume overload is basically the body acting like the tank is too full, so fluid spills into the lungs, legs, and veins.

---

## Pearl

Loop diuretics like furosemide block the Na-K-2Cl cotransporter (sodium-potassium-2 chloride cotransporter) in the thick ascending limb, so they dump sodium and water and are high yield for treating volume overload.

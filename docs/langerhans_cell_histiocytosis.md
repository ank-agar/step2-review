### Core idea

Langerhans cell histiocytosis, or LCH (Langerhans cell histiocytosis), is a clonal proliferation of Langerhans cells.

Langerhans cells are dendritic antigen-presenting cells, meaning immune cells that show antigens (foreign material pieces) to T cells.

In LCH, these cells build up in tissues and cause destructive inflammatory lesions.

Common sites:

* bone
* skin
* pituitary/hypothalamus
* lung
* lymph nodes
* liver/spleen/bone marrow in severe disease

---

### Classic findings

Think:

* lytic bone lesions
* skin rash
* diabetes insipidus
* Birbeck granules
* CD1a positive
* S100 positive
* langerin/CD207 positive

Lytic bone lesions means areas where bone is being destroyed, so they look like punched-out holes on x-ray.

Diabetes insipidus means low antidiuretic hormone effect, causing lots of dilute urine and intense thirst.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Bone pain/lytic lesions:** Langerhans cells collect in bone and activate inflammation, which destroys bone
* **Skin rash:** abnormal Langerhans cells infiltrate the skin
* **Diabetes insipidus:** infiltration of the pituitary/hypothalamic region disrupts ADH (antidiuretic hormone)
* **Eosinophils in lesions:** the inflammatory environment recruits eosinophils, which is why older terms include eosinophilic granuloma

---

### Microscopy buzzwords

The cells can have:

* grooved “coffee bean” nuclei
* Birbeck granules

Birbeck granules are rod/racket-shaped organelles seen on electron microscopy.

The Step 1 phrase is:

> tennis racket-shaped Birbeck granules

---

## One-line intuition

LCH is when dendritic Langerhans-type cells pile up and chew into tissues, especially bone, skin, and pituitary.

## Atrial flutter

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8b/Atrial_conduction_circles.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c3/Atrial_flutter34.svg)

### Core idea

Atrial flutter is a fast, regular atrial rhythm where the atria (upper heart chambers) fire in a repeating loop.

Usually this loop is a re-entry circuit (an electrical impulse keeps circling back on itself) in the right atrium, often around the tricuspid valve.

---

### What the ECG shows

ECG (electrocardiogram) classically shows:

* sawtooth flutter waves
* best seen in leads II, III, and aVF
* atrial rate around 250-350 bpm (beats per minute), often about 300 bpm

The AV node (atrioventricular node, the electrical gate between atria and ventricles) blocks some of those impulses.

So a classic pattern is:

* atria: 300 bpm
* ventricles: 150 bpm

That is called 2:1 conduction, meaning every 2 atrial beats produces 1 ventricular beat.

---

### Why it happens

Think of the atrium like a race track.

Instead of one normal signal starting at the SA node (sinoatrial node, the heart's natural pacemaker), the signal keeps looping around the atrium again and again.

That repeated loop makes the atria contract very fast and very regularly.

---

### Atrial flutter vs atrial fibrillation

* Atrial flutter = organized, regular loop
* Atrial fibrillation = chaotic, irregular atrial activity

So:

* flutter = regular sawtooth
* fibrillation = irregularly irregular rhythm

---

## One-line intuition

Atrial flutter is a regular electrical loop in the atria that makes sawtooth waves on ECG.

---

## Pearl 🔥

A narrow-complex tachycardia (fast rhythm from above the ventricles) at about 150 bpm should make you suspect atrial flutter with 2:1 AV (atrioventricular) conduction.

### Core idea

Lower gastrointestinal bleeding means bleeding from the lower GI tract (gastrointestinal tract), usually:

* colon
* rectum
* anus

Classically it presents as hematochezia (bright red or maroon blood in stool).

---

### Big causes to know

Common causes:

* diverticulosis (outpouchings of colon wall)
* angiodysplasia (fragile abnormal blood vessels)
* hemorrhoids (swollen rectal/anal veins)
* colorectal cancer
* inflammatory bowel disease, or IBD (chronic gut inflammation)
* ischemic colitis (colon injury from low blood flow)

---

### Why the blood is usually bright red

Blood from the lower GI tract has less time to be digested.

So it often comes out as:

* bright red blood
* maroon stool

Upper GI bleeding (bleeding from esophagus, stomach, or duodenum) has more time to be digested, so it can cause melena (black, tarry stool).

Important exception: a massive upper GI bleed can also cause bright red blood because the blood moves through the gut very fast.

---

### Classic Step 1 intuition

Diverticular bleeding is usually:

* older patient
* painless
* large-volume hematochezia

Why painless?

Diverticula (colon wall outpouchings) can erode into nearby vessels and bleed, but there may be no inflammation, so no pain.

---

### One-line intuition

Lower GI bleeding is blood coming from the colon/rectum/anus, so it usually exits before it gets digested into black stool.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Diverticulosis is the most common cause of major lower GI bleeding in older adults, and it is classically painless.

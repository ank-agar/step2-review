### Core idea

Cauliflower ear is a permanent deformity of the outer ear after trauma.

Usually the sequence is:

* blunt trauma to the ear
* auricular hematoma (blood collection in the outer ear)
* cartilage loses blood supply
* cartilage dies/remodels badly
* lumpy scar tissue/new cartilage forms

That lumpy thickened ear = cauliflower ear.

---

### Why trauma causes it

The outer ear is mostly cartilage.

Cartilage does not have a great blood supply by itself. It depends on the perichondrium (connective tissue layer around cartilage) for nutrients.

When the ear gets hit, blood can collect between:

* cartilage
* perichondrium

That blood pocket separates cartilage from its nutrient supply.

So:

> hematoma blocks nutrition to cartilage -> ischemia (low blood flow) -> necrosis (tissue death) -> disorganized healing.

That disorganized healing is why the ear becomes thick, bumpy, and folded instead of smooth.

---

### Who gets it

Classically seen in contact sports:

* wrestling
* boxing
* martial arts
* rugby

because repeated ear trauma keeps causing hematomas.

---

### Treatment idea

Early auricular hematoma needs:

* drainage of the blood
* compression/pressure dressing

The pressure keeps the perichondrium stuck back down to the cartilage so blood does not reaccumulate.

Once the ear is already fibrosed/scarred, it is much harder to fix.

---

## One-line intuition

Cauliflower ear is what happens when blood lifts the ear cartilage away from its food supply, so it heals back as ugly scarred cartilage.

---

## HY pearl

Relapsing polychondritis (autoimmune inflammation of cartilage) can also affect the ear, but it classically spares the lobule (earlobe) because the lobule has no cartilage.

# Cellulitis

![Image](https://www.mayoclinic.org/-/media/kcms/gbs/patient-consumer/images/2013/08/26/10/40/ds00450_im02430_r7_cellulitisthu_jpg.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/2379.jpg)

Cellulitis is a bacterial infection of the deeper skin layers:

* dermis = deeper layer of skin
* subcutaneous tissue = fat/soft tissue under the skin

Most common bugs:

* Streptococcus pyogenes
* Staphylococcus aureus

Usually it happens when bacteria enter through a break in the skin, like:

* cut
* ulcer
* insect bite
* athlete foot cracks
* surgical wound

---

### What it looks like

Classic findings:

* red
* warm
* swollen
* tender
* poorly demarcated borders

Poorly demarcated means the edge is blurry, not sharply outlined.

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Redness/warmth:** inflammation causes vasodilation (blood vessels widen)
* **Swelling:** leaky inflamed vessels let fluid enter tissue
* **Pain/tenderness:** inflammatory chemicals irritate nerves
* **Fever/chills:** cytokines (immune signaling proteins) tell the hypothalamus (temperature-control center in brain) to raise body temperature

---

### Cellulitis vs erysipelas

Cellulitis:

* deeper infection
* poorly demarcated borders

Erysipelas:

* more superficial infection
* sharply demarcated, raised borders
* usually Streptococcus pyogenes

So the quick visual clue:

> blurry red swollen skin = cellulitis
> sharp raised red plaque = erysipelas

---

## One-line intuition

Bacteria get under the skin, and the immune system floods the area with inflammation, making it hot, red, swollen, and painful.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Orbital cellulitis is dangerous because infection can spread to the cavernous sinus (venous space near the brain) and cause cavernous sinus thrombosis (clot in that venous space).

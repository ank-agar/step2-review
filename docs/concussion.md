# Concussion

A concussion is a mild TBI (traumatic brain injury) where a blow/jolt makes the brain temporarily not work normally.

Key idea:

* the brain is soft
* the skull is hard
* sudden acceleration/deceleration makes the brain stretch, twist, or hit the inside of the skull

So the main problem is usually functional brain disruption, not a big visible structural lesion.

---

### What happens in the brain

After the hit, neurons (nerve cells) get stretched.

That causes:

* ion shifts, especially K+ (potassium) leaving cells
* Ca2+ (calcium) entering cells
* increased ATP (adenosine triphosphate, the cell's energy currency) demand
* temporary energy mismatch

Intuition:

The brain is trying to reset its electrical balance, but that takes energy, so the patient feels foggy, dizzy, tired, and slow.

---

### Common symptoms

Concussion can cause:

* headache
* dizziness
* confusion
* nausea/vomiting
* blurry vision
* photophobia (light sensitivity)
* phonophobia (sound sensitivity)
* trouble concentrating
* memory problems
* sleep changes
* irritability or mood changes

Important:

You do not need LOC (loss of consciousness) to have a concussion.

---

### Imaging

CT (computed tomography) or MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) is often normal in uncomplicated concussion.

Why?

Because concussion is usually a microscopic/functional injury, not a big bleed or mass that imaging can easily see.

---

## One-line intuition

A concussion is when a sudden hit shakes the brain enough to temporarily scramble neuron function, even if the scan looks normal.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Second impact syndrome = a second head injury before the first concussion fully heals, causing dangerous cerebral edema (brain swelling), classically in young athletes.

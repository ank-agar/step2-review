### Core idea

Polymyositis = autoimmune inflammation of skeletal muscle.

Break it down:

* poly- = many
* myo- = muscle
* -itis = inflammation

So it means:

> inflammation of many muscles.

---

### Classic presentation

Symmetric proximal muscle weakness.

Proximal means closer to the trunk, so think:

* shoulders
* hips

Patients have trouble with:

* combing hair
* raising arms
* climbing stairs
* standing up from a chair

Usually no rash. If there is a classic rash, think dermatomyositis.

---

### Why weakness happens

In polymyositis, CD8+ T cells (cytotoxic T lymphocytes) attack muscle fibers.

That causes:

* muscle fiber injury
* leakage of muscle enzymes into blood
* weak inflamed muscle

So labs show increased CK (creatine kinase), because damaged muscle cells spill CK into the blood.

---

### Biopsy clue

Muscle biopsy shows endomysial inflammation.

Endomysium = connective tissue around individual muscle fibers.

So the Step 1 image is:

* CD8+ T cells
* invading individual muscle fibers
* endomysial inflammation

---

### Important associations

Can be associated with:

* anti-Jo-1 antibody
* ILD (interstitial lung disease)
* dysphagia (difficulty swallowing), due to pharyngeal/esophageal muscle weakness

---

## One-line intuition

Polymyositis is when cytotoxic T cells attack skeletal muscle, causing symmetric proximal weakness and high CK.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Polymyositis = endomysial CD8+ T cell inflammation; dermatomyositis = perimysial/perivascular CD4+ T cell and antibody-mediated inflammation with rash.

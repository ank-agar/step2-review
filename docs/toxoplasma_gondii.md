### Core idea

*Toxoplasma gondii* is an intracellular protozoan parasite (single-celled eukaryotic parasite) that causes toxoplasmosis.

Big Step 1 idea:

* cats are the definitive host (host where sexual reproduction happens)
* humans get infected from cat feces-contaminated food/water or undercooked meat
* it likes to form cysts in brain, muscle, and eye

---

### Life cycle intuition

Cats shed oocysts (environmentally resistant egg-like forms) in feces.

Humans can ingest:

* oocysts from contaminated soil/food/water
* tissue cysts in undercooked meat

Then the parasite spreads as tachyzoites (fast-growing active form), and later hides as bradyzoites (slow-growing dormant form) inside tissue cysts.

So the simple picture is:

> cat feces or undercooked meat -> human infection -> brain/eye/muscle cysts

---

### Why it matters clinically

Most healthy people are asymptomatic or have mild flu-like symptoms.

But it becomes high-yield in:

* AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome): reactivation can cause brain abscesses
* pregnancy: primary maternal infection can cross placenta and infect fetus
* eye disease: can cause chorioretinitis (inflammation of choroid + retina)

---

### Congenital toxoplasmosis

Classic congenital triad:

* chorioretinitis
* hydrocephalus
* intracranial calcifications

Reasoning:

The parasite can cross the placenta during acute maternal infection, then damages fetal brain and eye tissue.

---

### AIDS association

In AIDS, toxoplasmosis commonly causes:

* multiple ring-enhancing brain lesions
* seizures
* focal neurologic deficits

This is usually reactivation of old cysts when CD4 T cells (helper T cells) are low.

Why?

CD4 T cells help keep intracellular infections controlled. When they drop, dormant bradyzoite cysts can reactivate into destructive tachyzoites.

---

## One-line intuition

*Toxoplasma gondii* is the cat-associated parasite that hides in brain/eye/muscle cysts and is especially dangerous in AIDS and pregnancy.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Congenital toxoplasmosis classically causes diffuse intracranial calcifications, while congenital CMV (cytomegalovirus) classically causes periventricular calcifications.

### Core idea

Tobacco use disorder is basically nicotine addiction.

Nicotine binds nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs = receptors normally activated by acetylcholine that are also activated by nicotine).

This increases dopamine in the mesolimbic reward pathway:

* ventral tegmental area (VTA = dopamine neuron area in the midbrain)
* nucleus accumbens (reward/pleasure center)

So the brain learns:

> tobacco = dopamine reward

That is why tobacco becomes reinforcing and hard to stop.

---

### What is withdrawal?

Withdrawal means symptoms that happen when a dependent brain/body suddenly loses the drug it adapted to.

For nicotine withdrawal, think:

* irritability
* anxiety/restlessness
* depressed mood
* trouble sleeping
* trouble concentrating
* increased appetite/weight gain
* strong cravings

Why?

The brain got used to nicotine-driven dopamine stimulation. When nicotine is removed, dopamine signaling feels low, so the patient feels uncomfortable and craves nicotine to normalize it.

---

### Diagnosis intuition

Tobacco use disorder is not just “uses tobacco.”

It means problematic use with impairment, like:

* cannot cut down
* cravings
* tolerance
* withdrawal
* continued use despite health/social problems
* lots of time spent getting/using tobacco

---

### Treatments to know

* Nicotine replacement therapy (NRT = safer nicotine delivery without tobacco smoke): patch, gum, lozenge
* Varenicline: partial nicotinic acetylcholine receptor agonist, so it reduces cravings and blocks some reward from smoking
* Bupropion: norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitor (NDRI = increases norepinephrine and dopamine signaling), also helps depression

---

## One-line intuition

Nicotine hijacks the dopamine reward pathway, and withdrawal is the brain complaining when that nicotine-driven dopamine boost disappears.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For USMLE Step 1, remember: varenicline is a partial agonist at nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, while bupropion is also used for major depressive disorder and smoking cessation.

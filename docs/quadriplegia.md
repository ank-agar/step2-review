# Quadriplegia

![Image](https://www.sciontario.org/wp-content/uploads/SCI_SpinalCordDiagram.jpg)

![Image](https://images.pexels.com/photos/6010899/pexels-photo-6010899.jpeg)

Quadriplegia, also called **tetraplegia**, means weakness or paralysis of **all 4 limbs**:

* both arms
* both legs

Usually this comes from damage to the **cervical spinal cord** (neck-level spinal cord).

---

### Core idea

The cervical spinal cord is like the main highway between the brain and the body.

If the injury is high in the neck, signals from the brain cannot travel normally to:

* arms
* trunk
* legs
* bowel/bladder pathways

So the patient can lose motor function, sensation, and autonomic function below the lesion.

Autonomic function = automatic body control, like blood pressure, sweating, bowel, bladder, and sexual function.

---

### Why arms AND legs are affected

The arms get their nerves from the cervical spinal cord.

The legs are controlled by nerve tracts that pass through the cervical spinal cord on their way down.

So a cervical cord lesion can hit:

* arm pathways directly
* leg pathways as they pass through

That is why cervical injury can cause quadriplegia.

---

### Complete vs incomplete

**Complete spinal cord injury** means no motor or sensory function below the lesion.

**Incomplete spinal cord injury** means some function is preserved below the lesion.

So quadriplegia does **not** always mean zero movement in all limbs. Some patients may still have partial arm, hand, or leg function depending on the exact level and severity.

---

### Common causes

* trauma
* cervical vertebral fracture/dislocation
* spinal cord compression
* tumor
* infection/abscess
* inflammatory disease like transverse myelitis

Transverse myelitis = inflammation across a segment of the spinal cord.

---

## One-line intuition

Quadriplegia usually means a neck-level spinal cord problem blocks brain signals before they can reach both the arms and legs.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

High cervical spinal cord lesions can impair breathing because the **phrenic nerve** comes from **C3-C5** and controls the diaphragm.

Classic memory:

> C3, C4, C5 keep the diaphragm alive.

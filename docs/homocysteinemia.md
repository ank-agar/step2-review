# Homocysteinemia

Homocysteinemia means homocysteine in the blood.

Usually, when people say this clinically, they mean **hyperhomocysteinemia** = elevated homocysteine in the blood.

Homocysteine is a sulfur-containing amino acid made from **methionine**.

Your body normally gets rid of homocysteine by converting it into:

* methionine, using folate and vitamin B12
* cysteine, using vitamin B6

So if those pathways are blocked, homocysteine builds up.

---

### Main causes

High homocysteine can come from:

* folate deficiency
* vitamin B12 deficiency
* vitamin B6 deficiency
* MTHFR mutation
* cystathionine beta-synthase deficiency

MTHFR (methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase) helps make the folate form needed to remethylate homocysteine back to methionine.

Cystathionine beta-synthase is the enzyme that helps convert homocysteine toward cysteine.

---

### Why it matters

Excess homocysteine damages endothelium (blood vessel lining).

That makes blood vessels more thrombogenic, meaning more likely to form clots.

So high homocysteine is associated with:

* arterial thrombosis
* venous thrombosis
* stroke risk
* premature atherosclerosis

Atherosclerosis means plaque buildup in arteries.

---

### Big distinction

**Hyperhomocysteinemia** = high homocysteine in blood.

**Homocystinuria** = homocysteine/homocystine spilling into urine, classically from cystathionine beta-synthase deficiency.

Homocystinuria is the Step 1 disease with:

* developmental delay
* lens dislocation downward and inward
* marfanoid body habitus
* thrombosis

Marfanoid means tall, thin, long-limbed appearance like Marfan syndrome.

---

## One-line intuition

Homocysteine is a methionine-breakdown intermediate, and if folate/B12/B6-dependent cleanup pathways fail, it accumulates and injures blood vessels.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Vitamin B12 deficiency causes increased homocysteine **and** increased methylmalonic acid, while folate deficiency causes increased homocysteine with normal methylmalonic acid.

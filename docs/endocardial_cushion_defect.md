### Core idea

Endocardial cushion defect = AVSD (atrioventricular septal defect), where the middle part of the heart does not form correctly.

The endocardial cushions normally help form:

* lower atrial septum (wall between atria)
* upper ventricular septum (wall between ventricles)
* AV valves (atrioventricular valves: mitral + tricuspid valves)

So if they fail, you can get:

* primum ASD (atrial septal defect: hole between atria)
* inlet VSD (ventricular septal defect: hole between ventricles)
* abnormal AV valves, sometimes one common AV valve

---

### Why the findings happen

Normally, the cushions grow inward and fuse in the center of the heart.

If they do not fuse:

* blood can pass between chambers abnormally
* oxygen-rich blood from the left heart can flow to the right heart
* extra blood goes to the lungs
* lungs and heart get overloaded

That causes:

* pulmonary overcirculation
* heart failure symptoms in infants
* poor feeding / poor weight gain
* tachypnea (fast breathing)
* murmur from turbulent flow

---

### Key association

Strongly associated with Down syndrome (trisomy 21).

So on Step 1, if you see:

* newborn with Down syndrome
* heart failure symptoms
* septal defect involving atria + ventricles

think:

> endocardial cushion defect / AV canal defect.

---

## One-line intuition

The "center wall + AV valve area" of the heart failed to close, so all four chambers can partially communicate.

---

## Pearl

Endocardial cushion defects classically cause an ostium primum ASD (atrial septal defect), not an ostium secundum ASD.

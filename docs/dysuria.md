### Core idea

Dysuria = painful or burning urination.

Think:

* dys- = bad/painful
* -uria = urine/urination

So dysuria literally means:

> painful peeing.

---

### Why it happens

Usually, dysuria happens because the lower urinary tract is inflamed:

* bladder inflammation = cystitis (bladder infection/inflammation)
* urethral inflammation = urethritis (urethra inflammation)

The urethra is the tube that carries urine from the bladder out of the body.

When bacteria or irritation inflame that lining, urine passing over it feels like burning.

---

### Common causes

Most common:

* UTI (urinary tract infection)

Other causes:

* STI (sexually transmitted infection), especially urethritis
* kidney stone irritating the urinary tract
* prostatitis (prostate inflammation) in males
* vaginitis (vaginal inflammation) in females
* chemical irritation, like soaps/spermicides

---

### Classic UTI symptoms with dysuria

Bladder infection often gives:

* burning with urination
* urinary frequency = peeing often
* urinary urgency = sudden strong need to pee
* suprapubic pain = pain above the pubic bone, where the bladder sits

If fever/flank pain (side/back pain near the kidneys) shows up, think upper UTI/pyelonephritis (kidney infection), not just simple cystitis.

---

## One-line intuition

Dysuria is usually urine passing over an inflamed urethra/bladder outlet, so peeing burns.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Nitrites on UA (urinalysis, urine test) suggest nitrate-reducing bacteria, especially gram-negative rods like E. coli, which is the most common cause of uncomplicated UTI (urinary tract infection).

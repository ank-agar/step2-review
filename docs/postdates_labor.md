**Postdates labor** = labor that happens after the due date.

More specific:

* **Late-term pregnancy** = 41 weeks 0 days to 41 weeks 6 days
* **Postterm pregnancy** = 42 weeks or more

So the intuition is:

> the pregnancy has gone past the ideal delivery window, so the placenta may start becoming less reliable.

---

### Why it matters

As pregnancy goes longer, the placenta can start to "age" and become less efficient.

That can cause:

* less oxygen/nutrient delivery to fetus
* oligohydramnios (low amniotic fluid)
* meconium passage in utero
* fetal distress
* macrosomia (large baby), which can make delivery harder

Oligohydramnios happens because fetal urine makes most amniotic fluid late in pregnancy.

So:

> poor placental blood flow -> less fetal kidney perfusion -> less fetal urine -> low amniotic fluid.

Low amniotic fluid also means the umbilical cord has less cushioning, so cord compression becomes more likely.

---

### Classic baby findings

A postmature baby can look:

* thin
* dry/peeling skin
* long nails
* meconium-stained skin or nails

This is basically because the baby has been in the uterus "too long," and if placental function drops, the baby may start using up fat/glycogen stores.

---

### Management idea

Postdates pregnancy is watched closely with fetal testing, like:

* NST (nonstress test: fetal heart rate response to movement)
* BPP (biophysical profile: ultrasound-based fetal well-being score)
* AFI (amniotic fluid index: ultrasound estimate of amniotic fluid)

If fetal testing is bad, or pregnancy reaches true postterm range, delivery is usually recommended.

---

## One-line intuition

Postdates labor = labor after the due date; the big concern is that an aging placenta can lead to low fluid, fetal distress, meconium, and delivery complications.

---

## Pearl

HY Step pearl: Postterm pregnancy increases risk of meconium aspiration syndrome, because fetal stress can cause meconium passage before birth, and the baby can aspirate that meconium into the lungs.

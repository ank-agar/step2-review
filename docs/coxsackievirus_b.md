### Core idea

Coxsackievirus B is an enterovirus, meaning it is a virus that enters through and replicates in the GI tract (gastrointestinal tract).

It is:

* Picornavirus
* positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus
* non-enveloped
* fecal-oral spread

Positive-sense RNA means the viral RNA can act like mRNA (messenger RNA), so host ribosomes can translate it directly into viral proteins.

---

### What it classically causes

Coxsackievirus B is most associated with:

* myocarditis = inflammation of the myocardium (heart muscle)
* pericarditis = inflammation of the pericardium (sac around the heart)
* pleurodynia = sharp pleuritic chest/rib pain from inflamed chest wall muscles
* aseptic meningitis = meningitis symptoms with negative routine bacterial culture

It can also cause neonatal sepsis-like disease, which is dangerous in newborns.

---

### Why heart symptoms happen

Coxsackievirus B can infect cardiac myocytes (heart muscle cells).

So the chain is:

* virus spreads through blood
* reaches myocardium
* infects/injures heart muscle cells
* immune cells attack infected tissue
* heart muscle becomes inflamed and weak

That is why patients can get:

* chest pain
* elevated troponin (cardiac muscle injury marker)
* arrhythmias (abnormal heart rhythms)
* heart failure in severe cases

Troponin rises because injured cardiac myocytes leak their intracellular proteins into the blood.

---

### Coxsackie A vs B

Easy Step 1 split:

* Coxsackie A = hand-foot-mouth disease and herpangina
* Coxsackie B = myocarditis, pericarditis, pleurodynia

Herpangina means painful vesicles/ulcers in the back of the mouth.

---

## One-line intuition

Coxsackievirus B is the enterovirus that starts in the gut but loves to cause inflammation in the heart and chest wall.

---

## HY pearl

Coxsackievirus B is a classic cause of viral myocarditis, which can later progress to dilated cardiomyopathy (enlarged, weak heart chambers) because damaged myocardium remodels and stretches over time.

## Alcohol intoxication

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/62/Ethanol_and_GABA_Receptor.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Ethanol_metabolism.svg/1280px-Ethanol_metabolism.svg.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/da/Relative_risk_of_an_accident_based_on_blood_alcohol_levels.png)

Alcohol intoxication = acute CNS (central nervous system) depression from ethanol.

Core idea:

* alcohol turns up GABA-A (gamma-aminobutyric acid type A receptor), the brain's main inhibitory signal
* alcohol turns down NMDA (N-methyl-D-aspartate) glutamate receptors, one of the brain's main excitatory signals

So:

> more brake + less gas = slowed brain activity.

---

### Classic findings

As BAC (blood alcohol concentration) rises:

* disinhibition/euphoria
* impaired judgment
* slurred speech
* ataxia (poor coordination)
* nystagmus (rhythmic eye movements)
* vomiting
* stupor/coma at very high levels
* respiratory depression in severe poisoning

---

### Why the symptoms happen

* **Disinhibition:** frontal cortex gets suppressed first, so judgment drops
* **Ataxia/slurred speech:** cerebellum gets depressed, so coordination and speech worsen
* **Blackouts:** NMDA blockade impairs memory formation
* **Coma/respiratory depression:** severe CNS depression affects brainstem arousal/breathing centers

---

### Metabolism

Ethanol is mainly metabolized in the liver:

* ethanol --ADH (alcohol dehydrogenase)--> acetaldehyde
* acetaldehyde --ALDH (aldehyde dehydrogenase)--> acetate

This creates lots of NADH (reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide).

High NADH pushes metabolism away from gluconeogenesis (making new glucose), so alcohol can cause hypoglycemia, especially in fasting patients.

---

## One-line intuition

Alcohol intoxication is the brain being pharmacologically slowed down: more inhibition, less excitation, and worse coordination/judgment as BAC rises.

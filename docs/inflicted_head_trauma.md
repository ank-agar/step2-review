### Core idea

Inflicted head trauma = head/brain injury caused intentionally by another person.

In babies and young children, this is often called AHT (abusive head trauma).

Classic mechanism:

* violent shaking
* blunt impact
* shaking + impact

The key idea is:

> the infant brain moves inside the skull, stretching and tearing fragile vessels.

---

### Why babies are high risk

Infants have:

* large heads relative to body size
* weak neck muscles
* soft, developing brain tissue
* fragile bridging veins

Bridging veins are veins that travel from the brain surface to the dural venous sinuses (large venous drainage channels in the dura mater, the tough outer brain covering).

When the head rapidly accelerates and decelerates, the brain lags behind the skull.

That shears the bridging veins and can cause:

* subdural hematoma (blood between dura mater and arachnoid mater)
* cerebral edema (brain swelling)
* brain ischemia (low blood flow/oxygen to brain tissue)
* seizures

---

### Classic findings

Think inflicted head trauma when a young child has neurologic symptoms plus injuries that do not fit the story.

Common findings:

* subdural hemorrhage (bleeding under the dura mater)
* retinal hemorrhages (bleeding in the retina, the light-sensing layer of the eye)
* cerebral edema (brain swelling)
* skull fractures
* bruises in different stages of healing
* rib fractures or metaphyseal fractures

Metaphyseal fractures are fractures near the growth plate of long bones and are highly concerning for child abuse.

---

### Why retinal hemorrhages happen

The retina is attached to the vitreous (gel-like material inside the eye).

With violent acceleration/deceleration:

* the vitreous pulls on the retina
* tiny retinal vessels shear
* bleeding appears in the back of the eye

So retinal hemorrhages are not random eye bleeding.

They are a clue that strong shearing forces may have occurred.

---

### Clinical clues

Symptoms can be nonspecific, so the diagnosis can be missed.

Look for:

* vomiting
* lethargy (abnormally sleepy/low responsiveness)
* seizures
* apnea (stopped breathing)
* poor feeding
* irritability
* bulging fontanelle (tense soft spot on infant skull)
* altered mental status

Important:

* absence of external bruising does not rule it out
* the history may be vague or inconsistent
* no single finding alone proves abuse

Diagnosis depends on the whole clinical picture.

---

### Imaging intuition

CT (computed tomography) is often used first because it quickly shows acute bleeding.

MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) is better for detailed brain injury and dating injury patterns.

Subdural blood often appears crescent-shaped because it spreads along the brain surface under the dura.

---

## One-line intuition

Inflicted head trauma is abusive acceleration/deceleration or impact injury where the infant brain moves inside the skull, tearing bridging veins and retinal vessels.

---

## Pearl

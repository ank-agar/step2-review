Vomiting, or emesis, is forceful expulsion of stomach contents through the mouth.

It is controlled mainly by the medulla oblongata (brainstem area that controls autonomic reflexes), especially the vomiting center and area postrema.

---

### Core idea

Vomiting is a protective reflex:

* body senses toxin/irritation
* medulla coordinates the response
* abdominal muscles + diaphragm contract
* stomach contents are pushed upward and out

So the stomach is not "deciding" alone; the brainstem is coordinating a whole-body reflex.

---

### Why vomiting happens

Common triggers feed into the medulla:

* GI (gastrointestinal) irritation, like infection or food poisoning
* blood-borne toxins/drugs detected by the area postrema
* vestibular system irritation, like motion sickness
* increased ICP (intracranial pressure), meaning increased pressure inside the skull
* pregnancy hormones

The area postrema is important because it has a weak BBB (blood-brain barrier), so it can detect toxins/drugs in the blood.

That is why some drugs cause nausea/vomiting: the brainstem can "sample" the blood there.

---

### What actually moves

During vomiting:

* lower esophageal sphincter relaxes
* diaphragm contracts downward
* abdominal muscles squeeze hard
* pylorus closes, so contents move upward instead of into the duodenum
* stomach contents go up the esophagus and out the mouth

One-line intuition:

> Vomiting is the brainstem hitting the emergency eject button for the stomach.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Area postrema = chemoreceptor trigger zone, and it is outside/weakly protected by the BBB (blood-brain barrier), so it detects blood-borne emetics (vomit-triggering substances).

HY drug tie-in:

* ondansetron blocks 5-HT3 (serotonin type 3) receptors
* metoclopramide blocks D2 (dopamine type 2) receptors

Both reduce vomiting signals going into the medullary vomiting pathway.

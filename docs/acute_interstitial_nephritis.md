### Core idea

Acute interstitial nephritis is inflammation of the kidney interstitium.

Interstitial = the space between the kidney tubules.

Nephritis = kidney inflammation.

So acute interstitial nephritis means:

> sudden inflammation in the space around the renal tubules.

---

### What causes it?

Most commonly: drug hypersensitivity reaction.

Classic drugs:

* beta-lactams (penicillin-like antibiotics)
* NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs)
* PPIs (proton pump inhibitors, acid-reducing drugs)
* diuretics

The immune system reacts to the drug and attacks the tubulointerstitium (tubules + surrounding interstitial tissue).

---

### Why the findings happen

Inflamed interstitium damages nearby tubules, so you get acute kidney injury.

Acute kidney injury means the kidneys suddenly filter worse, so creatinine rises.

Classic findings:

* fever
* rash
* eosinophilia
* sterile pyuria
* white blood cell casts

Eosinophilia = increased eosinophils, which are allergy/parasite-type white blood cells.

Sterile pyuria = white blood cells in urine without bacteria growing on culture.

Why sterile pyuria?

The kidney is inflamed, so white blood cells enter the urine, but it is not necessarily a bacterial infection.

---

### Biopsy idea

Kidney biopsy shows:

* interstitial edema
* inflammatory cells
* eosinophils
* tubulitis

Tubulitis = inflammatory cells invading the renal tubules.

---

## One-line intuition

Acute interstitial nephritis is basically an allergic-type drug reaction in the kidney interstitium, causing swollen inflamed tissue around tubules and acute kidney injury.

---

## Pearl 🔥

AIN (acute interstitial nephritis) classically causes the triad of fever, rash, and eosinophilia, but all 3 together are not always present.

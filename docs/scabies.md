# Scabies

Scabies is a contagious skin infestation caused by **Sarcoptes scabiei** (the human itch mite).

Core idea:

* tiny mites burrow into the stratum corneum (outer dead layer of skin)
* female mites lay eggs in the burrows
* your immune system reacts to the mites, eggs, and feces
* that immune reaction causes intense itching

---

### Classic presentation

Scabies causes:

* severe itching, especially at night
* small papules (raised bumps)
* burrows, which look like thin wavy/serpiginous lines
* excoriations (scratch marks)

Common locations:

* finger webs
* wrists
* elbows
* axillae (armpits)
* waistline
* genitals

---

### Why itching is worse at night

It is not because the mites are dramatically “more active” only at night.

It is mostly because:

* warmth in bed increases itching
* fewer distractions make itching feel stronger
* the immune reaction is already primed against mite antigens (foreign proteins)

So think:

> scabies = mite burrow + hypersensitivity itch.

---

### Timing

First infection:

* symptoms may take weeks to appear

Reinfection:

* symptoms can appear much faster

Why?

Because the immune system has already been sensitized (trained to recognize the mite antigens).

---

### Diagnosis

Usually clinical:

* intense nocturnal pruritus (itching)
* burrows
* classic distribution
* close contacts also itchy

Can confirm with skin scraping showing:

* mite
* eggs
* fecal pellets

---

### Treatment

Main treatment:

* permethrin cream

Alternative:

* oral ivermectin

Important:

* treat close contacts at the same time
* wash bedding/clothes or seal them away for several days

Otherwise, people keep passing it back and forth.

---

## One-line intuition

Scabies is a tiny mite tunneling in the outer skin, but the miserable itching is mainly your immune system reacting to the mite stuff.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Crusted scabies, also called Norwegian scabies, occurs especially in immunocompromised patients and has **tons of mites** but may have surprisingly little itching.

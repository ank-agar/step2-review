### Core idea

A thyroid cyst is a fluid-filled nodule inside the thyroid gland.

Think:

* thyroid nodule = lump in the thyroid
* cyst = fluid-filled space

So a thyroid cyst is basically a thyroid lump that is mostly fluid.

---

### Why it happens

Most thyroid cysts come from degeneration of a benign thyroid nodule.

Benign means non-cancerous.

The nodule can break down internally and leave a fluid pocket.

Sometimes the fluid is:

* clear fluid
* colloid, which is thick thyroid protein material
* blood, if there was bleeding into the cyst

That is why a cyst can suddenly get bigger or painful if it bleeds.

---

### What it looks like

On ultrasound, a simple cyst is usually:

* dark/black because fluid does not reflect many echoes
* smooth-walled
* well-circumscribed, meaning sharply bordered

Ultrasound is the key test because it tells you if a thyroid lump is solid or cystic.

---

### Cancer risk intuition

Purely cystic thyroid nodules are usually benign.

The more solid the nodule is, especially with suspicious features, the more you worry.

Suspicious ultrasound features include:

* irregular borders
* microcalcifications, meaning tiny calcium deposits
* taller-than-wide shape
* hypoechoic appearance, meaning darker than surrounding thyroid tissue

---

## One-line intuition

A thyroid cyst is a fluid-filled thyroid nodule, and fluid-filled usually means less scary than solid.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, remember: a thyroid nodule with low TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone) is evaluated with radioactive iodine uptake because a “hot” nodule is usually benign, while a “cold” nodule has higher malignancy concern.

### Core idea

Epidermolysis bullosa means:

* epidermolysis = breakdown/separation of epidermis (outer skin layer)
* bullosa = blister-forming

So it is a group of inherited skin-fragility disorders where minor trauma causes blisters.

---

### Why does the skin blister?

Normally, the epidermis (outer skin layer) is anchored tightly to the dermis (deeper skin layer).

In epidermolysis bullosa, mutations damage structural proteins that act like “glue” or “rivets” between skin layers.

So when the skin is rubbed:

* the layers separate too easily
* fluid fills the space
* a blister forms

---

### Main types

The type depends on where the skin splits:

* **Epidermolysis bullosa simplex:** split within the epidermis
* **Junctional epidermolysis bullosa:** split at the basement membrane zone (layer between epidermis and dermis)
* **Dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa:** split below the basement membrane, in upper dermis

Deeper split = more scarring.

---

### Classic findings

* fragile skin
* blisters after minor trauma
* erosions/ulcers
* scarring, especially in dystrophic forms
* nail loss or deformity
* mucosal blistering sometimes, meaning blisters in mouth/esophagus (food tube)

---

### Intuition

Think of the skin like wallpaper glued to a wall.

In epidermolysis bullosa, the glue is defective, so even gentle rubbing can peel the “wallpaper” away and create a blister.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa is classically due to defective type VII collagen, which normally forms anchoring fibrils that attach the epidermis to the dermis.

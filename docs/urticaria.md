### Core idea

Urticaria means **hives**: itchy, raised, red or pale skin welts caused by **superficial dermal edema** (fluid buildup in the upper dermis, which is the skin layer under the epidermis).

The key cell is the **mast cell** (allergy cell packed with histamine-containing granules).

---

### What is happening

Trigger hits mast cells:

* allergen
* infection
* drug
* food
* cold/heat/pressure
* sometimes unknown

Mast cells release **histamine** (inflammatory chemical that makes vessels leaky and itchy).

Histamine causes:

* vasodilation (wider blood vessels) -> redness
* increased vascular permeability (leaky vessels) -> swelling/wheal
* nerve irritation -> itching

---

### Why the lesions look like that

The swelling is **shallow**, so you see raised skin welts.

Each hive usually moves around and fades within **less than 24 hours** because the fluid leak is temporary.

That is different from a fixed rash that stays in the exact same place for days.

---

### Angioedema connection

Angioedema means deeper swelling in the skin or mucosa (moist lining like lips, mouth, airway).

Same basic idea as urticaria, but deeper:

* urticaria = superficial dermis -> itchy wheals
* angioedema = deeper dermis/subcutaneous tissue -> swollen lips/eyelids/tongue, often less itchy

---

## One-line intuition

Urticaria is mast cells dumping histamine into skin, making tiny vessels leaky, so fluid puffs up the superficial dermis into itchy wheals.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Step 1 loves this: **acute urticaria is usually histamine-mediated**, so it responds to H1 antihistamines (histamine-1 receptor blockers), while dangerous airway angioedema is an emergency because swelling can obstruct breathing.

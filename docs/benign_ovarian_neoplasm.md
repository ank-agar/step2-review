### Core idea

A benign ovarian neoplasm means:

* benign = not invasive and not metastatic
* ovarian = arising from the ovary
* neoplasm = abnormal clonal growth of cells, basically a tumor

So it is an ovarian tumor that grows abnormally but does **not** invade nearby tissue or spread to distant sites.

---

### Common types

Benign ovarian tumors can come from different ovarian cell types:

* **Epithelial tumors**: from surface lining cells, like serous cystadenoma or mucinous cystadenoma
* **Germ cell tumors**: from egg-forming cells, like mature cystic teratoma
* **Sex cord-stromal tumors**: from ovarian support/hormone-producing tissue, like fibroma

The big Step 1 one is:

* **Mature cystic teratoma** = benign germ cell tumor that can contain tissues like hair, teeth, fat, or skin

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Pelvic mass/fullness:** the tumor physically takes up space
* **Pain:** stretching of the ovarian capsule or pressure on nearby structures
* **Torsion risk:** a heavy ovary can twist on its blood supply
* **Usually no spread:** benign cells grow locally but do not invade basement membrane or metastasize

Torsion means twisting of an organ around its vascular pedicle (blood supply stalk), which can cut off venous drainage first, then arterial flow.

---

### One-line intuition

Think: an ovarian tumor can be scary-looking or large, but if it is **benign**, it is a local growth problem, not a spreading cancer problem.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Mature cystic teratoma is the most common benign ovarian germ cell tumor and is especially associated with ovarian torsion.

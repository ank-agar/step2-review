## Rotavirus enteritis

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Rotavirus_virion_image.svg/960px-Rotavirus_virion_image.svg.png)

![Image](https://nigms.nih.gov/sites/nigms/files/styles/wide/public/image-and-video-gallery/Carragher_Rota_Virus.jpg?itok=P68L4o7K)

Rotavirus enteritis means inflammation/infection of the small intestine caused by rotavirus.

Break it down:

* rota = wheel-like virus shape
* enteritis = inflammation of the intestine

So:

> rotavirus infects the small intestine and causes watery diarrhea, especially in young children.

---

### Core idea

Rotavirus infects mature enterocytes (absorptive intestinal cells) at the tips of small intestinal villi.

Villi are finger-like projections that increase absorption.

When rotavirus damages those villi:

* less absorption of water/electrolytes
* less brush border enzyme function
* more fluid stays in the gut lumen
* watery diarrhea happens

---

### Why it causes diarrhea

There are 2 big mechanisms:

* **Malabsorptive diarrhea:** damaged villi cannot absorb properly
* **Secretory diarrhea:** NSP4 (nonstructural protein 4) acts like a viral enterotoxin (toxin that affects the intestine), causing chloride and water secretion

Reasoning:

If villi are damaged, the gut loses surface area.

Less surface area = less absorption.

So water stays inside the intestine and comes out as diarrhea.

---

### Classic presentation

Usually in infants/young children:

* vomiting first
* fever
* watery, non-bloody diarrhea
* dehydration risk

Non-bloody matters because this is usually a viral, toxin/absorption problem, not invasive bloody colitis.

---

### Transmission

Spread is fecal-oral.

That means virus from stool gets transmitted through contaminated:

* hands
* surfaces
* food/water
* daycare settings

Rotavirus is very contagious.

---

## One-line intuition

Rotavirus knocks out the small intestinal villi, so the gut cannot absorb water well and also secretes extra fluid.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Rotavirus is prevented by a live attenuated oral vaccine (weakened virus vaccine given by mouth), but avoid live vaccines in severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID, severe T-cell and B-cell immunodeficiency).

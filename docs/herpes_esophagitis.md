# Herpes esophagitis

Herpes esophagitis = infection/inflammation of the esophagus by HSV (herpes simplex virus).

It classically happens in immunocompromised patients, like:

* HIV/AIDS (human immunodeficiency virus/acquired immunodeficiency syndrome)
* transplant patients on immunosuppression
* chemotherapy patients

But it can rarely happen in immunocompetent people too.

---

### Core idea

HSV infects the squamous epithelial cells lining the esophagus.

That causes painful ulcers, so the patient gets:

* odynophagia = painful swallowing
* dysphagia = trouble swallowing
* chest pain

Intuition:

The esophagus becomes ulcerated, so every swallow scrapes over painful viral sores.

---

### Endoscopy finding

EGD (esophagogastroduodenoscopy, camera exam of the esophagus/stomach/duodenum) shows:

* multiple small, punched-out ulcers
* normal mucosa between ulcers

This is different from Candida esophagitis, which usually shows white plaques.

---

### Histology finding

HSV causes the classic 3 Ms:

* multinucleation
* nuclear molding
* chromatin margination

You can also see Cowdry type A inclusions (eosinophilic/pink viral inclusions inside the nucleus).

Why?

HSV is a DNA virus, so it replicates in the nucleus and messes up nuclear appearance.

---

## One-line intuition

Herpes esophagitis is HSV ulcerating the esophagus, causing very painful swallowing, especially in immunocompromised patients.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Biopsy HSV ulcers from the ulcer edge, not the center, because infected viable squamous cells are at the margin.

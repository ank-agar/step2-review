# Mixed connective tissue disease

Mixed connective tissue disease is an autoimmune overlap disease.

Autoimmune means the immune system attacks the body's own tissue.

Overlap means it has features of multiple diseases, especially:

* SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus)
* systemic sclerosis/scleroderma (hardening and fibrosis of skin/organs)
* polymyositis (inflammatory muscle disease causing proximal muscle weakness)

So the intuition is:

> Mixed connective tissue disease = lupus-like + scleroderma-like + myositis-like symptoms in one patient.

---

### Classic antibody

The big test association is:

* anti-U1 RNP antibody

RNP means ribonucleoprotein, which is an RNA-protein complex in the nucleus.

So the immune system is making antibodies against a nuclear RNA-protein particle.

---

### Classic findings

Common findings include:

* Raynaud phenomenon (fingers/toes turn white or blue from cold/stress-induced vasospasm)
* swollen or puffy fingers
* arthritis (joint inflammation)
* myositis (muscle inflammation)
* esophageal dysmotility (poor esophagus movement, causing reflux/dysphagia)
* pulmonary hypertension (high blood pressure in lung arteries)

Pulmonary hypertension is especially important because it can be a major cause of morbidity/mortality.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

The immune system attacks connective tissue and small vessels.

That explains:

* Raynaud phenomenon: small blood vessels spasm, so fingers lose blood flow temporarily
* puffy fingers: inflammation/edema in skin and soft tissue
* arthritis: immune inflammation in joints
* muscle weakness: inflammatory damage to skeletal muscle
* esophageal problems: scleroderma-like fibrosis affects smooth muscle movement

---

## One-line intuition

Mixed connective tissue disease is an anti-U1 RNP autoimmune overlap syndrome that combines SLE-like, scleroderma-like, and polymyositis-like features.

---

## Pearl 🔥

For Step 1, if you see Raynaud phenomenon + puffy hands + muscle weakness + anti-U1 RNP, think mixed connective tissue disease.

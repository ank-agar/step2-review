## Onychomycosis

### Core idea

Onychomycosis = fungal infection of the nail.

Break the word apart:

* onycho- = nail
* mycosis = fungal infection

So:

> fungus growing in/under the nail plate.

The nail plate means the hard keratin nail itself.

---

### What it looks like

Classically causes nails that are:

* yellow-white or brown
* thickened
* brittle/crumbly
* distorted
* separated from the nail bed

Nail bed = skin underneath the nail.

Separation of the nail from the nail bed is called **onycholysis**.

---

### Why the nail gets thick and crumbly

Most cases are caused by dermatophytes, which are fungi that like keratin.

Keratin = tough structural protein in:

* skin
* hair
* nails

The fungus digests/invades keratin in the nail, so the nail becomes:

* discolored because the normal clear nail plate is disrupted
* thick because debris builds up under the nail
* crumbly because the keratin structure is weakened

---

### Common association

Often occurs with tinea pedis, which means athlete's foot.

Reason:

The same dermatophyte fungus can infect the skin of the foot first, then spread into the toenail.

---

### Diagnosis

Usually confirmed with:

* KOH prep (potassium hydroxide preparation)
* fungal culture
* PAS stain (periodic acid-Schiff stain)

KOH dissolves human skin/nail cells and leaves fungal elements easier to see.

---

### Treatment idea

Topical treatments often fail because the nail plate is thick and hard for medication to penetrate.

Oral terbinafine is commonly used because it gets into the nail as it grows out.

---

## One-line intuition

Onychomycosis is basically "athlete's foot fungus invading the keratin nail," making it yellow, thick, brittle, and crumbly.

---

## HY pearl

Terbinafine treats dermatophyte infections by inhibiting **squalene epoxidase**, which blocks ergosterol synthesis.

Ergosterol = the fungal cell membrane sterol, like cholesterol in human cell membranes.

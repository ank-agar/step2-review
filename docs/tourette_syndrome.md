# Tourette syndrome

### Core idea

Tourette syndrome is a neurodevelopmental tic disorder.

It means the patient has:

* multiple motor tics
* at least one vocal tic
* symptoms for more than 1 year
* onset before age 18

A tic is a sudden, repetitive, nonrhythmic movement or sound.

Examples:

* motor tic: eye blinking, shoulder shrugging, facial grimacing
* vocal tic: throat clearing, sniffing, grunting, repeating words

Important: vocal tic does **not** have to mean saying words. It can just be a sound.

---

### Why the tics happen

Think of Tourette syndrome as abnormal "movement filtering."

The basal ganglia (deep brain nuclei that help start/stop movements) normally help suppress unwanted movements.

In Tourette syndrome, circuits involving:

* cortex
* basal ganglia
* thalamus

do not filter motor signals normally.

So unwanted motor programs slip through:

* blink
* jerk
* shrug
* throat clear

That is why tics feel involuntary or hard to resist.

---

### Premonitory urge

Many patients feel a premonitory urge before the tic.

Premonitory urge = uncomfortable internal sensation before a tic.

The tic temporarily relieves that feeling.

Intuition:

> like an itch building up, and the tic is the scratch.

---

### Associations

Tourette syndrome is commonly associated with:

* OCD (obsessive-compulsive disorder)
* ADHD (attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder)

This makes sense because basal ganglia-frontal circuits are involved in:

* movement control
* impulse control
* repetitive thoughts/behaviors
* attention regulation

---

### Treatment idea

First-line behavioral treatment is CBIT (comprehensive behavioral intervention for tics).

If severe, medications can include dopamine-blocking drugs.

Why dopamine blockers?

Dopamine helps promote movement signaling in basal ganglia circuits, so blocking dopamine D2 receptors (dopamine type 2 receptors) can reduce tic intensity.

---

## One-line intuition

Tourette syndrome is a childhood-onset disorder where the brain has trouble suppressing unwanted movements/sounds, causing motor and vocal tics.

---

## Pearl

Coprolalia (involuntary obscene words) is classic in media but **not required** for Tourette syndrome and is present in only a minority of patients.

## Cerebral aneurysm

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/12/Aneurysem.jpg)

### Core idea

A cerebral aneurysm is a balloon-like outpouching of a weakened artery wall in the brain.

Most are **saccular aneurysms**, also called **berry aneurysms**, because they look like a little berry coming off the vessel.

Break it down:

* cerebral = brain
* aneurysm = abnormal dilation/outpouching of a blood vessel

So:

> a weak spot in a brain artery balloons outward.

---

### Why it matters

The danger is rupture.

If it ruptures, blood leaks into the **subarachnoid space** (space between the arachnoid mater and pia mater around the brain), causing **subarachnoid hemorrhage**.

Classic presentation:

* sudden “worst headache of life”
* neck stiffness
* nausea/vomiting
* loss of consciousness sometimes

Why neck stiffness?

Blood irritates the meninges (brain coverings), causing meningeal irritation.

---

### Where they usually form

Cerebral aneurysms usually form at arterial branch points in the **Circle of Willis** (arterial ring at the base of the brain).

Common sites:

* anterior communicating artery
* posterior communicating artery
* middle cerebral artery bifurcation

Why branch points?

Blood flow hits those areas with high shear stress, so a weak vessel wall can gradually balloon outward.

---

### Associations

Think conditions that weaken vessel walls:

* autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease
* Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (collagen disorder causing hyperextensible skin/joints and fragile vessels)
* smoking
* hypertension

---

## One-line intuition

A cerebral aneurysm is a weak spot in a brain artery that balloons out and can pop, spilling blood into the subarachnoid space.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Berry aneurysm rupture is the classic cause of spontaneous subarachnoid hemorrhage, and Step 1 loves the clue: **sudden worst headache of life**.

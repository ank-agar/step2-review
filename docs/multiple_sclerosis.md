### Core idea

Multiple sclerosis (MS) is an autoimmune demyelinating disease of the central nervous system (CNS = brain, spinal cord, and optic nerves).

Autoimmune means the immune system attacks the body’s own tissue.

Demyelinating means it damages myelin, which is the fatty insulation around axons (nerve fibers).

So MS is basically:

> immune system attacks CNS myelin → nerve signals slow down or fail → neurologic symptoms.

---

### Why the symptoms happen

Myelin is like insulation on an electrical wire.

If the insulation gets stripped off:

* the signal travels slower
* the signal can “leak”
* the axon can eventually get damaged

That is why MS symptoms can be scattered and weird:

* optic neuritis (inflammation of the optic nerve) → painful vision loss
* spinal cord lesions → weakness, numbness, bladder problems
* cerebellar lesions (cerebellum = coordination center) → ataxia (uncoordinated movement), tremor
* brainstem lesions → diplopia (double vision), dizziness

The symptoms depend on where the plaques/lesions are.

---

### Why it is called “multiple sclerosis”

Break it down:

* multiple = many areas
* sclerosis = scarring/hardening

So:

> many scarred plaques in the CNS.

These plaques are areas where inflammation destroyed myelin and left scar-like tissue behind.

---

### Classic Step 1 associations

Think:

* young woman
* neurologic symptoms separated in time and space
* worse with heat
* optic neuritis
* internuclear ophthalmoplegia (INO = impaired eye adduction with nystagmus of the abducting eye)
* MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) showing periventricular plaques
* CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) with oligoclonal IgG bands

“Separated in time and space” means:

* time = attacks happen at different times
* space = lesions affect different CNS locations

---

### One-line intuition

MS is the immune system stripping insulation off CNS nerves, causing scattered episodes of neurologic dysfunction.

---

## Pearl 🔥

MS affects oligodendrocytes, because oligodendrocytes make CNS myelin.

Guillain-Barre syndrome (GBS = acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy) affects Schwann cells, because Schwann cells make peripheral nervous system (PNS = nerves outside the brain and spinal cord) myelin.

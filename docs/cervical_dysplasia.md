## Cervical dysplasia

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/17034.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/ff/Blausen_0221_CervicalDysplasia.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/14/Spectrum_of_SIL_cervical_dysplasia.png)

Cervical dysplasia means abnormal, precancerous cell growth on the cervix (the lower part of the uterus that opens into the vagina).

It is not cancer yet, but it can progress to cervical cancer if the abnormal cells keep accumulating mutations.

---

### Core idea

The cervical epithelium (surface lining cells of the cervix) starts becoming disorganized:

* cells look atypical
* cells mature abnormally
* cells pile up through the thickness of the epithelium

The more of the epithelial thickness involved, the more severe the dysplasia.

---

### Main cause

Most cervical dysplasia is caused by high-risk HPV (human papillomavirus), especially:

* HPV 16
* HPV 18

Why HPV causes dysplasia:

* HPV E6 protein inhibits p53 (tumor suppressor protein that helps trigger DNA repair or apoptosis)
* HPV E7 protein inhibits Rb (retinoblastoma protein, a tumor suppressor that normally blocks excess cell-cycle progression)

So the cell loses important brakes on growth.

---

### CIN grading

CIN (cervical intraepithelial neoplasia) is the biopsy term for cervical dysplasia.

* CIN 1 = mild dysplasia, lower 1/3 of epithelium
* CIN 2 = moderate dysplasia, lower 2/3 of epithelium
* CIN 3 = severe dysplasia/full-thickness dysplasia

Think:

> higher CIN number = more epithelial thickness replaced by abnormal cells.

---

### Pap smear wording

Pap smear (cervical screening test that samples cells from the cervix) usually reports SIL (squamous intraepithelial lesion).

* LSIL (low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion) usually corresponds to CIN 1
* HSIL (high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion) usually corresponds to CIN 2 or CIN 3

---

## One-line intuition

Cervical dysplasia is HPV-driven abnormal growth of cervical squamous cells before they invade through the basement membrane.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Carcinoma in situ (cancer cells still above the basement membrane) becomes invasive carcinoma only when malignant cells break through the basement membrane.

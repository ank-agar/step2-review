# Dizziness

![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK557380/bin/Vestibulocochlear__System.jpg)

![Image](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/images/ency/fullsize/19706.jpg)

Dizziness is a vague symptom meaning the patient feels abnormal balance, motion, or orientation.

It can mean a few different things:

* **Vertigo:** false sensation of spinning/movement
* **Presyncope:** feeling like you might faint
* **Disequilibrium:** feeling off-balance while walking
* **Lightheadedness:** nonspecific woozy feeling

---

### Core idea

Balance comes from 3 main inputs:

* inner ear vestibular system
* vision
* proprioception (body position sense from muscles/joints)

Your brain compares all 3.

If one input disagrees with the others, you feel dizzy.

---

### Why inner ear problems cause vertigo

The semicircular canals sense head rotation using fluid movement.

When your head moves:

* fluid bends hair cells
* hair cells send motion signals to the brain

But if the inner ear sends a false motion signal while you are still, the brain thinks:

> “The body is moving.”

That mismatch causes:

* spinning sensation
* nausea/vomiting
* nystagmus (rhythmic involuntary eye movements)
* imbalance

---

### Common causes

Peripheral causes = inner ear/vestibular nerve:

* BPPV (benign paroxysmal positional vertigo): brief vertigo triggered by head position changes
* vestibular neuritis (inflammation of the vestibular nerve): acute severe vertigo, usually no hearing loss
* labyrinthitis (inner ear inflammation): vertigo + hearing symptoms
* Meniere disease: episodic vertigo + tinnitus (ear ringing) + hearing loss + ear fullness

Central causes = brainstem/cerebellum:

* stroke
* multiple sclerosis
* tumor
* migraine

Non-vestibular causes:

* orthostatic hypotension (blood pressure drops when standing)
* anemia (low red blood cell/hemoglobin level)
* hypoglycemia (low blood glucose)
* arrhythmia (abnormal heart rhythm)
* medications
* anxiety/panic

---

### One-line intuition

Dizziness happens when the brain gets unreliable balance information, especially from the inner ear, eyes, blood pressure, or cerebellum.

---

### Red flags

Worry more about central causes if dizziness comes with:

* weakness
* slurred speech
* double vision
* severe headache
* inability to walk
* direction-changing nystagmus

These point toward brainstem/cerebellar pathology, not just inner ear disease.

---

## Pearl

For Step 1, vertigo + hearing loss points more toward **labyrinthitis or Meniere disease**, while vertigo without hearing loss after a viral illness points more toward **vestibular neuritis**.

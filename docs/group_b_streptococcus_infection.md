# Group B Streptococcus infection

![Image](https://www.cdc.gov/group-b-strep/media/images/StrepB.jpg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Streptococcus_group_b.JPG/960px-Streptococcus_group_b.JPG)

### Core idea

Group B Streptococcus infection means infection caused by **Streptococcus agalactiae**, also called **GBS (group B Streptococcus)**.

It is a **gram-positive coccus** (purple round bacterium) that often lives harmlessly in the **GI tract (gastrointestinal tract)** and **genital tract**.

Big Step 1 association:

* pregnant patient is colonized with GBS
* baby is exposed during vaginal delivery
* newborn develops sepsis, pneumonia, or meningitis

---

### Why newborns get sick

GBS can sit in the maternal vagina without causing symptoms.

During delivery, the baby can inhale or swallow the bacteria.

Then GBS can invade:

* blood -> sepsis (dangerous whole-body inflammatory response to infection)
* lungs -> pneumonia (lung infection)
* meninges -> meningitis (infection of the brain/spinal cord coverings)

Newborns are vulnerable because their immune system is immature, so bacteria can spread fast.

---

### Pregnancy screening/treatment

Pregnant patients are screened at **36-37 weeks**.

If positive, they get **intrapartum antibiotics** (antibiotics during labor), usually penicillin or ampicillin.

Why during labor?

* antibiotics before labor do not work well because GBS can grow back
* antibiotics during labor reduce bacteria the baby is exposed to during delivery

---

### Adult disease

GBS can also cause infection in adults, especially older adults or people with diabetes/immunosuppression (weakened immune system).

Common adult infections:

* bacteremia (bacteria in the blood)
* skin/soft tissue infection
* pneumonia
* UTI (urinary tract infection)

---

## One-line intuition

GBS is usually harmless colonization in the mom, but during birth it can become a serious invasive infection in the newborn.

---

## HY Step 1 pearl

GBS is:

* beta-hemolytic (fully lyses red blood cells on blood agar)
* bacitracin resistant
* CAMP test positive
* major cause of neonatal sepsis, pneumonia, and meningitis

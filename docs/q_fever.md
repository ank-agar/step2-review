### Core idea

Q fever is an infection caused by **Coxiella burnetii**, a **Gram-negative** bacterium (thin cell-wall bacteria that stain pink/red on Gram stain).

It is:

* **zoonotic** (spread from animals to humans)
* **obligate intracellular** (must live inside host cells)
* classically linked to **sheep, goats, and cattle**

The “Q” originally stood for **query**, because the cause was unknown at first.

---

### How people get it

Think:

> farm animal birth products + inhaled dust = Q fever

Coxiella burnetii is shed in:

* placenta
* amniotic fluid (fluid around the fetus)
* urine
* feces
* milk

When these dry out, the bacteria can become airborne. Then people inhale contaminated dust.

That is why Q fever is high-yield in:

* farmers
* veterinarians
* slaughterhouse workers
* people around sheep/goats/cattle giving birth

---

### Why it causes pneumonia and hepatitis

After inhalation, Coxiella reaches the alveoli (tiny lung air sacs).

Macrophages (immune cells that “eat” microbes) take it up, but Coxiella can survive inside them instead of being killed.

So it can cause:

* **atypical pneumonia** (lung infection that often has dry cough and less classic lobar findings)
* **hepatitis** (liver inflammation)
* nonspecific flu-like illness

Common symptoms:

* high fever
* severe headache
* myalgias (muscle aches)
* dry cough
* elevated liver enzymes

---

### Acute vs chronic Q fever

**Acute Q fever:**

* flu-like illness
* atypical pneumonia
* hepatitis

**Chronic Q fever:**

* classically causes **culture-negative endocarditis**

Endocarditis means infection/inflammation of the inner heart lining, especially valves.

Culture-negative means the blood cultures do not grow the organism well, even though infection is present.

Big risk factors for chronic Q fever:

* abnormal heart valves
* prosthetic heart valves
* vascular grafts
* pregnancy
* immunosuppression (weakened immune system)

---

### Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually by serology (blood antibody testing).

Treatment:

* acute Q fever: **doxycycline**
* chronic Q fever: **doxycycline + hydroxychloroquine**

Hydroxychloroquine helps because Coxiella lives in acidic intracellular compartments, and hydroxychloroquine raises the pH (makes it less acidic), helping doxycycline work better.

---

## One-line intuition

Q fever is what happens when you inhale Coxiella-contaminated farm dust, especially from sheep/goat/cattle birth products, leading to fever with atypical pneumonia or hepatitis.

---

## Pearl

Step 1 loves this antibody pattern:

* **acute Q fever:** high **Phase II IgG** (immunoglobulin G antibody)
* **chronic Q fever:** high **Phase I IgG**

Also remember: unlike many rickettsial-type organisms, Coxiella is mainly spread by **inhalation**, not a tick bite.

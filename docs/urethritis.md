### Core idea

Urethritis = inflammation of the urethra.

The urethra is the tube that carries urine from the bladder to the outside of the body.

So urethritis basically means:

> the pee tube is irritated/inflamed.

---

### Why symptoms happen

Inflammation makes the urethral lining swollen and sensitive, so urine passing over it causes:

* dysuria (painful urination)
* burning with urination
* itching/irritation at the urethral opening
* urethral discharge

Discharge happens because infection/inflammation recruits white blood cells and fluid into the urethra.

---

### Common causes

Most high-yield causes are sexually transmitted infections, or STIs (sexually transmitted infections):

* Chlamydia trachomatis
* Neisseria gonorrhoeae
* Mycoplasma genitalium

Noninfectious irritation can also cause it, like trauma, catheters, or chemical irritants.

---

### Gonococcal vs nongonococcal

Gonococcal urethritis = caused by Neisseria gonorrhoeae.

Usually more:

* abrupt
* purulent discharge, meaning thick pus-like discharge
* more obvious symptoms

Nongonococcal urethritis = urethritis not caused by gonorrhea.

Commonly due to Chlamydia trachomatis, and tends to have:

* milder dysuria
* clearer or thinner discharge
* sometimes minimal symptoms

---

### Diagnosis

Use NAAT (nucleic acid amplification test), which detects organism DNA/RNA.

Usually test urine or a urethral/cervical swab for:

* gonorrhea
* chlamydia

---

## One-line intuition

Urethritis is an inflamed pee tube, usually from an STI, causing burning pee and possible discharge.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Chlamydia trachomatis can cause reactive arthritis:

* arthritis
* conjunctivitis (eye inflammation)
* urethritis

Classically associated with HLA-B27 (human leukocyte antigen B27).

### Core idea

Vertebral artery dissection = a tear in the inner lining of the vertebral artery.

The vertebral arteries run up the back of the neck and supply the posterior brain circulation.

Dissection means:

* the intima (inner blood vessel layer) tears
* blood enters the vessel wall
* an intramural hematoma (blood clot inside the vessel wall) forms
* the true lumen (normal blood channel) gets narrowed

So blood flow to the posterior brain can drop.

---

### Why symptoms happen

The vertebral artery supplies the posterior circulation, especially:

* cerebellum
* brainstem
* occipital lobes

So vertebral artery dissection can cause posterior circulation stroke symptoms:

* neck pain/headache
* dizziness/vertigo
* ataxia (poor coordination)
* diplopia (double vision)
* dysarthria (slurred speech)
* dysphagia (trouble swallowing)

Why?

The torn artery wall can either:

* narrow the artery and reduce flow
* form a thrombus (clot) that embolizes (travels downstream)

Either way, posterior brain tissue gets ischemic (low blood flow/oxygen).

---

### Classic setting

Often after neck trauma or sudden neck movement, like:

* chiropractic neck manipulation
* sports injury
* coughing/sneezing
* motor vehicle accident

Also associated with connective tissue disorders, like Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (collagen disorder causing hyperflexible tissue/vessel fragility) or Marfan syndrome (fibrillin disorder causing tall habitus and weak connective tissue).

---

## One-line intuition

The vertebral artery gets a tear in its wall, blood tracks into the wall, and the swollen/clotted wall can block or embolize to the posterior brain.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Young patient + posterior circulation stroke symptoms + new neck pain/headache after neck movement = think vertebral artery dissection.

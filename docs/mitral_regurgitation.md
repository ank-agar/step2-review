## Mitral regurgitation

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/54/Mitral_Regurgitation_scheme.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4c/Mitral_Valve_Prolapse.png)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/08/Mitral_regurgitation_echo_4chamber.jpg)

Mitral regurgitation means the mitral valve is leaky.

The mitral valve sits between:

* left atrium (LA; upper left heart chamber)
* left ventricle (LV; lower left heart chamber)

Normally:

* LA → LV during diastole (heart relaxation/filling)
* LV → aorta during systole (heart contraction/pumping)

In mitral regurgitation:

* during systole, some blood goes backward from LV → LA

So the key idea is:

> the LV squeezes, but blood leaks backward into the LA instead of all going forward into the aorta.

---

### Why the murmur is holosystolic

Holosystolic means throughout all of systole.

Once the LV starts contracting, LV pressure becomes higher than LA pressure, so blood keeps leaking backward across the bad mitral valve for the whole contraction.

That gives:

* holosystolic murmur
* best heard at the apex
* radiates to the axilla

Radiates to the axilla because the mitral valve/left ventricle are oriented so the turbulent sound travels toward the left armpit area.

---

### Why the LA and LV enlarge

The LA receives extra backward blood during systole.

Then during the next diastole, that extra blood returns to the LV.

So:

* LA gets volume overloaded → LA dilation
* LV gets volume overloaded → LV dilation

This is volume overload, not pressure overload.

---

### Classic causes

Common causes include:

* mitral valve prolapse
* rheumatic heart disease
* infective endocarditis (infection of the heart valve)
* papillary muscle rupture after MI (myocardial infarction; heart attack)

Papillary muscles help tether the mitral valve shut. If they rupture after an MI, the valve suddenly cannot close well → acute severe MR (mitral regurgitation).

---

## One-line intuition

Mitral regurgitation is a leaky mitral valve causing blood to shoot backward into the left atrium every time the left ventricle contracts.

---

## Pearl 🔥

Acute MR after MI is classically due to papillary muscle rupture, especially the posteromedial papillary muscle, because it usually has a single blood supply from the PDA (posterior descending artery).

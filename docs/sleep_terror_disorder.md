## Sleep terror disorder

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3e/Sleep_Hypnogram.svg)

![Image](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/db/Sleep_EEG_Stage_4.jpg)

Sleep terror disorder is a **NREM parasomnia**.

NREM = non-rapid eye movement sleep  
Parasomnia = abnormal behavior during sleep

So this is:

> partial arousal out of deep NREM sleep, where the body wakes up in panic but the brain is not fully conscious.

Classic episode:

* child suddenly sits up
* screams or looks terrified
* tachycardia (fast heart rate)
* sweating
* dilated pupils
* hard to comfort
* does not remember it the next morning

---

### Core idea

Sleep terrors happen during **deep slow-wave sleep**, especially **N3 sleep**.

N3 sleep = deepest NREM sleep, also called slow-wave sleep

This usually happens in the **first third of the night**, because that is when deep NREM sleep is most common.

---

### Why they do not remember it

The person is not fully awake.

They are stuck between:

* deep sleep
* partial arousal
* sympathetic activation

Sympathetic activation = fight-or-flight body response

So the body looks terrified, but the conscious memory system is not fully online.

That is why sleep terrors have **little to no dream recall**.

---

### Sleep terror vs nightmare

Sleep terror:

* NREM sleep
* first third of night
* screaming/autonomic symptoms
* confused and hard to wake
* no memory after

Nightmare:

* REM sleep (rapid eye movement sleep)
* later in the night
* vivid scary dream
* wakes up alert
* remembers the dream

---

## One-line intuition

Sleep terror = the body wakes up scared from deep NREM sleep, but the conscious brain does not fully wake up.

---

## HY pearl

Sleep terrors and sleepwalking are both **NREM parasomnias**, usually in children, and usually occur during **slow-wave sleep in the first third of the night**.

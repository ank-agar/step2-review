### Core idea

Cholestasis = decreased or blocked bile flow.

Bile is fluid made by the liver that carries:

* bile acids
* bilirubin
* cholesterol

from the liver/gallbladder into the small intestine.

So cholestasis means bile is getting stuck instead of draining properly.

---

### Two big types

* **Intrahepatic cholestasis:** problem is inside the liver
* **Extrahepatic cholestasis:** problem is outside the liver, usually in the bile ducts

Examples:

* intrahepatic = pregnancy, drugs, primary biliary cholangitis
* extrahepatic = gallstone, pancreatic cancer, cholangiocarcinoma (bile duct cancer)

---

### Why the findings happen

* **Jaundice:** bilirubin cannot leave through bile, so it backs up into blood
* **Dark urine:** conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble, so kidneys pee it out
* **Pale stools:** less bilirubin reaches the gut, so less brown stool pigment forms
* **Pruritus (itching):** bile acids build up in blood/skin
* **Steatorrhea (fatty stools):** less bile reaches intestine, so fat digestion gets worse

Break the word apart:

* chole- = bile
* stasis = standing still / not moving

So:

> bile is not moving.

---

### Lab pattern

Cholestasis classically gives high:

* alkaline phosphatase
* gamma-glutamyl transferase
* direct bilirubin

Direct bilirubin means conjugated bilirubin (bilirubin already processed by the liver).

---

## One-line intuition

Cholestasis is like a traffic jam in bile flow: bile products back up into the body instead of draining into the intestine.

---

## Pearl 🔥

High alkaline phosphatase + high gamma-glutamyl transferase points toward a biliary/cholestatic process, while high alkaline phosphatase alone could also come from bone.

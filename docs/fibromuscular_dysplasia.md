![Image](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK493204/bin/fibromuscular__dysplasia.jpg)

### Core idea

Fibromuscular dysplasia (FMD) is abnormal growth of fibrous and smooth muscle tissue in the wall of medium-sized arteries.

It is:

* nonatherosclerotic (not due to cholesterol plaque)
* noninflammatory (not due to vasculitis)
* classically in young to middle-aged women

Most commonly affects:

* renal arteries
* carotid arteries

---

### Classic finding

Angiography shows:

* **"string of beads"**

Why?

Because the artery has alternating:

* stenosis (narrowing)
* dilation/aneurysm-like bulging

So the vessel looks bumpy instead of smooth.

---

### Why hypertension happens

If FMD affects the renal artery:

* renal artery narrows
* kidney thinks blood pressure is low
* kidney releases renin
* renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS; hormone system that raises blood pressure) activates
* blood pressure increases

So renal artery FMD causes **secondary hypertension**.

---

### Buzzword picture

Young woman + severe hypertension + renal artery stenosis + string of beads =

> fibromuscular dysplasia

---

## One-line intuition

FMD is a bumpy abnormal artery wall disease that narrows arteries, especially renal arteries, causing renovascular hypertension.

---

## Pearl

Fibromuscular dysplasia affects the **distal two-thirds** of the renal artery, while atherosclerosis (cholesterol plaque buildup) usually affects the **proximal/ostial** renal artery near the aorta.
